Superhero box office  ·  2008–2026

The Cape Index

Every Marvel and DC film since 2008, plus Sony’s Spider-Man spin-offs, and what each one made. Worldwide grosses come from Box Office Mojo, checked one film at a time. Any number on the page switches to 2025 dollars.

It also works as a set of worked examples. Flip Design notes on, up in the toolbar, and every chart explains why it’s built the way it is, the form, the colour, and what it can’t tell you.

Act I  ·  The scoreboard

how big, and who
§ 01  /  MARVEL v DC

Marvel has out-grossed every DC film combined, close to four to one

Worldwide gross by release year
Stacked by universe. 2019 peaked at $6.5B; 2020 is the shutdown. 2026 is still partial.
Design note
Question
How has total superhero box office moved year to year, and how does each year split between the studios?
Why this form
A stacked column reads as one total per year (the height) and a split within it (the segments). Separate lines would bury the “how big was the whole year” question; a grouped column would make the year total hard to see.
Choices here
Segments are held apart by a 2 px gap in the page colour, not a stroke, a border is ink that isn’t data. Universe order is fixed, so a colour never jumps position between years. Y-axis labels sit flush left, close to the title.
Watch for
Stacking hides the individual films. Good for the total and rough shares; useless for “which 2021 Marvel film was that.” That’s what the table is for.
Spread of worldwide grosses, by universe
Box spans the 25th–75th percentile; the line is the median; dots are individual films. Marvel’s median film out-grosses roughly three of every four DC films.
Design note
Question
Beyond the averages, how are each universe’s grosses actually spread out?
Why this form
A box plot shows the middle 50%, the median and the tails at once. An average alone would hide that DC’s range is both wider and lower. The dots put every film back on the chart so the summary isn’t a black box.
Choices here
The line is the median, not the mean, one $2.8B film drags a mean around. DCU and Elseworlds have two films each, so they get dots and a median tick only; a quartile box on n = 2 is theatre.
Watch for
With this few films per group, a single film moves the quartiles a lot. Read it as “roughly,” not “precisely.”
§ 02  /  TREEMAP

Only three films have passed $2 billion, and all three are Marvel

Every film is a rectangle, area set by worldwide gross. Endgame leads at $2.80B, then Brand New Day at $2.34B and Infinity War at $2.05B. The largest DC film is Aquaman, at $1.15B.

Design note
Question
Where is the money concentrated across all 63 films at once?
Why this form
A treemap fits every film on one screen with area doing the work. A 57-row bar chart would need a long scroll and lose the sense of proportion. It’s built for “a few things are huge, most are not.”
Choices here
Squarified layout keeps rectangles near square so their areas stay comparable. A label appears only where it fits with padding, a clipped title is worse than none. Colour is universe identity, never a value ramp.
Watch for
People judge area badly, long thin rectangles especially. Use it for the gestalt; use the tooltip or the table for the exact number.

Act II  ·  Over time

cadence, scale, budgets
§ 03  /  BY RELEASE DATE

Marvel put out more films, and bigger ones, through the 2010s

Each film sits on its release date, sized by worldwide gross. The densest, largest run is 2018–19: Infinity War, Captain Marvel, Endgame, Aquaman. The year after is almost blank.

Design note
Question
When did each film come out and how big was it, without films from the same summer hiding each other?
Why this form
A plain timeline stacks dots on top of each other around each release window. The beeswarm nudges them apart vertically so every film stays visible at its real date.
Choices here
Circle area is proportional to gross, the radius scales with the square root, so the big films aren’t exaggerated. Each new circle is pushed to the nearest open spot. Vertical position on its own means nothing.
Watch for
The jitter is cosmetic. Don’t read height as a second variable.
§ 04  /  SCATTER

Marvel has cleared $1 billion twelve times. DC twice, Sony never

Worldwide gross by release date. The only DC films above the billion-dollar line are Aquaman (2018) and Joker (2019); everything else up there is Marvel.

Design note
Question
Is there a trend in film size over 18 years, and which films broke away from the pack?
Why this form
Two continuous axes, date and gross, is the textbook scatter. Reference lines at $1B and $2B give the eye something to measure against.
Choices here
The palette is a colour-blind-safe set, checked pair by pair, but five hues can’t all stay distinct under every kind of colour blindness, so each universe also carries a shape: circle, square, triangle, diamond, plus. Only the six biggest films are labelled; a label on all 63 is noise.
Watch for
No trend line. Fitting one here would invent a relationship the data doesn’t support, the story is the spread and the outliers, not a slope.
§ 05  /  THE BUDGET ARMS RACE

The typical superhero budget has roughly doubled since 2008, before inflation

The median production budget by release year. Iron Man cost $140M in 2008; a mid-tier tentpole now runs $200–250M, and the biggest Avengers films clear $350M. Flip to 2025 dollars to see how much of that is real growth.

Design note
Question
Have production budgets actually climbed once you strip out inflation?
Why this form
One measure over time is a line. The faint dots behind it are the individual films, so you can see the spread the line is summarising.
Choices here
The line is the median budget per year, the mean would swing with a single $350M Avengers. One line, not four: split by universe and the yearly counts get too small to read.
Watch for
Budgets are the softest number on the site. Studios don’t disclose them; anything not straight from Box Office Mojo is an estimate, marked est. in the ledger.

Act III  ·  Beyond the gross

profit, legs, reviews, geography
§ 06  /  DID IT MAKE MONEY?

Eighteen of these films failed to clear their break-even point

Each film’s worldwide gross measured against an estimated break-even of 2.5× its production budget. To the right is profit, to the left is loss. The Marvels is the widest miss; Joker the widest hit, at nearly 20× its $55M budget.

Design note
Question
Which films actually cleared their break-even point, and by how much?
Why this form
A diverging bar anchored at zero puts profit right, loss left, and sorts the whole slate best to worst in one read. Gross alone can’t answer “did it make money”, a $400M film is a hit or a bomb depending on its budget.
Choices here
Zero is break-even, estimated at 2.5 × budget. Bar colour and sign carry the outcome, green/right made money, orange/left lost it, and every value label sits in one column clear of the film names. The universe rides along as a chip on the title.
Watch for
Break-even is a rule of thumb, not studio accounting, home video, streaming and TV deals aren’t in it. Trust the ranking more than the exact dollar figure.
§ 07  /  LEGS

Some films made most of their money in three days; others kept selling for months

For each film, its opening weekend and its full domestic run, connected. A long line is a film with legs: Aquaman finished at 4.9× its opening. A short one is front-loaded: Joker: Folie à Deux managed 1.5×.

Design note
Question
How much of each film’s domestic run came from its opening weekend versus everything after?
Why this form
A dumbbell connects two related numbers, opening weekend and lifetime domestic, for each film, sorted by the multiplier (lifetime ÷ opening) so the leggy films rise to the top.
Why a log axis
On a linear dollar axis the connector length would be dollars added after opening, not the ratio, a $260M opener that triples looks longer than a $30M opener that quadruples. A log axis makes the pixel length equal to the multiplier, which is the thing being ranked.
Watch for
Big openings and long legs aren’t opposites, Endgame had both. A huge multiple on a tiny platform release isn’t the same phenomenon as a wide release with staying power.
§ 08  /  REVIEWS & REVENUE

Reviews barely move with box office, and critics and audiences often part ways

Two questions the enriched data can finally answer: do better-reviewed films make more, and where do the critics and the crowd disagree?

Rotten Tomatoes critic score vs worldwide gross
One point per film. If reviews drove grosses, this would slope up. It mostly doesn’t.
Design note
Question
Do better reviews go with bigger box office?
Why this form
Two continuous variables, one point per film: a scatter. The question is whether there’s a pattern, and the honest answer is “barely.”
Choices here
Deliberately no trend line. The correlation is weak; a line would imply something predictable, which this isn’t. The spread is the finding.
Watch for
Reviews and gross are both downstream of the same thing: franchise heat, timing, spend. A scatter shows association, never cause.
Critics vs audiences
RT critic score against IMDb user rating, both on 0–100. The diagonal is where they agree.
Design note
Question
Where do critics and the audience disagree, and who liked it more?
Why this form
Plot one score against the other and draw the 45° line where they’d match. Distance from the line is the size of the disagreement; the side tells you who was warmer.
Choices here
Both scores are put on 0–100 so the parity line is a true diagonal. The line is drawn thin and grey. It’s a reference, not data.
Watch for
RT critic % (share of positive reviews) and an IMDb rating (average score) aren’t the same kind of measure. The diagonal is a visual aid, not a claim that the scales are equivalent.
§ 09  /  DOMESTIC v OVERSEAS

Most of this money comes from outside North America

Each film split into its domestic and overseas gross. The overseas share runs from 43% (Superman, Supergirl) to 72% (Wonder Woman 1984). Sort by it to see which films travelled.

Design note
Question
For each film, how much of the gross came from North America versus everywhere else?
Why this form
Part-to-whole, one film per row. A pie per film would be 57 pies; a stacked bar per row shows the split and lets you scan the whole list.
Choices here
Domestic is the solid bar, overseas the same colour at low opacity, one hue keeps the universe identity while the fill separates the two parts. Two sort orders: by total gross, or normalised so you compare shares directly.
Watch for
“Overseas” lumps very different markets together. China alone swings some of these films by ten points.

Act IV  ·  The roster

who shows up where
§ 10  /  WHO'S IN WHICH FILM

Marvel built a shared cast; DC kept starting over

Every costumed hero and villain, and the films they appear in, in release order. A filled cell is an appearance. The Avengers films are the vertical stripes; DC’s rows barely connect.

Design note
Question
Which characters recur, and how connected is each universe’s filmography?
Why this form
A presence grid, character × film, is the honest way to show set membership across many items. Density and gaps do the talking; no summary stat captures “the roster hangs together” as directly.
Choices here
Rows are the 26 most-recurring characters, ordered by appearance count. Columns are all 63 films in release order, with year ticks. A recast is one character (Hulk = Norton then Ruffalo); a passed-on mantle is not (Sam Wilson’s Falcon and his Captain America are separate rows, as are Natasha’s and Yelena’s Black Widow).
Watch for
“Codenamed” is a judgement call. Plain-name roles are out even when the person is central: Nick Fury, Pepper Potts, Alfred. And a character counts under a codename only from the film they take it up: Thaddeus Ross is five films of General Ross and one of Red Hulk, so he shows here once. Cast lists are from Wikipedia and skew to billed roles, so quick cameos are undercounted.
Codenamed characters per film
How many heroes and villains each film puts on screen, in release order. Infinity War tops out at 22.
§ 11  /  MOST-TRAVELLED

Iron Man leads at nine films; Superman, at five, is the most-travelled DC character

Characters ranked by how many films they turn up in. The long tail, 90-odd characters with a single appearance, is trimmed; the full list is in “Show data”.

Design note
Question
Who carries the most cross-film weight?
Why this form
A lollipop, not a bar: the counts are small integers (1–9), so a dot on a light stem reads the value cleanly without a heavy block of ink for “3”.
Choices here
Sorted by count, coloured by the universe the character appears in most. Single-appearance characters are cut, there are ~90 of them and they’d bury the signal.
Watch for
Each identity is one person here. “Captain America” is Steve Rogers; Sam Wilson’s turn is its own row, as are Yelena’s Black Widow and Shuri’s Black Panther. A codename counts from the film the character takes it up, not before, so Red Hulk and Mighty Thor sit at one film each.

Reference

the raw table & the method
§ 12  /  THE LEDGER

The whole thing, sortable

Every film and every field behind the charts above. Click a column header to sort. Mult. is domestic gross ÷ opening weekend: how long a film kept selling. ROI is worldwide gross ÷ production budget.

Design note
Why this form
A sorted table is the most information-dense view there is, and it’s the accessibility backbone: every value a chart encodes with position or colour is a plain number here.
Choices here
Click any header to sort. Money columns use a monospaced figure so the digits line up. Universe is a colour chip, not a colour-coded row, the text still has to work on its own.
Watch for
Sorting by a derived column (Mult., ROI) rewards small films with freak ratios. Read those next to the raw gross.
§ 13  /  METHOD

How this was built

Sources

Worldwide, domestic and overseas grosses and opening weekends come from Box Office Mojo title pages, read one at a time. Scores are from OMDb: Rotten Tomatoes (critics), Metacritic, IMDb. Budgets are Box Office Mojo where it lists one, otherwise a widely-cited figure marked est. Always the production budget, never a number with marketing folded in.

Definitions

2025 $ rescales every gross and budget by annual CPI-U to 2025 dollars. Break-even is estimated at 2.5× production budget: the rough point a theatrical release clears once exhibitors take their cut. Still in release films (marked in release) are included with grosses as of early September 2026 and will keep climbing.

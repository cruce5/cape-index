'''''Black Widow''''' is a 2021 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the character [[Black Widow (Natasha Romanova)|of the same name]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 24th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Cate Shortland]] from a screenplay by [[Eric Pearson]] and stars [[Scarlett Johansson]] as [[Natasha Romanoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Natasha Romanoff / Black Widow]] alongside [[Florence Pugh]], [[David Harbour]], [[O-T Fagbenle]], [[Olga Kurylenko]], [[William Hurt]], [[Ray Winstone]], and [[Rachel Weisz]]. Mostly set during the events of ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016), the film sees Romanoff on the run and forced to confront her past as a Russian spy before she became an [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avenger]].

[[Lionsgate Films]] began developing a ''Black Widow'' film in April 2004, with [[David Hayter]] attached to write and direct. The project did not move forward and the character's film rights had reverted to Marvel Studios by June 2006. Johansson was cast in the role for several MCU films beginning with ''[[Iron Man 2]]'' (2010), and began discussing a solo film with Marvel. Work began in late 2017 and Shortland was hired in July 2018. [[Jac Schaeffer]] and [[Ned Benson]] contributed to the script before Pearson joined. The film was written to be a [[prequel]] that expands on Romanoff's history and helps end her MCU story following the character's death in ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019). Shortland put an emphasis on the fight sequences and said this was the most violent MCU film so far. Filming took place from May to October 2019 in [[Norway]], England, [[Budapest]], [[Morocco]], and [[Macon, Georgia]].

''Black Widow'' premiered at events around the world on June 29, 2021, and was released in the United States on July 9, [[Simultaneous release|simultaneously]] in theaters and through [[Disney+]] with [[Disney+ Premier Access|Premier Access]]. It is the first film in [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU, and was [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema|delayed three times]] from an original May 2020 release date due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. ''Black Widow'' broke several pandemic box office records and grossed over $379&nbsp;million worldwide.{{efn|name=PremierAccessRevenue|Disney announced that ''Black Widow'' also earned $67&nbsp;million globally from Disney+ Premier Access in its opening weekend. This is not factored into box office grosses.<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" /><ref name="DeadlineIntlWknd1" /><ref name="DisneyMotionLawsuit" />}} The film received positive reviews from critics, with particular praise for the action sequences and for the performances of Johansson and Pugh. In July 2021, Johansson filed a lawsuit against Disney over the simultaneous release, which was settled two months later.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films are 400 to 700 words only. -->
In 1995, [[super soldier]] [[Alexei Shostakov (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Alexei Shostakov]] and [[Black Widows (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Black Widow]] assassin [[Melina Vostokoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Melina Vostokoff]] work as [[Soviet Union|Russian]] undercover agents, posing as a family in [[Ohio]] with [[Natasha Romanoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Natasha Romanoff]] and [[Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yelena Belova]] as their daughters. They steal [[S.H.I.E.L.D. (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|S.H.I.E.L.D.]] intel and escape to [[Cuba]] where their boss, General [[Dreykov]], has Romanoff and Belova taken to the [[Red Room (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Red Room]] for training as Widows. In the following decades, Shostakov is imprisoned in Russia while Romanoff and Belova become successful, dangerous assassins. Romanoff eventually defects to S.H.I.E.L.D. after helping [[Clint Barton (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Clint Barton]] bomb Dreykov's [[Budapest]] office, which apparently kills Dreykov and his young daughter [[Antonia Dreykov|Antonia]].

In 2016, Romanoff is a fugitive for violating the [[Sokovia Accords]].{{efn|name=Timeline|As depicted in ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016)}} She escapes from [[U.S. Secretary of State]] [[Thaddeus Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thaddeus Ross]] and flees to a safehouse in [[Norway]] supplied by [[Rick Mason (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Rick Mason]]. Meanwhile, Belova kills a rogue former Widow but comes in contact with a [[synthetic gas]] that neutralizes the Red Room's chemical [[Brainwashing|mind-control]] agent. Belova sends antidote vials to Romanoff, hoping she and the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]] can free the other Widows, and goes into hiding. When Romanoff is unknowingly driving with the vials in her car, Red Room agent [[Taskmaster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Taskmaster]] attacks her. Romanoff escapes from Taskmaster and realizes that the vials came from Budapest. There she finds Belova, who reveals that Dreykov is alive and the Red Room is still active. Widows and Taskmaster attack them, but Romanoff and Belova evade them and meet with Mason, who supplies them with a helicopter.

Romanoff and Belova break Shostakov out of prison to learn Dreykov's location, and he directs them to Vostokoff, who lives on a farm outside [[Saint Petersburg]]. There she is refining the chemical mind control process used on the Widows. Vostokoff alerts Dreykov and his agents arrive to take them, but Romanoff convinces Vostokoff to help them and the pair use face mask technology to switch places. At the Red Room, a secret aerial facility, Vostokoff frees Shostakov and Belova from their restraints. Dreykov sees through Romanoff's disguise. He reveals that Taskmaster is Antonia, who suffered damage in the Budapest bombing that was so severe Dreykov had to put technology in her head to save her. This turned Antonia into the perfect soldier, capable of mimicking the actions of anyone she sees. Romanoff is unable to attack Dreykov due to a [[pheromone]] lock installed in every Widow, but negates that by breaking her own nose and severing her [[olfactory nerve]]. Shostakov battles Taskmaster while Vostokoff disables one of the facility's engines, and they then lock Taskmaster in a cell.

Dreykov escapes as other Widows attack Romanoff, but Belova exposes them to the antidote. Romanoff copies the locations of other Widows worldwide from Dreykov's computer as the facility begins to explode and fall. She retrieves two surviving antidote vials and frees Taskmaster from the locked cell. Vostokoff and Shostakov escape via a plane while Belova blows up Dreykov's aircraft, killing him. In freefall, Romanoff gives Belova a parachute before battling Taskmaster. After landing, Romanoff uses one antidote vial on Taskmaster and gives the other to Belova along with the locations of the other mind-controlled Widows so she can find and free them. Belova, Vostokoff, and Shostakov say goodbye to Romanoff and leave with Antonia and the freed Widows. Two weeks later, Mason supplies Romanoff with a [[Quinjet (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Quinjet]] to use in freeing the imprisoned Avengers.{{efn|name=Timeline|As depicted in ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016)}}

In a [[post-credits scene]] set after Romanoff's death,{{efn|name=Timeline2|As depicted in ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019)}} [[Valentina Allegra de Fontaine (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valentina Allegra de Fontaine]] visits Belova at Romanoff's grave. De Fontaine blames Romanoff's death on Barton and assigns him as Belova's next target.

== Production ==
=== Background ===
{{Quote box|width=33%|align=right|quote="What I tried to do was use the backdrop of the splintered [[Soviet Empire]]—a lawless insane asylum with 400-some odd nuclear missile silos. It was all about loose nukes, and I felt it was very timely and very cool. Unfortunately, as I was coming up on the final draft, a number of female vigilante movies came out. We had ''[[Lara Croft: Tomb Raider|Tomb Raider]]'' and ''[[Kill Bill Volume 1|Kill Bill]]'', which were the ones that worked, but then we had ''[[BloodRayne (film)|BloodRayne]]'' and ''[[Ultraviolet (film)|Ultraviolet]]'' and ''[[Æon Flux (film)|Æon Flux]]''. ''Æon Flux'' didn't open well, and three days after it opened, the studio said, 'We don't think it's time to do this movie.'"|source=—David Hayter on his version of the film and why it failed to move forward<ref name="HayterNov2011" />}}
In February 2004, [[Lionsgate Films|Lionsgate]] acquired the film rights for the [[Marvel Comics]] character [[Black Widow (Natasha Romanova)|Black Widow]],<ref name="LionsGateRights" /> and in April announced [[David Hayter]] as writer and director of the film, with [[Marvel Studios]]' [[Avi Arad]] producing.<ref name="HayterDirectWrite" /> By June 2006, Lionsgate had dropped the project and the rights reverted to Marvel Studios. Hayter and Marvel tried getting another financier to develop the project, but Hayter did not feel that they found another place "that was willing to take the movie, and the character, seriously". Hayter said this left him heartbroken, and he hoped the film would be made some day.<ref name="LionsGateDrop" />

Marvel Studios entered early talks with [[Emily Blunt]] to play Black Widow in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) film ''[[Iron Man 2]]'' (2010) in January 2009,<ref name="BluntWidow" /> but she was unable to take the role due to a previous commitment to star in ''[[Gulliver's Travels (2010 film)|Gulliver's Travels]]'' (2010).<ref name="BluntOut" /> In March 2009, [[Scarlett Johansson]] signed on to play Natasha Romanoff / Black Widow, with her deal including options for multiple films.<ref name="JohanssonMarch2009" /> In September 2010, Marvel Studios President [[Kevin Feige]] said discussions with Johansson had already taken place regarding a Black Widow standalone film, but Marvel's focus was on the [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]] film ''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|The Avengers]]'' (2012).<ref name="IGNSep2010" /> Johansson reprised her role in that film,<ref name="Avengers" /> as well as in ''[[Captain America: The Winter Soldier]]'' (2014),<ref name="WinterSoldier" /> ''[[Avengers: Age of Ultron]]'' (2015),<ref name="AvengersAoU" /> ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016),<ref name="CapCW" /> ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018),<ref name="InfinityWar" /> and ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019).<ref name="Endgame" /> After the release of ''Age of Ultron'', Johansson revealed that the number of films on her contract had been adjusted since she first signed to match the "demand of the character", as Marvel had not anticipated the audience's positive response to the character and Johansson's performance.<ref name="JohanssonApril2015" />

After teasing Black Widow's past in ''Age of Ultron'', Feige said in February 2014 that he would like to see it explored further in a solo film. Development work for this had already started,<ref name="BlackWidowSolo" /> including a "pretty in depth" treatment by [[Nicole Perlman]] who co-wrote Marvel Studios' ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014).<ref name="PerlmanWidow" /> The following April, Johansson expressed interest in starring in a Black Widow film and said it would be driven by demand from the audience.<ref name="JohanssonApr2014" /> That July, Hayter expressed interest in reviving the project for Marvel,<ref name="HayterRevive" /> while director [[Neil Marshall]] said a month later that he "would {{em|love}} to do a Black Widow film". Marshall said he was fascinated by a superhero with "extraordinary skills" rather than superpowers who is also an ex-[[KGB]] assassin.<ref name="Marshall" /> In April 2015, Johansson spoke more on the possibility of a solo Black Widow film, seeing the potential to explore the character's different layers as depicted in her previous appearances. However, she felt that the character was being "used well in this part of the universe" at that time.<ref name="JohanssonApril2015" /> While promoting ''Civil War'' the next April, Feige noted that due to Marvel's announced schedule of films for [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three|Phase Three]] of the MCU, any potential ''Black Widow'' film would be four or five years away.<ref name="FeigeApril2016" /> He added that Marvel was "creatively and emotionally" committed to making a ''Black Widow'' film eventually.<ref name="FeigeMay2016" />

[[Joss Whedon]], the director of ''The Avengers'' and ''Avengers: Age of Ultron'', said in July 2016 that he was open to directing a ''Black Widow'' film. He liked the idea of making it a paranoid spy thriller that was "[[John le Carré]] on crack".<ref name="WhedonJuly2016" /> In October, Johansson discussed the potential film being a prequel, saying, "you can bring it back to Russia. You could explore the Widow program. There's all kinds of stuff that you could do with it." She cautioned that she may not want to wear a "skin-tight catsuit" for much longer.<ref name="JohanssonOct2016" /> The next February, Johansson said she would dedicate herself to making any potential Black Widow film "amazing. It would have to be the best version that movie could possibly be. Otherwise, I would never do it ... [it would] have to be its own standalone [film] and its own style and its own story."<ref name="JohanssonFeb2017" /> Due to the development work already done and the public support for the project, Marvel Studios decided to move forward with the film at the beginning of the MCU's [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] in 2020 following the conclusion of [[the Infinity Saga]] storyline in ''Endgame''.<ref name="PughVariety" /> Johansson said this decision had become "more of a reality" during the filming of ''Infinity War''. Following Romanoff's death in ''Endgame'', Johansson felt there was "no pressing urgency" to make a Black Widow film and this would improve the project's quality.<ref name="JohanssonCollider" />

=== Development ===
[[File:Cate Shortland by Gage Skidmore.jpg|thumb|upright|left|Director [[Cate Shortland]] at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con]]
Feige met with Johansson to discuss the direction of a Black Widow film in October 2017. Marvel then began meeting with writers for the project, including [[Jac Schaeffer]] and [[Jessica Gao]].<ref name="KrollThanksgiving" /><ref name="WrapGaoAug2022" /> Gao's [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] was influenced by ''[[Grosse Pointe Blank]]'' (1997), in which Romanoff would have been planted in a high school to assassinate a target and then 20 years later attends a high school reunion where she has to deal with the fallout of her earlier actions.<ref name="WrapGaoAug2022" /> The pitch heavily featured the character [[Jennifer Walters (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|She-Hulk]], and Marvel Studios executive [[Brad Winderbaum]] felt Gao was instead pitching a She-Hulk film that featured Black Widow. Gao would later be hired by Marvel Studios as the [[head writer]] for the [[Disney+]] television series ''[[She-Hulk: Attorney at Law]]'' (2022).<ref name="GaoVarietyAug2022" /> Schaeffer met with Feige in December, and was hired to write the screenplay by the end of 2017.<ref name="SchaefferJan2018" /><ref name="KrollSchaeffer" /> Schaeffer and Johansson were set to discuss the direction of the film at the beginning of February 2018.<ref name="KrollUpdate" />

Marvel began meeting with female directors to potentially take on the project, part of a priority push by major film studios to hire female directors for franchises.<ref name="Apr2018Directors" /> By the end of April 2018, the studio had met with over 65 directors for the project in an "extremely thorough" search,<ref name="May2018Directors" /> including [[Deniz Gamze Ergüven]], [[Chloé Zhao]], [[Amma Asante]],<ref name="Apr2018Directors" /> and [[Lynn Shelton]].<ref name="SheltonConsidered" /> [[Lucrecia Martel]] was also approached, but was discouraged when she was told that she would not have to "worry about the action scenes". She also felt the music and visual effects of Marvel films were "horrible".<ref name="Martel" /> [[Coralie Fargeat]] was in contention following the success of her film ''[[Revenge (2017 film)|Revenge]]'' (2017), but chose to develop ''[[The Substance]]'' (2024) instead, believing Marvel would not give her [[final cut privilege]].<ref name="Fargeat" /> Zhao took herself out of contention after deciding that ''Black Widow'' was not the right story for her, and because the planned filming schedule conflicted with her film ''[[Nomadland]]'' (2020) which was a story Zhao wanted to tell; she went on to direct Marvel's ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'' (2021).<ref name="ZhaoPlaylistJan2026" /> In the following months, a shortlist of 49 directors was made before the top choices of [[Cate Shortland]], Asante, and [[Maggie Betts]] met with Feige and Johansson in June.<ref name="Jun2018Directors" /> [[Mélanie Laurent]] and [[Kimberly Peirce]] were also in the "next-to-final mix".<ref name="ShortlandDirect" /><ref name="Jul2018Directors" /> Johansson was a fan of Shortland's previous female-starring film ''[[Lore (2012 film)|Lore]]'' (2012),<ref name="ShortlandDirect" /> and was the one who had approached her about directing the film;<ref name="PressAdvance" />{{rp|1}} Shortland was hired in July,<ref name="ShortlandDirect" /> and watched all of Romanoff's MCU appearances in preparation for making the film.<ref name="ShortlandTheGuardian" />

''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' reported in October 2018 that Johansson would earn $15&nbsp;million for the film, an increase from the "low-seven figure salary" that she earned for starring in ''The Avengers''. [[Chris Evans (actor)|Chris Evans]] and [[Chris Hemsworth]] each earned $15&nbsp;million for the third films in their MCU franchises—''Civil War'' and ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'' (2017), respectively—as well as for ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame''. Despite ''The Hollywood Reporter'' claiming that their report had been confirmed by "multiple knowledgeable sources", Marvel Studios disputed its accuracy and said they "never publicly disclose salaries or deal terms".<ref name="JohannsonPayOct2018" /> [[The Walt Disney Company]] later said Johansson had earned $20&nbsp;million by the end of July 2021 for her work on the film,<ref name="JohanssonLawsuit" /> including her role as an executive producer.<ref name="FeigeTHRJul2021" />

As Romanoff dies during the events of ''Endgame'', the film is set earlier in the MCU timeline.<ref name="JohanssonCollider" /> It primarily takes place between the main plot of ''Civil War'' and that film's final scene, in which the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]] escape from prison.<ref name="TimelineExact" /> Johansson and Feige felt this was "the best place to start" because it is the first time that Romanoff is on her own and not tied to a larger organization such as the [[Red Room (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Red Room]], [[S.H.I.E.L.D. (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|S.H.I.E.L.D.]], or the Avengers.<ref name="JohanssonCollider" /><ref name="BWTimeline" /> This setting means the film is not an [[origin story]] for Romanoff, which Feige wanted to avoid because he felt that was the expected direction for a prequel to go.<ref name="EmpireJuly2020" /> He was influenced by the television series ''[[Better Call Saul]]'' (2015–2022), which is a prequel to the series ''[[Breaking Bad]]'' (2008–2013), calling it "a wonderful example of a prequel that almost completely stands on its own... because it informs you about so many things you didn't know about before".<ref name="Feigeio9June2019" />

=== Pre-production ===
In February 2019, [[Ned Benson]] was hired to rewrite the script,<ref name="BensonRewriteFebruary2019" /> and Feige confirmed that, despite rumors, the studio did not want the film to receive an [[R-rating (Motion Picture Association)|R-rating]] from the [[Motion Picture Association]].<ref name="RRatingFalse" /> The following month, [[Florence Pugh]] entered negotiations to join the cast as a spy,<ref name="PughTHR" /> later revealed to be [[Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yelena Belova]].<ref name="SDCCDetails" /> Marvel had been considering Pugh for the role since late 2018, but began looking at other actresses in early 2019, including [[Saoirse Ronan]],<ref name="PughVariety" /><ref name="RonanVariety" /> who passed on the role.<ref name="RonanPass" /> The studio returned to Pugh after she received strong reviews for her performance in the film ''[[Fighting with My Family]]'' (2019).<ref name="PughVariety" /> [[Devin Grayson]] and [[J. G. Jones]], who co-created Belova, expected to receive $25,000 for her role in the film based on a 2007 agreement with Marvel Comics, but ultimately received $5,000 due to a provision in the contract which allowed Marvel to reduce creators' payments;<ref name="CreatorPayments" /> after Grayson went public with this, Marvel agreed to pay her the remaining amount.<ref name="CreatorPayments2" /> In April 2019, Pugh was confirmed to have been cast alongside [[David Harbour]],<ref name="Harbour" /> [[Rachel Weisz]],<ref name="Fagbenle" /><ref name="Weisz" /> and [[O-T Fagbenle]].<ref name="Fagbenle" /> An early version of the script included a scene from ''Civil War'' featuring Romanoff and [[Robert Downey Jr.]]'s [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark / Iron Man]],<ref name="PearsonStarkScene" /> and Downey was reported to be appearing in the film.<ref name="Downey" /> Fans also expected [[Jeremy Renner]] to reprise his MCU role as [[Clint Barton (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Clint Barton]].<ref name="NoCameos" /> Shortland and Feige ultimately decided against including other heroes so Romanoff could stand on her own,<ref name="NoCameos" /><ref name="NoTonyStark" /> though Renner does have an uncredited voice-only cameo.<ref name="EasterEggsSR" />

[[Eric Pearson]], who started his writing career in Marvel's screenwriting program and went on to work on several MCU projects, was hired during [[pre-production]] to rewrite the film's script again.<ref name="PearsonCS" /><ref name="PearsonDF" /> He ultimately received sole screenwriter credit, with Schaeffer and Benson receiving story credits.<ref name="WritingCredits" /> Shortland and Johansson had decided to take inspiration from the television series ''[[The Americans]]'' (2013–2018), which is about a Russian undercover spy family, by introducing Romanoff's own "family" of Russian spies including Belova.<ref name="PearsonCS" /> When Pearson joined the film, the opening sequence—a prologue set in the 1990s with Romanoff and her "family" living undercover in the [[Midwestern United States]]—had already been written.<ref name="Pearson/Film2" /> The rest of the film focuses on this family coming back together in the "present day", with a pivotal scene later in the film where they are all reunited around a dining table.<ref name="PearsonCS" /> Of all the MCU projects that he had worked on, Pearson found ''Black Widow''{{'s}} setting within the MCU timeline to be the most difficult to get right. This was because he wanted the audience to feel that the villain could succeed without breaking the timeline, which would be difficult considering audiences had already seen ''Civil War'', ''Infinity War'', and ''Endgame''. He chose to focus on the Red Room—where the Black Widows are trained—and its leader, who "wields power from the shadows". Pearson thought that the film's [[spy thriller]] tone helped alleviate his concerns about the villain.<ref name="Pearson/Film2" /><ref name="PearsonCB" /> A scene at the end of the film in which [[Thaddeus Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thaddeus Ross]] pursues Romanoff was deliberately left unresolved, as Shortland wanted to leave the audience "on a high" and questioning how Romanoff escapes rather than exhausted with another fight.<ref name="ShortlandTheWrap" /> This is then followed by a scene that connects this film to the end of ''Civil War'' by showing Romanoff leaving on a [[Quinjet (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Quinjet]] to save the Avengers. She is wearing a vest given to her by Belova and has dyed her hair blonde, which matches the character's appearance in ''Infinity War''.<ref name="PearsonDF" />

Pearson said there were discussions about ensuring the film was an appropriate farewell for Romanoff. They wanted it to feature "the greatest hits" of the character, including expanding on the backstory that was mentioned in ''The Avengers''. In that film, Romanoff and Barton discuss a past mission in [[Budapest]] and there is a mention of "[[Antonia Dreykov|Dreykov's daughter]]". ''Black Widow'' expands on the Budapest hints by having Romanoff return there and revealing some of that mission's details. [[Dreykov]] is revealed to be the head of the Red Room, who Romanoff believes to have been killed during the earlier Budapest mission.<ref name="PearsonCB" /> Johansson wanted the film to comment on the [[Me Too movement|#MeToo movement]] which saw women supporting each other and "coming through these shared experiences of trauma on the other side",<ref name="YahooMeToo" /> and this is seen in the way that Romanoff is forced, by the arrival of Belova, to confront Dreykov and her past trauma. Johansson was grateful to have the film to comment on these ideas.<ref name="YahooMeToo" /> Pearson felt the truth behind "Dreykov's daughter" needed to be "something pretty bad. Natasha is ashamed of it. We've been doing allusions to her having a dark past [throughout the MCU]... It had to be something actively tough that she did that haunts her dreams." He settled on the idea that Romanoff and Barton used Dreykov's young daughter as bait to kill him and believed the girl had also died in the process. There was "heated conversation" with Marvel over how much of Romanoff's dark past to show, and Pearson successfully argued for including this element. Early versions of the script included the comic book character [[Taskmaster (character)|Tony Masters / Taskmaster]] as a villain. Pearson moved towards the more subtle villain Dreykov who he felt fit better into the story and the timeline, but decided to reveal that Dreykov's daughter is alive and now a version of Taskmaster that he compared to a [[Terminator (character concept)|Terminator]]. He felt this was a way to have some "Marvel fun" within the otherwise grounded story.<ref name="PearsonCB" /> Fagbenle auditioned for the original version of Taskmaster, who he called a maniac with an African accent, and said the role was down to him and another actor. After the story changes, Marvel asked if Fagbenle was interested in playing [[Rick Mason (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Rick Mason]] instead, which he accepted.<ref name="FagbenleTaskmaster" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began on May 28, 2019, in [[Norway]].<ref name="FilmingStart" /> Shortland took inspiration from such films as ''[[How to Train Your Dragon (2010 film)|How to Train Your Dragon]]'' (2010),<ref name="EmpireMagazineMay2020" />{{rp|63}} ''[[No Country for Old Men]]'' (2007), and ''[[Thelma & Louise]]'' (1991), as well as ''Captain America: The Winter Soldier''. She looked at combat films and those with armies and militia, allowing her to imagine females in those roles to help translate that to ''Black Widow''.<ref name="FandangoJune2021" /> Shortland also referenced fight scenes involving female characters in ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]'' (1979) and the [[Terminator (franchise)|''Terminator'' franchise]], and wanted the film's fights to resemble "real fights" rather than wrestling matches.<ref name="ShortlandTheGuardian" /> She edited together fight scenes from different films into shorts that helped her explore the style of fighting that she wanted in the film. Shortland said this was the most violent film in the MCU and she wanted to approach that aspect with the same "truth" that she was approaching the rest of the film.<ref name="NoCameos" />

Early reports suggested that [[Rob Hardy (cinematographer)|Rob Hardy]] would be the film's cinematographer, but he left the production before filming began. [[Gabriel Beristain]] served as cinematographer instead,<ref name="Beristain" /> having previously done so for the [[Marvel One-Shot]] short films ''[[Item 47]]'' (2012) and ''[[Agent Carter (film)|Agent Carter]]'' (2013), for the television series ''[[Agent Carter (TV series)|Agent Carter]]'' (2015–2016),<ref name="PressAdvance" />{{rp|3}}<ref name="BeristainACTV" /> and for additional photography on ''[[Iron Man (2008 film)|Iron Man]]'' (2008) and six other MCU films.<ref name="BeristainJuly2021" /> Beristain was initially not credited in ''Black Widow''{{'s}} trailers, as pointed out by Jeff Sneider at ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]'' who wondered if contractual obstacles were to blame for Marvel not crediting the cinematographer at that point. Sneider felt Beristain would be credited in later marketing materials,<ref name="Beristain" /> and his hiring was confirmed in the film's official press advance.<ref name="PressAdvance" />{{rp|3}} Beristein later confirmed that he joined the film after production had already begun, opting to leave a television directing job and move to London so he could be the main cinematographer for an MCU film.<ref name="BeristainJuly2021" /> Shortland preferred to avoid technical equipment where possible and prioritize natural lighting, which provided a challenge for Beristain in having to match the light of the location shoots to later additional photography filmed in Los Angeles. The naturalistic approach was also reflected in interior scenes, lighting from outside the set or using lights with strong diffusion to create the same effect. An [[anamorphic format]] was chosen to highlight the expansive movements and landscapes in the film, with a special system used that split the camera's [[image sensor]] from its body to help with this approach.<ref name="BeristainOct2021" />

[[File:Steep track going north on Hankley Common - geograph.org.uk - 1249952.jpg|thumb|[[Hankley Common]] in [[Surrey]], England, was made to look like a Russian farmstead for the film.<ref name="HankleyCommon" /><ref name="HankleyCommon2" />]]
The production moved to [[Pinewood Studios]] in London in early June.<ref name="PinewoodFilming" /> Shortland hired someone to help Johansson and Pugh bond through trust exercises and other bonding activities, but the pair felt their "real bonding" happened during Pugh's first two days of filming when they were working on an early fight scene between Romanoff and Belova. Pugh had some stunt experience from her work on ''Fighting with My Family'', and Johansson gave her advice that she had received from [[Samuel L. Jackson]] while working on ''Iron Man 2''. Pearson was on set to hear a joke from Pugh about the "ridiculous" pose that Johansson often lands in while fighting as Romanoff throughout her MCU appearances, which led to him adding some jokes from Belova to Romanoff about the poses in the script.<ref name="JohanssonPughBonding" /> [[Ray Winstone]] joined the film's cast later in June,<ref name="Winstone" /> when filming took place on location in Budapest.<ref name="BudapestFilming" /> 13 [[BMW X3]]s were used to create a [[car chase]] involving Romanoff and Belova in Budapest, with the crew often switching off the [[electronic stability control]] and safety assistance functions to be able to do what the script asked for. They also swapped the X3's [[electronic parking brake]] for a hydraulically actuated one. Second unit director Darrin Prescott explained that the crew would often "replace the engine or tear the entire body off the car and rebuild it from scratch". The sequence's plot and locations were adjusted to help Prescott create the "spontaneous" stunts that he wanted.<ref name="TopGear" />

Shooting took place in mid-July 2019 at [[Hankley Common]] in [[Surrey]], England, under the [[working title]] ''Blue Bayou''. The site was made to look like a Russian farmstead, with added helicopter crash sites. The Thursley Parish Council objected to this filming, as it had occurred while Marvel Studios' application to use the site was still pending. The production planned to revisit the site in late August for further filming.<ref name="HankleyCommon" /><ref name="HankleyCommon2" /> ''Black Widow'' was officially announced at the 2019 [[San Diego Comic-Con]] later in July, with a release date of May 1, 2020, revealed alongside roles for some of the new cast members.<ref name="SDCCDetails" /> In August, bodybuilder [[Olivier Richters]] revealed that he had been cast in the film,<ref name="Richters" /> playing a character that was subsequently confirmed to be [[Ursa Major (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ursa Major]].<ref name="EasterEggsSR" /><ref name="EWEasterEgg" /> That same month, crew undertook scanning and texture photography at the Well-Safe Guardian oil rig in the [[North Sea]] as a reference for visual effects.<ref name="OilRigFilming" /> A [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrap]] party was held at the end of September, before production moved to [[Macon, Georgia]], for the week of September 30. Filming locations in Macon, including [[Terminal Station (Macon, Georgia)|Terminal Station]], were dressed to portray [[Albany, New York]].<ref name="Macon" /> Set photos from the start of October revealed that [[William Hurt]] would appear in the film, reprising his MCU role as Thaddeus Ross.<ref name="Hurt" /> Additional filming in Georgia took place in [[Atlanta]] and [[Rome, Georgia|Rome]].<ref name="RomeGeorgiaFilming" /> Also in early October, filming took place in [[Tangier]], [[Morocco]], with local production services provided by Zak Productions.<ref name="TangierFilming" /><ref name="ZakProdsFilming" /> Principal photography lasted for 87 days<ref name="PressAdvance" />{{rp|3}} and officially wrapped on October 6, 2019.<ref name="Wrap" />

=== Post-production ===
{{external media|float=left|video1=[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SWu3ACMGtIA Marvel Studios' Black Widow Opening Title Sequence] presents the film's main title sequence, [[YouTube]] video from [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]]'s channel}}
[[Matthew Schmidt]] and Leigh Folsom Boyd edited the film.<ref name="PressAdvance" />{{rp|3}} Pearson said the  handling of Romanoff's Budapest backstory was "85% cracked" prior to filming, and the rest was determined during editing and [[additional photography]].<ref name="PearsonCB" /> Winstone disliked the additional photography, saying it changed the character from what he and Shortland created and feeling that his performance was being criticized by Marvel Studios executives. He wanted his role to be recast instead, but was contractually obligated to return.<ref name="WinstoneReshoots" /><ref name="WinstoneReshootsVariety" /> A scene of Romanoff wearing underwear and a T-shirt that Shortland enjoyed due to "how sexy [Johansson] is [when] she's in control" was cut because test audiences criticized it as using the "[[male gaze]]".<ref name="ShortlandTheGuardian" /> ''Black Widow'' is one of the few Marvel films to feature an [[opening credits]] sequence, which Feige explained was because Shortland felt strongly about exploring what Dreykov is responsible for at the beginning of the film.<ref name="FeigeTweet" /><ref name="DigitalSpyFeige" /> The sequence was designed by [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]], who worked with Shortland to film various vignettes on set that were made to look as if they were captured on 8&nbsp;mm film, 90's digital video, and [[VHS]]. These were combined with old photographs and documents that Perception's team created and manipulated to tell the story.<ref name="Perception" />

In mid-March 2020, Disney removed the film from its release schedule due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="Delayed" /> In early April, Disney announced that ''Black Widow'' would be released on November 6, 2020, and the rest of their Phase Four slate of films were shifted to accommodate this.<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> In September 2020, Disney pushed the release back again to May 7, 2021,<ref name="SecondReschedule" /> followed by a third shift in March 2021 to July 9, 2021.<ref name="JulyRelease" /> In April 2021, following [[Julia Louis-Dreyfus]]'s appearance as [[Valentina Allegra de Fontaine (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valentina Allegra de Fontaine]] in the Marvel Studios television series ''[[The Falcon and the Winter Soldier]]'' (2021), Joanna Robinson of ''[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]'' reported that Louis-Dreyfus had been expected to first appear in ''Black Widow'' before the delays pushed its release to after ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''{{'s}} premiere on [[Disney+]].<ref name="LouisDreyfusVF" /> Feige later confirmed this, explaining that ''Black Widow''{{'s}} [[post-credits scene]] was originally intended to be the character's introduction but instead became a reference to her appearance in the series. He said this was the only change to Marvel's Phase Four plans that was forced by the pandemic.<ref name="GizmodoJuly2021" /> The post-credits scene was added after ''[[Hawkeye (miniseries)|Hawkeye]]'' (2021) showrunner [[Jonathan Igla]] expressed interest in having Yelena Belova appear in his series. Pearson was asked to write the scene, in which De Fontaine assigns Clint Barton as Belova's next target, without knowing who the target would be.<ref name="PostCreditsHawkeye" /> Shortland considered the setting of the scene, at Romanoff's grave in a remote clearing, to be a response to fans who were upset that Romanoff did not receive a funeral in ''Endgame''. Shortland said the character was private and would not have wanted a large funeral, so this scene allowed the character's ending "to be the grief the individuals felt, rather than a big public outpouring".<ref name="EmpireJuly2020" /><ref name="VarietyPostCredits" /> Shortland said in May 2021 that the film had been completed a year prior and had not been altered, despite the release delays.<ref name="ShortlandMay2021" /> [[Olga Kurylenko]]'s role as Antonia Dreykov / Taskmaster was revealed with the film's release.<ref name="Kurylenko" />

[[File:Free fall VFX sequence in Black Widow (2021 film).jpg|thumb|upright=1|Original plate (top) and the completed shot (bottom) with visual effects by [[Digital Domain]], for the film's final battle sequence]]
The film's visual effects vendors included [[Cinesite]], [[Digital Domain]], [[Industrial Light & Magic]], Mammal Studios, SSVFX, [[Scanline VFX]], [[Trixter]], and [[Wētā FX]].<ref name="VFXVendors" /> Digital Domain was responsible for the final battle sequence and completed around 320 visual effects shots for the film. They created the Red Room, an airborne fortress hidden in the clouds, which was inspired by the [[brutalist architecture]] of the [[Soviet Union]].<ref name="FXGuide" /><ref name="DigitalTrendsVFX" /> A combination of practical and visual effects were used to create the sequence where the Red Room is destroyed and falls from the sky: actors and stunt performers were filmed in a [[wind tunnel]] against [[Chroma key|bluescreens]] while being held with wires and mechanical arms, and the surrounding environment of sky and debris was created digitally.<ref name="FXGuide" /><ref name="DigitalTrendsVFX" /> Cinesite primarily handled the Russian prison that Harbour's [[Alexei Shostakov (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Alexei Shostakov / Red Guardian]] is found in.<ref name="Cinesite" /> Wētā FX created the mountain range that surrounds the prison, based on photography from [[Svalbard, Norway]], as well as an avalanche. Wētā also created the [[Mil Mi-8]] helicopter that is seen in that sequence,<ref name="Weta" /> while Cinesite created it for other sequences.<ref name="Cinesite" /> For the pigs on the farm of Weisz's [[Melina Vostokoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Melina Vostokoff]], Wētā filmed live [[Kunekune]] pigs as reference for the digital pigs in the final shots.<ref name="Weta" />

Industrial Light & Magic completed approximately 800 VFX shots for the film, working on the opening airplane escape scene, the Budapest apartment fight and subsequent motorcycle and car chase, and the fight between Romanoff and a group of Black Widows in Dreykov's office. The opening airplane scene was created with half of an airplane rig on a hydraulic platform. Footage filmed on location in Atlanta was played on LED screens on the soundstage to create interactive lighting for the actors on set. To create the Budapest scenes, ILM worked with second unit director Darren Prescott. Romanoff and Belova's chase across the rooftops was a combination of practical and visual effects, while scenes involving smokestacks were created using [[virtual actor]]s. The car chase scene in Budapest features a practical tank driven by Taskmaster that was created by Paul Corbould, the film's special effects supervisor. Digital cars were added to the scene to make it appear more dangerous and for better interaction with the tank. For the fight scene in Dreykov's office, ILM worked on the graphic displays in the background as well as the lighting that interacts with the Widows' bodies.<ref name="AWNVFX" /> In addition to creating the film's opening credits sequence, Perception designed the graphics for Dreykov's screens and other Red Room technology, researching Soviet Union technology for inspiration.<ref name="Perception2" /> Trixter created Taskmaster's mask and worked on several action sequences as well.<ref name="Cinesite" />

== Music ==
{{Main|Black Widow (soundtrack)}}
[[Alexandre Desplat]] was revealed to be composing the music for the film in January 2020.<ref name="Desplat" /> Late in post-production, [[Lorne Balfe]] replaced Desplat as composer, which Desplat confirmed in May 2020.<ref name="Balfe" /><ref name="DesplatBalfe" /> Balfe briefly references [[Alan Silvestri]]'s [[Leitmotif|motif]] for Romanoff and main theme for the Avengers from ''The Avengers'' in the score,<ref name="BalfeSyFy" /><ref name="SilvestriMotif" /> but otherwise wanted to focus on creating a new identity for Romanoff. His discussions with Shortland led to him writing an original piece in the style of [[Russian folk music]] that he thought Romanoff and Belova may have grown up listening to. The rest of his score developed from that piece, and also took inspiration from Russian composers [[Sergei Prokofiev]] and [[Igor Stravinsky]]. Balfe completed recording for the score at [[Abbey Road Studios]] as the COVID-19 pandemic began to impact studio recording sessions.<ref name="BalfeSyFy" />

The film's opening credits sequence features a cover of [[Nirvana (band)|Nirvana]]'s "[[Smells Like Teen Spirit]]" by Think Up Anger, featuring Malia J,<ref name="VarietyReview" /> which was suggested by Perception.<ref name="Perception" /> "[[American Pie (song)|American Pie]]" by [[Don McLean]] and "[[Cheap Thrills (song)|Cheap Thrills]]" by [[Sia]] are also featured in the film.<ref name="Songs" /> A soundtrack album featuring Balfe's score was released digitally by [[Marvel Music (record label)|Marvel Music]] and [[Hollywood Records]] on July 9, 2021.<ref name="Soundtrack" />

== Marketing ==
[[File:Kevin Feige (48462887397) (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|left|Producer [[Kevin Feige]] at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con]]
The film was officially announced at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con by Feige, Shortland, and members of the cast. They discussed the film and introduced footage from the first 30 days of production.<ref name="SDCCDetails" /> Some of that footage was included in a teaser trailer for the film that was released in December,<ref name="TeaserCNN" /><ref name="TeaserVerge" /> with several commentators highlighting its spy thriller tone<ref name="TeaserMTV" /><ref name="TeaserForbes" /> and calling the film "long awaited" or "highly anticipated" by fans.<ref name="TeaserCNN" /><ref name="TeaserVerge" /><ref name="TeaserMS" /> Rachel Leishman of ''[[The Mary Sue]]'' said finally seeing a trailer for the film was "surprisingly emotional". She felt that setting it between ''Civil War'' and ''Infinity War'' would allow the character to grow into her more mature form from the latter film after earlier MCU films had depicted her in a supporting role to the male Avengers.<ref name="TeaserMS" /> Scott Mendelson of ''[[Forbes]]'' compared the trailer's story and tone to the films ''[[Atomic Blonde]]'' (2017), ''[[Red Sparrow]]'' (2018), and ''[[Anna (2019 feature film)|Anna]]'' (2019), but felt ''Black Widow'' had a commercial advantage over those films since it stars a familiar character. Mendelson thought this familiarity could outweigh the teaser's focus on "family melodrama" over superheroics, which he compared to Marvel Studios' ''[[Thor (film)|Thor]]'' (2011).<ref name="TeaserForbes" /> ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]''{{'}}s Richard Newby found noticeable differences in Shortland's shot composition and cinematography in the trailer compared to the styles of [[Jon Favreau]], Joss Whedon, and the [[Russo brothers]], all directors who helped define Black Widow in previous MCU films, indicating a move away from the MCU's "house style".<ref name="TeaserTHR" />

What was intended to be the final trailer for the film debuted in March 2020. Nicole Carpenter of ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]'' said it was the most in-depth look at the film yet with a more "intimate look" at the relationship between Romanoff and Belova.<ref name="PolygonFinalTrailer" /> Josh Weiss of ''[[Syfy Wire]]'' enjoyed the trailer's quieter moments in addition to the expected action sequences.<ref name="SyfyWireFinalTrailer" /> Mendelson found the trailer to be an improvement over the teaser, attributing this to its theme of "found families (the Avengers), forced families ([the Black Widows]) and actual family" portrayed by Pugh, Harbour, and Weisz.<ref name="ForbesFinalTrailer" />

Disney's president of marketing Asad Ayaz said that after ''Black Widow'' was delayed from its original May 2020 release date, the marketing team paused their campaign for the film. Once they began working towards a new release date in 2021, they were able to use character looks and story points they had not revealed in the initial campaign to build a new approach to the film. Ayaz explained that they did not want it to feel like they had returned to the same marketing campaign, which focused on the Black Widow symbol and her traditional black costume. The marketing team differentiated the new campaign by instead featuring the character's new white costume from the film, and by focusing on her legacy as an Avenger.<ref name="AyazJun2021" /> The campaign featured 30 brands, including co-branded opportunities with [[GEICO]], [[Ziploc]], [[BMW]], and [[Synchrony Bank]]. Additional custom partnerships occurred with [[Fandango Media|Fandango]], [[YouTube]], [[Roku]], [[TikTok]], and [[Amazon (company)|Amazon]], with a [[Twitter]] [[E3]] gaming sponsorship as well as announcements, posters, and collectibles for premium theater experiences such as [[IMAX]].<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" />

In September 2020, [[Barbie]] released two Black Widow dolls featuring Romanoff's black and white outfits from the film.<ref name="Barbie" /> Marvel released another trailer in April 2021, which Austen Goslin at ''Polygon'' described as a new "final" trailer for the film's new release date. He said it only had a few new scenes in it but provided the best look yet at the villain Taskmaster. Goslin highlighted the Russian-inspired version of ''The Avengers'' theme music used at the end of the trailer.<ref name="Polygon2021Trailer" /> Germain Lussier of ''[[io9]]'' also highlighted the use of ''The Avengers'' theme, feeling that the music combined with footage from previous MCU films as well as flashback moments of Romanoff and her family made the trailer feel more epic than the previous "final" trailer. Lussier said it was a trailer that "gets you excited for the return" of MCU films after the pandemic delays.<ref name="io92021Trailer" /> Ethan Anderton of ''[[/Film]]'' said the free-falling fight with Taskmaster showcased in the trailer "looks like a sequence unlike any other" in the MCU.<ref name="/Film2021Trailer" /> The trailer received over {{nowrap|70 million}} views in its first 24 hours.<ref name="THR2021Trailer" /> On July 5, ''Moneymaker: Behind the Black Widow'', a half-hour documentary special centered on Johansson's stunt double Heidi Moneymaker, premiered on [[ESPN+]] as part of [[ESPN]]'s ''[[E:60|E60]]'' series. The special was directed by Martin Khodabakhshian and narrated by Johansson. A subsequent, eight-minute version of the special aired on ESPN's ''[[Outside the Lines]]'' on July 10.<ref name="MoneymakerESPN" /><ref name="MoneymakerMarvel" /> An episode of the series ''[[Marvel Studios: Legends]]'' was released on July 7, exploring Black Widow using footage from her previous MCU appearances.<ref name="LegendsEp" />

== Release ==
=== Theatrical and Premier Access ===
{{Further|Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on The Walt Disney Company}}
''Black Widow'' premiered on June 29, 2021, at various red carpet fan events in London, Los Angeles, Melbourne, and New York City.<ref name="FanPremiereABC" /><ref name="FanPremieresReuters" /> It was screened at the [[Taormina Film Fest]] on July 3,<ref name="TaorminaFilmFest" /> and was released in the United States on July 9, [[Simultaneous release|simultaneously]] in theaters and on [[Disney+]] with [[Disney+ Premier Access|Premier Access]] for {{USD|30}}.<ref name="JulyRelease" /> It premiered in 46 territories over the course of its first weekend. In the U.S. it opened in 4,100 theaters, with 375 in IMAX, over 800 in premium large formats, 1,500 in [[3D film|3D]], and 275 in specialty [[D-Box]], [[4DX]], and [[ScreenX]] theaters.<ref name="DeadlineGlobalWknd1Projection" /> In IMAX screenings, approximately 22 minutes of the film appeared in IMAX's expanded aspect ratio.<ref name="IMAXScenes" /> ''Black Widow'' is the first film released in Phase Four of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFour" /><ref name="SecondReschedule" /> Release dates for China, Taiwan, India, parts of Australia, and other Southeast Asia and Latin America markets were not set by the film's opening weekend,<ref name="THRWknd1" /> and by September the film was not expected to be released theatrically in China.<ref name="NoChinaRelease" /> ''Black Widow'' was the first of several MCU films to not receive a theatrical release in China, a "de facto ban" that was not lifted until 2023.<ref name="DeFactoChinaBan" />

The film was originally scheduled for release on May 1, 2020.<ref name="SDCCDetails" /> In early March 2020, after the COVID-19 pandemic had caused the [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema|closure of theaters in many countries]], the release date for the film ''[[No Time to Die]]'' was shifted from April 2020 to November 2020. Commentators began speculating about the potential for other major films like ''Black Widow'' to be postponed as well. ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' reported on rumors in the film distribution industry suggesting that ''Black Widow'' would take the November release date of Marvel's ''Eternals'', with the latter being delayed until 2021, but Disney said that it still intended to release ''Black Widow'' in May 2020.<ref name="CoronavirusNoDelay" /> After a trailer was released for the film a week later, Scott Mendelson at ''Forbes'' said the trailer's existence and use of the May 2020 release date confirmed that the film was not being delayed. He said this was "the logical choice" because it was an ideal release date for the film and there was no evidence that the pandemic would affect its performance in the U.S.<ref name="ForbesFinalTrailer" /> A week later, theaters across the U.S. were closed due to the pandemic and gatherings larger than 50 people were discouraged by the [[Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] (CDC); Disney removed the film from its May release date.<ref name="Delayed" /> Adam B. Vary and Matt Donnelly at ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' questioned whether the MCU could be impacted more by pandemic delays than other franchises due to its interconnected nature. A Marvel Studios source told them that changing the film's release date would not affect the MCU timeline, and the pair speculated that this was due to the film being a prequel.<ref name="DelayedDiscussion" /> In April, Disney gave ''Eternals''{{'s}} November 6, 2020, release date to ''Black Widow'' and shifted all other Phase Four films back in the release schedule to accommodate this.<ref name="Phase4Delay" />

Anthony D'Alessandro of ''Deadline Hollywood'' reported in September 2020 that Disney was considering rescheduling ''Black Widow'' again,<ref name="DeadlinePotentialSecondReschedule" /> with ''Variety'' also reporting this and attributing it to the low box office returns for Disney's ''[[Mulan (2020 film)|Mulan]]'' in China and [[Warner Bros.]]' ''[[Tenet]]'' in North America.<ref name="VarietyPotentialSecondReschedule" /> Later that month, Disney pushed the release to May 7, 2021, rescheduling ''Eternals'' and ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]'' (2021) as a result.<ref name="SecondReschedule" /> In January 2021, Feige said he still expected ''Black Widow'' to debut in theaters,<ref name="FeigeRelease" /> but ''Variety'' reported that Disney was considering releasing the film on its streaming service Disney+. There was also potential to delay the film's release again, or release it concurrently in theaters and on Disney+ with Premier Access—as was done with Disney's ''[[Raya and the Last Dragon]]'' (2021)—if the effects of the pandemic did not improve leading up to May 2021. ''Variety'' felt it would be "insurmountably more challenging" for ''Black Widow'' to become profitable if it did not have a traditional theatrical release.<ref name="VarietyPotentialDelayDisney+Release" /><ref name="VarietyPotentialDelayDisney+Release2" /> In early February, Disney CEO [[Bob Chapek]] reaffirmed that ''Black Widow'' was intended to be solely released in theaters, but Disney was cognizant of theaters reopening, particularly in large cities such as New York and Los Angeles, as well as consumer desire to return to theaters.<ref name="ChapekFeb2021Release" /> According to ''Variety'', Feige was opposed to a hybrid release for the film.<ref name="VarietyFeb2021Release" /> If the film was delayed again, the film distribution industry believed Disney would move it to July 9, 2021, which at that point was the release date for ''Shang-Chi''.<ref name="PotentialJulyMove" /> Chapek reiterated the next month that Disney planned to release ''Black Widow'' in theaters on May 7,<ref name="ChapekMar2021Release" /> while ''Deadline Hollywood'' again noted the possibilities of delaying the film, releasing it simultaneously on Disney+, or releasing it in theaters for a short time before making it available on Disney+.<ref name="ChapekMar2021Release" /> Chapek soon stated that Disney was remaining flexible as they gauged consumer behavior, and they would make a final decision at the "last minute".<ref name="ChapekMar2021Release2" />

In late March, Disney moved the film's release date to July 9, 2021, and announced that it would be released simultaneously in theaters and on Disney+ with Premier Access. ''Shang-Chi'' was delayed again as a result. [[Kareem Daniel]], the chairman of [[Disney Media and Entertainment Distribution]], said the simultaneous release gave fans options to see the film while serving the "evolving preferences of audiences".<ref name="JulyRelease" /> Chaim Gartenberg at ''[[The Verge]]'' opined that Disney had to move forward with a simultaneous release because they could not afford to delay Marvel's Phase Four television series, which were some of the few "high-profile, must-watch shows" on Disney+ and once they began with ''[[WandaVision]]'' in January 2021 there was only so long that the films could be delayed before the interconnected nature of Marvel's storytelling began causing issues. For instance, the series ''Hawkeye'' was expected to be released later in 2021 and contain spoilers for ''Black Widow'', so the film needed to be released before then. Gartenberg said Disney and Marvel were victims of their own success, but felt the potential revenue loss from the simultaneous release could lead to long-term positives such as fans who otherwise would not have watched Marvel's series potentially discovering them when signing up for Disney+ to watch ''Black Widow''.<ref name="SimultaneousVerge" />

==== Lawsuit ====
In July 2021, Johansson filed a lawsuit in [[Los Angeles County Superior Court]] against The Walt Disney Company, alleging that the simultaneous release of ''Black Widow'' in theaters and on Disney+ breached a stipulation in her contract that the film be released exclusively in theaters.<ref name="JohanssonLawsuit" /> The suit claimed that the simultaneous release exempted Disney from paying "very large box office bonuses" that Johansson would have been entitled to.<ref name="JohanssonBonuses" /> According to ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]'', Johansson had been concerned about the possibility of the film being released on Disney+ as early as prior to the release of ''Avengers: Endgame'' when ''Black Widow'' was still in development.<ref name="LawsuitWSJ" /> ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'' contributor Chris Lee opined that not having any planned appearances in future MCU projects might have influenced Johansson's decision to move forward with filing the lawsuit.<ref name="LawsuitVulture" /> Disney issued a statement in response to the suit, saying it had "no merit whatsoever" and that Johansson had shown a "callous disregard for the horrific and prolonged global effects of the COVID-19 pandemic". The company claimed that they had fully complied with Johansson's contract and that the Disney+ Premier Access release of the film had "significantly enhanced [Johansson's] ability to earn additional compensation" beyond the $20&nbsp;million she had already received for the film.<ref name="JohanssonLawsuit" />

[[Bryan Lourd]], Johansson's agent and co-chairman of [[Creative Artists Agency]], condemned Disney's response as "shamelessly and falsely accus[ing] Ms. Johansson of being insensitive to the global COVID pandemic". He accused the company of "leaving artistic and financial partners" out of their streaming profits and denounced the disclosure of Johansson's $20&nbsp;million earnings as "an attempt to weaponize her success as an artist and businesswoman, as if that were something she should be ashamed of".<ref name="LourdResponse" /><ref name="VarietyLawsuitResponses" /> The advocacy organizations [[Women in Film]], [[ReFrame]], and [[Time's Up (organization)|Time's Up]] jointly denounced Disney's response, calling it a "gendered character attack" and saying they "stand firmly against Disney's recent statement which attempts to characterize Johansson as insensitive or selfish for defending her contractual business rights".<ref name="DisneyResponseCriticism" /> ''[[TheWrap]]'' reported that Johansson was shocked by the tone of Disney's response,<ref name="JohanssonResponse" /> while Disney chairman and then-former CEO [[Bob Iger]] was allegedly mortified.<ref name="IgerResponse" /> Feige was reported to be angered and embarrassed by Disney's handling of the situation, and wanted the company to make it right with Johansson.<ref name="FeigeResponse" /> [[SAG-AFTRA]] president [[Gabrielle Carteris]] also condemned Disney's response, saying they "should be ashamed of themselves for resorting to tired tactics such as gender-shaming and bullying".<ref name="CarterisResponse" /> In response to these criticisms, Disney attorney [[Daniel Petrocelli]] called the suit a "highly orchestrated [public relations] campaign to achieve an outcome that is not obtainable in a lawsuit".<ref name="PetrocelliResponse" />

Eriq Gardner at ''The Hollywood Reporter'' believed Johansson's case was potentially weak since disputes of this kind usually take place in [[arbitration]], and that she had been forced into presenting a claim of [[tortious interference]] rather than a standard contract breach due to a clause in her contract. Gardner added that Disney could defend not waiting for the market to recover from the pandemic since they had already delayed the film's release by a year and needed ''Black Widow'' to be released so they could continue the MCU, considering the film introduces important new characters such as Yelena Belova who was expected to appear in future MCU projects.<ref name="THRGardnerLawsuit" /> ''[[Screen Daily]]'' editor Matt Mueller told [[BBC News]] that he expected the case would be resolved between Disney and Johansson before reaching court, and expressed surprise that Disney had let it get to this stage, given [[Warner Bros.]] had made agreements with stars for simultaneous releases in theaters and on [[HBO Max]] for films like ''[[Space Jam: A New Legacy]]'' (2021). Mueller also believed the case would prompt other studios with streaming services to look at what contractual steps they would need to prevent further legal action like this.<ref name="MatMuellerBBC" /> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' reported in July 2021 that the case had prompted other Disney actors to consider similar litigations.<ref name="VarietyLawsuitResponses" />

In August 2021, Disney filed a motion to move the lawsuit to arbitration, citing that ''Black Widow'' had outgrossed other MCU films in its opening weekend with an "impressive pandemic-era showing".<ref name="DigitalGross" /> Johansson's attorney John Berlinski criticized this move as an attempt by Disney to "hide its misconduct in a confidential arbitration", while calling the company's initial response to the case misogynistic.<ref name="BerlinskiResponse" /> The suit was settled a month later under undisclosed terms, though ''Deadline Hollywood'' reported that Johansson would receive over $40&nbsp;million from Disney.<ref name="Settlement" /><ref name="SettlementDeadline" /> The settlement came after Disney chose to give theatrical-only releases to subsequent 2021 films following the box office success of ''Shang-Chi'' and ''[[Free Guy]]'' (2021), which both received exclusive theatrical windows when initially released.<ref name="SettlementWrap" /> In November 2021, Johansson said in regards to the lawsuit that she felt "very fortunate that nobody will have to go through what I went through" and thought the case had made "a positive impact in the industry and hopefully for artists and creatives' lives and livelihood[s]."<ref name="JohanssonCommentSettlement" />

=== Home media ===
''Black Widow'' was released by [[Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment]] on [[Digital distribution|digital download]] in the U.S. on August 10, 2021, and was released on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[DVD-Video|DVD]] on September 14. Deleted scenes, a gag reel, and behind-the-scenes featurettes were included. The film was made available to all Disney+ subscribers starting October 6.<ref name="HomeMedia" /> The IMAX Enhanced version of the film was made available on Disney+ on November 12.<ref name="IMAXEnhanced" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Black Widow'' grossed $183.7&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $196.1&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $379.8&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /> The film's opening weekend earned $226.2&nbsp;million globally, which included $80.4&nbsp;million at the domestic box office, $78.8&nbsp;million at the international box office, and $67&nbsp;million in Disney+ Premier Access global revenue.<ref name="DeadlineIntlWknd1" /><ref name="DisneyMotionLawsuit" /> The opening weekend gross was within or had exceeded various pre-release projections.<ref name="DeadlineGlobalWknd1Projection" /><ref name="BoxOfficePro" /> In June 2021, Fandango reported that the film had the most ticket presales in 2021, and surpassed other MCU films from previous years like ''[[Doctor Strange (2016 film)|Doctor Strange]]'' (2016) and ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017).<ref name="DeadlineFandango" />

''Black Widow'' earned $39.5&nbsp;million on its opening day, including $13.2&nbsp;million from Thursday night previews, which was the best preview night and opening day since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in March 2020. Its total weekend gross was $80.4&nbsp;million, making it the top film of the weekend.<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" /><ref name="BOMWKnd1" /><ref name="GuardianNG" /> This was the largest box office opening since the COVID-19 pandemic began, surpassing ''[[F9 (film)|F9]]''{{'s}} opening of $70&nbsp;million,<ref name="THRWknd1" /> and the largest opening weekend since ''[[Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker]]'' (2019).<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" /> The domestic gross was within some of the pre-release projections for the film,<ref name="DeadlineGlobalWknd1Projection" /><ref name="BoxOfficePro" /> but it was below some updated industry projections that were made during the weekend after the opening night and preview grosses were known; ''Deadline Hollywood'' partially attributed this to the film's availability on Disney+ with Premier Access.<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" /> When the $80.4&nbsp;million theatrical gross was combined with the domestic Premier Access revenue of $55&nbsp;million, totaling over $135.4&nbsp;million for its opening weekend, Disney noted that ''Black Widow'' was the only film to surpass $100&nbsp;million in domestic consumer spend on opening weekend since the start of the pandemic, and it marked the third largest opening ever for an MCU origin film behind ''[[Black Panther (film)|Black Panther]]'' ($202&nbsp;million) and ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' ($153.4&nbsp;million).<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" /><ref name="BerlinskiResponse" /> Following its opening weekend, ''Black Widow'' posted the largest non-holiday Monday ($7.16&nbsp;million) and Tuesday ($7.6&nbsp;million) gross in the pandemic.<ref name="DeadlineWk1Weekday" /> The film passed $100&nbsp;million at the domestic box office in six days, the fastest to do so in the pandemic.<ref name="100M" />

In its second weekend, the film grossed $25.8&nbsp;million,<ref name="BOMWKnd2" /> finishing second behind ''Space Jam: A New Legacy''. Its 67% drop marked the largest sophomore week decline for an MCU film, passing ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp]]'' (62%).<ref name="DeadlineWknd2" /> Box office analysts attributed ''Black Widow''{{'s}} second-week decline to its Disney+ Premier Access release, as well as widely reported piracies of the film online.<ref name="VarietyBoxOffice" /><ref name="DeadlineBODrop" /> In its third weekend, the film earned a further $11.6&nbsp;million and became the fastest film to reach $150&nbsp;million in total domestic box office gross in the pandemic.<ref name="DeadlineWknd3" /><ref name="Old" /> ''Black Widow'' was the [[List of American films of 2021#Box office|fourth-highest-grossing film of 2021 in the United States and Canada]].<ref name="DomesticHighestGrossing" />

Outside of North America, ''Black Widow'' earned $78.8&nbsp;million in its opening weekend, from 46 markets. It was the number one film in nearly all of these markets, including the markets in the Asia Pacific region where it opened—excluding Japan, where it was third<ref name="DeadlineIntlWknd1" /><ref name="JapanBoxOffice" />—and all markets in the Latin America region. ''Black Widow'' had the top pandemic opening weekend in 15 European markets. IMAX accounted for $4.8&nbsp;million of the weekend gross, from 59 countries, 11 of which set opening weekend records for the pandemic. In South Korea, the film's opening day was the second-best of 2021, with $3.3&nbsp;million, and Hong Kong had the best opening of the pandemic, with $3.2&nbsp;million. The film had the largest opening day of the pandemic in Austria, the Czech Republic, Qatar, and Slovakia, while in Saudi Arabia, the film earned the highest opening day for a Disney release ever. It was the number one film on opening day in many other markets. As of October 2021, the top markets for the film outside of North America were South Korea ($26.3&nbsp;million), the United Kingdom ($25.8&nbsp;million), and France ($15.1&nbsp;million).<ref name="BOM" /><ref name="DeadlineIntlWknd1" />

=== Streaming viewership ===
With Disney+ Premier Access, ''Black Widow'' earned $67&nbsp;million worldwide in its opening weekend.<ref name="DeadlineIntlWknd1" /><ref name="DisneyMotionLawsuit" /> It was the first film that Disney revealed Premier Access revenue for, with the revenue skewing heavily towards the United States with $55&nbsp;million.<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" /><ref name="BerlinskiResponse" /> Analytics company [[Samba TV]], which measures at least five minutes of viewership on smart televisions in over 3 million U.S. households, reported that 1.1&nbsp;million households watched the film in its opening weekend. ''Deadline Hollywood'' noted that this viewership translated to about $33&nbsp;million in revenue for Disney, considering the {{USD|30}} price of Premier Access, which lined up with the announced worldwide revenue.<ref name="SambaWknd1Viewership" /> The site also stated that Disney was receiving about 85% of the Disney+ Premier revenue, sharing the rest with platform providers such as [[Amazon Fire TV|Amazon Firestick]] and [[Apple TV+]].<ref name="Samba10DayViewership" /> The following weekend, ''Deadline Hollywood'' reported that ''Black Widow'' had become the most-pirated film of the past week,<ref name="DeadlineWknd2" /> while some industry sources believed it had become the most-pirated film of the pandemic.<ref name="DeadlineBODrop" /> 

Samba TV later updated the film's Disney+ Premier Access viewership, reporting that the film had been streamed over 2 million times in the U.S. over its first 10 days of release, which resulted in around $60&nbsp;million in overall domestic revenue from Disney+. Samba TV also reported updates to 10-day viewership in the United Kingdom (258,000), Germany (116,000), and Australia (47,000).<ref name="Samba10DayViewership" /> In October, Samba TV reported that over 1.1&nbsp;million U.S. households had watched ''Black Widow'' in the first five days of its availability to all subscribers on Disney+. They also reported viewership in the United Kingdom (190,000) and Germany (96,000) during that same time frame.<ref name="SambaOctober" /> As of August 2021, ''Black Widow'' had earned $125&nbsp;million through streaming and digital downloads.<ref name="DigitalGross" /> In its first 30 days, the film was watched in over 2.8&nbsp;million U.S. households. ''Deadline Hollywood'' reported the film had been pirated over 20&nbsp;million times, resulting in at least $600&nbsp;million in lost revenue for Disney.<ref name="PiracyDeadline" /> [[Nielsen Media Research]], which records streaming viewership on certain U.S. television screens, reported that ''Black Widow'' was the eleventh most-streamed film of 2021 with 4.410 billion minutes streamed.<ref name="Nielsen2021" /> In January 2022, tech firm Akamai listed ''Black Widow'' as the third-most pirated film of 2021.<ref name="AkamaiPirated" />

The review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of {{RT data|score}}, with an average score of {{RT data|average}}, based on {{RT data|count}} reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Black Widow''{{'}}s deeper themes are drowned out in all the action, but it remains a solidly entertaining standalone adventure that's rounded out by a stellar supporting cast."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=black_widow_2021 |type=m |title=Black Widow |access-date=June 26, 2023}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a weighted average, assigned the film a score of 68 out of 100 based on reviews from 58 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=black-widow |type=movie |title=Black Widow (2021) Reviews |access-date=June 26, 2023}}</ref> Critics commonly praised the cast, particularly Johansson and Pugh, as well as the action sequences.<ref name="ReviewRoundupEW" /><ref name="ReviewRoundupMPA" /><ref name="ReviewRoundupMashable" /> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale, while [[PostTrak]] reported that 88% of audience members gave it a positive score and 69% said they would definitely recommend the film.<ref name="DeadlineWknd1" />

[[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''Variety'' initially feared that ''Black Widow'' would just be two hours of Johansson fighting, but found the film to be more interesting than that despite still having fight sequences that would "give a mainstream audience that getting-your-money's-worth feeling". He praised Shortland's direction and said, "from the opening credits, most of it has a gritty, deliberate, zap-free tone that is strikingly—and intentionally—earthbound for a superhero fantasy".<ref name="VarietyReview" /> Writing for ''The Hollywood Reporter'', David Rooney felt the film's move away from the superhero genre into a "high-octane espionage thriller" made it a more satisfying female-starring MCU film than the "blandly bombastic" ''Captain Marvel''.<ref name="THRReview" /> At ''[[IGN]]'', Nicole Clark discussed the ambition of the film to go beyond the superhero genre to explore the abuse and "intense tragedy" from Romanoff's past.<ref name="IGNReview" />

At ''The Hollywood Reporter'', Rooney described the film as a "stellar vehicle" for Johansson and praised her vulnerability in the film,<ref name="THRReview" /> while Gleiberman said Johansson "holds the film together and gives it its touch of soul" with her vulnerability which he said was unusual for a superhero film performance.<ref name="VarietyReview" /> Several critics praised the film's supporting cast,<ref name="ReviewRoundupMashable" /> with the chemistry between Johansson and Pugh in particular a highlight.<ref name="ReviewRoundupEW" /> Pete Hammond of ''Deadline Hollywood'' said "Natasha's awkward shyness [is] counterpunched by the lively and cynical Yelena", and praised Johansson's presence in her role. He felt Pugh was ready to lead her own franchise and also praised the performances of Weisz, Harbour, and Winstone.<ref name="DeadlineReview" /> Leah Greenblatt of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' said the relationship between Romanoff and Belova was the "real love story" of the film,<ref name="EWReview" /> and Nicole Clark felt the film was at its strongest in the scenes where the pair were fighting against or beside each other.<ref name="IGNReview" /> Brian Tallerico of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' said Pugh was the film's MVP and had found "just the right shades of strength and vulnerability".<ref name="EbertReview" /> [[Caryn James]] at the ''[[BBC]]'' also praised Pugh and said Belova was "more lived-in than most action-movie characters".<ref name="BBCReview" />

''[[IndieWire]]''{{'s}} Eric Kohn gave the film a B grade, saying it delivered on action and praising the lower stakes. He positively compared the film's fight sequences to the [[Bourne (franchise)|''Bourne'' franchise]] and said, "If this is the last time we get to see Johansson mete out justice to her assailants with gymnastic velocity, it's an apt send-off."<ref name="IndieWireReview" /> Hammond of ''Deadline Hollywood'' agreed, writing that Johansson "goes out with all guns blazing as this [film...] does not stint one bit on the action".<ref name="DeadlineReview" /> Gleiberman also compared the film to the ''Bourne'' franchise,<ref name="VarietyReview" /> while James compared it to the ''[[Mission: Impossible]]'' franchise and said it was "the least Avenger-like movie in the [MCU] so far". She gave the film four out of five stars, but did criticize the ending as being typical of the franchise.<ref name="BBCReview" /> Writing for ''Vanity Fair'', Richard Lawson praised Shortland's direction of the action sequences and the film's more grounded physics.<ref name="VanityFairReview" />

Despite calling the film stylish and fun, Joshua Rivera of ''Polygon'' said it came across as "hollow" and like an "apology" for Romanoff's death in ''Endgame''.<ref name="PolygonReview" /> Similarly, Hoai-Tran Bui at ''/Film'' felt the film was "too little, too late" after the character's death,<ref name="/FilmReview" /> and Matt Goldberg at ''Collider'' felt the film wasted the chance to further explore the Budapest mission in Romanoff's past in favor of introducing Belova as a replacement Black Widow.<ref name="ColliderReview" /> Ann Hornaday at ''[[The Washington Post]]'' had similar thoughts, noting the set-up for Pugh to continue in the franchise and feeling the film did not make up for Johansson getting "short shrift" in earlier MCU installments.<ref name="WashingtonPostReview" />

== Documentary special ==
{{Main|Marvel Studios: Assembled}}
In February 2021, the documentary series ''[[Marvel Studios: Assembled]]'' was announced. The specials go behind the scenes of the MCU films and television series with cast members and additional creatives. A special for this film, "[[The Making of Black Widow|The Making of ''Black Widow'']]", featuring Johansson,<ref name="Assembled" /> was released on Disney+ on October 20, 2021.<ref name="AssembledRelease" />

== Future ==
Pugh reprised her role as Belova in the Disney+ series ''[[Hawkeye (miniseries)|Hawkeye]]'', continuing the storyline that is set up by the film's post-credits scene.<ref name="VarietyPostCredits" /> In June 2021, Shortland expressed interest in directing another film in the MCU, and noted that a potential sequel to ''Black Widow'' would likely revolve around a different character following Romanoff's death.<ref name="FutureRadioTimes" /> Weisz said she would be interested in a future storyline featuring Vostokoff assuming her Iron Maiden comic book persona.<ref name="WeiszCB" />

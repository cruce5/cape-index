'''''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania''''' is a 2023 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the characters [[Ant-Man (Scott Lang)|Scott Lang / Ant-Man]] and [[Hope Pym]] / [[Wasp (character)|Wasp]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is the sequel to ''[[Ant-Man (film)|Ant-Man]]'' (2015) and ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp]]'' (2018), and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 31st film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). It was directed by [[Peyton Reed]], written by [[Jeff Loveness]], and stars [[Paul Rudd]] as [[Scott Lang (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Scott Lang]] and [[Evangeline Lilly]] as [[Hope van Dyne]], alongside [[Jonathan Majors]], [[Kathryn Newton]], [[David Dastmalchian]], [[Katy O'Brian]], [[William Jackson Harper]], [[Bill Murray]], [[Michelle Pfeiffer]], [[Corey Stoll]], and [[Michael Douglas]]. In the film, Scott, Hope, and their family are accidentally transported to the [[Quantum Realm]] and face off against [[Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kang the Conqueror]] (Majors).

Plans for a third ''Ant-Man'' film were confirmed in November 2019, with Reed and Rudd returning. Loveness was hired by April 2020, with development beginning during the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. The title and new cast members, including the additions of Majors and Newton, were announced in December 2020. Filming in Turkey began in early February 2021, and additional filming occurred in San Francisco in mid-June. [[Principal photography]] began at the end of July at [[Pinewood Studios]] in [[Buckinghamshire]] and ended in November. With a net production budget of $330.1 million, it is [[List of most expensive films|one of the most expensive films ever made]].

''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania'' premiered in [[Westwood, Los Angeles]], on February 6, 2023, and was released in the United States on February 17. It is the first film, and beginning, of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU. The film received mixed reviews from critics and was a [[box-office disappointment]], grossing $476.1&nbsp;million worldwide and becoming one of the few MCU films to not [[break-even]] in its theatrical run.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. -->
Following the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]]'s [[Battle of Earth (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|battle]] against [[Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thanos]],{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019)}} [[Scott Lang (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Scott Lang]] has become a successful memoirist and has been living happily with his girlfriend, [[Hope van Dyne]]. Scott's now-teenage daughter [[Cassie Lang (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Cassie]] has become an activist, helping people displaced by [[the Blip]], resulting in her having a strained relationship with her father. While visiting Hope's parents, [[Hank Pym (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hank Pym]] and [[Janet van Dyne (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Janet van Dyne]], Cassie reveals that she has been working on a device that can establish contact with the [[Quantum Realm]]. Upon learning of this, Janet panics and forcefully shuts off the device, but the message is received, resulting in a portal that opens and sucks the five of them, and a number of Hank's lab ants, into the Quantum Realm. Scott and Cassie are found by natives who are rebelling against their ruler, while Hope, Janet, and Hank explore a sprawling city to get answers.

Hope, Janet, and Hank meet with [[Lord Krylar]], a former ally of Janet's, who reveals that things have changed since she left,{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp]]'' (2018)}} and that he is now working for [[Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kang]], the Quantum Realm's new ruler. The three are forced to flee and steal Krylar's ship. The Langs, meanwhile, are told by rebel leader [[Jentorra]] that Janet's involvement with Kang is indirectly responsible for his rise to power. The rebels soon come under attack by Kang's forces led by [[M.O.D.O.K. (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|M.O.D.O.K.]], who is revealed to be [[Darren Cross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Darren Cross]], having survived his apparent death at the hands of Scott,{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Ant-Man (film)|Ant-Man]]'' (2015)}} and who previously received Cassie's message. Aboard Krylar's ship, Janet confesses to Hope and Hank that she met Kang when she was previously in the Quantum Realm. He claimed that he and Janet could both escape from the Quantum Realm if she helped him rebuild his [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiversal]] power core. After they managed to repair it, Janet saw a vision of Kang conquering and destroying entire timelines. Kang revealed he was exiled by his variants out of fear, which drove Janet to turn against him. Outmatched, Janet used her Pym Particles to enlarge the power core beyond use. Kang, having regained his powers, eventually conquered the Quantum Realm afterward.

The Langs are taken to Kang, who demands that Scott help get his power core back or else he will kill Cassie. Scott is then taken to the core's location and shrinks down. In the core, he encounters a probability storm, which causes him to split into multiple copies of himself, nearly overwhelming him, but Hope arrives and helps him acquire the power core. However, Kang reneges on the deal, capturing Janet with M.O.D.O.K. destroying her ship with Hank on it. After being rescued by his ants, who rapidly evolved and became hyper-intelligent in the Quantum Realm, Hank helps Scott and Hope as they make their way to Kang. Cassie rescues Jentorra and they commence an uprising against Kang and his army. During the fight, Cassie convinces Cross to switch sides and fight Kang, with him eventually sacrificing his life.

Janet fixes the power core as she, Hank, Hope, and Cassie jump through a portal home. Kang attacks Scott at the last minute. Before he can beat Scott into submission, Hope returns, and she and Scott throw Kang and the Pym Particles into the power core, destroying them both. Cassie reopens the portal for Scott and Hope to return home. As Scott happily resumes his life, he begins to rethink what he was told about Kang's death being the start of something terrible happening, but brushes it off. In a [[Post-credits scene|mid-credits scene]], [[Council of Kangs (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|numerous variants of Kang]], led by [[Immortus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Immortus]], commiserate Kang's death and plan their multiversal uprising. In a post-credits scene, [[Loki (Marvel Cinematic Universe)#2012 variant|Loki]] and [[Mobius M. Mobius (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mobius M. Mobius]] encounter another Kang variant, [[Victor Timely (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Victor Timely]], at the [[World's Columbian Exposition|Chicago World's Fair]] in 1893.{{efn|As depicted in the [[Loki season 2|''Loki'' season 2]] episode "[[1893 (Loki)|1893]]" (2023)<ref name="LokiS2Ep3Polygon" /><ref name="LokiS2Ep3Collider" /><ref name="LokiS2Ep3ScreenRant" />}}

== Production ==
=== Development ===
Ahead of the release of ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp]]'' (2018), director [[Peyton Reed]] said there were elements of that film that leave "a lot to play with" in a potential third film in the franchise. He highlighted the [[Quantum Realm]], which was introduced in ''[[Ant-Man (film)|Ant-Man]]'' (2015) and explored further in ''Ant-Man and the Wasp''; Reed said they were "just dipping our toes into it" for the previous films.<ref name="ReedQRJul2018" /> Reed added that he and [[Marvel Studios]] were hopeful about a third film and had discussed potential story points for such a sequel.<ref name="ReedTHRJul2018" /> In February 2019, [[Hank Pym (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hank Pym]] actor [[Michael Douglas]] confirmed that informal discussions regarding a sequel to ''Ant-Man and the Wasp'' had taken place,<ref name="DouglasFuture" /> though by that time [[Evangeline Lilly]] had not heard of any plans for her character [[Hope van Dyne|Hope van Dyne / Wasp]] following her role in ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019). Lilly stated that "Hope is mid-journey. I don't see her journey as being over by any stretch."<ref name="LillyFeb2019" /> That October, [[Michelle Pfeiffer]] expressed interest in reprising her role of [[Janet van Dyne (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Janet van Dyne]] in a sequel,<ref name="PfeifferOct2019" /> while Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]] was asked about the future of [[Paul Rudd]]'s [[Scott Lang (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Scott Lang / Ant-Man]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) following ''Endgame'', and responded that "the chess pieces were arranged very purposefully" at the end of that film, with some taken "off the board" and others, like Ant-Man, "still on [the board, so] you never know".<ref name="RuddFuture" /> Rudd was asked whether he would be returning to the role, either in a third ''Ant-Man'' film or as part of another hero's MCU franchise, and said that both of those options had been discussed.<ref name="RuddOct2019" />

Reed officially signed on to direct a third ''Ant-Man'' film at the start of November 2019. Rudd was set to star as Lang,<ref name="THRReedAM3" /> with Lilly and Douglas also returning.<ref name="VarietyReedAM3" /> Filming was scheduled to begin in January 2021 with a likely release date in 2022.<ref name="January2021FilmingDouglas" /><ref name="THRReedAM3" /> Reed was hired again, despite Marvel's interest in new filmmakers bringing different takes to their heroes for each film, because executives felt he had "a real grasp on the ''Ant-Man'' universe and want[ed] to see him return to wrap up his trilogy".<ref name="VarietyReedAM3" /> [[Jeff Loveness]] was hired to write the script for the film during "the early days of Hollywood's shutdown" due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], and he had begun working on the screenplay by April 2020. At that point, it was no longer clear when production would begin on the film due to the effects of the pandemic on all film productions.<ref name="Loveness" /> [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]], [[Eric Pearson]], Rudd, and [[Erik Sommers]] received credit for additional off-screen literary material.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ant Man and the Wasp: Quantumania |url=https://directories.wga.org/project/1206073/ant-man-and-the-wasp-quantumania/ |access-date=2025-03-11 |website=[[Writers Guild of America West]]}}</ref> In August 2020, Reed confirmed that development on the film was continuing during the pandemic. He said that Lilly would continue to get equal billing in the film alongside Rudd as she is "a very, very important part" of the partnership between Ant-Man and the Wasp, despite rumors that her role would be reduced following controversial comments about the pandemic. Reed added that the story for the film had been "cracked", though "nothing [was] official yet",<ref name="YahooReedAM3" /> and said the third film would be a "bigger, more sprawling movie than the first two [with] a very different visual template".<ref name="ReedColliderAug2020" /> Reed stated they wanted to further explore the Quantum Realm in the film as a "massive [[Worldbuilding|world-building]] undertaking", and compared its design to [[electron microscope]] photography, the ''[[Heavy Metal (magazine)|Heavy Metal]]'' magazine issues of the 1970s and 1980s and his favorite [[science fiction]] cover artists such as [[Jean Giraud]] for [[Magic realism|fantastical realism]].<ref name="ReedEmpireNov2022" /> Marvel Studios producer [[Brian Gay (executive)|Brian Gay]] said the film would "feel like a real departure" from the first two ''Ant-Man'' films and be a big adventure.<ref name="GayDeparture" /> Fellow Marvel Studios executive [[Stephen Broussard]] produced the film alongside Feige.<ref name="DeadlineTrailer" />

=== Pre-production ===

[[Jonathan Majors]] was cast in September 2020 in a "lead role" for the film, which was reported to be [[Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kang the Conqueror]].<ref name="MajorsCast" /> Because the actor cast as Kang would first portray an alternate version of the character, named [[He Who Remains (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|He Who Remains]], in the [[Disney+]] series ''[[Loki (TV series)|Loki]]'', [[Kate Herron]] and [[Michael Waldron]]—the director and [[head writer]] of ''Loki''{{'}}s [[Loki season 1|first season]], respectively—were involved in casting Majors in addition to Reed and executives at Marvel Studios;<ref name="MajorsLoki" /><ref name="LokiHeWhoRemains" /> Marvel Studios were eager to work with and cast Majors after seeing his performance in ''[[The Last Black Man in San Francisco]]'' (2019), with Reed very supportive of approaching Majors.<ref name="FeigeFeb2023" /> Majors was approached for the role without an audition,<ref name="MajorsNoAudition" /> and later noted he had been involved in the film "since the beginning" before joining ''Loki''.<ref name="MajorsOct2021" /> Reed was interested in having Ant-Man and the Wasp go up against a formidable foe in the film, believing Kang to be one of Marvel Comics' "all-timers" like [[Loki (Marvel Comics)|Loki]] and [[Doctor Doom]].<ref name="ReedEWDec2022" />

Newton]] had joined the film as [[Cassie Lang (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Cassie Lang]].<ref name="DisneyInvestorDay" /> Newton originally auditioned for the role of [[Kate Bishop (Marvel Cinematic === Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began on July 26, 2021,<ref name="FilmingStart" /> at [[Pinewood Studios]] in [[Buckinghamshire]],<ref name="PinewoodVolume" /> using the [[working title]] ''Dust Bunny'',<ref name="WorkingTitle" /> with [[Bill Pope]] serving as cinematographer.<ref name="IndieWireVFX" /> [[Industrial Light & Magic]] provided the same [[StageCraft]] virtual production technology Reed used while directing episodes of the [[Disney+]] ''[[Star Wars]]'' series ''[[The Mandalorian]]''.<ref name="PinewoodVolume" /> Will Htay served as the production designer.<ref name="Htay" /> Principal photography was previously set to begin in January 2021 but was postponed due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="January2021FilmingDouglas" /><ref name="Loveness" /><ref name="LillyMid2021Filming" /> It was then expected to take place between May 31 and September 24.<ref name="FilmingTimeLocations" /> By September 16, over 50 crew members from Pinewood Studios productions, mainly ''Quantumania'' and ''[[Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny]]'' (2023), contracted [[norovirus]] following an outbreak at the studio. The main cast was not affected.<ref name="NorovirusOutbreak" /> In October, the film's release was delayed to July 28, 2023.<ref name="October2021Delay" /> Later that month, [[Bill Murray]] revealed he had shot material for a Marvel film with Reed, which was believed to be ''Quantumania''. Murray explained he joined the project as he liked Reed and his work on ''[[Bring It On (film)|Bring It On]]'' (2000), despite not being interested in superhero films,<ref name="Murray" /> before he further indicated his involvement in the film, but said he could not comment on his prior statement;<ref name="MurrayKimmel" /><ref name="MurrayKimmel2" /> Murray later said he was playing a "bad guy" in the film.<ref name="MurrayVillain" /> During the filming process, the screenplay was reportedly still being worked on at the same time, with multiple scenes involving Murray and [[Katy O'Brian]]'s [[Jentorra]] later being scrapped, resulting in Murray's character seemingly dying.<ref name="OBriansInverse" /> Principal photography was completed in November 2021.<ref name="FilmingComplete" /> Filming was also expected to take place in [[Atlanta]],<ref name="FilmingTimeLocations" /> and the production was slated to move to San Francisco in 2022 with the cast.<ref name="SanFranFilmingShots" />

=== Post-production ===
In April 2022, the film was moved back to its February 17, 2023, release date, swapping places with ''[[The Marvels]]'' because ''Quantumania'' was further along in production than that film.<ref name="Feb2023DateSwap" /> In September 2022, [[Randall Park]] was confirmed to be reprising his role as [[Jimmy Woo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Jimmy Woo]],<ref name="Park" /> and Feige called the film "a direct line" into [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] and the planned [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Six|Phase Six]] film ''[[Avengers: The Kang Dynasty]]'',<ref name="FeigeKangDynastyConnection" /> with Majors attached to reprise his role in ''The Kang Dynasty''.<ref name="ReedEWDec2022" /> Reed stated that ''Quantumania'' would have a "profound impact" on the MCU and that the impact of Kang's appearance in this film was discussed with Loveness for ''The Kang Dynasty'', which he was also writing.<ref name="EmpireNov2022" /> Loveness felt that having Kang as the main villain of ''Quantumania'' would set up the dynamic story he was developing for ''The Kang Dynasty''.<ref name="LovenessKangView" /> Marvel Studios was not initially planning to have "[[The Multiverse Saga]]", which comprises Phases Four, Five, and Six, revolve around Kang, but decided to after seeing Majors's performance in the first season of ''Loki'' and the [[dailies]] while filming ''Quantumania''.<ref name="RobinsonKangFocus" /> Reed also hoped the film would not be viewed as a "palate cleanser" the previous two films had been following ''Avengers'' films, but would instead feel as big as an ''Avengers'' film.<ref name="ReedEWDec2022" /> [[Gregg Turkington]] was also revealed to be reprising his role as Dale, a [[Baskin-Robbins]] manager, from ''Ant-Man''.<ref name="Turkington" /> In October 2022, [[William Jackson Harper]] was revealed to be appearing in the film in an undisclosed role,<ref name="Harper" /> and in November, O'Brian was revealed to be appearing in the film alongside [[David Dastmalchian]], who portrayed [[Kurt (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kurt]] in the first two films,<ref name="DastmalchianOBrian" /> after he previously said he was not involved in ''Quantumania''.<ref name="DastmalchianNo" /> In January 2023, Dastmalchian, Harper, O'Brian, Murray, and Stoll were revealed to be playing Veb, Quaz, Jentorra, [[Lord Krylar]],<ref (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|M.O.D.O.K.]], respectively.<ref name="StollMODOK" /> [[Pick-up (filmmaking)|Pick-up shots]] with Rudd occurred by then.<ref name="PickupShots" />

The film's [[mid-credits scene]] sees the introduction of the [[Council of Kangs (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Council of Kangs]], particularly [[Immortus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Immortus]], [[Rama-Tut (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Rama-Tut]], and [[Centurion (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Centurion]]. These variants, all portrayed by Majors, discuss how the Conqueror has been killed and that the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]] are starting to infringe on the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]] and what they have built. Mobius (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mobius M. Mobius]].<ref name="IndieWirePostCredits" /><ref name="THRPostCredits" /><ref name="LokiS2Ep3Polygon" /><ref name="LokiS2Ep3Collider" /><ref name="LokiS2Ep3ScreenRant" />

The film's main-on-end title sequence was designed by [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]], which emulated the visuals of the Quantum Realm using [[fluid motion]] and microscopic chemical reactions.<ref name="MainOnEnd" /> The visual effects were made by [[Sony Pictures Imageworks]], [[Industrial Light & Magic]], [[Digital Domain]], [[Moving Picture Company]], Fin Design + Effects, Spin VFX, [[Rising Sun Pictures]], Folks VFX, Barnstorm VFX, [[Base FX]], [[Pixomondo]], MARZ, [[Luma Pictures]], Atomic Arts, Territory Studio, and Stereo D,<ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> with Jesse James Chisholm as the visual effects supervisor,<ref name="IndieWireVFX" /> while Adam Gerstel and Laura Jennings co-edited the film.<ref name="EditorsVariety" /> Due to the shifting release schedule of the film, the post-production period for ''Quantumania'' was reduced by four-and-a-half months, which resulted in reportedly at least 10 rushed visual effects scenes being added at the last minute.<ref name="VarietyNov2023CoverStory" /> Several visual effects companies that worked on ''Quantumania'' were also in post-production on ''[[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever]]'' (2022) around the same time, which resulted in "critical resources" being diverted to focus on ''Wakanda Forever''.<ref name="VultureFeb2023VFX" /> Disney spent $194.7&nbsp;million during pre-production and filming and an additional $131.9&nbsp;million on post-production in 2022 for a total gross production budget of $326.6&nbsp;million, which was over 63% more than an initially estimated $200&nbsp;million production budget and exceeded that of Disney's internal projections for the budget. == Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania'' grossed $214.5&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $261.6&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $476.1&nbsp;million.<ref name="NUM" /><ref name="BOM" /> It was a [[box-office disappointment]], falling short of its reported [[break-even point]] of $600&nbsp;million,<ref name="VarietyBoxOffice" /> against a gross production budget of $388.4&nbsp;million.<ref name="Forbes-UpdatedBudget"/> This budget was initially reported to be around $200&nbsp;million.<ref name="Budget" /><ref name="VarietyBoxOffice" />

In January 2023, ''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania'' was projected to earn $120&nbsp;million in North America over the four-day [[Presidents' Day]] opening weekend.<ref name="DeadlineBoxOffice" /> The following month, it was projected to debut at $105–110&nbsp;million domestically and $280&nbsp;million globally in its opening weekend.<ref name="DeadlineBoxOffice2" /> The film made $46&nbsp;million on its first day, including $17.5&nbsp;million from Thursday previews that began at 3&nbsp;pm. It went on to debut to $106.1&nbsp;million (and a total of $120.4&nbsp;million over the four-day frame) from 4,345 theatres, marking the best opening of the ''Ant-Man'' series and the third-best for a February release, behind ''[[Black Panther (film)|Black Panther]]'' ($242.1&nbsp;million in 2018) and ''[[Deadpool (film)|Deadpool]]'' ($152.1&nbsp;million in 2016).<ref name="Budget" /><ref name="NUM" /><ref name="DeadlineOpening" /> The film's second weekend saw a 69% drop, down to $32&nbsp;million in North America, the largest second-week domestic drop-off of any [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|Marvel Cinematic Universe film]].<ref name="Variety2ndDrop" /> In its third weekend, the film earned $12.4&nbsp;million, for a drop of 61%.<ref name="DH3rdDomestic" />

Outside the United States and Canada, the film grossed $121.3&nbsp;million in its opening weekend.<ref name="DHIntBOOpening" /> In its second weekend, the film earned $46.4&nbsp;million, for a drop of 57%, and remained the number one non-local movie in most markets.<ref name="DHInt2ndBO" /> The film grossed $22&nbsp;million from 52 markets in its third weekend, for a decline of 53%.<ref name="DHInt3rdBO" /> The top five international markets were China ($39.4&nbsp;million), the UK ($23.9&nbsp;million), Mexico ($18&nbsp;million), France ($13.6&nbsp;million), and South Korea ($12.6&nbsp;million).<ref name="DHInt4thBO" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of 46% with an average rating of 5.5/10, based on 414 reviews. The site's critics consensus reads: "''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania'' mostly lacks the spark of fun that elevated earlier adventures, but Jonathan Majors' Kang is a thrilling villain poised to alter the course of the MCU."<ref name="RTReview" /> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 48 out of 100, based on 61 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="MCReview" /> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale, and those polled by [[PostTrak]] reported 75% of audience members gave the film a positive score, with 60% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="DeadlineOpening" />

[[Owen Gleiberman]] from ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' was critical of the film, calling it "at once fun and numbing" and stating "...if this is what Phase 5 looks like, God save us from Phases 6, 7 and 8."<ref name="VarietyReview" /> [[Caryn James]] from the [[BBC]] stated that the movie has "Marvel's next big villain but other than that, it has nothing to offer beyond drab-looking action."<ref name="BBCReview" /> Wendy Ide from ''[[The Guardian]]'' called Majors's performance the film's "magnetic core", but said that overall the film was "baffling and illogical".<ref name="GuardianReview" /> Ross Bonaime from ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]'' also praised Majors's performance, writing he "makes for an excellent villain, who brings nuance and subtlety to his character ... Majors makes this character [Kang] likable in the beginning, but also never hides the menace and terror that he can cause at any moment." Giving the film a B−, Bonaime stated: "''Quantumania'' is a promising, but shaky start for Phase 5 of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, it's just a shame it comes at the sake of the little guy."<ref name="ColliderReview" /> [[Frank Scheck]] of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' commended Majors for "bringing real gravitas" to the film, and investing "his performance with such an arrestingly quiet stillness and ambivalence that you're on edge every moment he's onscreen." Scheck also praised Pfeiffer, writing she "is terrific in her expanded role, given the opportunity to be a badass heroine and making the most of it."<ref name="THRReview" /> [[Manohla Dargis]] from ''[[The New York Times]]'' felt Pfeiffer, Majors, and Douglas were the "truer stars of this show", but felt the overall film was "busy, noisy and thoroughly uninspired".<ref name="NYTReview" />

[[Richard Roeper]] of ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' rated the film three out of four stars, writing it is a "mid-tier MCU film, with decent enough battle sequences and some nifty visuals".<ref name="CSTReview" /> Leah Greenblatt of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' graded the film a B+, concluding: "At just over 120 minutes, though — a blink in Marvel time — this ''Ant-Man'' is clever enough to be fun, and wise enough not overstay its welcome. Who better understands the benefits, after all, of keeping it small?"<ref name="EWReview" /> [[Michael Phillips (critic)|Michael Phillips]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' gave the film two stars out of four, stating it is "less fun, and blandly garish visually. The earlier films' throwaway jokes and welcome aversion to brutal solemnity have largely been ditched in favor of endless endgame stuff and weirdly cheesy digital world-building in the Quantum Realm."<ref name="CTReview" /> Similarly, David Sims of ''[[The Atlantic]]'' disapprovingly compared the film to the first two entries, writing in his review: "That cleverness, combined with the special-effect goofiness of people and objects getting big and small, powered the series—and it is basically been junked here, replaced by a bunch of celestial showdowns between Kang and Ant-Man. Anytime ''Quantumania'' allows itself to get a little silly, it's in much better shape."<ref name="AtlanticReview" />

=== Accolades ===
''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania'' received a nomination for Best Music Supervision in a Trailer – Film at the 2023 [[Guild of Music Supervisors Awards]].<ref name="GMS" /> At the [[2023 MTV Movie & TV Awards]], it was nominated for [[MTV Movie Award for Best Hero|Best Hero]] (Rudd) and Best Kick-Ass Cast.<ref name="MTVAwards" /> The film garnered a nomination for Best Fantasy Adventure TV Spot (for a Feature Film) at the 2023 [[Golden Trailer Awards]].<ref name="GTANoms" /><ref name="GTAWins" /> The film is also nominated for [[Saturn Award for Best Comic-to-Film Motion Picture|Best Superhero Film]] at the [[51st Saturn Awards]],<ref name="SaturnAwards" /> and for [[AACTA Award for Best Visual Effects or Animation|Best Visual Effects or Animation]] at the [[13th AACTA Awards]].<ref name="AACTAAwards" /> At the [[49th People's Choice Awards]], it is nominated for Action Movie of the Year.<ref name="PCANoms" /> At the [[44th Golden Raspberry Awards]], the film was nominated for [[Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Supporting Actor|Worst Supporting Actor]] for both Douglas and Murray, [[Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Remake, Rip-off or Sequel|Worst Remake, Rip-off or Sequel]], and [[Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Director|Worst Director]] for Reed.<ref name="RazzieNoms" />

== Future ==
In February 2023, Broussard said he had started to discuss a potential fourth film with Feige and Reed.<ref name="BroussardFuture" /> Douglas said he would be interested in returning for a fourth film if Pym died in it,<ref name="DouglasFutureFeb2023" /> but in April 2024 he said "I don't think I'm going to show up" for a fourth film because he had requested for Pym to die in ''Quantumania'' and was disappointed that this did not happen.<ref name="DouglasApr2024" />

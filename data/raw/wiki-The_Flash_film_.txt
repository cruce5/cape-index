'''''The Flash''''' is a 2023 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[DC Comics]] character [[Barry Allen]] / [[The Flash]]. Directed by [[Andy Muschietti]] from a screenplay by [[Christina Hodson]] and a story by the duo of [[John Francis Daley]] and [[Jonathan Goldstein (filmmaker)|Jonathan Goldstein]] as well as [[Joby Harold]], it is the 13th film in the [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU). [[Ezra Miller]] stars as [[Barry Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Barry Allen]], who travels back in time to prevent his mother's death and becomes stranded in an alternate past. [[Sasha Calle]] also stars in her film debut, alongside [[Michael Shannon]], [[Ron Livingston]], [[Maribel Verdú]], [[Kiersey Clemons]], [[Antje Traue]], and [[Michael Keaton]]. [[Barbara Muschietti]] and [[Michael Disco]] produced the film for [[DC Films]] and [[Warner Bros. Pictures]].

Development on a film featuring the Flash began in the late 1980s, with various iterations planned through 2014, when it was redeveloped for the DCEU and Miller was cast. Multiple writers and directors were attached over the following years, with [[Seth Grahame-Smith]], [[Rick Famuyiwa]], Daley, and Goldstein all departing the project over creative differences with the studios. Muschietti and Hodson became involved in July 2019, and pre-production began in January 2020. Influenced by the 2011 comic book storyline ''[[Flashpoint (comics)|Flashpoint]]'', the film explores the [[Multiverse (DC Comics)|multiverse]] and features other DC characters, such as Calle's [[Kara Zor-El (DC Extended Universe)|Supergirl]] and multiple versions of [[Batman]], particularly [[Bruce Wayne (1989 film series character)|Keaton's portrayal]] from the films ''[[Batman (1989 film)|Batman]]'' (1989) and ''[[Batman Returns]]'' (1992), while others were digitally included. Filming lasted from April to October 2021 at [[Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden]] and on location throughout the United Kingdom. The film's ending was altered several times during [[post-production]] due to changes in DC's wider plans under different studio leadership.

''The Flash'' premiered at [[Grauman's Chinese Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on June 12, 2023, and was released in the United States on June 16, following multiple delays caused by the director changes and the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. The film received <!--Please do not change or remove this reception or box office summary per WP:SYNTH and MOS:ACCLAIMED, as these are supported by the Reception section.-->mixed reviews from critics, who praised its humor, action sequences, and performances, but criticized the visual effects and [[third act]]. It was also a [[box-office bomb]], grossing $271.4&nbsp;million worldwide against a budget of $200–220&nbsp;million. Its box office performance was attributed to factors such as [[Ezra Miller#Legal issues|Miller's legal issues and controversies]], the franchise's imminent [[reboot (fiction)|reboot]] with the [[DC Universe (franchise)|DC Universe]] (DCU), [[superhero fatigue]], and the film's mixed reception and marketing practices.

== Plot ==
After helping [[Bruce Wayne (DC Extended Universe)|Bruce Wayne / Batman]] and [[Diana Prince (DC Extended Universe)|Diana Prince / Wonder Woman]] stop a robbery in [[Gotham City]], [[Barry Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Barry Allen / The Flash]] revisits his childhood home, where he lived with his parents [[Nora Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Nora]] and [[Henry Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Henry]], before Henry's wrongful imprisonment for Nora's murder.{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Justice League (film)|Justice League]]'' (2017) and ''[[Zack Snyder's Justice League]]'' (2021)<ref name="CameosRT" />}} On the day of her death, Nora sent Henry to pick up groceries she forgot to buy, leaving her alone in the kitchen where she was killed by an unidentified assailant.{{efn|Identified off-screen as [[Eobard Thawne|Eobard Thawne / Reverse-Flash]] by director [[Andy Muschietti]]<ref name="ReverseFlash" />}} Overcome by emotions, Barry accidentally uses the [[Speed Force]] to [[Time travel in fiction|travel back in time]] to earlier that day. Returning to the present, he discusses the incident with Bruce, who warns of the unintended consequences of time travel. Despite this, Barry travels to the day of Nora's death and places the missing groceries in Nora's cart to prevent Henry from leaving the house. As he returns to the present, Barry is attacked by an unknown [[Speedster (fiction)|speedster]] and emerges in an alternate 2013 where Nora is alive. He encounters his parents and his past self, and realizes this is the day he was struck by lightning and obtained his powers.

The two Barrys go to the [[Central City (DC Comics)|Central City]] Police Department, where Barry reenacts the event so 2013-Barry can gain his powers. Both are struck by lightning, granting 2013-Barry powers but leaving Barry without his own. As Barry struggles to train his younger self on properly using his powers, the Barrys learn that [[Zod (DC Extended Universe)|General Zod]] is preparing to invade Earth.{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Man of Steel (film)|Man of Steel]]'' (2013)<ref name="Characters/Film" />}} To fight Zod, the Barrys attempt to assemble the [[Justice League (DC Extended Universe)|Justice League]] but are unsuccessful; in this timeline, Diana cannot be located, [[Victor Stone (DC Extended Universe)|Victor Stone]] has not gained his abilities yet, and [[Arthur Curry (DC Extended Universe)|Arthur Curry]] never existed. They travel to [[Wayne Manor]], hoping to find Bruce, but they instead find [[Bruce Wayne (1989 film series character)|an alternate version]] who has long retired. Bruce theorizes that altering history through time travel affects events both before and after the alteration, causing several realities to collapse into a single one. They convince Bruce to return as Batman and help them find [[Superman (DC Extended Universe)|Kal-El / Superman]].

The Barrys and Bruce locate a [[Kryptonian]] pod discovered in [[Siberia]]. Upon arrival, they instead find Kal-El's cousin, [[Supergirl (Kara Zor-El)|Kara Zor-El]], imprisoned in the facility. They rescue Kara and return to Wayne Manor, where Barry enlists Bruce's help to regain his powers. The first two attempts fail, prompting Kara to fly Barry into a storm, where he regains his powers. Kara, Bruce, and the Barrys fight Zod and his forces. Kara learns that Zod intercepted Kal-El's escape pod from Krypton and, in an attempt to extract the Codex needed to repopulate the Kryptonian species, killed him. Zod reveals that the Codex is actually within Kara before subduing and killing her, extracting the Codex from her blood. Meanwhile, Bruce sacrifices himself in an unsuccessful attempt to destroy Zod's ship. The Barrys repeatedly time-travel to save Bruce and Kara, but keep failing. Barry realizes the sequence of events cannot be changed, but 2013-Barry keeps trying, which causes the [[Multiverse (DC Comics)|multiverse]] to begin imploding.

The unknown speedster who initially knocked Barry out of the Speed Force returns and is revealed to be an older, future version of 2013-Barry who still believes he can defeat Zod and save his Earth. Barry decides to reverse the changes he made to the timeline by letting Nora die. The elder 2013-Barry attempts to kill Barry to stop him, but young 2013-Barry sacrifices himself and is impaled instead, causing the elder 2013-Barry to fade from existence. Barry reverts the timeline and comes to terms with his mother's death; however, he subtly alters the past by moving the groceries to a higher shelf. Returning to the present day, Barry attends Henry's court hearing, where the security footage now reveals Henry's previously obscured face at the grocery store, verifying his alibi and allowing his exoneration. Barry is then contacted by Bruce, whose appearance has changed once again due to the timeline alteration.<!--Do NOT add the post-credits scene as it is not important to the plot!-->

== Production ==
=== Development ===
==== Early attempts ====
Development on a film based on the [[DC Comics]] character [[the Flash]] began in the late 1980s when [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] hired television writer [[Jeph Loeb]] to write a screenplay, before DC Comics president [[Jenette Kahn]] hired him to instead write a revivial of the comic book ''[[Challengers of the Unknown]]'' (1991).<ref name="LoebScript" /> After impressing Warner Bros. with his script for the DC Comics–based film ''[[Batman Begins]]'' (2005), the studio offered [[David S. Goyer]] the choice to work on a film featuring either the Flash or [[Green Lantern]], but it was also primarily interested in one for [[Wonder Woman]]. Goyer chose the Flash due to the cinematic possibilities of the character and his abilities,<ref name="GoyerChoice" /><ref name="GoyerDialogueInterview" />{{rp|23:57}} and he was hired to write, direct, and produce a new version of ''The Flash'' in December 2004, as part of an overall production deal with the studio. Warner Bros. [[executive vice president]] (EVP) of production Lynn Harris was set to oversee the film's development after working with Goyer on the [[Marvel Comics]]–based film ''[[Blade: Trinity]]'' (2004) at Warner Bros.'s [[New Line Cinema]].<ref name="GoyerHelm" /> Goyer approached his ''Blade: Trinity'' co-star [[Ryan Reynolds]] to portray the [[Wally West]] version of the Flash and intended to have the character [[Barry Allen]] appear in a supporting role. For the film's tone, Goyer was influenced by director [[Sam Raimi]]'s [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' film trilogy]] (2002–2007) and [[Mike Baron]], [[Mark Waid]], and [[Geoff Johns]]'s 1987–2006 [[The Flash (comic book)#Volume 2 (1987–2006, 2007–2008)|''The Flash'' comic book runs]].<ref name="GoyerChoice" /><ref name="GoyerBooted" /> Goyer was also inspired by the [[Greek myth]] of [[Icarus]] for handling Wally West's use of [[Speedster (fiction)|super speed]] and the character being a "screw up".<ref name="GoyerDialogueInterview" />{{rp|24:48–27:51}}<ref name="GoyerScript" /> He contrasted the Flash with [[Batman]], noting that he is not a vigilante, does not have a [[secret identity]], and enjoys being a superhero.<ref name="GoyerAug2005" /> Reynolds met with Goyer to discuss the role in March 2005, when Goyer was writing the script,<ref name="ReynoldsMeeting" /><ref name="GoyerAug2005" /> which was described as having dark themes.<ref name="LevyHelm" /> Reynolds said he would portray the Flash if Warner Bros. [[greenlit]] the film.<ref name="ReynoldsMeeting" />

{{Quote box|width=30%|align=right|quote="I was involved at one point with ''The Flash''. And [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] came to me and said, 'The work that you've been doing hasn't yet resulted in something that any of us, including the filmmaking team, feel could be [[greenlit]] as a movie. We're trying to accomplish something that takes into account the entire, rich [[DC Comics|DC]] character world, and we'd like to pull it back. [...] We just want to take a different kind of approach. Do you mind if we try that?' If we had something that was really working […] But we didn't. The [[David Goyer]] screenplay, that didn't work. Goyer left the project. We then embarked with [[David Dobkin (director)|David Dobkin]], trying to come up with another approach. We hadn't even hired a writer at that point. So for us, we completely understood."|author=Producer [[Charles Roven]] on why earlier iterations of ''The Flash'' did not move forward<ref name="RovenHobbled" />}}

In June 2005, Goyer said ''The Flash'' would be his next film after ''Batman Begins'', confirmed that Barry Allen and Wally West were both featured, and revealed that an [[MIT]] personnel consulted on the film's approach to the character's more cosmic aspects, such as depicting the [[Speed Force]] through the [[relativistic Doppler effect]].<ref name="GoyerJun2005" /> A year later, Goyer planned to direct ''The Flash'' as his follow-up to ''[[The Invisible (2007 film)|The Invisible]]'' (2007),<ref name="GoyerDialogueInterview" />{{rp|1:19:45}} and completed the first draft by early October.<ref name="Brancato" /> He quietly left in the following months over creative differences with the studio, and announced his exit in early February 2007,<ref name="GoyerBooted" /><ref name="LevyHelm" /> when [[Shawn Levy]] was hired to direct.<ref name="LevyHelm" /> He intended to direct the planned film ''[[Shawn Levy's unrealized projects#The Hardy Men|The Hardy Men]]'' before ''The Flash''.<ref name="LevyFeb2007EW" /> For approximately three-and-a-half months, he oversaw the writing of a new draft by [[Chris Brancato]], which used elements from Goyer's script.<ref name="LevyHelm" /><ref name="LevySep2011" /><ref name="Brancato" /> [[Charles Roven]] and Alex Gartner joined as producers, with Levy also expected to produce in some capacity. Unlike his prior works, Levy did not intend for the film to be a comedy; instead, he wanted it to have a lighter tone than some previous DC films.<ref name="LevyHelm" /> Later that month, Warner Bros. announced the development of a [[Justice League]] film, with Michelle and [[Kieran Mulroney]] writing the screenplay.<ref name="JusticeLeague" /> In August, Reynolds reaffirmed that ''The Flash'' was in development alongside the Justice League film,<ref name="ReynoldsAug2007" /> and Brancato had completed a draft by early September.<ref name="Brancato" /> [[George Miller (filmmaker)|George Miller]] signed on to direct the Justice League film, titled ''[[Justice League: Mortal]]'', later that month. It was envisioned as the start of a franchise with planned sequels and spin-offs, including the Flash film.<ref name="GeorgeMiller" />

Levy felt they "never got the ''Flash'' script right",<ref name="LevySep2011" /> and he left in October 2007 due to scheduling conflicts with ''[[Night at the Museum: Battle of the Smithsonian]]'' (2009).<ref name="DobkinDirect" /><ref name="LevyApr2009" /> [[David Dobkin (director)|David Dobkin]] took over as director and began developing the film as a spin-off from ''Justice League: Mortal'', with a focus on Wally West,<ref name="DobkinDirect" /><ref name="WrightScript" /> but it was soon reported not to be connected to that film.<ref name="WrightScript" /><ref name="WrightScript2" /> On his approach to the film, Dobkin said it was about how "[y]ou can't outrun yourself".<ref name="DobkinDirect" /> [[Craig Wright (playwright)|Craig Wright]] was writing a script for the film early the next month,<ref name="WrightScript" /><ref name="WrightScript2" /> days before the start of the [[2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike]], which delayed further development on the film.<ref name="WGAStrikeDelay" /><ref name="WGAStrikeDelay2" /><ref name="RovenHobbled" /> In December 2007, [[Adam Brody]] was cast as the Wally West version of the Flash for ''Justice League: Mortal''.<ref name="AdamBrody" /><ref name="AdamBrody2" /> In January 2008, Roven said that work on the script for ''The Flash'' had already begun with Dobkin's take and would resume following the strike, but later said there was no script or writer.<ref name="WGAStrikeDelay" /><ref name="WGAStrikeDelay2" /><ref name="RovenHobbled" /> ''Justice League: Mortal'' was also paused by the strike and later canceled.<ref name="WGAStrikeDelay" /><ref name="WSJAug2008" />

''The Flash'' was in active development by August 2008.<ref name="WSJAug2008" /> Johns was quietly hired to consult on the film around this time, as part of a strategic shift by [[Warner Bros. Pictures Group]] president Jeff Robinov to centralize control of Warner Bros.'s DC Comics adaptations within the studio rather than [[Option (filmmaking)|optioning]] them to individual producers, such as Roven on ''The Flash''.<ref name="JohnsMazeau" /><ref name="RobinovRestructure" /> By July 2009, Johns wrote a [[film treatment]] that used elements of Brancato's draft, which [[Dan Mazeau]] adapted into a screenplay.<ref name="JohnsMazeau" /><ref name="Brancato" /> Johns was producing the film alongside Roven, but no director was attached at that point.<ref name="JohnsMazeau" /> In September, Robinov restructured DC Comics under [[DC Entertainment]], a newly formed division of Warner Bros. led by [[Diane Nelson (businesswoman)|Diane Nelson]], as part of his strategy to reclaim oversight and ownership of their adaptations. As such, Roven was pulled from ''The Flash'', although there was potential for him and other producers to return to DC projects with adjusted deals.<ref name="RobinovRestructure" /> In October, Roven confirmed he was no longer involved in the film and said the studio was not confident enough in their take to greenlight the film,<ref name="RovenHobbled" /> although Mazeau disputed this and said the film was moving forward as planned.<ref name="MazeauTalks" /> In June 2010, [[Greg Berlanti]], [[Michael Green (writer)|Michael Green]], and [[Marc Guggenheim]]—the writers of the film ''[[Green Lantern (film)|Green Lantern]]'' (2011) starring Reynolds—were hired to write a new treatment for ''The Flash'' based on a recent comics run by Johns that featured Barry Allen.<ref name="GLFlashWriters" /> The pair had started work by August and completed a draft by the middle of February 2011,<ref name="BerlantiAug2010" /><ref name="Brancato" /> before ''Green Lantern'' was a critical and commercial failure upon its release in June 2011.<ref name="Brancato" />

==== DC Extended Universe ====
Warner Bros. was planning a new [[shared universe]] of films based on DC characters by July 2013 and had tentative plans to release a ''Flash'' film in 2016. Berlanti was still involved in developing the film while also co-developing a television series for [[the CW]] about the character, ''[[The Flash (2014 TV series)|The Flash]]'' (2014–2023), which is set in its DC-based shared universe, the [[Arrowverse]]. These adaptations were not expected to conflict with each other.<ref name="2016Release" /> In October 2014, Warner Bros. and DC Entertainment announced a slate of planned projects as part of their new franchise, the [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU). ''The Flash'' was set for release on March 23, 2018,<ref name="DCFilmsSlate" /> with [[Ezra Miller]] attached to star in the film as [[Barry Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Barry Allen / The Flash]];<ref name="MillerFlash" /><ref name="DCFilmsSlate" /> Miller first made [[cameo appearance]]s in ''[[Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice]]'' (2016) and ''[[Suicide Squad (2016 film)|Suicide Squad]]'' (2016),<ref name="MillerFlashBvS" /><ref name="MillerFlashSS" /> and starred in the team-up film ''[[Justice League (film)|Justice League]]'' (2017).<ref name="DCFilmsSlate" /> Warner Bros. offered [[James Wan]] the choice of directing a film about either [[Arthur Curry (DC Extended Universe)|Aquaman]] or the Flash, and he ultimately chose to make ''[[Aquaman (film)|Aquaman]]'' (2018).<ref name="Wan" />

By April 2015, [[Phil Lord and Christopher Miller]] were writing a story treatment for the ''Flash'' film with the possibility of directing it.<ref name="LordMiller" /> After they decided to direct ''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]'' (2018) instead, ''[[The Lego Batman Movie]]'' (2017) co-writer [[Seth Grahame-Smith]] entered negotiations to write and direct the film based on Lord and Miller's treatment in October 2015. Grahame-Smith was set to make his [[directorial debut]] with the film, with Roven once again producing and [[Deborah Snyder|Deborah]] and [[Zack Snyder]] executive producing.<ref name="Grahame-SmithHelm" /> [[Jay Oliva]], a [[storyboard artist]] on several DCEU films and the CW's series as well as the director of the animated film ''[[Justice League: The Flashpoint Paradox]]'' (2013), worked with Grahame-Smith on a storyboard and test which he said prompted the studio to greenlight the film.<ref name="OlivaAug2023" /> In February 2016, the film's release date was moved forward to March 16, 2018.<ref name="March2018Date" /> Grahame-Smith left the project that April, citing creative differences. Warner Bros. chose to retain his script,<ref name="Grahame-SmithOut" /> and he was still expected to be involved in the project moving forward, while Lord and Miller remained producers. A search for a replacement director began immediately,<ref name="Grahame-SmithOut2" /> with the studio wanting to hire a more experienced director. At that point, [[screen test]]s between Miller and multiple actresses for the female lead had taken place for several weeks.<ref name="Grahame-SmithOut3" /> After the divisive reception to ''Batman v Superman'', Warner Bros. Pictures sought to stabilize the direction of the DCEU. In May 2016, the studio reorganized to have genre-responsible film executives, with the studio's DC Entertainment films placed under a new division, [[DC Films]], to be led by Johns—who had since become DC Comics's [[chief creative officer]]—and Warner Bros. EVP [[Jon Berg]]. The division was formed to compete more directly with [[Marvel Studios]] and its franchise, the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU),<ref name="BvSFallout" /> but it was not designed to override the "director-driven" mandate for the franchise.<ref name="DCFilmsShakeup" />

[[Rick Famuyiwa]] was hired to take over as director in June 2016. Warner Bros. believed Famuyiwa's vision for the film would resonate with younger audiences and also be compatible with Grahame-Smith's existing script.<ref name="FamuyiwaDirect" /> Filming was expected to begin later in 2016 and was not believed to be delayed by the director change. Famuyiwa's top choice to portray the film's female lead, [[Iris West (DC Extended Universe)|Iris West]], was [[Kiersey Clemons]], who worked with him on ''[[Dope (2015 film)|Dope]]'' (2015).<ref name="ClemonsTopChoice" /> [[Rita Ora]] and [[Lucy Boynton]] were also in the running for the role,<ref name="OraBoynton" /> but Clemons was cast at the end of July.<ref name="ClemonsCast" /> At that time, Warner Bros. gave the film's release date to ''[[Tomb Raider (film)|Tomb Raider]]'' (2018), leaving ''The Flash'' without a release date.<ref name="NoMarch2018Date" /> In August, [[Ray Fisher (actor)|Ray Fisher]] was set to appear in the film, reprising his role as [[Victor Stone (DC Extended Universe)|Victor Stone / Cyborg]] from ''Batman v Superman'' and ''Justice League''.<ref name="FisherCyborg" /> Famuyiwa completed a revision of the script a month later,<ref name="FamuyiwaRevision" /> when [[Gal Gadot]] was set to reprise her role as [[Diana Prince (DC Extended Universe)|Diana Prince / Wonder Woman]] from ''Batman v Superman'', ''[[Wonder Woman (2017 film)|Wonder Woman]]'' (2017), and ''Justice League'',<ref name="GadotCast" /> and [[Billy Crudup]] was in negotiations to portray Barry's father, [[Henry Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Henry Allen]].<ref name="CrudupCast" /> Clemons and Crudup both filmed cameo appearances for ''Justice League'' after being cast in ''The Flash''.<ref name="CrudupReturnTalks" /><ref name="ClemonsJL" /> [[Pre-production]] began by October 2016 ahead of a filming start in March 2017, scheduled before another commitment that Ezra Miller had in July.<ref name="FamuyiwaOut" /> Oliva and Famuyiwa worked together for six-to-seven months up to that point. They planned for the character [[Eobard Thawne|Eobard Thawne / Reverse-Flash]] to serve as a background antagonist before being revealed as the main antagonist of the DCEU in an adaptation of the animated series ''[[Justice League Unlimited]]'' (2004–2006) and ''The Flashpoint Paradox'', which was intended to [[Reboot (fiction)|reboot]] the DCEU.<ref name="OlivaAug2023" /> At the end of October, Famuyiwa left the project after he could not "come together creatively" with the studio, which disagreed with the more mature direction that he wanted to take the film.<ref name="FamuyiwaOut" />

After Famuyiwa's exit, the film was put on hold while the studio searched for a new director, Miller prepared to film ''[[Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald]]'' (2018),<ref name="HaroldRewrite" /> and Oliva transitioned to work on reshoots for ''Wonder Woman''.<ref name="OlivaAug2023" /> During that time, Warner Bros. decided to take the film in a new direction, and in January 2017, [[Joby Harold]] was hired to perform a page-one rewrite of the script.<ref name="HaroldRewrite" /> He turned in a draft by May, when the studio's top choices to direct were [[Robert Zemeckis]] and [[Matthew Vaughn]]. Both had expressed interest in the project, but had potential scheduling issues that could prevent them from taking it on.<ref name="ZemeckisVaughnShortlist" /> Raimi, [[Marc Webb]], [[Jordan Peele]], and the DCEU's [[Bruce Wayne (DC Extended Universe)|Bruce Wayne / Batman]] actor [[Ben Affleck]] had already turned down offers to direct the film.<ref name="RaimiWebbOffers" /><ref name="PeeleDecline" /><ref name="DaleyGoldsteinTalks" /> Lord and Miller had also met with DC Films executives about potentially writing and directing ''The Flash'' during a production hiatus from ''Solo''.<ref name="LordMillerMeeting" /> At the July 2017 [[San Diego Comic-Con]], the film was announced with the new title ''Flashpoint'', based on [[Flashpoint (comics)|the 2011 comic book storyline]], in which Allen travels back in time to save his mother's life and accidentally creates an alternate timeline.<ref name="FlashpointFreshStart" /> Dan Mazeau returned to contribute to the script during this time.<ref name="MazeauScripts" /> Johns confirmed in November that the ''Flashpoint'' concept would allow the film to tell a unique story about [[Batman]], with the comic book storyline exploring a timeline where [[Batman (Thomas Wayne)|Thomas Wayne]] is Batman;<ref name="ThomasWayneBatman" /> [[Jeffrey Dean Morgan]] expressed interest in reprising his role as [[Thomas Wayne (DC Extended Universe)|Thomas Wayne]] from ''Batman v Superman''.<ref name="DeanMorganBatman" />

In January 2018, Warner Bros. executive [[Walter Hamada]] was appointed to oversee the DCEU as the new president of DC Films.<ref name="HamadaDCFilms" /> Later that month, the filmmaking duo of [[John Francis Daley]] and [[Jonathan Goldstein (filmmaker)|Jonathan Goldstein]] entered negotiations to direct ''Flashpoint'' after the studio chose not to wait for Zemeckis's schedule to be free.<ref name="DaleyGoldsteinTalks" /> Warner Bros. approached the duo about working on a DC property due to their work with that studio on the film ''[[Game Night (film)|Game Night]]'' (2018) as well as the MCU film ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017). Daley and Goldstein read several scripts before selecting ''Flashpoint'' because they were fans of the Flash character. They [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitched]] depicting the Flash as a grounded, imperfect superhero who is just learning his powers. Negotiations with the duo continued through February,<ref name="DaleyGoldsteinFeb2018Collider" /><ref name="DaleyGoldsteinMar2023" /> and they were confirmed as directors in March.<ref name="DaleyGoldsteinDirect" /> The film's title reverted to ''The Flash'' the next month.<ref name="MazeauScripts" /> Filming was expected to begin in [[Atlanta]] in February or March 2019,<ref name="Feb2019Filming" /><ref name="Feb2019AtlantaFilming" /><ref name="2021Release" /> but Miller's commitments to ''[[Fantastic Beasts: The Secrets of Dumbledore]]'' (2022) delayed filming again by October 2018, when it was expected to start later in 2019. ''The Flash'' was aiming for a 2021 release at that point.<ref name="2021Release" /> Daley and Goldstein were also set as the film's writers by the end of February 2019, when [[Michael Disco]] joined to produce the film through his newly formed production company, the Disco Factory, after working with the pair on ''Game Night''.<ref name="DiscoProduce" />

In March 2019, Miller was revealed to be writing a new version of the film's script with comic book writer [[Grant Morrison]]. Miller was not pleased with earlier drafts and disagreed with the lighthearted approach to the film that Daley and Goldstein were taking, although that was Warner Bros.'s preferred direction for the film,<ref name="MillerMorrisonScript" /><ref name="MillerScreenwriterNewby" /><ref name="MorrisonJun2022" /> and they subsequently approached Morrison with their ideas. Warner Bros. gave the pair two weeks to write the script in Scotland. Morrison described their script as a science fiction Flash story similar to Zemeckis's film ''[[Back to the Future]]'' (1985) that incorporated dark aspects related to the ''Flashpoint'' story but did not have a dark tone.<ref name="MorrisonAug2020" /><ref name="MorrisonJun2022" /> The new script could be submitted to the studio by the end of the month, and if the studio did not like Miller and Morrison's take, there was potential for the actor to leave the film; Miller's holding deal to star in the film was expected to end in May.<ref name="MillerMorrisonScript" /><ref name="MillerScreenwriterNewby" /> The studio rejected Miller and Morrison's script in May,<ref name="MuschiettiDirect" /> instead wanting the film to explore the [[Multiverse (DC Comics)|DC multiverse]] and other DC characters,<ref name="MorrisonJun2022" /> but asked Miller to remain as the star of the film.<ref name="MuschiettiDirect" /> Daley and Goldstein left the project in June 2019, due to creative differences, and they chose to work on ''[[Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves]]'' (2023) instead.<ref name="DaleyGoldsteinMar2023" /> The duo's exit was confirmed the following month.<ref name="MuschiettiDirect" />

Warner Bros. chose [[Christina Hodson]] to write a new screenplay for ''The Flash'' in July 2019, after she wrote its DCEU film ''[[Birds of Prey (2020 film)|Birds of Prey]]'' (2020). She was set to write another DCEU film, ''[[Batgirl (film)|Batgirl]]'', after completing her work on ''The Flash''. [[Andy Muschietti]] entered negotiations to direct ''The Flash'', with his sister [[Barbara Muschietti|Barbara]] joining to produce alongside Disco.<ref name="MuschiettiDirect" /> Muschietti intended for ''The Flash'' to be his next project after taking a short break following ''[[It Chapter Two]]'' (2019),<ref name="MuschiettiAug2019" /> and a January 2020 pre-production start was expected.<ref name="MuschiettiDirect" /> He did not intend for the film to have horror elements and described his take as fun, emotional, and a "beautiful human story".<ref name="MuschiettiAug2019" /> The involvement of Muschietti and Hodson was confirmed in November 2019, when filming was expected to begin in 2021 after Miller finished filming ''The Secrets of Dumbledore''.<ref name="2021Filming" /> While the screenplay for ''The Flash'' was being written around this time, DC Comics president [[Jim Lee]] approached Miller about appearing alongside [[Grant Gustin]] as [[Barry Allen (Arrowverse)|Barry Allen / The Flash]] from the Arrowverse in that franchise's television [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]] event "[[Crisis on Infinite Earths (Arrowverse)|Crisis on Infinite Earths]]" (2019–20), which acknowledges a wider DC multiverse, during editing of the event. Guggenheim wrote a brief scene featuring both actors, while Miller and Gustin improvised much of their dialogue on set, including Gustin's version inspiring Miller's character to use "the Flash" as his superhero alias.<ref name="GuggenheimEWJan2020" /><ref name="THRFanDome" /><ref name="KeatonTalks" /> Guggenheim would not address whether the events of the Arrowverse event, which saw some universes destroyed, meant that Miller's Flash still existed,<ref name="GuggenheimVarietyJan2020" /> while Lee and Adam Schlagman, the vice president of film at DC Entertainment, ensured Guggenheim's scripts did not conflict with this film.<ref name="GuggenheimEWJan2020" /> In December 2019, Warner Bros. scheduled ''The Flash'' for release on July 1, 2022.<ref name="July2022Date" />

=== Pre-production ===
In January 2020, Muschietti said the film would still adapt elements of the ''Flashpoint'' comic book storyline, because the studio wanted to use that story, but it would tell a different version of it.<ref name="FlashpointMuschietti" /><ref name="FlashpointInspiration" /><ref name="MuschiettiMay2023" /> This allowed for Batman to be featured in the film and for that comic's focus on [[time travel]] to be incorporated with the exploration of the Flash's [[origin story]], in which his mother is killed and his father is wrongfully convicted for her murder. Muschietti compared the ''Flashpoint'' comic to the horror film ''[[Jacob's Ladder (1990 film)|Jacob's Ladder]]'' (1990) for its structure and likened the film's version of the comic story to a [[suspense film]] because of its build-up. He felt that the Flash's prior appearances in the DCEU gave the audience a deeper understanding of the character, allowing for a more emotional experience and a richer story.<ref name="FlashpointInspiration" /> In April, the film's release date was moved forward to June 3, 2022, when Warner Bros. adjusted its release schedule because of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="June2022Date" />

[[Michael Keaton]] entered early negotiations in June 2020 to reprise his role as [[Bruce Wayne (1989 film series character)|Bruce Wayne / Batman]] from director [[Tim Burton]]'s films ''[[Batman (1989 film)|Batman]]'' (1989) and ''[[Batman Returns]]'' (1992). ''The Flash'' was set to disregard the subsequent [[Batman (1989 film series)|''Batman'' films]] that did not feature Keaton—''[[Batman Forever]]'' (1995) and ''[[Batman & Robin (film)|Batman & Robin]]'' (1997)—and was reaffirmed to introduce the multiverse to the DCEU, with Keaton's version of the character originating from an alternate universe Barry creates in the film. Filming was eyed to begin in the first quarter of 2021, depending on COVID-19 protocols.<ref name="KeatonTalks" /><ref name="KeatonTalks2" /> Fisher discussed his role as Cyborg in the film with Muschietti that month before Warner Bros. set a two-week shoot for Fisher to film what was referred to as a cameo appearance alongside other Justice League actors. Fisher said the studio offered him only a fraction of his traditional salary to reprise the role.<ref name="FisherSpeaks" /><ref name="FisherApproached" /><ref name="FisherCameoOffer&March2021Filming" /> In August, Keaton was confirmed to be appearing in the film, and Affleck agreed to reprise his version of Batman. Muschietti explained that the film would introduce the idea of the multiverse to general audiences by featuring multiple versions of characters and acknowledging past DC film franchises as alternate universes. It was important for Muschietti to include Affleck in the film since his version of Batman is the "baseline" for the DCEU, and he felt the introduction of Keaton's Batman would not work as well without first seeing the Flash's relationship with Affleck's Batman. Affleck chose to return after retiring from the role because he would have a smaller role in the film.<ref name="BatmanVanityFair" />

{{Quote box|width=28%|align=left|quote="We almost started from scratch. We did have a base of a story that was inherited from previous iterations. The studio knew they wanted to do some kind of interpretation of ''Flashpoint'', but I didn't want this to be a literal translation of the comic book. I respect the fandom a lot, but I think the fandom would also expect new things from a movie. Just a literal interpretation of ''Flashpoint'' the comic would fall flat in my opinion, so we populated it with twists and turns to make the story a little more fun."|author=Andy Muschietti on how the film's prior iterations and the ''Flashpoint'' comic influenced the final film<ref name="MuschiettiMay2023" />}}

During the virtual [[DC FanDome]] event "Explore the Multiverse" in September 2020, Barbara Muschietti said the film would feature many characters from the [[DC Universe]], and the Flash would serve as the bridge between them and their different timelines. She added that the film would be used to restart the continuity of the DCEU without disregarding the events of the prior films.<ref name="DCEURestart&Characters" /> Crudup, who left the film during the changes in directors, entered negotiations to rejoin a month later. Clemons's involvement was uncertain at that point.<ref name="CrudupReturnTalks" /> The film's release date was pushed back to November 4, 2022, due to further pandemic-related delays,<ref name="Nov2022Date" /> and filming was set to begin in March 2021 in London.<ref name="CrudupReturnTalks" /><ref name="FisherCameoOffer&March2021Filming" /> Warner Bros. had Cyborg written out of the film by January 2021 after Fisher refused to work on any project involving Hamada. Fisher said this was due to Hamada's handling of [[Production of Justice League (film)#Significant changes to the theatrical cut|an investigation into the on-set behavior]] of ''Justice League''{{'s}} replacement director, [[Joss Whedon]].<ref name="FisherSpeaks" /><ref name="NoFisherCyborg" /> The role of Cyborg was not expected to be recast.<ref name="NoFisherCyborg" /> At that time, the Muschiettis were preparing for production in the United Kingdom, with filming set to begin in April at [[Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden]], after Miller finished on ''The Secrets of Dumbledore''.<ref name="LeavesdenFilming" /><ref name="MuschiettisFilming" /> Construction of the sets at Leavesden Studios had begun by February,<ref name="LeavesdenFilming" /><ref name="BarbaraMuschiettiFeb2021" /> with the production creating a life-sized replica of the Batcave from scratch while the Batmobile used in Keaton's films was transported to the set from Los Angeles using an airport cargo truck, due to a global shortage of shipping containers at the time.<ref name="FilmingTimelineBatmobile" /> Paul Austerberry and [[Alexandra Byrne]] were the respective production and costume designers.<ref name="Austerberry" /><ref name="LicensingExpo" />

[[Sasha Calle]] was cast as [[Kara Zor-El (DC Extended Universe)|Kara Zor-El / Supergirl]] later in February 2021, and signed a multi-film contract for the DCEU.<ref name="CalleOct2024" /><ref name="CalleSupergirl" /> Calle was chosen from a group of more than 425 actresses that also included [[Bruna Marquezine]] and [[Rachel Zegler]], who were later cast in the DCEU as [[Jenny Kord (DC Extended Universe)|Jenny Kord]] for ''[[Blue Beetle (film)|Blue Beetle]]'' (2023) and [[Anthea (DC Extended Universe)|Anthea]] for ''[[Shazam! Fury of the Gods]]'' (2023).<ref name="CalleSupergirl" /><ref name="MarquezineSupergirl" /><ref name="ZeglerSupergirl" /> All auditions for the role, as well as chemistry tests with Miller, took place over [[Zoom (software)|Zoom]].<ref name="CalleSupergirl" /> At that time, Crudup was confirmed to be returning as Henry Allen,<ref name="CalleSupergirl2" /> but was forced to drop out of the film the following month because of scheduling conflicts with his series ''[[The Morning Show (American TV series)|The Morning Show]]'' (2019–present).<ref name="Verdu" /> [[Ron Livingston]] was subsequently cast to replace Crudup as Henry Allen,<ref name="Livingston" /> alongside [[Maribel Verdú]] as Barry's mother, [[Nora Allen (DC Extended Universe)|Nora Allen]],<ref name="Verdu" /> while Clemons signed a new deal to star as Iris West in the film after her role in ''Justice League'' was cut, but that cameo was restored in the [[director's cut]], ''[[Zack Snyder's Justice League]]'' (2021).<ref name="ClemonsRejoin" /> Other actors cast then were Ian Loh as a young Barry Allen,<ref name="Livingston" /> [[Saoirse-Monica Jackson]] as [[Patty Spivot (DC Extended Universe)|Patty Spivot]], and [[Rudy Mancuso]] as [[Albert Desmond (DC Extended Universe)|Albert Desmond]].<ref name="SpivotDesmond" /><ref name="Livingston" /> At the end of the month, Keaton said he had received an older version of the script but had not yet read it and would have to read the latest draft before deciding if he would commit to the film. He also cited the [[COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom]] as a concern for his involvement, as well as balancing his other commitments.<ref name="KeatonMarch2021" /> Before filming began, Keaton was reaffirmed to be starring in the film after his prior comments cast some doubt about his involvement,<ref name="KeatonYes" /> with Muschietti believing Keaton's decision to commit to ''The Flash'' was due to the script that he had been sent. Muschietti said Keaton felt honored to play Batman again.<ref name="WhatConvincedKeaton" /> The Muschiettis formed a production company, Double Dream, to co-produce the film, and Marianne Jenkins was revealed as an executive producer.<ref name="MuschiettisStudio" /> Fisher said he would be disappointed if the situation surrounding his involvement in the film was not resolved,<ref name="FisherApril2021" /> and that he was open to returning to the role of Cyborg for the film if Warner Bros. were to make amends with him.<ref name="FisherPossibleApril2021" />

=== Filming ===

[[Principal photography]] began on April 19, 2021,<ref name="FilmingStart" /> at [[Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden]], in Watford, England,<ref name="LeavesdenFilming" /><ref name="BarbaraMuschiettiFeb2021" /> under the [[working title]] ''Baby Shower''.<ref name="WorkingTitle" /> [[Henry Braham]] was the cinematographer after previously working on the DCEU film ''[[The Suicide Squad (film)|The Suicide Squad]]'' (2021). He described the film as "technically complex" despite not being based in realism.<ref name="Braham" /> In early May, filming took place at [[Burghley House]] in [[Stamford, Lincolnshire]], which doubled for [[Wayne Manor]].<ref name="BurghleyHouseFilming" /> In the middle of June, Miller, Clemons, Keaton, and Calle filmed scenes at [[St Paul's Cathedral]] in London, with the surrounding locations designed to portray [[Central City (DC Comics)|Central City]].<ref name="StPaulsCathedral" /> Filming was also set to occur later that month in [[Edinburgh]] and [[Glasgow]]—doubling for [[Gotham City]]—for scenes with Affleck and Keaton.<ref name="EdinburghGlasgow" /><ref name="EdinburghGlasgow2" /> In late July, filming continued in Glasgow at [[Ingram Street]], [[George Square]], John Street, and Cochrane Street and involved several vehicles, while filming with the [[Batmobile]] occurred in George Square,<ref name="GlasgowJulyFilming" /> but was temporarily halted after a camera operator on a motorcycle shooting behind the [[Batcycle]] on Renfield Street collided with it near West George Street. === Post-production ===
==== Visual effects work and Ezra Miller controversy ====
In December 2021, [[Michael Shannon]] and [[Antje Traue]] were revealed to be reprising their roles from ''[[Man of Steel (film)|Man of Steel]]'' (2013) as [[Zod (DC Extended Universe)|General Zod]] and [[Faora-Ul]], respectively. Daley and Goldstein, former co-writers and co-directors of the film, were also confirmed to be receiving story credit for the film alongside Hodson.<ref name="FlashZodFaora" /> [[Temuera Morrison]], who portrays Aquaman's father, [[Thomas Curry (DC Extended Universe)|Thomas Curry]], in ''[[Aquaman (film)|Aquaman]]'' (2018) and ''[[Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom]]'' (2023), was reported to be in the film in February 2022.<ref name="Morrison" /><ref name="CameosVariety" /> In March, Warner Bros. adjusted its release schedule due to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the workload of visual effects vendors. ''The Flash'' was moved to June 23, 2023, to give VFX teams more time to complete work; it had around 2,500 visual effects shots.<ref name="Mar2022ReleaseChanges" /> In April 2022, [[Discovery, Inc.]] and WarnerMedia merged to become [[Warner Bros. Discovery]] (WBD), led by president and CEO [[David Zaslav]]. The new company was expected to restructure DC Entertainment, and Zaslav began searching for an equivalent to Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]] to lead the new subsidiary.<ref name="ZaslavApr2022" />

The final writing credits for ''The Flash'' were also revealed in April: Hodson received screenplay credit; Daley, Goldstein, and Harold received screen story credit; and off-screen credit for additional writing material went to [[Rebecca Drysdale]], Famuyiwa, Grahame-Smith, Johns, Lord, Christopher Miller, Ezra Miller, Morrison, and [[Adam Sztykiel]].<ref name="WGAWritingCredits" /> In May, ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' reported that an unspecified "high-profile" comic book film that would be released in 2023 had its script recently submitted to the [[Writers Guild of America]] (WGA), having a total of 45 writers involved in it throughout its development.<ref name="Deadline45Writers" /> Some commentators believed that the film was ''The Flash'', due to its lengthy development process.<ref name="ScreenRant45Writers" /><ref name="CBR45Writers" /> Miller later participated in "regularly scheduled [[additional photography]]" in the middle of 2022.<ref name="THRDCFilmsReset" /> By August, the visual effects were expected to be completed by the end of the year,<ref name="MillerWBMeeting" /> and were provided by [[DNEG]], [[Scanline VFX]], and [[Wētā FX]]. John "DJ" Desjardin served as the VFX supervisor after working on several DCEU films,<ref name="ArtOfVFX" /><ref name="Desjardin" /> while [[Paul Machliss]] and [[Jason Ballantine]] co-edited the film; Ballantine previously collaborated on Muschietti's films ''[[It (2017 film)|It]]'' (2017) and ''It Chapter Two''.<ref name="BallantineGarrick" /><ref name="MachlissTechnology" /> Machliss said the film was the first to use new technology to feature different versions of an actor on-screen,<ref name="MachlissTechnology" /> while Muschietti said the film's visual effects were intentionally designed as being viewed from the Flash's perspective.<ref name="MuschiettiWeirdVFX" /> He added that his initial cut of the film was around four hours long, and featured additional "nostalgic" cameos and a different ending that were cut.<ref name="MuschiettiEWApr2023" /><ref name="MuschiettiVFJun2023" /><ref name="MuschiettiCameosNerdist" />

I want to apologize to everyone that I have alarmed and upset with my past behavior. I am committed to doing the necessary work to get back to a healthy, safe and productive stage in my life."|source=– Ezra Miller's statement apologizing for their controversial behavior, released following reports that it could impact the film's fate<ref name="MillerTreatmentApology" />}}

Following several [[Controversies surrounding Ezra Miller|controversial incidents and arrests involving Ezra Miller]] throughout 2022, WBD considered various options for the film after previously hoping that delaying its release would help avoid controversy. In August, WBD was considering three options: providing Miller an interview explaining their behavior and doing limited press for the film should they choose to accept professional help, with the film being released as planned; excluding Miller from all press of the film and recast their character in future projects should they choose to reject professional help; or cancel the film as a "last resort" if the situation further deteriorated. The latter was considered an "unprecedented move" due to its large budget, although it would have come after WBD already canceled the nearly complete $90&nbsp;million film ''Batgirl'' that was being produced for the streaming service [[HBO Max]]. WBD was not considering recasting Miller's role in the film because they were portraying multiple characters and appearing in nearly every scene. It was also considered "key" to the studio's future DCEU plans and was well-received during [[test screening]]s despite Miller's legal troubles.<ref name="THRMillerAug2022" /> Zaslav reiterated that ''The Flash'' received positive responses from the studio, and that they were committed to releasing it in theaters.<ref name="THRDCFilmsReset" /><ref name="THRMillerAug2022" /> Miller released a public apology through their representative soon afterward and announced they were seeking professional treatment for "complex mental health issues".<ref name="MillerTreatmentApology" /> Soon after, Miller and their agent met with [[Michael De Luca]] and [[Pamela Abdy]], the co-chairpersons and co-CEOs of Warner Bros. Pictures Group, to apologize for their behavior.<ref name="MillerWBMeeting" /> Barbara Muschietti later rejected reports that the film's release would have been canceled due to Miller's actions.<ref name="EWFilmSafe" />

==== Revisions to the ending and character cameos ====
The ending of the film, which features Miller's Barry believing he successfully altered the timeline before realizing it has been further altered, was changed multiple times during post-production due to leadership changes at Warner Bros. throughout 2022. The originally filmed ending featured Calle's Supergirl and Keaton's Batman, reversing those characters' deaths from earlier in the film, and was planned by Hamada and Warner Bros. Pictures chairman [[Toby Emmerich]] to set up a sequel and a film based on the "[[Crisis on Infinite Earths]]" (1985–1986) crossover storyline that was intended to reset the DCEU. After WBD was formed, Hamada and Emmerich exited their roles, and De Luca and Abdy were appointed to temporarily oversee its DC productions. An alternate ending was filmed in September 2022 that included Miller, Calle, and Keaton alongside [[Henry Cavill]] and Gal Gadot in their DCEU roles of [[Superman (DC Extended Universe)|Kal-El / Superman]] and Wonder Woman.<ref name="THREnding" /> Calle remained in this scene, which was intended to set up future appearances for Supergirl, because executives did not want her character's last appearance to be a death scene and were open to Calle reprising her role.<ref name="THREnding" /><ref name="CalleOct2024" /> Despite this, a planned ''Supergirl'' spin-off film written by [[Ana Nogueira]] had already been canceled at this time.<ref name="NogueiraSupergirl" /> Cavill's involvement came after he made a cameo appearance in the DCEU film ''[[Black Adam (film)|Black Adam]]'' (2022) and it was intended to set up a [[Untitled Man of Steel sequel|planned ''Man of Steel'' sequel]], while Gadot's appearance was intended to "keep Wonder Woman in the cultural conversation" while a sequel to ''Wonder Woman'' and ''[[Wonder Woman 1984]]'' (2020) was in development.<ref name="THREnding" /> Keaton had also been slated to reprise his role as Batman in various DC projects to take on a mentor role similar to that of [[Samuel L. Jackson]]'s MCU character [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]], and was cast to appear in ''Batgirl'' and a film based on the animated series ''[[Batman Beyond]]'' (1999–2001), also written by Hodson, before both were shelved.<ref name="KeatonTalks2" /><ref name="THRJul2023" /><ref name="BatmanBeyond" />

Writer/director [[James Gunn]] and producer [[Peter Safran]], who had worked on prior DCEU media, were hired as the co-chairmen and co-CEOs of the newly formed [[DC Studios]] at the end of October 2022.<ref name="GunnSafran" /> A week after starting their new roles, the pair had begun developing an eight-to-ten-year plan for a new franchise called the [[DC Universe (franchise)|DC Universe]] (DCU) that would be a [[soft reboot (fiction)|soft reboot]] of the DCEU.<ref name="GunnSafranTownHall" /><ref name="DCUSlateTHR6Qs" /> In December, the film's release date was moved forward to June 16, 2023, following positive test screenings.<ref name="Jun2023ReleaseUp" /> Cavill's involvement was revealed then along with [[Jason Momoa]] having a cameo as [[Arthur Curry (DC Extended Universe)|Aquaman]].<ref name="THRCavillMomoaDec2022" /> At that time, Gunn and Safran felt that including Cavill and Gadot's scenes in the ending could potentially promise audiences something that was not part of their plans for the DCU. WBD was also reconsidering whether to retain their scenes, depending on those plans, which had not yet been finalized since ''The Flash'' had not yet reached [[picture lock]].<ref name="THRCavillMomoaDec2022" /><ref name="NoCavillGadot" /><ref name="THREnding" /> Cavill and Gadot's cameos in the ending were ultimately cut after DC Studios chose not to move forward with the sequels to ''Man of Steel'' and ''Wonder Woman 1984''. Cavill was paid $250,000 for his cameo,<ref name="NoCavillGadot" /> and his likeness was briefly used in the film through [[computer-generated imagery]] (CGI).<ref name="SupermanCGI" /> Gunn and Safran enjoyed Nogueira's work on the ''Supergirl'' film so much that they had her return to write their own take for the DCU film ''[[Supergirl (2026 film)|Supergirl]]'' (2026), which does not star Calle.<ref name="NogueiraSupergirl" /><ref name="CalleOct2024" />

Following the alterations made in post-production, Gunn and Safran arranged for [[George Clooney]] to reprise his role as another version of Batman from ''[[Batman & Robin (film)|Batman & Robin]]'' (1997), replacing Affleck's version in the timeline, in a third version of the ending. This idea came after a recurring joke during production about how many versions of Batman could be included in the film. Clooney agreed to join the project after he and his talent agent [[Bryan Lourd]] were shown a mostly completed cut, which they reportedly liked, and Clooney filmed his scenes with Miller for half a day in January 2023, in what was described as a "quick and efficient shoot". Miller received advice from Clooney on handling publicity and his behavior between takes, and Miller was said to be in "top form". This ending with Clooney was kept a secret until the film's release.<ref name="THREnding" /> In March, Affleck revealed that he had filmed a scene with Gadot,<ref name="AffleckTHRMar2023" /> who appeared in an in-progress cut of the film screened at [[CinemaCon]] the following month.<ref name="CinemaConScreeningDeadline" />

Later in April 2023, Muschietti revealed some of the planned cameos that did not make the final film, including [[Lynda Carter]]'s Wonder Woman from the television series ''[[Wonder Woman (TV series)|Wonder Woman]]'' (1975–1979), [[Marlon Brando]]'s [[Jor-El]] from ''[[Superman (1978 film)|Superman]]'' (1978), [[Burgess Meredith]]'s [[the Penguin]] and [[Cesar Romero]]'s [[the Joker]] from the series ''[[Batman (TV series)|Batman]]'' (1966–1968), and Grant Gustin's Barry Allen from the Arrowverse continuity (2013–2023).<ref name="MuschiettiCameosNerdist" /> Gustin was not approached to appear in the film,<ref name="NoGustin" /> although the [[Golden Age (comics)|Golden Age]] incarnation of the Flash, [[Jay Garrick]], does appear in the film's Chronobowl [[Speed Force]] sequence, with the character's likeness based on Ballantine;<ref name="BallantineGarrick" /><ref name="BallantineGarrick2" /> the character was initially believed to be based on the appearance of [[Teddy Sears]]'s [[Hunter Zolomon (Arrowverse)|Hunter Zolomon / "Jay Garrick"]] from the Arrowverse, but he was not involved.<ref name="BallantineGarrick2" />

Multiple other cameos are featured in the film as part of the Chronobowl sequence. [[Nicolas Cage]] portrays an alternate version of Superman, whom he was cast to play in 1997 for Tim Burton's unproduced film ''[[Superman in film#Superman Lives|Superman Lives]]''. Cage filmed his scenes using [[volumetric capture]] and was [[De-aging in motion pictures|digitally de-aged]] for the role. He is depicted fighting a giant spider, a move that paid homage to a scene producer [[Jon Peters]] wanted to include in Burton's film.<ref name="Cage" /><ref name="CageCGI" /> Cage originally filmed a different scene of his character standing in an alternate universe while witnessing its destruction. For that scene, Cage said that he conveyed the character's emotions in his eyes with no dialogue.<ref name="CageExplainsCameo" /> Previous incarnations of Superman, Batman, and Supergirl also appear in the sequence through the use of [[artificial intelligence]] and [[deepfake]] technology, including [[Christopher Reeve]] as [[Superman (1978 film series character)|Superman]] from the [[Superman (1978 film series)|1978–1987 ''Superman'' films]], [[Helen Slater]] as [[Supergirl (1984 film character)|Supergirl]] from ''[[Supergirl (1984 film)|Supergirl]]'' (1984), [[Adam West]] as Batman from the film ''[[Batman (1966 film)|Batman]]'' (1966) and the 1966 ''Batman'' series, and [[George Reeves]] as his version of Superman from ''[[Superman and the Mole Men]]'' (1951) and ''[[Adventures of Superman (TV series)|Adventures of Superman]]'' (1952–1958), through [[archive footage]].<ref name="CameosRT" /> West's appearance was almost cut before Muschietti chose to retain it because he and Barbara felt personally attached to the series, which they watched [[rerun]]s of during their childhood in Argentina.<ref name="MuschiettiVFJun2023" /> Muschietti said the crew was given "total freedom" with these cameos.<ref name="CameoFreedom" />

== Marketing ==
Miller debuted the first footage from the film at the virtual DC FanDome event in October 2021.<ref name="DCFanDome2021THR" /> They said there was not enough footage available to make a full trailer or teaser, but William Hughes at ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' felt the footage could comfortably be categorized as a teaser. He said being able to see it was proof of the film being made after its long and troubled production history.<ref name="DCFanDome2021AVClub" /> ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]''{{'s}} Matt Patches and ''[[io9]]''{{'s}} James Whitbrook both highlighted the footage as the beginning of the DC multiverse on film, with its hints at Keaton's version of Batman and the reveal that Miller would portray multiple versions of Barry Allen.<ref name="DCFanDome2021Polygon" /><ref name="DCFanDome2021io9" /> In February 2022, more footage was released as part of a teaser for Warner Bros.'s 2022 slate of DC films, which also included ''[[The Batman (film)|The Batman]]'', ''Black Adam'', and ''Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom'' (before ''The Flash'' and ''Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom'' were delayed to 2023).<ref name="Morrison" /><ref name="Mar2022ReleaseChanges" /> A new trailer was shown at Warner Bros.'s [[CinemaCon]] panel in April 2022,<ref name="WBCinemaConTHR" /> and was noted by attendees for Keaton's Batman reusing the line "You wanna get nuts? Let's get nuts" from ''Batman''.<ref name="WBCinemaConDeadline" /><ref name="WBCinemaConIGN" /><ref name="WBCinemaConWrap" /> The following month, the costumes for the Flash and Supergirl were shown at Warner Bros.'s booth at the Licensing Expo in Las Vegas.<ref name="LicensingExpo" /><ref name="LicensingExpo2" />

DC Comics published a three-issue prequel comic book limited series titled ''The Flash: The Fastest Man Alive'', starting in September 2022, written by Kenny Porter. It is set after the events of ''Justice League'' and depicts Batman training the Flash and the Flash's early days as he attempts to defeat various supervillains.<ref name="PrequelComic" /><ref name="PrequelComicIGN" /> The first trailer for ''The Flash'' was shown during [[Super Bowl LVII]] on February 12, 2023, before playing in theaters ahead of ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania]]''.<ref name="SuperBowlTrailer" /><ref name="SuperBowlPromo" /> The trailer had the most engagement on social media, outpacing other trailers that were shown for ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' and ''[[Transformers: Rise of the Beasts]]'', with RelishMix reporting the trailer gained 97.4M views within 24 hours.<ref name="Trailer1Engagement" /> Many commentators highlighted Keaton's reprisal as Batman and Calle's role as Supergirl and also noted the de-emphasized focus on Miller's Flash following their controversies. Charles Pulliam-Moore of ''[[The Verge]]'' particularly felt that "it's the other heroes they're [the Flash] going to be allying with who are likely to steal the show", and felt that Supergirl and Keaton's Batman were being posited as the film's "[[World's Finest Team|World's Finest]]", and expressed enthusiasm for Supergirl's role in the story.<ref name="VergeTrailer1" /> Alli Rosenbloom at [[CNN]] also praised Keaton's appearance, highlighting the usage of [[Danny Elfman]]'s score from ''Batman'', while ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'s}} Collier Jennings felt that trailer "certainly is working to sell the image that the wait was worth it".<ref name="CNNTrailer1" /><ref name="ColliderTrailer1" />

== Release ==
=== Theatrical ===
''The Flash'' premiered on June 12, 2023, at [[Grauman's Chinese Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]],<ref name="VarietyPremiereSecretSequel" /> and was released by Warner Bros. Pictures in the United States on June 16, 2023.<ref name="Jun2023ReleaseUp" /> The film was initially scheduled for release on March 23, 2018, when Warner Bros. first announced its slate of DCEU films,<ref name="DCFilmsSlate" /> before it was moved to March 16.<ref name="March2018Date" /> In July 2016, this release date was given to ''Tomb Raider'',<ref name="NoMarch2018Date" /> and ''The Flash'' was not given another release date until Muschietti's hiring in 2019, after which the film was slated for release on July 1, 2022.<ref name="July2022Date" /> It was then moved up to June 3,<ref name="June2022Date" /> before it was delayed to November 4, 2022, when Warner Bros. adjusted its release schedule because of the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="Nov2022Date" /> The studio then delayed the release date to June 23, 2023, due to the pandemic's impact on the workload of visual effects vendors,<ref name="Mar2022ReleaseChanges" /> before moving it forward a week to June 16 after positive test screenings.<ref name="Jun2023ReleaseUp" />

=== Home media ===
''The Flash'' was released on [[Digital distribution|digital download]] and on the [[non-fungible token]] (NFT) Eluvio [[blockchain]] by [[Warner Bros. Home Entertainment]] on July 18, 2023, making it one of the first feature films to be made available on a blockchain.<ref name="DigitalRelease" /><ref name="NFT" /> This was followed by its release on the Warner Bros. streaming service [[Max (streaming service)|Max]] on August 25, and on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[DVD-Video|DVD]] on August 29, 2023.<ref name="DigitalRelease" />

== Reception ==
=== Box-office performance ===
''The Flash'' grossed $108.1&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $163.3&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide gross of $271.4&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /><ref name="NUM" /> It was a <!--Please do not change or remove this summary per MOS:ACCLAIMED, as this is supported by the citations.-->[[box-office bomb]],<ref name="Bomb" /> with projected losses for Warner Bros. to be as much as $200&nbsp;million.<ref name="DHNetProfit" /><ref name="LATWoes" /><ref name="USATodayBomb" /><ref name="BudgetBreakdown" /> Several publications commented on its box office revenue, with ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' calling it "snubbed" and ''[[/Film]]'' saying that the hype "proved to be hot air" following poor box-office returns. The film's low box-office opening was attributed to various controversies surrounding Ezra Miller, mixed word-of-mouth, competition from Sony's ''[[Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse]]'', early hype being "unwarranted", the imminent DCU reboot, [[superhero fatigue]] from audiences, and middling critical reviews.<ref name="THRBadBO" /><ref name="Bomb/Film" />

In the United States and Canada, ''The Flash'' was released alongside ''[[Elemental (2023 film)|Elemental]]'' and ''[[The Blackening (film)|The Blackening]]'', and was initially projected to gross $68–85&nbsp;million from 4,234 theaters in its opening weekend.<ref name="VarietyBOP" /> It was also expected to gross $85–95&nbsp;million internationally, for a global opening of $155–165&nbsp;million.<ref name="DeadlineBOP" /> However, after making $24.1&nbsp;million on its first day (including from $9.7&nbsp;million Thursday night previews), weekend estimates were lowered to $60&nbsp;million.<ref name="DHDay1" /> It went on to have a $55&nbsp;million domestic opening, with an additional $75&nbsp;million from international markets, for a worldwide debut of $130&nbsp;million.<ref name="Opening" /><ref name="DHBadBO" /> In the United Kingdom and Ireland, the film debuted leading the box office with £4.2&nbsp;million ($5.4&nbsp;million).<ref name="UKIrishBO" /> The film retained [[IMAX]] screens and other premium formats into the following weekend, which could have provided some padding from what was expected to be a steep box-office decline from its opening weekend.<ref name="IMAXScreen" /> In its second weekend, the film dropped 72% to $15.1&nbsp;million,<ref name="Week2Drop" /> the third-largest such drop ever for a superhero film up to that point, behind ''[[Morbius (film)|Morbius]]'' (2022) and ''[[Steel (1997 film)|Steel]]'' (1997).<ref name="BadDrop" /><ref name="DropGR" /> In its third weekend, the film dropped another 65% to $5.2&nbsp;million, losing 1,538 theaters and finishing in eighth place in the process.<ref name="Week3Drop" />

=== Critical response ===
{{Very long section|date=September 2025|nosplit=yes}}
''The Flash'' received mixed reviews from critics.<ref name="THRBadBO" /><ref name="MC" /><ref name="FlashNYT" /> Early reviews generally praised the humor, action sequences, and the performances, but criticized the [[third act]] and visual effects.<ref name="RTRoundup" /> The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] calculated that 63% of 390 critic reviews were positive and the average rating was 6.3 out of 10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''The Flash'' is funny, fittingly fast-paced, and overall ranks as one of the best DC movies in recent years."<ref name="RT" /> [[Metacritic]] assigned the film a weighted average score of 55 out of 100 based on 55 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="MC" /> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale, tied with ''[[Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice]]'' (2016) as the lowest grade for a DCEU film, while [[PostTrak]] reported 77% of filmgoers gave it a positive score, with 59% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="DHBadBO" />

''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'s}} David Fear identified the film as being the best in the DCEU but cited the complicated production and Ezra Miller's legal controversies as detracting elements. Nevertheless, he acknowledged that Miller could adequately portray the two different Barrys, but lamented, "It is not what we'll talk about when we talk about Ezra Miller." He praised Keaton's and Calle's performances, feeling that Batman felt well into the narrative while commenting that the script was "primed for both nimbly quickening the action and slowing down enough to wink, nudge, and crack jokes". He criticized the action sequences, comparing them to [[cutscene]]s and the [[post-credits scene]].<ref name="RSReview" /> Joshua Yehl of ''[[IGN]]'' gave the film a seven out of ten, praising Muschietti's direction and its focus on Barry's "emotional journey". He enjoyed the film's logic and depiction of time travel, while noting that it effectively integrated the Flash's origin story. However, he disliked the inclusion of [[fan service]], the third act, and Supergirl's role in the film. Nevertheless, he praised Keaton and Affleck's performances as Batman and also said Calle did "as much as she can given the thin nature of the role". Ultimately, he opined that it felt like a "love letter to the [[Snyderverse]]" and called it a "fitting [[swan song]]" for the DCEU.<ref name="IGNReview" />

From ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'', Angelia Jade Bastién criticized the film for being too dependent upon previous DCEU elements and disliked that the film did not explore Barry's emotions following his mother's death, but instead used the event as a "springboard for uninspired multiverse plotting". She felt Miller's performance was acceptable, the script limited Calle's, and Keaton's was enjoyable. Bastién disliked the visual aesthetic and use of computer-generated imagery (CGI), ultimately describing the film as "the cinematic equivalent of a snake eating its own tail" and "closing down of all the possibilities a multiverse is meant to represent".<ref name="VultureReview" /> David Rooney of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' praised Miller's performance despite noting their legal troubles impacted the film's reception. He also called Calle a "scene-stealer", and Keaton's "sadness" in portraying Batman. His biggest highlights were the opening sequence, which he felt firmly established the comedic tone; Hodson's writing and characterization of Barry as a "virginal nerd who has gone through college without managing to acquire much self-assurance, even after mastering his superpowers"; and Muschietti's overall direction. However, he criticized the runtime, thought the final act was formulaic, and thought the nostalgic elements "often threatens to marginalize the central plotline" but still managed to "yield [[pathos]]" in the narrative.<ref name="THRReview" />

[[Manohla Dargis]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' gave a mostly positive albeit mixed review, appreciating its "relatively brisk" runtime and felt it was a comparatively better superhero film, enjoying the overall cast performances, saying the "overall vibe is upbeat", and praised Muschietti's direction and pacing. However, she criticized the beginning scene, as she did not enjoy the "creepy setup" of falling newborn babies, and also could not ignore Miller's real-life controversies, and felt that the substantial presence of other characters had taken the film's focus away from establishing the Flash as an individual character.<ref name="NYTReview" /> Reviewing the film for ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', [[Matt Zoller Seitz]] gave the film two-and-a-half out of four stars, summarizing his thoughts on the film as being "simultaneously thoughtful and clueless ... Like its sincere but often hapless hero, it keeps exceeding every expectation we might have for its competence only to instantly face-plant into the nearest wall". He felt that Miller's legal issues had undermined the film's comedy but credited Muschietti's direction and Hodson's writing for "taking its ideas and the pain of its characters seriously without devolving into glum, colorless machismo", enjoying how the film's science fiction elements allowed it to provide a positive ending for the character while also noting other scientific and philosophical issues "raised elsewhere". However, he felt Miller's dual role was executed well and commended the accompanying visual effects, but he heavily criticized the use of CGI to depict alternate realities.<ref name="RogerEbert.comReview" />

The film's use of digital imagery to recreate the likenesses of deceased actors was criticized, with commentators questioning its ethical implications.<ref name="IGNEthics" /> Jack King from ''[[GQ]]'' expressed concerns that the use of actors such as [[Christopher Reeve]] and [[Adam West]] was "the next step in the normalization of a queasy, questionable trend in Hollywood, one in which actors are brought back from the grave using new computer techniques".<ref name="GQEthics" /> [[Tim Burton]], who directed the ''Batman'' films starring Keaton, expressed his distaste for Warner Bros. "misappropriat[ing]" his version of Batman and the film's reference to his unproduced film ''Superman Lives''.<ref name="BurtonMisappropriate" /> Likewise, Reeve's children—Will, Matthew, and [[Alexandra Reeve Givens]]—were not consulted on the use of his likeness and did not watch the film.<ref name="ReeveChildrenCameoResponse" /> Reflecting on the film's reception in January 2025, Muschietti said the film failed critically and commercially because it did not appeal to the "[[Four-quadrant movie|four quadrants]]" of audience demographics: both men and women under and over the age of 25. He also stated that several audiences, especially women, did not care about the Flash character.<ref name="MuschiettiFailReason" /> Several commentators disagreed with Muschietti's reasoning, particularly citing the popularity of [[the CW]] television series ''[[The Flash (2014 TV series)|The Flash]]'' (2014–2023).<ref name="MuschiettiFailReasonResponses" />

=== Accolades ===
''The Flash'' was nominated for Best Teaser at the 2022 [[Golden Trailer Awards]].<ref name="GoldenTrailerAwards" /> At the [[51st Saturn Awards]], it received nominations for [[Saturn Award for Best Comic-to-Film Motion Picture|Best Superhero Film]] and [[Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor in a Film]] for Keaton.<ref name="SaturnAwards" /> ''The Flash'' won the Emerging Technology Award at the [[22nd Visual Effects Society Awards]] for the development of "Volumetric Capture".<ref name="VESWin" /> Verdú won Best Female Performance in an International Production at the [[32nd Actors and Actresses Union Awards]].<ref name="SpanishActorsUnionAwards" />

== Future ==
By October 2022, a script for a sequel had been written by [[David Leslie Johnson-McGoldrick]], who wrote the ''Aquaman'' films, in the event ''The Flash'' performed well.<ref name="SequelScriptOct2022" /> The script reportedly included Keaton's Batman and Calle's Supergirl.<ref name="VarietyPremiereSecretSequel" /> Warner Bros. was not expected to retain Miller for future films because of the actor's controversies and legal issues,<ref name="THRMillerAug2022" /> although some Warner Bros. executives were open to continuing with Miller by January 2023, since they began treatment.<ref name="MillerWBJan2023" /> Later that month, Gunn and Safran said there was potential for Miller to reprise their role in the new DCU franchise, but a decision on the character had not been made.<ref name="DCUSlateGizmodo" /> Gunn confirmed that ''The Flash'' would reset the [[Continuity (fiction)|continuity]] of the DCEU and lead into the DCU's first film, ''[[Superman (2025 film)|Superman]]'' (2025).<ref name="DCUSlateGizmodo" /> The Muschiettis intended for Miller to return for a potential sequel because they preferred the actor for the role,<ref name="MuschiettiMillerMay2023" /> while the director also expressed interest in featuring [[Eobard Thawne|Eobard Thawne / Reverse-Flash]] as an antagonist for a sequel after the character was responsible for Nora Allen's death in the film.<ref name="ReverseFlash" />

Ahead of ''The Flash''{{'s}} release in June 2023, ''[[TheWrap]]'' reported that a sequel was "on the table" with the potential for it to be greenlit being dependent on the film's box-office performance; ''The Flash'' needed to earn around the box-office gross of DC's ''The Batman'' ($770&nbsp;million) for a sequel to move forward.<ref name="FlashSequelProspects" /> The poor box-office performance of ''The Flash'' led many commentators to question and doubt the prospects of a sequel being made.<ref name="LATWoes" /><ref name="USATodayBomb" /><ref name="BudgetBreakdown" /> Upon the film's release, DC Studios hired Muschietti to direct a new Batman film entitled ''[[The Brave and the Bold (film)|The Brave and the Bold]]'' for the DCU.<ref name="MuschiettiB&TB" /> In October 2023, ''Variety'' reported that no actors from Zack Snyder's DCEU films, including Miller, would reprise their roles in the DCU.<ref name="MillerNoReturn" />

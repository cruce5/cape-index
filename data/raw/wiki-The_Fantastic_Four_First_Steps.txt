'''''The Fantastic Four: First Steps'''''<!--Do not add stylization here as the number "4" being used is not different enough. See [[Talk:The Fantastic Four: First Steps/Archive 1#Title Stylization]] for more.--> is a 2025 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[Marvel Comics]] superhero team the [[Fantastic Four]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 37th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) and the second [[Reboot (fiction)|reboot]] of the [[Fantastic Four in film|''Fantastic Four'' film series]]. The film was directed by [[Matt Shakman]] from a screenplay by [[Josh Friedman]], [[Eric Pearson]], and the team of Jeff Kaplan and Ian Springer. It features an [[ensemble cast]] including [[Pedro Pascal]], [[Vanessa Kirby]], [[Ebon Moss-Bachrach]], and [[Joseph Quinn]] as [[Fantastic Four (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|the titular team]], alongside [[Julia Garner]], [[Sarah Niles]], [[Mark Gatiss]], [[Natasha Lyonne]], [[Paul Walter Hauser]], and [[Ralph Ineson]]. The film is set in the 1960s of a [[retro-futuristic]] world which the Fantastic Four must protect from the planet-devouring cosmic being [[Galactus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Galactus]] (Ineson).

[[20th Century Fox]] began work on a new Fantastic Four film following the failure of ''[[Fantastic Four (2015 film)|Fantastic Four]]'' (2015). After the studio was [[Acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney|acquired by Disney]] in March 2019, control of the franchise was transferred to Marvel Studios, and a new film was announced that July. [[Jon Watts]] was set to direct in December 2020, but stepped down in April 2022. Shakman replaced him that September when Kaplan and Springer were working on the script. Casting began by early 2023, and Friedman joined in March to rewrite the script. The film is differentiated from previous ''Fantastic Four'' films by avoiding the team's [[origin story]]. Pearson joined to polish the script by mid-February 2024, when the main cast and the title ''The Fantastic Four'' were announced. The subtitle was added in July, when filming began. It took place until November 2024 at [[Pinewood Studios]] in England, and on location in England and Spain.

''The Fantastic Four: First Steps'' premiered at the [[Dorothy Chandler Pavilion]] in Los Angeles on July 21, 2025, and was released in the United States on July 25, as the first film in [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Six|Phase Six]] of the MCU. It received generally positive reviews from critics and grossed $521.9&nbsp;million worldwide, making it the highest-grossing ''Fantastic Four'' film. A sequel is in development.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400-700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. -->
On [[Earth-828]] in 1964<!-- The telecast celebrating the fourth anniversary of the team has a copyright date of 1964 (MCMLXIV). -->, the world celebrates the fourth anniversary of astronauts [[Reed Richards (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Reed Richards]], [[Sue Storm (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sue Storm]], [[Ben Grimm (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ben Grimm]], and [[Johnny Storm (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Johnny Storm]] becoming the superhero team known as the [[Fantastic Four (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Fantastic Four]] after they gained superhuman abilities from exposure to [[cosmic rays]] during a space mission. Since then, the team have become celebrities and fought supervillains, while Reed's inventions have progressed technology and Sue's diplomacy via the [[Future Foundation (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Future Foundation]] has led to demilitarization and peace for much of the world. When Reed and Sue reveal that they are expecting a child, the world prepares for the new arrival and questions whether the child will also have superpowers.

Months later, the [[Shalla-Bal (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Silver Surfer]] comes to Earth and declares that it has been marked for destruction by [[Galactus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Galactus]], a planet-devouring cosmic being. Reed studies the disappearance of other planets and verifies this claim, and the team decides to return to space to stop Galactus from coming to Earth. They track the Silver Surfer's energy signature and, using [[faster-than-light]] (FTL) travel, arrive at a new planet just as it is destroyed by Galactus's ship. The team is captured and brought to Galactus, who reveals that he has an insatiable hunger that has driven him to consume planets for eons. Galactus senses that Reed and Sue's unborn child has an immense cosmic power and could take on his hunger, freeing himself from it. He offers to spare Earth in exchange for the child, and induces Sue to go into labor. The team refuses the offer and escapes from the ship. Using the gravity from a [[neutron star]], which destroys their FTL system, the team delays the chasing Silver Surfer and [[Gravity assist|propels themselves]] back to Earth. Sue gives birth to a boy, [[Franklin Richards (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Franklin]], on the way.

On their return to Earth a month later, Reed reveals the details of their encounter during a press conference. Their decision to save one child over the billions of other people on Earth leads to a public outcry, with many calling for Franklin to be sacrificed to Galactus. Johnny begins to decipher the Silver Surfer's native language using his interactions with her and some [[Interstellar communication|deep space transmissions]] Reed intercepted from her planet. They also have transmissions from planets that Galactus destroyed. As Galactus draws near and protests against the Fantastic Four escalate, Sue takes Franklin to meet with protesters. She explains that they will not sacrifice their child, but they will also not give up on the rest of humanity, regaining public trust. Using a [[teleportation]] system that he has been working on, Reed devises a plan to build large teleportation bridges across Earth so they can transport the entire planet to another solar system where Galactus cannot reach them. Through the Future Foundation, Sue organizes the world's nations to build the bridges and conserve the energy needed to use them.

As the bridges are being activated, the Silver Surfer returns and begins destroying them. Johnny stops her from destroying the last bridge in [[Times Square]] and reveals what he learned from the transmissions: her name is Shalla-Bal and she became Galactus's herald to spare her world, [[Zenn-La (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Zenn-La]]. Hearing transmissions from planets she helped destroy, Shalla-Bal expresses remorse but refuses to help and flees. Using Franklin as bait, the team plans to lure Galactus to the last bridge and teleport him away. Sue negotiates with [[Harvey Elder (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Harvey Elder]] to evacuate New York City's citizens to [[Subterranea (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Subterranea]], his underground city. Galactus makes his way through the city and captures Franklin, avoiding the trap. Sue uses all of her power to push him towards the portal with a [[Force field (technology)|force field]] while Reed rescues Franklin. Johnny attempts to sacrifice himself to give Galactus a final push into the portal, but is stopped by Shalla-Bal. She pushes Galactus into the portal herself and it closes behind them. Sue dies from her efforts, but is revived by Franklin. The team later goes on a new mission as the world celebrates their fifth anniversary.

In a [[mid-credits scene]] set four years later, Sue is reading to Franklin at home and goes to get a different book. She returns to see him interacting with a man in a green cloak who is holding a metal mask.{{efn|This scene was directed by ''[[Avengers: Doomsday]]'' (2026) directors [[Anthony and Joe Russo]] during [[Production of Avengers: Doomsday and Avengers: Secret Wars|the production]] of that film. The man was identified off-screen as [[Victor von Doom (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Victor von Doom / Doctor Doom]].<ref name="DoomPostCreditsScene" />}}

== Production ==
=== Background ===
After the critical and commercial failure of [[20th Century Fox]]'s 2015 film ''[[Fantastic Four (2015 film)|Fantastic Four]]'', co-written and directed by [[Josh Trank]] and based on the [[Marvel Comics]] superhero team the [[Fantastic Four]], Fox began looking at new directions to take the franchise. Having also produced [[Fantastic Four in film#Tim Story films (2005–2007)|two earlier ''Fantastic Four'' films]] directed by [[Tim Story]] a decade prior, the studio did not want to just make another ''Fantastic Four'' film.<ref name="HawleyDoom" /> By June 2017, [[Seth Grahame-Smith]] was writing a new film that would shift focus to [[Franklin Richards (character)|Franklin]] and [[Valeria Richards]], the children of the original Fantastic Four leaders [[Mister Fantastic|Reed Richards / Mister Fantastic]] and [[Invisible Woman|Sue Storm / Invisible Woman]]. Taking inspiration from the comic book series ''[[Ultimate Fantastic Four]]'' (2004–2009), the screenplay included original Fantastic Four members [[Thing (Marvel Comics)|Ben Grimm / The Thing]] and [[Human Torch|Johnny Storm / Human Torch]], and was described as kid-centric with a tone closer to the animated film ''[[The Incredibles]]'' (2004) than Trank's darker film.<ref name="Grahame-Smith" /> The basis of Grahame-Smith's screenplay came from a separate script that Carter Blanchard had written as an adaptation of the unpublished children's book ''Kindergarten Heroes'' by [[Mark Millar]]. Millar previously consulted with Fox on their Marvel-based films.<ref name="KindergartenHeroes" /> In July 2017, [[Noah Hawley]], the creator of the [[Marvel Television]] series ''[[Legion (TV series)|Legion]]'' (2017–2019), was hired to develop [[Doctor Doom (film)|a separate film]] focused on [[Doctor Doom|Victor von Doom / Doctor Doom]], the Fantastic Four's primary antagonist in the comics and previous films.<ref name="HawleyDoom" /> [[The Walt Disney Company]] officially [[Acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney|acquired 21st Century Fox]] in March 2019 and gained the film rights for the Marvel Comics characters that Fox controlled for its subsidiary [[Marvel Studios]], including the Fantastic Four.<ref name="DisneyFoxDealClose" /><ref name="FoxDisneyPressRelease" /> The Marvel-based films that Fox had been developing were placed "on hold".<ref name="FoxMarvelOnHold" />

=== Development ===
[[File:Kevin Feige (48462887397) (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|Producer [[Kevin Feige]] at [[San Diego Comic-Con]] in July 2019, where he confirmed that development had begun|alt=Picture of producer Kevin Feige in 2019]]
Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]] confirmed in July 2019 that the company was developing a new Fantastic Four film for their [[shared universe]], the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU).<ref name="SDCC2019" /> Feige said he was excited about the characters and about "bringing Marvel's first family up to the sort of platform and level they deserve",<ref name="FeigeExcited" /> wanting them to take their rightful place within the "pantheon of Marvel heroes" and bring the importance they had in the comic books like those that had inspired the MCU, such as [[Jim Starlin]]'s "[[The Infinity Gauntlet]]" (1991) and Millar's "[[Civil War (comics)|Civil War]]" (2006–2007) storylines, which included the Fantastic Four but whose MCU adaptations did not because of the film rights issues at the time.<ref name="FeigeJuly2025" /> In December 2020, Feige announced that [[Jon Watts]] would direct the film after doing so for the [[Spider-Man (2017 film series)|first three MCU ''Spider-Man'' films]] (2017–2021).<ref name="Watts/Film" /><ref name="WattsMarvel" /> Marvel Studios began meeting with writers by February 2021.<ref name="KrollWriters" /> That June, Disney CEO [[Bob Chapek]] said Feige and Marvel Studios were planning to "mine" the ''Fantastic Four'' franchise.<ref name="ChapekJune2021THR" /><ref name="ChapekJune2021MovieWeb" /> Watts stepped down as director in April 2022, choosing to take a break from the superhero genre after being "out of gas" from dealing with the [[COVID-19]] protocols on the MCU film ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'' (2021) and its post-production process; he chose to direct the film ''[[Wolfs (film)|Wolfs]]'' (2024) instead.<ref name="WattsExit" /><ref name="WattsExitReason" /> [[Grant Curtis]] and Nick Pepin were producing the new ''Fantastic Four'' film after working on the Marvel Studios miniseries ''[[Moon Knight (miniseries)|Moon Knight]]'' (2022).<ref name="CurtisPepin" />

[[John Krasinski]] portrays [[Reed Richards (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Reed Richards / Mister Fantastic]] in the MCU film ''[[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness]]'' (2022). Krasinski had been a popular suggestion for the role among fans for some time, and rumors that he had been cast as Reed had especially circulated since confirmation of the new film's development. The version of the character that he plays in ''Multiverse of Madness'' is from [[Earth-838]]—an alternate universe to the MCU's main timeline, [[Earth-616 (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Earth-616]] (also referred to as the [[Sacred Timeline]])—and is a member of the [[Illuminati (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Illuminati]].<ref name="KrasinskiMoM" /> This version of Reed is killed, along with most of that team, by [[Wanda Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wanda Maximoff / Scarlet Witch]].<ref name="RaimiKrasinskiFuture" /><ref name="KrasinskiOneOff" /> ''Multiverse of Madness'' director [[Sam Raimi]] said Feige cast Krasinski as the character because it was for an alternate universe [[cameo appearance]] that was also an opportunity to deliver on a popular fan casting. This led to speculation about whether Krasinski's role was a one-off or if he would return to star in the new ''Fantastic Four'' film.<ref name="RaimiKrasinskiFuture" /> Krasinski later said he appreciated the role, but there had been no discussions about him reprising it.<ref name="KrasinskiOneOff" />

Marvel Studios was searching for a new director by June 2022, when the studio was not close to shortening their list of candidates. The list reportedly included high-profile filmmakers, and Feige was said to be looking for a director who could oversee the filming process without him needing to be on set, similar to Raimi.<ref name="DirectorSearchJun2022" /> At [[San Diego Comic-Con]] (SDCC) the next month, Feige announced that the film would be released on November 8, 2024, as the first film in [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Six|Phase Six]] of the MCU.<ref name="SDCC2022" /> Filming was set to begin in 2023.<ref name="2023FilmingStart" /> [[Matt Shakman]] was in early talks to direct by late August.<ref name="ShakmanDeadlineEarlyTalks" /> He and [[Michael Matthews (director)|Michael Matthews]] were considered the finalists in a search that also considered [[Reid Carolin]].<ref name="ShakmanTHREarlyTalks" /> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' said Shakman was the person to beat given his familiarity with Feige after directing the Marvel Studios miniseries ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021).<ref name="ShakmanDeadlineEarlyTalks" /> Shakman's [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] to Marvel Studios involved telling a family-centric story of the Fantastic Four, with Reed and Sue becoming parents after they were well-known superheroes; executives were convinced Shakman was the best choice after he showed a picture of him holding his newly born daughter in a meeting while negotiating.<ref name="ShakmanVarietyJul2025" /> Shakman subsequently exited a [[Star Trek 4 (unproduced film)|planned ''Star Trek'' film]] due to the schedule for ''Fantastic Four'',<ref name="ShakmanNoStarTrek" /> choosing to work on the latter because he did not want to pass up the opportunity and was excited by its focus on family, optimism, and technology.<ref name="ShakmanFeb2023TheWrap" /> Feige confirmed that Shakman would direct at Disney's [[D23 (Disney)|D23]] convention in September.<ref name="ShakmanConfirm" /> Jeff Kaplan and Ian Springer were writing the script with Feige for some time before Shakman's hiring. The four were set to align their visions for the film while Feige and Shakman began casting.<ref name="Writers" /> In October, the release date was pushed back to February 14, 2025.<ref name="Feb2025Release" />

=== Pre-production ===
==== Casting start and search during the 2023 labor strikes ====
[[File:Matt Shakman.png|thumb|left|upright|[[Matt Shakman]] was hired to direct the film by September 2022.|alt=Picture of director Matt Shakman in 2023]]
Casting for the [[Fantastic Four (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Fantastic Four]] began by February 2023, with a focus on casting the role of [[Sue Storm (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sue Storm / Invisible Woman]] first.<ref name="KrollCasting" /><ref name="ShakmanFeb2023Collider" /><ref name="CastingJan2023" /> Shakman said the casting process was the biggest challenge because they wanted to find actors who could convey the team's family dynamics.<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> Feige said the group would be a big pillar in the MCU moving forward,<ref name="FeigeFeb2023" /> and the studio had "set a very high bar for ourselves" in wanting to live up to the group's long history in the comics going back to 1961.<ref name="FeigeNotAnOrigin" /> [[Josh Friedman]] was hired to rewrite the script at the end of March. Borys Kit of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' believed Friedman's hiring indicated a possible tonal shift, because he had written many science fiction projects while Kaplan and Springer were known more for comedy.<ref name="Friedman" /> Journalist [[Jeff Sneider]] reported that Friedman was hired to bring the film closer to Feige and Shakman's vision to heavily feature cosmic elements from the 1960s comic books, including the characters [[Galactus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Galactus]] and [[Silver Surfer]].<ref name="SneiderApr2023SR" /><ref name="SneiderApr2023BGR" /> Shakman noted that previous Fantastic Four adaptations combined an [[origin story]] for the team with Doctor Doom, a character who "takes up a lot of air", as the villain. By avoiding a re-telling of the origin story—the film begins four years after the team gains their powers—and using Galactus as the antagonist, he felt they were bringing a fresh perspective to the film.<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /><ref name="TieInComic" /> Feige said the decision to avoid re-telling the origin story was similar to how the MCU introduced [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]] without retelling his well-known origin story.<ref name="FeigeNotAnOrigin" /> Another difference is the introduction of Reed and Sue's son [[Franklin Richards (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Franklin Richards]], who was not included in previous film adaptations.<ref name="FeigeJuly2025" /> Sneider reported that [[Victor von Doom (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Victor von Doom / Doctor Doom]] could have a small role such as a cameo appearance in a [[post-credits scene]],<ref name="SneiderDoomSmallRole" /> and Marvel Studios wanted a major star to play the character.<ref name="SneiderAug2023Castings" />

By the start of the [[2023 Writers Guild of America strike]] in May, filming was planned to begin in January 2024 in London.<ref name="Jan2024FilmingStart" /> Sneider reported that Marvel Studios wanted [[Adam Driver]] and [[Emma Stone]] to portray Reed and Sue, but they both declined offers in part because the studio did not want to pay the high salaries they asked for;<ref name="SneiderDriverOffer" /><ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /><ref name="SneiderStoneChoice" /> Sneider said Feige wanted to contain the studio's talent spending.<ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /> In June, the release date was pushed back to May 2, 2025.<ref name="May2025Release" /> Before the start of the [[2023 SAG-AFTRA strike]] in July, Marvel Studios held [[screen test]]s for Reed with several actors, including [[Christopher Abbott]] and [[Jamie Dornan]], which Sneider said "did {{em|not}} go very well".<ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /> The studio offered the role to [[Jake Gyllenhaal]], who previously portrayed [[Mysterio (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Quentin Beck / Mysterio]] in the MCU film ''[[Spider-Man: Far From Home]]'' (2019), but he also asked for a salary that Marvel did not want to pay.<ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /><ref name="GyllenhaalPriorMCU" /> They were considering non-white actors for Reed and [[Pedro Pascal]] became interested, though he would not engage in conversations during the strike.<ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /> In August, [[Ebon Moss-Bachrach]] was offered the role of [[Ben Grimm (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ben Grimm / The Thing]],<ref name="BachrachFeb2024" /> after playing [[David Lieberman (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|David Lieberman / Micro]] in the [[The Punisher season 1|first season]] of the MCU television series ''[[The Punisher (TV series)|The Punisher]]'' (2017).<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> Marvel Studios was committed to casting a Jewish actor like Moss-Bachrach as the Thing, who is depicted as Jewish in the comics but was not portrayed by Jewish actors in the previous ''Fantastic Four'' films.<ref name="SneiderBardemGalactus" /><ref name="FirstJewishActorThing" /> [[David Krumholtz]] met with Shakman about the role after campaigning for it online, but was unsuccessful. He began campaigning to portray the villain [[Mole Man|Harvey Elder / Mole Man]] instead.<ref name="Krumholtz" /> Sneider reported that Marvel Studios was looking at [[Vanessa Kirby]] and [[Joseph Quinn]] to portray Sue and her younger brother [[Johnny Storm (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Johnny Storm / Human Torch]].<ref name="SneiderAug2023Castings" /><ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /> [[Nicholas Galitzine]] was also rumored to be portraying Johnny and had conversations about the role.<ref name="Galitzine" /> Marvel approached [[Matt Smith]] about portraying Reed, but that was not expected to work out, and they were instead looking for "a ''[[bona fide]]'' movie star".<ref name="SneiderAug2023Castings" /> [[Tom Ellis (actor)|Tom Ellis]] was also asked to audition for the role.<ref name="EllisReed" /> A Latino actor was expected to be hired as Galactus.<ref name="SneiderAug2023Castings" />

In October 2023, Shakman said they planned to start filming in early 2024 at [[Pinewood Studios]] in England,<ref name="ShakmanColliderOct2023" /> and the cast would be announced once the SAG-AFTRA strike ended. During the strikes, the crew worked on production design and early tests of the visual effects for the team's abilities.<ref name="ShakmanColliderOct2023" /> [[Kasra Farahani]] returned as production designer from the Marvel Studios series ''[[Loki (TV series)|Loki]]'' (2021–2023) and [[Alexandra Byrne]] returned as costume designer from several MCU films.<ref name="CrewMembers" /> The filmmakers chose to embrace the line design of Fantastic Four co-creator [[Jack Kirby]] from the comics, and worked with the production designer to get more Kirby designs into shots and backgrounds, such as in Galactus's helmet.<ref name="FeigeJuly2025" /> The costumes worn by the Fantastic Four in the film are pale blue and, with the exception of Reed's, have white necklines,<ref name="MisterFantasticSuit" /> similar to the designs introduced in the comics with ''[[Fantastic Four (comic book)|Fantastic Four]]'' vol. 1 #256 (1983) and those worn in [[Roger Corman]]'s [[The Fantastic Four (unreleased film)|unreleased ''The Fantastic Four'' film]] (1994).<ref name="TeaserTrailerEEsTotalFilm" /> The team briefly wears their original costumes from the comics at the beginning of the film.<ref name="ClassicSuits" /> They also wear two different sets of space suits that Byrne said were inspired by those used in [[NASA]]'s [[Project Gemini|Gemini]] and [[Project Mercury|Mercury]] programs during the 1950s and 1960s.<ref name="LoveLetterSpaceAge" /> Concept artist Seth Engstrom helped conceive the design for the comic book character [[H.E.R.B.I.E. (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|H.E.R.B.I.E.]], a robot companion to the Fantastic Four, and stated that Marvel Studios wanted to "hold true to the classic [H.E.R.B.I.E.] while exploring different possibilities for what the robot could be". Engstrom added that [[household appliance]]s and other everyday items served as inspiration for H.E.R.B.I.E.'s appearance.<ref name="HERBIEDesign" />

==== Post-labor strikes and cast announcement ====
Following the conclusion of the actors' strike in November 2023, Pascal entered talks to portray Reed and was negotiating his schedule around other commitments. Marvel Studios was expected to make offers for the other lead roles soon after, having deemed it essential to find the actor for Reed first.<ref name="Pascal" /> According to Shakman, Reed was the most difficult role to cast.<ref name="ShakmanVarietyJul2025" /> Moss-Bachrach was the first actor officially cast in the film.<ref name="MossBachrachFirst" /> Quinn sought advice on the film from Pascal during the production of their film ''[[Gladiator II]]'' (2024), not knowing that Pascal was in talks to play Reed.<ref name="QuinnPascalAdvice" /> Pascal had not planned to take on such a role and said the film changed his plans "in a very sudden way".<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> [[Javier Bardem]] was being eyed to portray Galactus after [[Antonio Banderas]] was linked to the character, though Bardem had a potential scheduling conflict with the film ''[[F1 (film)|F1]]'' (2025).<ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /> Sneider reported that the studio was looking to cast a woman as the Silver Surfer, who is usually the male character Norrin Radd.<ref name="SneiderNov2023Castings" /> In January 2024, Sneider reported that filming had been delayed to a start between July and September, causing Pascal to exit his role in the film ''[[Weapons (2025 film)|Weapons]]'' (2025).<ref name="SneiderFilmingDelay" /><ref name="PascalFilmingDelay" />

[[File:The Fantastic Four First Steps Valentines Day artwork.jpg|thumb|upright|The Valentine's Day–themed artwork released by Marvel Studios in February 2024 to confirm the casting for the titular team. This led to speculation about the film's 1960s-inspired setting.|alt=Old-fashioned art poster featuring the Fantastic Four and H.E.R.B.I.E. under a banner that reads "Happy Valentine's Day"]]
On February 14, 2024, Marvel Studios released a [[Valentine's Day]]–themed announcement that confirmed the casting of Pascal as Reed, Kirby as Sue, Quinn as Johnny, and Moss-Bachrach as the Thing. The film, officially titled ''The Fantastic Four'', was delayed to July 25, 2025, swapping places with the MCU film ''[[Thunderbolts*]]''.<ref name="CastConfirm" /> Feige said "90 percent of the work was done" by confirming the main cast.<ref name="FeigeFamilyFocus" /> The announcement's promotional art revealed the inclusion of H.E.R.B.I.E. in ''The Fantastic Four'',<ref name="HERBIE" /> marking the character's first live-action appearance. A deactivated version of H.E.R.B.I.E. was slated for a brief cameo in Fox's 2005 film ''[[Fantastic Four (2005 film)|Fantastic Four]]'', but the [[Deleted scene|scene was ultimately cut]].<ref name="HERBIEFF2005DeletedScene" /> The promotional art also included the December 13, 1963, issue of ''[[Life (magazine)|Life]]'' magazine with [[Lyndon B. Johnson]] on the cover, indicating a 1960s setting,<ref name="1963Image" /><ref name="/Film1960sSetting" /> and Pascal referenced 1960s songs from [[the Beatles]] when sharing the promotional art on [[social media]].<ref name="/Film1960sSetting" /><ref name="PascalConfirm1960sSongs" /> Feige later confirmed that the film would be a 1960s [[period piece]], but indicated that it would be set in a universe alternative to Earth-616 within the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]];<ref name="FeigePeriodPiece" /> it is set on [[Earth-828]].<ref name="Earth828" /> Setting the story in an alternate universe was "very freeing" and one of the film's earliest ideas.<ref name="FeigeJuly2025" /> The decision came about due to Shakman acknowledging that the source material's comic books were created by Jack Kirby and [[Stan Lee]] in the 1960s back when people dreamed about the stars and the unknown, wanting to put them "closer to their point of origin", and because Shakman did not want a [[plot hole]] in regards to where the titular team may have been when [[Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thanos]] attacked and [[The Blip|killed]] most of the heroes in the MCU [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]] film ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018).<ref name="WhyAltEarth" /> Shakman called it a "[[retro-future]] '60s" aesthetic, "part what you know from the '60s, but part what you've never seen".<ref name="SDCC2024EW2" /> He looked to the optimism of the [[Space Race]] and ideas of future space travelers that people had in the 1960s when the comics were first written. Shakman stated, "I really wanted to take all of that great stuff from [[Apollo 11]] and just imagine that instead of [[Neil Armstrong]] and [[Buzz Aldrin]], it was the Storms and Ben Grimm and Reed Richards heading off into space".<ref name="SDCC2024EW" /> The idea of a Fantastic Four film set in the mid-20th century had previously been considered by [[Peyton Reed]] when he was attached to direct Fox's ''Fantastic Four''; he ultimately left the project and later directed the MCU film ''[[Ant-Man (film)|Ant-Man]]'' (2015) and its two sequels, ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp]]'' (2018) and ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania]]'' (2023).<ref name="FeigeFFDoomsday" /> Shakman studied all of the Fantastic Four comics, from their original creation up to present day,<ref name="ShakmansComicBookInspirations" /> and took particular inspiration from [[Jonathan Hickman]]'s run on ''Fantastic Four'' vol. 3 #570–588 (2009–2011) for how to approach the main characters. ''The Fantastic Four'' includes the philanthropic organization [[Future Foundation (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Future Foundation]], which was introduced during that same run.<ref name="ShakmanForeword" /> Shakman was also inspired by the films ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey]]'' (1968) and ''[[Blade Runner]]'' (1982),<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /><ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" /> as well as the works of industrial designer and artist [[Syd Mead]].<ref name="SDCC2024Variety" /> He said his work on the television series ''[[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia]]'' (2005–present) helped inform his approach to the film's playfulness, collaboration, and improvisation.<ref name="ShakmanNov2023" />

Also in February 2024, Sneider reported that Bardem was the studio's top choice for Galactus; they had begun meeting with actors for Silver Surfer; and Doctor Doom would be cast during filming.<ref name="SneiderBardemGalactus" /> [[Eric Pearson]], who had a reputation for "taking projects over the finish line" for Marvel Studios, had been "quietly hired" to polish the script.<ref name="Pearson" /> In April, [[Julia Garner]] was cast as [[Shalla-Bal (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Shalla-Bal / Silver Surfer]].<ref name="Garner" /><ref name="Garner2" /> In the comics, Shalla-Bal is not the Silver Surfer, but rather the love interest—and occasionally an adversary—of Norrin Radd. However, an alternate version of her becomes the second Silver Surfer alongside Radd as "twin heralds" of Galactus in the 1999–2000 ''[[Earth-X]]'' limited series by [[Jim Krueger]] and [[John Paul Leon]].<ref name="ShallaBalComics" /><ref name="ShallaBalEarthX" /><ref name="SilverSurferComicsHistory" /> Justin Kroll of ''Deadline Hollywood'' reported that no actors held an offer or were in talks for Galactus at that time.<ref name="KrollGalactus" /> ''WandaVision'' writer Peter Cameron had contributed to the script by the following month.<ref name="Hauser" /><ref name="WGACredits" /> Several actors joined the cast in May, including [[Paul Walter Hauser]] in the role of [[Harvey Elder (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Harvey Elder / Mole Man]], reportedly for a sequence at the beginning of the film.<ref name="Hauser" /><ref name="HauserMoleMan" /><ref name="MoleMan" /> Hauser worked with Shakman on ''It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia'' and met with him to discuss joining ''The Fantastic Four''. He was initially reluctant due to the poor reception of the previous ''Fantastic Four'' films, but was won over by the script, cast, and crew.<ref name="HausersReservations" /> [[John Malkovich]] was cast as [[Red Ghost (character)|Ivan Kragoff / Red Ghost]],<ref name="Malkovich" /><ref name="MalkovichCut" /> after declining offers for previous Marvel films because he felt the proposed pay was not equal to the work involved. He chose to accept a role in this film to work with Shakman again after the film ''[[Cut Bank (film)|Cut Bank]]'' (2014).<ref name="MalkovichAcceptReason" /> [[Ralph Ineson]] was cast as Galactus,<ref name="Ineson" /><ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> after appearing as a [[Ravagers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ravager]] pilot in the MCU film ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014);<ref name="InesonGotG" /> Sneider posited that Marvel chose Ineson for his voice acting proficiency, not wanting for another A-list actor after failing to pull Bardem due to the scheduling difficulties.<ref name="DowneySneider" /> [[Natasha Lyonne]] also joined the cast,<ref name="Lyonne" /> after voicing the original MCU character [[Byrdie the Duck]] in the [[What If...? season 3|third season]] of the Marvel Studios animated series ''[[What If...? (TV series)|What If...?]]'' (2024).<ref name="LyonneWhatIf" />

Three weeks of rehearsals were held with the actors to work through the script with Shakman and Friedman to help define their relationships to one another and how they would exist in their 1960s retro-futuristic setting. Discussing these rehearsals, Pascal explained that it allowed the actors to approach the script "dramaturgically as you would a play" while Moss-Bachrach, who believed the script was not completed yet, said it allowed them to share research and consult with astronauts and scientists.<ref name="ShakmanVarietyJul2025" /> At the end of June 2024, Feige said filming would begin at the end of July.<ref name="JulyFilming" /><ref name="FeigeJulyFilming" /> A few days of pre-filming occurred before SDCC in late July, capturing footage of an astronaut preparation area, the Thing as a contestant on a [[dating game show]] titled ''Let's Make a Match'', and a [[Children's television series|children's science show]] hosted by Reed titled ''Fantastic Science with Mister Fantastic''.<ref name="SDCC2024EW" /> The ''Let's Make a Match'' scene was not included in the final cut of the film.<ref name="Let'sMakeaMatchDeletedScene" /> At the convention, the film's new title was announced to be ''The Fantastic Four: First Steps''.<ref name="SDCC2024Avengers" /> The subtitle references the film's ideas of optimism and space exploration,<ref name="SDCC2024EW" /><ref name="ComicBookFirstStepsMeaning" /> but Shakman said it also has additional meanings. This led to speculation about what those meanings could be.<ref name="ComicBookFirstStepsMeaning" /> Also at SDCC, the main cast were revealed to be reprising their roles in the MCU crossover films ''[[Avengers: Doomsday]]'' (2026) and ''[[Avengers: Secret Wars]]'' (2027),<ref name="SDCC2024" /> and [[Robert Downey Jr.]] was announced to be portraying Doctor Doom in those two films.<ref name="SDCC2024Avengers" /> Feige wanted Downey to return to the MCU, after he portrayed [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark / Iron Man]] in the franchise from 2008 to 2019, and had discussed the actor taking on the role of Doom around a year before the SDCC announcement.<ref name="DowneyTHRAug2024" /> Sneider reported that Doom would be introduced in a post-credits scene at the end of ''First Steps'' ahead of Downey's appearances in the ''Avengers'' films.<ref name="DowneySneider" /> Shakman said he knew about Downey's casting for a while before the announcement,<ref name="DowneyShakman" /> but he later said Doctor Doom was "not a part of [his] film, and therefore not a part of [his] purview".<ref name="ShakmanNoDoom" /> Feige later added that the events of ''First Steps'' would lead directly into the story of ''Doomsday''.<ref name="CineEurope2025" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began on July 30, 2024,<ref name="FilmingStart" /> at Pinewood Studios in [[Buckinghamshire]], England,<ref name="ShakmanColliderOct2023" /><ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> under the [[working title]] ''Blue Moon''.<ref name="WorkingTitle" /> The film was shot for [[IMAX]],<ref name="ShakmanColliderOct2023" /><ref name="IMAXFilming" /> with [[Jess Hall]] returning from ''WandaVision'' as cinematographer.<ref name="CrewMembers" /> Filming was originally reported to be starting in 2023,<ref name="2023FilmingStart" /> and then early 2024, before the strikes.<ref name="Jan2024FilmingStart" /> Shakman wanted the film to look as if it were made by ''2001: A Space Odyssey'' director [[Stanley Kubrick]] in 1965, including with its camera lens choices and general "approach to filmmaking".<ref name="ShakmanEmpireApr2025" /> [[Second unit]] director of photography Tim Wooster used his father's [[16 mm film]] camera for some sequences.<ref name="ColliderSetVisit" /> Practical sets and props were employed whenever possible,<ref name="ShakmanEmpireApr2025" /> with most of the film's locations—including the [[Baxter Building (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Baxter Building]], [[Times Square]], and [[Yancy Street (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yancy Street]]—created on 30 sets spread across eight [[sound stage]]s on the two [[backlot]]s of Pinewood Studios,<ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" /> one of which was used in its entirety for the Times Square set.<ref name="ShakmanVarietyJul2025" /> Real-life establishments from the 1960s—such as [[Horn & Hardart]], [[Whelan's]], Leighton's, [[Palace Theatre (New York City)|RKO Palace]], [[Embassy Theatre (New York City)|Embassy Theatre]], and [[Forum Cinema]]—were recreated on that set, the area of which was 22% smaller than the actual Times Square.<ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" /> Imaginarium Studios—founded by [[Andy Serkis]], who portrays the character [[Ulysses Klaue (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ulysses Klaue]] in the MCU, and based at Pinewood Studios—assisted with and enhanced the [[motion capture]] process performed by both Moss-Bachrach and Garner for ''First Steps''.<ref name="GarnerFeb2026TD" /><ref name="MossBachrachJan2025EW" /> Garner filmed her scenes on a real [[surfboard]] to lend realism to her movements and positioning, seeking to understand the "possibilities achievable" with the Silver Surfer's iconic cosmic board, while also focusing on the character's "silhouette and shape" during her performance.<ref name="GarnerFeb2026TD" /> Garner also shared most of her scenes with Quinn.<ref name="GarnerEWApr2025" /><ref name="GarnerFeb2026TD" />

The retro-futuristic design of New York City in the film was based on the work of architects such as [[Eero Saarinen]] and [[Oscar Niemeyer]], as well as illustrators such as Mead, [[John Berkey]], Charles Schridde, and [[Arthur Radebaugh]].<ref name="FarahaniBaxterBuilding" /> The Baxter Building, headquarters and residence of the Fantastic Four, is located at the site of the [[Headquarters of the United Nations|headquarters]] of the [[United Nations]] (UN) in [[Midtown Manhattan]],<ref name="FarahaniAug2025Dezeen" /> and the base of the building also serves as the headquarters of the Future Foundation.<ref name="BaxterBuildingZillow" /> The penthouse of the Baxter Building was a two-story contiguous set inspired by "cozy" [[Mid-century modern#Mid-century modern in the United States|mid-century modern California]] homes.<ref name="ShakmanVarietyJul2025" /><ref name="ColliderSetVisit" /> Described as a "cabin within a penthouse", the space featured warm-toned wood paneling, a central [[fireplace]], a sunken seating area, and a kitchen—the latter designed based on futuristic exhibits from the [[1964 New York World's Fair]], as well as [[Frigidaire]]'s 1956 "Kitchen of Tomorrow" concept.<ref name="FarahaniBaxterBuilding" /> Another key location within the Baxter Building is Reed's lab, inspired by ''2001: A Space Odyssey'',<ref name="ColliderSetVisit" /> which is divided into three sections: a red room for research and inventions, a yellow room with blackboards for thinking, and a blue room with communications equipment for monitoring.<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /><ref name="FarahaniBaxterBuilding" /> A fictionalized version of New York's Yancy Street was built based on how the city's Jewish areas looked during the childhood of Jack Kirby, including [[kosher]] grocery stores and a [[synagogue]].<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> Set decorator Jille Azis, who worked on the film, said the filmmakers wanted Yancy Street to "feel like part of the normal world, not the futuristic one", stating that "[w]hen Ben Grimm visits, it feels like home – a place to escape the stress and reconnect with the past". The Yancy Street set had previously been used in the MCU film ''[[Deadpool & Wolverine]]'' (2024) and was repurposed and redecorated for ''First Steps''.<ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" />

Practical props included a {{convert|14|ft|m|adj=mid}} [[scale model]] of the ''Excelsior''—the Fantastic Four's spaceship, which is named after one of Lee's most famous catchphrases—to create a [[miniature effect]];<ref name="ColliderSetVisit" /><ref name="ShakmanEmpireApr2025" /><ref name="IGNEasterEggs" /> H.E.R.B.I.E., realized through a combination of a wooden puppet, a remote-controlled [[animatronic]] that had a functioning head and arms, and visual effects;<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /><ref name="Wood" /> and two models of the team's [[Fantasticar (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Fantasticar]], one that allowed for interior filming with the actors and another that was "stripped down" for use in effects shots.<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" /> The filmmakers drew inspiration for the design of the ''Excelsior'' from 1960s [[space technology]], particularly that used in the [[Apollo program]] [[List of Apollo missions|missions]], and also consulted former NASA astronaut [[Rick Mastracchio]] to give the spaceship authentic details such as Velcro tabs to secure equipment and handrails for moving around in [[zero gravity]].<ref name="FarahaniBaxterBuilding" /><ref name="LoveLetterSpaceAge" /> The launch platform for the ''Excelsior'' juts out from the Baxter Building toward the middle of the [[East River]], with Farahani stating that he and the rest of the creative team "kind of went totally overboard in designing all the functionality of how you could launch a rocket in midtown Manhattan without creating chaos". To achieve this, they devised a rotating system of blast shields, as well as a catchment system that draws water from the river to create massive steam curtains, dampening the flames and protecting the public during liftoff.<ref name="FarahaniBaxterBuilding" /> Farahani said the Fantasticar was based on "mid-'60s American concept cars that were actually referencing European cars". Its accent features, such as its turbine intakes and interface controls, have a 1950s "retrofuture" look.<ref name="EWCoverStoryApr2025" />

[[Location shooting]] occurred at [[Durdle Door]] in [[Dorset]], England, in mid-October for a scene featuring Johnny that was ultimately cut from the film.<ref name="DurdleDoorFilming" /><ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" /> Another deleted scene involved a conversation between Johnny and the Silver Surfer at the Baxter Building.<ref name="GarnerFeb2026TD" /> Lyonne finished filming her scenes later that month.<ref name="LyonneWrap" /> In mid-November, [[Sarah Niles]] was revealed to have been cast.<ref name="ProjectProfile" /> Filming took place from November 19–22 at the [[Palacio de Congresos de Oviedo|Palace of Congresses]] in [[Oviedo]], Spain, for scenes set in the lobby of the Baxter Building and the assembly hall of the Future Foundation.<ref name="OviedoFilming" /><ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" /> Filming also took place in the [[Middleton-by-Wirksworth|Middleton]] mine in [[Derbyshire Dales]], England,<ref name="MiddletonMine" /> with a {{convert|725|ft|m|adj=mid}} section of the mine decorated with weathered pipes and other industrial props for scenes set in Mole Man's underground realm of [[Subterranea (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Subterranea]].<ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" /> Filming [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] at the end of November,<ref name="FeigeNov2024Wrap" /> having lasted 85 days.<ref name="FilmingLocationsTO" />

=== Post-production ===
In April 2025, [[Mark Gatiss]] was revealed to be appearing as [[Ted Gilbert]], the talk show host of ''The Ted Gilbert Show''.<ref name="Gatiss" /> He was offered the role without having to audition.<ref name="GatissFeb2026Collider" /> A month later, sound designer [[Matthew Wood (sound editor)|Matthew Wood]] was revealed to be the voice of H.E.R.B.I.E.<ref name="Wood" /> He had previously voiced the Quantumnauts, the foot soldiers of [[Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kang the Conqueror]], in ''Quantumania''.<ref name="WoodJul2025DS" /> Additional photography occurred by then,<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> with Garner seen filming in [[Los Angeles]], California,<ref name="GarnerMay2025RSB" /> for a scene depicting Shalla-Bal's origin story, set on her home planet [[Zenn-La (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Zenn-La]].<ref name="ZennLaConceptArt" /> Shakman said the character's role had evolved throughout the writing process, and he came to feel during post-production that her backstory—in which she chooses to give herself to Galactus to save her planet and family—was reflective of the film's themes and the Fantastic Four's choices. Shakman decided that the backstory should be seen by the audience.<ref name="ShakmanTHRJul2025" /> The creative team also considered the idea of having Shalla-Bal's characteristic silvery appearance shift to gold—drawing inspiration from the character [[Nova (Frankie Raye)|Frankie Raye / Nova]]—whenever she used her powers.<ref name="GoldenSurfer" /> The film's final screenwriting credits were determined through arbitration with the [[Writers Guild of America]] (WGA), with the screenplay attributed to Friedman, Pearson, Kaplan, and Springer from a story by Pearson, Kaplan, Springer, and Kat Wood.<ref name="WGACredits" /><ref name="VarietyNextMovesJul2025" /> Pearson's work on the film included moving Franklin's birth from the beginning of the film to during the team's space escape from Galactus in the middle of the film, along with moving the final fight against Galactus from space to Earth to highlight Galactus's scale. He also worked on Johnny researching and unlocking Shalla-Bal's language, with his drafts having more "flirtatious" moments between the two than what was in the final film.<ref name="PearsonTHRAug2025" /><ref name="EndingExplainedEW" />

In July 2025, Shakman revealed that Malkovich's role as Red Ghost had been cut from the film and said this was a "heartbreaking" decision to make. Shakman explained that Red Ghost was originally planned to be introduced in a sequence at the beginning of the film that chronicles the Fantastic Four's early years as superheroes; this sequence would have included a scene of the team battling Red Ghost and his [[Super-Apes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Super-Apes]].<ref name="MalkovichCut" /> After this, Red Ghost would have returned in the [[Climax (narrative)|climax]] for a scene that initially depicted him destroying Reed's teleportation bridges, although Red Ghost was replaced by Shalla-Bal in that scene for the final cut of the film because Shakman felt "it just made more sense to have [Shalla-Bal] come back. It stayed more on story with the antagonist we knew, and were spending the most time with."<ref name="RedGhostCBR" /><ref name="RedGhostComicBook" /> Shakman praised Malkovich's performance in the role, but said his scenes were some of many that were cut to help balance the introduction of the Fantastic Four as a group and individually, their world, and the various other villains in the film.<ref name="MalkovichCut" /> Shakman also noted that an extended [[director's cut]] of ''First Steps'' is "not possible" because many of the cut sequences contain unfinished visual effects.<ref name="DirectorCutNotPossible" /> Although Malkovich was removed, the film retains a brief cameo appearance by a [[cartoon]] version of Red Ghost in the post-credits scene as part of an in-universe animated series titled ''The Fantastic Four: Power Hour'',<ref name="VarietyEasterEggs" /><ref name="PowerHour" /> which also features cartoon versions of other comic book villains not present in the film such as [[Diablo (Marvel Comics)|Diablo]], [[Dragon Man (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dragon Man]], [[Mad Thinker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mad Thinker]], [[Puppet Master (Marvel Comics)|Puppet Master]], and [[Bentley Wittman (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wizard]].<ref name="PostCreditVillains" /> Additional characters appearing in the film include the Super-Apes orangutan member [[Peotor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peotor]] and the ginormous Subterranean creature [[Giganto (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Giganto]].<ref name="VarietyEasterEggs" /> Leading up to the film's release, several actors's roles were revealed, including infant actor Ada Scott as Franklin Richards,<ref name="ScottFranklinRichards" /> Hauser as Mole Man,<ref name="HauserMoleMan" /> Niles as Lynne Nichols, and Lyonne as Rachel Rozman.<ref name="CharacterGuide" /><ref name="VarietyReview" /> The stars of the unreleased ''The Fantastic Four'' film–[[Alex Hyde-White]] (who portrayed Reed in that film), [[Rebecca Staab]] (Sue), [[Jay Underwood]] (Johnny), and [[Michael Bailey Smith]] (Grimm)–were also revealed to be appearing,<ref name="MarvelStudiosPressConference" /> making cameo appearances throughout ''First Steps''.<ref name="VarietyEasterEggs" />

The [[mid-credits scene]] of ''First Steps'' takes place four years after the film's main events and reintroduces Downey to the MCU as Doctor Doom; the scene was filmed by ''Doomsday'' directors [[Anthony and Joe Russo]] during [[Production of Avengers: Doomsday and Avengers: Secret Wars|the production]] of that film.<ref name="DoomPostCreditsScene" /><ref name="RussosPostCreditsScene" /><ref name="KirbyVarietyJul2025" /> As they began work on the story and scripts for ''Doomsday'' and ''Secret Wars'', the Russo brothers visited the sets of ''First Steps'' and viewed early cuts of scenes with Shakman. He said this allowed him to "pass the baton to them and [ensure that the Fantastic Four characters] would be well cared for".<ref name="ShakmanBaton" /> Sets for the Baxter Building from ''First Steps'' were reused, with some updates, for ''Doomsday''.<ref name="FantasticFourSetsDoomsday" /><ref name="FeigeFFDoomsday" /><ref name="FeigeJuly2025" /> ''First Steps'' ends with a quote from Jack Kirby: "If you look at my characters, you will find me. No matter what kind of character you create or assume, a little of yourself must remain there."<ref name="ColliderKirbyReferences" /><ref name="ForwardRoz" /> This is followed by the revelation that the numerical designation 828 for the universe in which the film is set was based on his date of birth: August 28, 1917.<ref name="ColliderKirbyReferences" /><ref name="VarietyEasterEggs" /> ''First Steps'' is dedicated to two people: producer Jamie Christopher, who previously worked on other MCU films and passed away in 2023 during the [[pre-production]] of ''First Steps'' due to [[heart complications]];<ref name="EWFilmDedications" /><ref name="EasterEggsTF" /> and Shakman's mother Inez, who passed away on April 3, 2025.<ref name="EWFilmDedications" />

Nona Khodai and Tim Roche edited ''First Steps'', having previously worked together on ''WandaVision'' and separately on other MCU projects.<ref name="VarietyReview" /> Visual effects for the film were provided by [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM), [[Framestore]], [[Sony Pictures Imageworks]], [[Digital Domain]], [[Rise FX]], and [[Wētā FX]]. [[Scott Stokdyk]] served as visual effects supervisor, while Lisa Marra was the visual effects producer.<ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> There were 2,015 visual effect shots in the final cut of the film,<ref name="StokdykVFX" /> of which Framestore worked on 417 and Digital Domain on 400.<ref name="AllmanVFX" /><ref name="CramerVFX" /> ILM worked on the scenes featuring Galactus;<ref name="StokdykVFX" /> Framestore worked on scenes such as the destruction of planet LHS-275 by Galactus's worldship, named ''[[Taa II]]'', and the Silver Surfer chase sequence, the latter featuring a [[neutron star]] that was based on ''2001: A Space Odyssey'', as well as the film ''[[Interstellar (film)|Interstellar]]'' (2014);<ref name="AllmanVFX" /> Imageworks worked on the scenes featuring the Silver Surfer;<ref name="StokdykVFX" /> Digital Domain worked on the scenes featuring H.E.R.B.I.E. and Franklin;<ref name="CramerVFX" /> and the others collaborated on the remaining scenes in the film.<ref name="StokdykVFX" /> Furthermore, ILM, Framestore, and Digital Domain worked on different scenes featuring the Thing,<ref name="StokdykVFX" /><ref name="AllmanVFX" /><ref name="CramerVFX" /> with Digital Domain also creating nine different life-sized Thing costumes.<ref name="CramerVFX" /> Much of the New York City shots had buildings built after the 1960s replaced with retro-futuristic buildings.<ref name="StokdykVFX" /> Framestore had a professional surfer perform surfing movements and poses—executed on a board resting on supports—and used photographs taken of her as a reference for the Silver Surfer.<ref name="AllmanVFX" /> The visual effects for the character's metallic skin consisted of two distinct layers: a "brushed metal" base layer to preserve Garner's facial expressions and features; and a "[[Chromium|chrome]]" layer overlaid on the first, providing realistic, brilliant reflections of the environment surrounding the Silver Surfer. The practical Galactus suit that Ineson wore was scanned, with ILM increasing the resolution of the model for close-up shots.<ref name="StokdykVFX" /> 

{{external media|float=left|video1=[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lbUlgp7JTUI Marvel Studios' The Fantastic Four: First Steps End Credits Main-On-End Title Sequence] presents the film's main-on-end title sequence, [[YouTube]] video from [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]]'s channel}}
The film's main-on-end title sequence—which unfolds through image grids, moving squares, and dynamic layouts—was designed by [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]], a long-time Marvel Studios collaborator. Inspired by both mid-century modern aesthetics and the analog charm of 1960s title design, the sequence combines vibrant color fields, [[hand-drawn animation]], and classic grid-based layouts to create an authentically [[Vintage design|vintage]] atmosphere. Original in-universe artifacts—such as magazine covers, trading cards, lunchboxes, and a commemorative Galactus plaque—were created specifically for the sequence and combined with materials provided by Marvel, including alternate takes, behind-the-scenes footage, and props from the film.<ref name="Perception" /> The animation for the ''Power Hour'' cartoon was produced by [[Titmouse, Inc.]]—the studio that had previously worked on the animated opening title sequence for the second episode of ''WandaVision'', "[[Don't Touch That Dial]]" (2021), as well as the [[stop motion]] yogurt commercial shown in that series's ninth and [[final episode]], "[[The Series Finale]]" (2021).<ref name="WilchesPolygonInterview" />

== Marketing ==
On April 4, 2024—known as "4–4 Day"—Marvel Studios released a teaser poster featuring the Human Torch creating the Fantastic Four symbol with flames in the sky. A link was included that led to a [[HTTP 404|404 error page]] featuring H.E.R.B.I.E., with an embedded [[QR code]] leading to a Future Foundation–themed page with a list of ''Fantastic Four'' comics to read on [[Marvel Unlimited]]:<ref name="4-4DayComics" /><ref name="4-4DayPoster" /> ''Fantastic Four'' vol. 1 #1 (1961), the team's first appearance; ''Fantastic Four'' vol. 1 #48–50 (1966), known as "[[The Galactus Trilogy]]" and featuring the first appearances of Galactus and the Silver Surfer; and ''[[Fantastic Four: Life Story]]'' #1 (2021), which is set in the 1960s.<ref name="4-4DayComics" /><ref name="GalactusTrilogy" /> Following a screening of ''Deadpool & Wolverine'' at SDCC on July 25, a drone show over [[Petco Park]] included images of Galactus and the Fantastic Four logo, teasing announcements at Marvel Studios's Hall H panel two nights later.<ref name="SDCCDroneShowTease" /><ref name="SDCCDroneShowTease2" /> That panel featured Shakman and the main cast, revealed the full title, showed a teaser reel, and had the film's futuristic Fantasticar float across the stage.<ref name="SDCC2024" /> Shakman explained that the teaser reel featured pre-filming footage combined with [[pre-visualization]]s and animatics that were created during development.<ref name="SDCC2024EW" /> The footage was presented in an "old-timey [[4:3 aspect ratio]]".<ref name="SDCC2024Inverse" /> Further footage was shown at [[D23 Brazil]] in November.<ref name="D23Brazil" />

The first teaser trailer was released on February 4, 2025. Pascal, Kirby, Quinn, and Moss-Bachrach appeared at a fan event at the [[U.S. Space & Rocket Center]] in [[Huntsville, Alabama]], which is known as "Rocket City", to launch the teaser.<ref name="TeaserTrailerEW" /> Leading up to this, a short "teaser for the first teaser" was released featuring children running to a television that displays Fantastic Four imagery. ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]''{{'s}} Jesse David Fox noted a [[stock sound effect]] used for the children's laughter, wondering if it was an intentional indication of something or if the footage had just been rushed.<ref name="TeaserTeaserVulture" /> ''[[Kotaku]]''{{'s}} Zack Zwiezen likened the widely used sound effect, which had originally been recorded for the film ''[[Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom]]'' (1984), to the [[Wilhelm scream]] sound effect. He was confused why it was used twice in such a short clip.<ref name="TeaserTeaserKotaku" /> Discussing the full teaser, Ben Travis at ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' said the film was "on the right track", appreciating the tone, style, and different references to the comics.<ref name="TeaserTrailerEmpire" /> Several commentators noted that all of the Fantastic Four's powers are seen except for Reed's stretching abilities.<ref name="TeaserTrailerEmpire" /><ref name="TeaserTrailerPolygon" /><ref name="TeaserTrailerGizmodo" /> A scene of the Thing cooking with H.E.R.B.I.E. was highlighted by commentators who compared it to Moss-Bachrach's role as chef Richie Jerimovich in the series ''[[The Bear (TV series)|The Bear]]'' (2022–2026).<ref name="ThingBearAVClub" /><ref name="ThingBearPopVerse" /><ref name="TeaserTrailerEEsTotalFilm" /> Commentators also discussed a scene at the end of the trailer—of the Fantastic Four on a raised stage, surrounded by arrows—that pays homage to the Beatles's [[The Beatles on The Ed Sullivan Show|first live television performance]] on ''[[The Ed Sullivan Show]]'' (1948–1971) in February 1964.<ref name="TeaserTrailerGizmodo" /><ref name="TheBeatlesHomage" /> The teaser was viewed 202 million times in its first 24 hours, the third-best for a Marvel Studios trailer after those for ''Deadpool & Wolverine'' (365M) and 2021's ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'' (355M). It became the top trending video on [[YouTube]], and a livestream of the teaser's launch became Marvel's most-viewed ever.<ref name="TeaserTrailerViews" /> Alongside the teaser, Marvel also released several posters featuring the tagline "Prepare 4 Launch".<ref name="TeaserTrailerMarvel" /> One of these was criticized by some who believed it was [[Artificial intelligence art|created with artificial intelligence]] (AI), pointing to a woman's face seemingly being used multiple times and hands that appear to have the wrong number of fingers. Footage was shown at the 2025 [[CinemaCon]], which gave a first look at Garner's Silver Surfer and revealed that Sue and Reed are expecting a child in the film.<ref name="CinemaConTHR" /><ref name="CinemaConVariety" /> Sydney Bucksbaum at ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' said Sue being pregnant gave "new meaning" to the film's title.<ref name="CinemaConEW" /> When the subtitle was originally announced, Jenna Anderson at ''[[ComicBook.com]]'' theorized "First Steps" could refer to the first steps of a child, indicating the film would see the introduction of either of Reed and Sue's children, Franklin and Valeria Richards; both were not seen in the previous ''Fantastic Four'' films.<ref name="ComicBookFirstStepsMeaning" /> The CinemaCon footage was not released online, despite speculation that a full trailer could be released on April 4, 2025. == Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''The Fantastic Four: First Steps'' grossed $274.3&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $247.6&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $521.9&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" />

On July 22, 2025, the film was projected to make $100–110&nbsp;million at the domestic box office, with an additional $90–100&nbsp;million overseas for a total of $190–210&nbsp;million during its opening weekend.<ref name="BOProjections" /> The film made $24.4&nbsp;million in Thursday night previews, the biggest preview box office of 2025, surpassing that of ''[[Superman (2025 film)|Superman]]'', which had been released two weeks earlier.<ref name="ThursdayPreviews" /><ref name="BOFirstWeekendDeadline" /> It collected $57&nbsp;million on its first day, ranking below ''[[A Minecraft Movie]]'' as the second-highest opening day for a 2025 film.<ref name="BOFirstDay" /> It ended up debuting to $117.6&nbsp;million at the domestic box office, below that of ''Superman'',<ref name="BOFirstWeekendTN" /><ref name="BOFirstWeekendBOM" /> and a total of $216.7&nbsp;million globally.<ref name="BOFirstWeekendTHR" /><ref name="BOFirstWeekendVariety" /><ref name="BOFirstWeekendDeadline" /> The film dropped 80% on its second Friday, the second biggest Friday-to-Friday drop for any comic book movie in 2025, behind ''[[Captain America: Brave New World]],'' earning $11.7&nbsp;million.<ref name="FridayDrop" />

''The Fantastic Four: First Steps'' retained the top spot at the US/Canadian box office in its second weekend, but it declined by 66% with a gross of $38.7 million.<ref name="BOSecondWeekendDeadline" /><ref name="BOSecondWeekendVariety" /><ref name="BOSecondWeekendBOM" /> Likewise, it remained at number one for two consecutive weekends in the United Kingdom and Ireland, grossing £3&nbsp;million ($4.1&nbsp;million) in its second week and bringing its cumulative total to £15.3&nbsp;million ($20.4&nbsp;million) in those markets.<ref name="Number1UKIreland" /> During its third weekend, the film fell to third place, grossing $15.8&nbsp;million behind ''[[Freakier Friday]]'' and ''[[Weapons (2025 film)|Weapons]]''.<ref name="BOThirdWeekendDeadline" /><ref name="BOThirdWeekendBOM" /> ''The Fantastic Four: First Steps'' finished its run as the [[List of American films of 2025|ninth highest-grossing film of 2025 in the U.S. and Canada]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Domestic Box Office For 2025 |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/2025/?grossesOption=totalGrosses&sort=grossToDate&ref_=bo_yld__resort#table |access-date=March 17, 2026 |website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===

Do NOT convert the prose here to them! -->On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 86% of 407 critics gave the film a positive review, with an average rating of 7.2/10. The site's critics consensus reads, "Benefitting from rock-solid cast chemistry and clad in appealingly retro 1960s design, this crack at ''The Fantastic Four'' does Marvel's First Family justice."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=the_fantastic_four_first_steps |type=m |title=The Fantastic Four: First Steps |access-date=June 6, 2026}}{{cbignore}}</ref><!-- Local consensus for MCU articles is to NOT use aggregate templates! Do NOT convert the prose here to them! --> [[Metacritic]] summarized the critical response as "generally favorable", based on a weighted average score of 65 out of 100 from 54 critics.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=the-fantastic-four-first-steps |type=movie |title=The Fantastic Four: First Steps |access-date=July 31, 2025}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale, while [[PostTrak]] reported 93% of audience members gave the film a positive score, with 70% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="BOFirstWeekendDeadline" /><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Rubin |first=Rebecca |date=July 28, 2025 |title=4 Reasons Why ''Fantastic Four'' Ended Marvel's Box Office Slump |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/box-office/fantastic-four-box-office-ended-marvel-slump-1236471537/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250729005306/https://variety.com/2025/film/box-office/fantastic-four-box-office-ended-marvel-slump-1236471537/ |archive-date=July 29, 2025 |access-date=August 3, 2025 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref><!-- Local consensus for MCU articles is to NOT use aggregate templates! Do NOT convert the prose here to them! -->

''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' said the film foregoing the [[origin story]] and taking place in the midst of the team as active heroes was a strong suit.<ref name="VarietyReview" /> ''[[The Guardian]]''{{'}}s [[Peter Bradshaw]] wrote it "hangs together as an entertaining spectacle in its own innocent self-enclosed universe of fantasy wackiness", writing the production design was "hallucinatory" in its 1960s aesthetic, and said it kept the superhero genre "aloft".<ref name="GuardianReview" /> ''The Hollywood Reporter''{{'}}s David Rooney similarly praised the 60s vibe, feeling Giacchino's orchestral score, the production design and costuming were complementary. Rooney felt that Moss-Bachrach portrayed "warmth and sensitivity" behind his motion-capture character and wrote of Garner's commanding portrayal of an "icy" to "sorrowful" [[character arc]].<ref name="HollywoodReporterReview" />

[[Robbie Collin]] of ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' said the film "makes you wish that Marvel had reached this point years ago – imagine if 2019's ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' had been followed by this. Yet at least they're here now, and the result is a very unusual sort of franchise instalment: one that feels every inch a one-off."<ref name="TelegraphReview" /> Sandra Hall of ''[[The Sydney Morning Herald]]'' wrote, "The action is as spectacular as you would expect, which doesn't mean that it's particularly suspenseful, but the film's success lies in the fact it puts the fun back into the franchise."<ref name="SydneyMorningHeraldReview" />

Esther Zuckerman of ''[[Bloomberg News]]'' was more critical, writing, "While ''Superman'' felt bracingly modern with the political sentiments to boot, ''The Fantastic Four'' has a halo of cobwebs it can't quite shake off."<ref name="BloombergReview" /> ''[[Livemint]]''{{'s}} Udita Jhunjhunwala praised the cast performances, but said the film was "competent at best—a [[Reboot (fiction)|reboot]] with cosmic ambition that barely attains lift-off."<ref name="LivemintReview" /> ''[[The Australian]]''{{'s}} Stephen Romei praised the opening sequence but criticized the film's dialogue, concluding, "The superhero movie franchise has its ups and downs. This one is definitely on the downside."<ref name="AustralianReview" />

== Other media ==
{{Further|Fantastic Four: First Steps (comic){{!}}''Fantastic Four: First Steps'' (comic)}}
At the end of April 2025, Marvel Comics and Marvel Studios announced a [[Marvel Cinematic Universe tie-in comics|tie-in]] [[one-shot comic]] titled ''[[Fantastic Four: First Steps (comic)|Fantastic Four: First Steps]]'',<ref name="TieInComic" /> which was released on July 9. It was written by [[Matt Fraction]] and drawn by [[Mark Buckingham (comic book artist)|Mark Buckingham]], with colors, lettering, and cover art provided by Alex Sinclair, Joe Caramagna, and [[Phil Noto]], respectively.<ref name="TieInComicPreview" /> Marvel Comics worked with the film's creative team to ensure the comic matched the film's style and tone, and the comics team visited the set during filming.<ref name="TieInComic" /> It is presented as an in-universe comic published by the Future Foundation on the fourth anniversary of the Fantastic Four gaining their powers, serving as an "authorized retelling" of the team's early adventures.<ref name="TieInComicMarvel" />

In December 2025, Marvel Comics announced four additional tie-in one-shot comics that, similar to the ''First Steps'' one-shot, are presented as in-universe comics published by the Future Foundation and expand on characters and moments from the film; the four comics are expected to be released quarterly throughout 2026.<ref name="FirstFoes" /> Buckingham, Sinclair, Caramanga, and Noto returned as artist, colorist, letterer, and cover artist, respectively, for the additional one-shots.<ref name="FirstFoesReview" /><ref name="FirstFoesShallaBalCBR" /><ref name="FirstFoesDragonManReleaseDate" />{{update inline|date=August 2026|reason=To include Sinclair and Caramanga for Dragon Man.}} The first one-shot, titled ''[[Fantastic Four: First Foes]]'',<ref name="FirstFoes" /> was released on March 25, 2026.<ref name="FirstFoesShallaBal1" /> It was written by [[Dan Slott]], and explores an early adventure of the Fantastic Four facing off against [[René Rodin (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|René Rodin / Mad Thinker]].<ref name="FirstFoes" /><ref name="FirstFoesMadThinker" /> A two-page short story written by [[Ryan North]], "Fantastic Science", is also included, which is inspired by Reed's educational television programming as seen in the film.<ref name="FirstFoes" /> The second one-shot, ''[[Fantastic Four: First Foes – Shalla-Bal]]'', was released on July 8, 2026, and written by [[Charles Soule]].<ref name="FirstFoesShallaBal2" /> It focuses on how Shalla-Bal became Galactus's herald, the Silver Surfer, before the events of the film.<ref name="FirstFoesShallaBal1" /> A "Fantastic Science" short story written by North is also included.<ref name="FirstFoesShallaBalCBR" /> The third one-shot comic, titled ''[[Fantastic Four: First Foes – Dragon Man]]'', is scheduled for release on September 30, 2026, and written by [[Greg Pak]].<ref name="FirstFoesDragonManReleaseDate" /> It sees Grimm struggling with his rocky form as a potential cure for his condition emerges and involves the android [[Dragon Man (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dragon Man]].<ref name="FirstFoesDragonMan" />

== Future ==
A sequel to ''First Steps'' was reported to be in development in June 2025.<ref name="Sequel" /> In August, Sneider reported that Shakman was expected to return to direct the sequel, which Marvel Studios believed would perform better financially than ''First Steps'' once the Fantastic Four had been seen in ''Doomsday'' and ''Secret Wars''.<ref name="ShakmanSequelSneiderAug2025" /> However, Shakman was revealed in May 2026 to be directing a new ''[[Planet of the Apes]]'' film and Sneider believed that he was no longer attached to the ''First Steps'' sequel as a result.<ref name="ShakmanSequelSneiderMay2026" />{{third party inline|date=May 2026}}

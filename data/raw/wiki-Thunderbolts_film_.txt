'''''Thunderbolts*'''''{{hsp}}{{efn|The asterisk refers to the reveal that the [[Thunderbolts (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thunderbolts team]] become the [[New Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|New Avengers]]. The title is changed to '''''The New Avengers''''' during the end-credits and in some post-release marketing,<ref name="NewTitleEW" /> but ''Thunderbolts*'' remains the film's official title.<ref name="NewTitleDeadline" />}}<!-- DO NOT MODIFY THIS WORDING WITHOUT DISCUSSING ON THE TALK PAGE --> is a 2025 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the team [[Thunderbolts (comics)|Thunderbolts]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 36th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Jake Schreier]] from a screenplay by [[Eric Pearson]] and [[Joanna Calo]], and stars an [[ensemble cast]] featuring <!-- Per WP:MCUFILMCAST, order per main titles -->[[Florence Pugh]], [[Sebastian Stan]], [[Wyatt Russell]], [[Olga Kurylenko]], [[Lewis Pullman]], [[Geraldine Viswanathan]], [[Chris Bauer]], [[Wendell Pierce]], [[David Harbour]], [[Hannah John-Kamen]], and [[Julia Louis-Dreyfus]]. In the film, a [[Thunderbolts (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|group of antiheroes]] are caught in a trap and forced to work together on a mission that uncovers their traumatic pasts.

Marvel Studios first teased the formation of an MCU Thunderbolts team in 2021. The film was revealed to be in development in June 2022, when Schreier and Pearson were attached. The main cast was revealed in September, with additional casting through early 2023. [[Lee Sung Jin]] joined to rewrite the script by March 2023, one of several creatives who returned to work with Schreier from the [[Netflix]] series ''[[Beef (TV series)|Beef]]'' (2023–present). Production was delayed by the [[2023 Hollywood labor disputes]], causing some cast changes in early 2024. Calo joined by then for further rewrites. Filming took place from February to June 2024 at [[Trilith Studios]] and [[Atlanta Metro Studios]] in [[Atlanta]], Georgia, and on location in [[Utah]] and [[Kuala Lumpur]].

''Thunderbolts*'' premiered on April 22, 2025, at the [[Cineworld Leicester Square]] in London, England, and was released in the United States on May 2 as the final film of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU. The meaning of the title's asterisk was the subject of commentary during marketing and was explained by the reveal at the end of the film that the Thunderbolts team is rebranded as the "[[New Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|New Avengers]]"; the title is changed to '''''The New Avengers''''' during the end credits and in some post-release marketing. The film received positive reviews from critics—with praise for its approach to mental health and the performances of Pugh and Pullman—and several accolades. It underperformed at the box office, grossing $382.4&nbsp;million.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. -->
In [[Kuala Lumpur]], [[Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yelena Belova]] destroys a laboratory on behalf of [[CIA]] director [[Valentina Allegra de Fontaine (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valentina Allegra de Fontaine]], who is attempting to destroy any evidence of her involvement with the [[O.X.E. Group]]'s "Project Sentry". As de Fontaine faces imminent [[Federal impeachment in the United States|impeachment]] for her work with the O.X.E. Group, she dispatches Yelena, [[John Walker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|John Walker]], [[Ava Starr]], and [[Antonia Dreykov]]—all operatives who have worked for her—to a covert O.X.E. facility with orders to kill each other. In the ensuing fight, Ava kills Antonia while an amnesiac man named [[Sentry (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bob]] is released from a suspended animation pod in the room. After realizing that they were sent by de Fontaine to be incinerated along with any evidence of her misconduct, they work together to escape from the trap. Upon coming into physical contact with Bob, Yelena and Walker briefly experience visions of some of their worst memories.

De Fontaine learns that the group has survived and that Bob was a presumed-dead volunteer in the Sentry trials. When she arrives at the site, Bob creates a diversion by drawing enemy fire, allowing Yelena, Walker, and Ava to escape. Bob sustains no injuries upon being shot, uncontrollably ascends into the air, crash-lands back at the compound, and is captured and transported to the former [[Avengers Tower (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers Tower]] in [[Manhattan]], now renamed the "Watchtower". De Fontaine intends to introduce Bob to the press as a superpowered protector akin to the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]], hoping the PR stunt will avert her impeachment. Meanwhile, Yelena's father-figure [[Alexei Shostakov (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Alexei Shostakov]], who overheard details of de Fontaine's plot while working as a freelance chauffeur, rescues Yelena, Walker, and Ava. Walker refers to the group as the "[[Thunderbolts (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thunderbolts]]", inspired by Yelena's childhood soccer team.

Congressman [[Bucky Barnes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bucky Barnes]] is tipped off about what has happened by de Fontaine's assistant Mel. He captures the Thunderbolts and intends to have them testify in the impeachment proceedings. When Mel expresses concern about Bob, Barnes takes the group to New York City to infiltrate the Watchtower. The Thunderbolts discover that de Fontaine has convinced Bob to join her and become the superhero "Sentry". Bob easily overpowers the Thunderbolts, but he ignores de Fontaine's order to kill them and allows them to retreat, believing that they are not a threat to him. Developing a god-like delusion of superiority, Bob turns on de Fontaine and is incapacitated by Mel using a failsafe [[kill switch]]. This triggers the emergence of the [[Void (Marvel Cinematic Universe character)|Void]], Bob's destructive [[alter ego]] and the embodiment of his depression and insecurities which have been amplified by the Sentry procedure. The Void begins to engulf New York City in supernatural darkness, trapping its citizens in visions of their own traumatic memories.

Realizing that the only way to stop the Void is from within, Yelena enters the darkness to reach Bob's consciousness. She faces her haunted past as a [[Black Widows (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Black Widow]], and finds Bob hiding in a recreation of his childhood bedroom where he hid from his abusive father. The other Thunderbolts join them, and together they face off with Bob's various traumas and drug addiction until they reach his memory of the Sentry procedure in the Kuala Lumpur laboratory. They find the Void and are swiftly overpowered. Bob's attempts to fight back threaten to consume him completely, until the Thunderbolts hug Bob and affirm their belief in him, empowering him to regain control and overcome the Void. Light returns to the city and the victims are freed. With the threat of the Void neutralized, the Thunderbolts prepare to apprehend de Fontaine, but she manipulates public perception by staging a press conference in which she introduces the team as the [[New Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|New Avengers]] and takes credit for their actions. The group goes along with the announcement, but Yelena quietly tells de Fontaine "we own you now."

== Production ==
=== Development ===
During the production of the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) film ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014), director [[James Gunn]] expressed interest in making a film based on the [[Marvel Comics]] team [[Thunderbolts (comics)|Thunderbolts]],<ref name="GunnInterest2014" /> a group of "anti-heroes and super-criminals".<ref name="JohnKamenJun2018Interest" /> [[Marvel Studios]] president [[Kevin Feige]] said this was a possibility based on the success of ''Guardians of the Galaxy''.<ref name="GunnInterest2014" /> By May 2021, Gunn was no longer interested in the idea after directing the [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU) film ''[[The Suicide Squad (film)|The Suicide Squad]]'' (2021), because [[Suicide Squad|that team]] from [[DC Comics]] is based on a similar concept to the Thunderbolts.<ref name="GunnSuicideSquad" /> In June 2018, [[Hannah John-Kamen]] expressed enthusiasm for reprising her role as [[Ava Starr|Ava Starr / Ghost]] from the MCU film ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp]]'' (2018) in a potential Thunderbolts film, considering the comics version of [[Ghost (Marvel Comics)|Ghost]] is a member of the team.<ref name="JohnKamenJun2018Interest" />

Speculation that a Thunderbolts team would be introduced to the MCU began in mid-2019 following the announcement that [[Daniel Brühl]] would appear as [[Helmut Zemo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Helmut Zemo]] in the [[Disney+]] series ''[[The Falcon and the Winter Soldier]]'' (2021), reprising his role from the MCU film ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016).<ref name="BruhlThunderboltsJul2019" /><ref name="SchreierTHR" /> That series introduced [[Julia Louis-Dreyfus]] as [[Valentina Allegra de Fontaine (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valentina Allegra de Fontaine]] and shows her recruiting [[Wyatt Russell]]'s [[John Walker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|John Walker / U.S. Agent]]. De Fontaine is also shown to be working with [[Florence Pugh]]'s [[Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yelena Belova]] in the [[post-credits scene]] of the MCU film ''[[Black Widow (2021 film)|Black Widow]]'' (2021). Commentators speculated that she was recruiting a team of villains or [[antihero]]es like the Thunderbolts,<ref name="SchreierTHR" /><ref name="SchreierVariety" /> and some felt that team could appear in ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''. Executive producer [[Nate Moore (film producer)|Nate Moore]] said the Thunderbolts were never considered for the project because they would "cloud the story" and take away from other aspects of the series. [[Head writer]] [[Malcolm Spellman]] said there was "a lot of chatter" around the team's potential introduction to the MCU and added, "I don't know if fans are crazy or not".<ref name="FWSNoThunderbolts" />

[[File:Thunderbolts* concept art.png|thumb|upright=1.5|left|alt=Concept art featuring the film's characters in their superhero costumes, surrounded by smoke and bright lights|Concept art released at the [[D23 Expo]] in September 2022, depicting the main characters in costume: (left to right) Julia Louis-Dreyfus's Valentina Allegra de Fontaine, Hannah John-Kamen's Ava Starr / Ghost, David Harbour's Alexei Shostakov / Red Guardian, Florence Pugh's Yelena Belova, Sebastian Stan's Bucky Barnes / Winter Soldier, Wyatt Russell's John Walker / U.S. Agent, and Olga Kurylenko's Antonia Dreykov / Taskmaster]]
Following his work on ''Black Widow'', writer [[Eric Pearson]] brought the idea of a Thunderbolts film to Marvel Studios, realizing that Pugh had the potential to headline the film, given that Yelena was "a natural leader". He was drawn to the story potential of her leading the [[Thunderbolts (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thunderbolts]] to go up against de Fontaine.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> Because he planned to center the story on Yelena, Pearson decided to include her father [[Alexei Shostakov (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Alexei Shostakov / Red Guardian]] on the team, while also including Ghost because he had wanted to write the character.<ref name="PearsonIGNMay2025" /> [[Bucky Barnes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bucky Barnes / Winter Soldier]] was also added to the team after a few drafts.<ref name="PearsonComicBookMay2025" /> The roster for the team eventually became Yelena, Shostakov, Ghost, Barnes, Walker, and [[Antonia Dreykov|Antonia Dreykov / Taskmaster]], with the character [[Bob Reynolds (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bob Reynolds / Sentry]] added shortly after; this roster stayed consistent through subsequent drafts.<ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> To choose the team members, the creatives had looked through the roster of MCU characters to find "not just bad guys who could be good but characters that exist more on that morally grey plane or who were potentially destined for something else but then something went awry".<ref name="SchreierManThing" /> Pearson likened Yelena to [[Michael Corleone]] and Barnes to [[Tom Hagen]], characters from [[Mario Puzo]]'s novel ''[[The Godfather (novel)|The Godfather]]'' (1969).<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> In some of the initial drafts, Pearson included [[Bill Foster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bill Foster / Goliath]] to continue his relationship with Ghost from ''Ant-Man and the Wasp'', before joining the team by the end of the film. He was ultimately removed from the film, however, partly because Foster "didn't have the same background trauma that these other characters had, which was the unifying theme".<ref name="PearsonIGNMay2025" /><ref name="PearsonComicBookMay2025" />

By June 2022, [[Jake Schreier]] was attached to direct ''Thunderbolts'',<ref name="Schreier" /><ref name="PhaseFiveDirectorsConfirm" /> from a screenplay by Pearson, with Feige producing. Schreier was hired after a [[Pitch (filmmaking)|presentation]] that "blew away" Marvel Studios executives. At that time, the studio had been in contact with actors to discuss their availability to reprise their roles for the film.<ref name="Schreier" /> Schreier had considered including [[Man-Thing (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Man-Thing]] as a member of the team while pitching for the film,<ref name="SchreierManThing" /> who Pearson called "an unpredictable creature" explaining that they could not find a way to have his inclusion make sense for the story in part because the character was drawing visual and narrative attention away from Bob.<ref name="PearsonComicBookMay2025" /> Another core concept that stayed consistent from the early drafts was the members being sent to kill each other, which Schreier believed provided a "neat twist" for audiences expecting Marvel Studios' version of the Suicide Squad;<ref name="EWAsterisk" /> Pearson also believed it was important for ''Thunderbolts'' not to "rehash" the story beats of that team – an authority figure forcing criminals to work together – since audiences would have been familiar with that from the DCEU film ''[[Suicide Squad (2016 film)|Suicide Squad]]'' (2016) and its sequel ''The Suicide Squad''.<ref name="PearsonComicBookMay2025" /><ref name="PearsonIGNCutsMay2025" /> Thus it did not make sense for the film to include Zemo or [[Thaddeus Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thaddeus Ross]], the leaders who had formed the team in the comics,<ref name="PearsonIGNCutsMay2025" /> despite Schreier wanting to try to incorporate Zemo.<ref name="PearsonComicBookMay2025" /> Pearson said the two characters' inclusion was discussed to consider whether it made sense narratively, as it would have honored the comics, but they would not have featured if it was only for [[fan service]]. Zemo was included in an early [[post-credits scene]] idea where he would have been seen in prison "pulling the strings" similar to [[Keyser Söze]], the antagonist in the film ''[[The Usual Suspects]]'' (1995), but this was quickly moved on from.<ref name="PearsonIGNCutsMay2025" /> The film was officially announced in July at [[San Diego Comic-Con]] (SDCC), with a release date of July 26, 2024. It was set to be the last film in [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU.<ref name="SDCC2022" />

=== Pre-production ===
In September 2022, Justin Kroll of ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' described the film as a spin-off for Yelena because she was expected to lead the antihero team, with Pugh and Russell believed to be reprising their roles, along with the inclusion of Brühl.<ref name="KrollCharactersConcept" /> At the [[D23 Expo]] later that month, Pugh, Russell, John-Kamen, and Louis-Dreyfus were confirmed to star, alongside [[Sebastian Stan]] as Barnes, [[David Harbour]] as Red Guardian, and [[Olga Kurylenko]] as Taskmaster, all reprising their roles from previous MCU projects.<ref name="D23CastAnnouncement" /> Pugh was set to be paid eight figures for ''Thunderbolts'' and another MCU film.<ref name="PughPayOct2022" /> Prior to the cast announcement for the team, commentators had also suggested it could have included characters such as [[Emil Blonsky (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Emil Blonsky / Abomination]] or [[Clint Barton (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Clint Barton / Hawkeye]].<ref name="Schreier" /><ref name="SchreierTHR" /><ref name="SchreierVariety" /><ref name="SchreierEW" /> Schreier compared the character dynamics to those in the films ''[[Reservoir Dogs]]'' (1992), ''[[Ronin (film)|Ronin]]'' (1998), and ''[[Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol]]'' (2011), which are all about a "mis-matched team forced to work together". He also pointed to a less-likely inspiration in the film ''[[Toy Story 3]]'' (2010), saying the Thunderbolts members are all facing obsolescence like the ''[[Toy Story (franchise)|Toy Story]]'' [[List of Toy Story characters|characters]] are in that film.<ref name="SchreierInfluencesEmpire" /> Harbour said the film would be unique in the MCU, describing the main cast as "a bunch of misfits and outcasts and losers and people who don't really live up to the super in superhero".<ref name="HarbourSept2022" /> Pugh said the joy of uniting these characters was that they "don't play well together",<ref name="SDCC2024Inverse" /> while Russell expressed confidence that the film would be interesting, fun, and not a "straightforward" Marvel film.<ref name="RussellNov2023" /> Journalist [[Jeff Sneider]] reported that Pearson's script focused too much on the characters returning from ''Black Widow'', which he also wrote, and Marvel Studios was looking for the other characters to have a more equitable role so it would feel like an [[ensemble film]].<ref name="SneiderRewriteReason" /> Schreier did not believe the film was a ''Black Widow'' sequel but rather "a sampling of people from different parts" of the MCU, acknowledging the writers pulled from all of their histories, including Yelena and Shostakov's relationship that was established in ''Black Widow''.<ref name="SchreierNotBlackWidowSequel" /> One of Pearson's drafts started the film with a scene of Yelena confronting de Fontaine about sending her to kill Barton, furthering the storyline that was established in the post-credits scene of ''Black Widow'' and continued in the Disney+ series ''[[Hawkeye (miniseries)|Hawkeye]]'' (2021); Pearson said their confrontation helped emphasize de Fontaine's manipulation but ultimately was not relevant to the larger story being told.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" />

[[File:Jake Schreier Deauville 2012.jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Picture of director Jake Schreier at the [[Deauville American Film Festival]] in 2012|Director Jake Schreier brought back several crew members from his television series ''Beef'' to work on ''Thunderbolts*''; both projects explore mental health issues.]]
[[Ayo Edebiri]] joined the cast in January 2023 as de Fontaine's assistant [[Mel (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mel]],<ref name="Edebiri" /><ref name="CastAndCharacters" /><ref name="Viswanathan" /> when Louis-Dreyfus said filming would begin that June.<ref name="LouisDreyfusFilmingStart" /> In February, [[Steven Yeun]] was cast as Sentry with the potential to continue in future MCU films.<ref name="Yeun" /><ref name="YeunSentry" /><ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> Yeun worked with Schreier on the [[Netflix]] series ''[[Beef (TV series)|Beef]]'' (2023–present), and the director had the actor in mind when adding the character.<ref name="YeunTHRRoundtable" /> The next month, ''Beef'' creator [[Lee Sung Jin]] revealed that he was rewriting the script at Schreier's request and said there were "a lot of themes and exciting things" that drew him to the project. He worked closely with Schreier on the script and noted that, unlike ''Beef'', ''Thunderbolts'' was Schreier's project and had different writing needs than the series, given the film's large scope and scale.<ref name="LeeSungJinWriting" /> With the team largely consistent, Schreier worked in subsequent drafts to "go deeper" with the characters "on the margins" such as Mel and Congressman Gary and "[tying] their [[Character arc|arcs]] together into something that would feel resonant".<ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" />

At the start of May 2023, filming was delayed due to the [[2023 Writers Guild of America strike]] and was planned to resume after the strike concluded.<ref name="WGAStrikeDelay" /> In June, the release date was pushed back to December 20, 2024,<ref name="Dec2024Release" /> and the [[Utah Film Commission]] announced that ''Thunderbolts'' would film in [[Emery County]] and [[Grand County, Utah]], spending over $4.5&nbsp;million in the state. Chapek said the "grounded, globe-trotting film" would use several practical filming locations and the Utah locations were chosen because they felt "untouched by the larger world".<ref name="UtahFilming" /> Sanja Milkovic Hays was the costume designer after working on the MCU films ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019) and ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'' (2021).<ref name="Hays" /> When the [[2023 SAG-AFTRA strike]] ended in November, the release date was pushed back again, this time to July 25, 2025.<ref name="Jul2025Release" /> Upon returning from the strike, Schreier and the creatives were able to reexamine the script. They determined that there was no sense of "tension and unpredictability", and the film felt "bloodless" when there should be the tension of not knowing who would survive, given who these characters are. To solve this, they decided to rework Taskmaster's involvement in the film, killing the character "early and dry" in the film to have her death "resonate" and inform the fates of the other characters throughout the rest of the film. Having the death occur later in the film was also discussed, but Schreier said the sadness from it would "hang over" the rest of the film and distract from the intended storyline of forming a strong bond between Yelena and Bob.<ref name="SchreierPhaseHero" />{{rp|0:55–3:55}}<ref name="SchreierEWTaskmasterDeath" /> In Pearson's original draft, Taskmaster survived until the end and had a sub-plot where she bonded with Ghost, as well as a recurring joke where she kept forgetting she had made friends with the other characters and was still trying to assassinate Walker because of her memory loss.<ref name="PearsonPolygonMay2025" /> Pearson understood why the decision was made to kill Taskmaster,<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> also believing the memory loss assassination joke drifted too close to Bob's arc as he also was dealing with memory loss.<ref name="PearsonPolygonMay2025" /> Additionally, his post-credit scene featuring [[Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kang the Conqueror]] was abandoned because Marvel Studios had decided to move away from that storyline.<ref name="PearsonIGNCutsMay2025" />

Following the strike-caused production delays, filming was slated to begin around March or April 2024.<ref name="RussellFilmingStart" /><ref name="StanFilmingStart" /> Yeun exited in January 2024 due to a scheduling issue caused by the delayed production,<ref name="YeunExit" /><ref name="YeunExitResponse" /> but expressed interest in working on a future MCU film.<ref name="YeunExitResponse" /> It was unclear if Marvel would recast or rethink Yeun's role,<ref name="YeunExitVariety" /> but at the end of the month [[Lewis Pullman]] was revealed to be the studios' top choice to replace Yeun as Sentry,<ref name="Pullman" /><ref name="Pullman2" /><ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> and his casting was soon confirmed.<ref name="Viswanathan" /> [[Geraldine Viswanathan]] was cast to replace Edebiri as Mel after she exited due to the delays.<ref name="Viswanathan" /><ref name="CastAndCharacters" /> In February, the release date was moved forward to May 2, 2025, swapping places with the MCU film ''[[The Fantastic Four: First Steps]]''.<ref name="May2025Release" /> By the end of the month, [[Joanna Calo]] had joined to serve as the writer during production and was rewriting the script.<ref name="Calo" /><ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> For the film's final writing credits<!-- set in March 2025-->, Pearson was credited for the story and received a screenplay credit alongside Calo, while additional off-screen literary material was attributed to Lee.<ref name="WGACredits" />

The script explores mental health, specifically loneliness and depression, particularly with Bob / Sentry.<ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> Pearson always envisioned ending the film with the emotional moment of giving someone a hug rather than fighting them.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> Before Sentry was added to the film, Walker was intended to be the main antagonist, with de Fontaine manipulating him into believing his [[Super Soldier Serum (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|super soldier serum]] was wearing off and giving him pills as a solution, making him into a "time bomb" that made him more unlikable and "the monster".<ref name="PearsonSRMay2025" /><ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> Pearson said this version "didn't ever totally work",<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> and Walker's actions felt too similar to the plot line of Thaddeus Ross becoming the [[Red Hulk (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Red Hulk]] in the MCU film ''[[Captain America: Brave New World]]'' (2025);<ref name="PearsonIGNMay2025" /> Pearson had considered Red Hulk for the film's villain early in the process, which Marvel Studios declined because of their intended plans for ''Brave New World''.<ref name="PearsonComicBookMay2025" /> Looking for a new antagonist that would not easily be beatable in a fight, Pearson remembered reading the ''Sentry'' miniseries years prior and felt Sentry and his [[alter ego]] the [[Void (Marvel Cinematic Universe character)|Void]] would work for "heroic ambition and self-esteem versus depression and self-loathing and loneliness and isolation";<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> he called Sentry "the entire journey for our heroes rolled into one entity" and his inclusion helped make the story cohesive.<ref name="PearsonSRMay2025" /><ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> Pearson was glad he was able to incorporate many elements of the character's comics backstory to fit into the MCU.<ref name="PearsonSRMay2025" /> Said backstory, consisting of Sentry erasing himself from the world's memories to stop the Void, was avoided because it was similar to the plot of ''No Way Home'', in which [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]] also has himself erased from memory.<ref name="NoWayHomeBackstoryPrevention" /> [[Paul Jenkins (writer)|Paul Jenkins]], co-creator of Sentry, discussed the character with the creatives, with Schreier calling Sentry and the work Jenkins did with the character "a parable for mental health".<ref name="SchreierVarietyMay2025" /> Schreier explained that Lee felt mental health discussion was "not niche anymore" and it allowed them to "tell a story about that internality and still have a lot of comedy and action for something that feels big and universal".<ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" />

The ''Thunderbolts*'' team believed the film was a good opportunity to further explore ideas they had started to explore in ''Beef'' on a larger scale. Executive producer [[Brian Chapek]] came up with the idea that, to defeat Sentry, the Thunderbolts would have to enter the Void, which manifested memories of their greatest shame, dubbed "shame rooms". Lee worked closely with production designer Grace Yun, who also served that role on ''Beef'', to create the initial specifics of each room.<ref name="SchreierVarietyMay2025" /> Pearson had originally envisioned them as "ethereal and dreamy" but believed making them mazes akin to those seen in the film ''[[Being John Malkovich]]'' (1999) helped with visualizing the concept and made them feel "more unsettling".<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> Calo further enhanced the shame rooms, suggesting that when Yelena enters to Void to help Bob that she return to her initial room seen earlier in the film. Schreier explained that this made a "callback of the idea that the greatest shame of all was thinking that you could be something bigger than yourself, that [Bob's] aspirations towards heroism are actually what brings everything down and that you need to learn to be ok with who you are without that". Shame rooms for Yelena, Walker, and de Fontaine are visible, with rooms for the other characters conceived for the film's finale, allowing the team to escape through them. However, Calo felt strongly that to journey to the heart of the Void, there needed to be a "[[Big Bad]] moment" beforehand, and the team should journey through Bob's shame rooms. The other shame rooms included: Shostakov being thrown in the [[gulag]]; Ghost growing up in an orphanage with no one wanting to be around her; and multiple different ones for Bucky that were "a little less than the expected idea", including a "shameful moment" in the [[Scouting America|Boy Scouts]].<ref name="SchreierVarietyMay2025" />

Schreier had suggested during his pitch meeting that one of the film's posters or trailers should use an asterisk in the title to imply they needed to "come up with something better"; Feige, Marvel Studios co-president [[Louis D'Esposito]], and the marketing team loved this idea and decided to fully embrace it for the title and marketing campaign;<ref name="SchreierPhaseHero" />{{rp|4:26–5:15}}<ref name="EWAsterisk" /> with the film formally retitled to ''Thunderbolts*'' by March 2024.<ref name="PughSetVideoTHR" /><ref name="TitleAsteriskConfirm" /> This allowed the creatives to realize that having the asterisk could lead to the reveal of a new title for the film once it was released,<ref name="SchreierPhaseHero" />{{rp|4:26–5:15}} which was subsequently done, changing the title to ''The New Avengers'' after the ending reveals the Thunderbolts to be the [[New Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|New Avengers]].<ref name="NewTitleEW" /><ref name="ThunderboltsEnding" /> Schreier said the ending was always going to have the Thunderbolts revealed as the New Avengers team, and the rest of the film became about "building the right story that lived up to that".<ref name="SchreierPostCreditsScene" /> Pearson explained that Feige suggesting de Fontaine should introduce the Thunderbolts to the public as the Avengers; additional discussions were had on what this meant, such as if the "n" in "new" should be upper or lowercase, and what the implications of each decision would mean.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> ''Thunderbolts*'' included "little hints" throughout so the ending did not come as a total surprise – such as the [[Battle of New York (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Battle of New York]] gala, [[Avengers Tower (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers Tower]] becoming the Watchtower, and Mel explaining how she was in high school when the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]] first formed. Additionally, the credits and the post-credit sequences acknowledge that the New Avengers is "not necessarily the most comfortable or perfect fit" for these characters.<ref name="EWAsterisk" /> The original Thunderbolts name was derived from Yelena's youth soccer team.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" />

=== Filming ===
[[File:Merdeka Tower 20241022 - full 2 (Cropped).jpg|thumb|upright=0.7|left|alt=Picture of the Merdeka 118 skyscraper in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, in 2024|Star Florence Pugh jumped off of the [[Merdeka 118]] skyscraper for the film's opening sequence.]]
Filming was originally scheduled to begin in mid-June 2023 and last for six months.<ref name="WGAStrikeDelay" /><ref name="LouisDreyfusFilmingStart" /><ref name="PughFilmingTime" /> It was initially not expected to be impacted by the writers' strike, with Marvel Studios reportedly planning to shoot what they could during principal photography and make any necessary writing adjustments during already scheduled reshoots.<ref name="WGAStrikeImpact" /> Filming was ultimately delayed by the writers' strike and subsequent SAG-AFTRA strike.<ref name="WGAStrikeDelay" /><ref name="Jul2025Release" /> The original filming schedule overlapped with Stan's work on the film ''[[The Apprentice (2024 film)|The Apprentice]]'' (2024), for which he gained weight to portray [[Donald Trump]]. He had begun the physical transformation needed to portray Barnes when the strikes began and he had to reverse course.<ref name="StanOct2024" /> Harbour planned to shoot the film concurrently with his scenes as [[Jim Hopper (Stranger Things)|Jim Hopper]] for the [[Stranger Things season 5|fifth season]] of ''[[Stranger Things]]'' (2025), also in Atlanta;<ref name="HarbourFilming" /> that production was also delayed by the strikes.<ref name="StrangerThingsStrikeDelay" />

[[Principal photography]] began by February 26, 2024,<ref name="PughFilmingStart" /> at [[Trilith Studios]] and [[Atlanta Metro Studios]] in Atlanta, Georgia,<ref name="TrilithStudios" /><ref name="MetroStudios" /> under the [[working title]] ''Oops All Berries'', which is a reference to a variation of the cereal [[Cap'n Crunch]] that only has berry-flavored pieces.<ref name="UtahFilming" /><ref name="MetroStudios" /> [[Andrew Droz Palermo]] was the cinematographer,<ref name="A24TrailerVariety" /><ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> having previously worked on the Marvel Studios series ''[[Moon Knight (miniseries)|Moon Knight]]'' (2022).<ref name="Palermo" /> He replaced [[Steve Yedlin]] who was originally intended to work on the film.<ref name="Yedlin2" /><ref name="Yedlin" /> The film was shot for [[IMAX]].<ref name="IMAXFilming" /> Filming occurred in Emery County and Grand County, Utah, from May 29 to June 12, 2024.<ref name="UtahFilmingSchedule" /> Louis-Dreyfus completed her scenes by early June,<ref name="DreyfusWrapped" /><ref name="DreyfusWrapped2" /> and said Marvel Studios was trying to "go back to their roots" with a focus on the characters' humanity, practical stunt sequences, and avoiding visual effects where possible.<ref name="DreyfusNYTimes" /> Furthermore, Schreier intended for the film's fight scenes to look as realistic as possible, though a notable exception is the fight between the Thunderbolts and Sentry at the penthouse of the former [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers Tower]], now referred to as the "Watchtower", as both Schreier and the stunt team wanted to use this scene to emphasize the magnitude of Sentry's powers—something that required weeks of rehearsal and careful choreography by Pullman and stunt double Alec Back to be conveyed. Pullman stated that the fight "was a big [scene] because that really is where you get to see Sentry. It's a small window of [[real estate]] where you get to really demonstrate what Sentry is capable of."<ref name="SentryPenthouseFight" />

Filming also took place at the [[Medan Pasar]] square and [[Merdeka 118]] skyscraper in [[Kuala Lumpur]], Malaysia.<ref name="MalaysiaFilming" /><ref name="MalaysiaFilming2" /> Pugh was insistent on filming the scene where Yelena jumps off the top of Merdeka 118 without the use of stunt doubles. Marvel Studios was initially reluctant to allow Pugh to perform the stunt for insurance reasons, but after continually pushing the matter, including to Feige, she was allowed to perform the jump.<ref name="PughMerdekaJump" /> For Yelena's trip through the shame rooms to save Bob, Palermo used a "handheld, hand-made aesthetic" similar to [[indie films]] such as ''Being John Malkovich'', ''[[Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind]]'' (2004), and those produced by [[A24]] such as ''[[Everything Everywhere All at Once]]'' (2022). Feige championed doing as many of the effects practically and in camera as possible and Schreier noted the creatives liked the idea of doing a "practical rendition of what getting stuck in a thought loop or a shame room would be".<ref name="SchreierVarietyMay2025" /> Filming concluded the week of June 19.<ref name="CineEurope" />

=== Post-production ===
In September 2024, [[Chris Bauer]] and [[Wendell Pierce]] were revealed to have been cast in the film,<ref name="PierceBauer" /> with Bauer playing Holt and Pierce playing Congressman Gary.<ref name="CastAndCharacters" /><ref name="PierceGary" /> The film's teaser trailer released that month revealed that [[Scarlett Johansson]], who portrayed [[Natasha Romanoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Natasha Romanoff / Black Widow]] in the MCU from 2010 to 2021, was credited as an executive producer.<ref name="TeaserTrailer" /> However, Johansson asked for her credit to be removed from the film because she had no involvement with it.<ref name="JohanssonNoEPCredit" /> In early December, [[additional photography]] took place in Atlanta,<ref name="Reshoots" /> including to shoot the "Meth Chicken" shame room sequence from Bob's memories.<ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> [[Harry Yoon]] and [[Angela Catanzaro]] edited the film; Yoon previously worked on the MCU film ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]'' (2021) and with Schreier on ''Beef''.<ref name="SchreierTHRMay2025" /> Jake Morrison is the visual effects supervisor, with visual effects provided by [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM), [[Framestore]], [[Digital Domain]], [[Rising Sun Pictures]] (RSP), Raynault VFX, [[Base FX]], [[Crafty Apes]], and Mammal Studios.<ref name="ArtOfVFX" />

''Thunderbolts*'' includes a [[post-credits scene]] set 14 months after the end of the film where the New Avengers, discussing a problem in outer space, are notified of an extradimensional spacecraft entering [[Earth-616 (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Earth-616]], which is revealed to be the [[Fantastic Four (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Fantastic Four]]'s.<ref name="ThunderboltsEnding" /><ref name="EWPostCredits" /> Schreier said this scene was filmed approximately a month before the release of ''Thunderbolts*'', on the set of the MCU [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]] film ''[[Avengers: Doomsday]]'' (2026) by its directors, the [[Russo brothers]], and was intended for that film; Schreier was on set when the post-credits scene was filmed.<ref name="SchreierPostCreditsScene" /><ref name="EWPostCredits" /><ref name="SchreierVarietyMay2025" /> Schreier helped conceive the scene while noting the characters were being given over to a "whole new world and new scope" to function in, and enjoyed seeing them "directed in another context and on a different level of scope" than had been done for this film.<ref name="SchreierVarietyMay2025" /> Pearson believed Calo had written the post-credits scene.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> It originally included a sequence that was cut where Barnes and Walker had a "[[Who's on First?]]"–style exchange about the ship having the number four on it. Stan called the bit "genius" and was sad it had been cut, with Russell saying the exchange became "a [[Leslie Nielsen]] sketch".<ref name="BarnesWalkerPostCreditsCut" />

== Marketing ==

After filming had begun in March 2024, Pugh and Marvel Studios released a set video in which she showcased her new [[Black Widows (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Black Widow]] costume and an updated logo for the film with Schreier. The new logo styled the title as "''Thunderbolts*''{{thin space}}".<ref name="PughSetVideoTheWrap" /><ref name="PughSetVideoGizmodo" /> Kristen Lopez at ''[[TheWrap]]'' felt viewers would "start deconstructing the brief images she's shown immediately",<ref name="PughSetVideoTheWrap" /> while Sabina Graves of ''[[Gizmodo]]'' felt the video was delightful and a fun tease for a scene featuring Yelena. She highlighted Pugh's short hair and her joking with Schreier that they "really shouldn't be showing any of this".<ref name="PughSetVideoGizmodo" /> ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'s}} Chris McPherson also discussed Pugh's costume, calling it "very military [and] quite tactical", and noted her teal eyeliner.<ref name="PughSetVideoCollider" /> Several commentators questioned why there was an asterisk in the updated logo.<ref name="PughSetVideoTHR" /> The next month, Feige confirmed that ''Thunderbolts*'' was the official title and said the use of the asterisk would be explained following the film's release,<ref name="TitleAsteriskConfirm" /> which led to continued speculation as to its meaning.<ref name="TitleAsteriskIGN" /><ref name="TitleAsteriskCinemaBlend" /> One theory suggested the team would not be called the Thunderbolts in the film, but rather a variation of the Avengers name such as the [[Dark Avengers]] or [[New Avengers]].<ref name="PosterAsteriskGizmodo" /><ref name="PosterAsteriskIGN" /> Another suggestion stated there was another Thunderbolts team in the MCU with characters more closely aligned to the rosters from the comics.<ref name="PosterAsteriskGizmodo" />

A behind-the-scenes look was shown at [[CineEurope]] in June 2024,<ref name="CineEurope" /> while the first footage from the film was shown at SDCC the next month, presented by Schreier and the cast. Harbour dressed as Red Guardian for the panel.<ref name="SDCC2024" /><ref name="SDCC2024Marvel" /> Following online leaks of the SDCC footage, Marvel released an official look at the Thunderbolts team within their video celebrating the company's 85th anniversary in August.<ref name="85thOfficialLook" /> A teaser trailer for the film was released in a month later,<ref name="TeaserTrailer" /> featuring the song "[[Where Is My Mind?]]" by the [[Pixies (band)|Pixies]].<ref name="Pixies" /> Tom Power at ''[[TechRadar]]'' said the teaser was a longer and higher quality version of the previously leaked footage, and felt the film was "looking very good". He highlighted the characters' struggles with identity as well as the action and tone of the teaser.<ref name="TeaserTrailerTechRadar" /> Writing for ''[[The A.V. Club]]'', Mary Kate Carr noted the various mysteries surrounding the film—including the asterisk in its title—and said the teaser "valiantly" refused to give up any secrets or plot details.<ref name="TeaserTrailerAVClub" /> A second trailer was shown during [[Super Bowl LIX]] in February 2025.<ref name="SuperBowlTrailer" /> Commentators stated that the trailer appeared to confirm speculation that the titular team would be fighting the Void, the malevolent alter-ego of Bob / Sentry.<ref name="SuperBowlTrailerEmpire" /><ref name="SuperBowlTrailerPolygon" /> In the trailer, Red Guardian expresses hope that the team's achievements would get them featured on the cover of [[Wheaties]], a cereal known for putting sports figures on its boxes and the slogan "The Breakfast of Champions". Alongside the trailer, Marvel Studios released a poster for the film that mimicked a Wheaties box featuring the team. Their social media posts for the poster were captioned "The Breakfast of <s>Champions</s> Thunderbolts*".<ref name="WheatiesPosterCBR" /><ref name="WheatiesPosterComicBook" /> An asterisk-themed international poster was also released, which included a footnote for the title asterisk which says "The Avengers Are Not Available".<ref name="PosterAsterisk/Film" /> Kieran Fisher at ''[[/Film]]'' questioned whether this was a joke, or if it was the true meaning behind the asterisk in the film's title.<ref name="PosterAsterisk/Film" /> James Whitbrook of ''[[Gizmodo]]'' and Scott Collura from ''[[IGN]]'' both believed the footnote fit with the film's marketing approach of presenting the team as a ragtag humorous group of heroes,<ref name="PosterAsteriskGizmodo" /><ref name="PosterAsteriskIGN" /> rather than the asterisk being an "actual serious mystery".<ref name="PosterAsteriskGizmodo" />

In March 2025, [[Letterboxd]] debuted a trailer for the film that is edited in the style of those for [[independent film]]s produced by [[A24]]. It features techno music, an "A24-esque makeover" for the Marvel Studios logo, and title cards that highlight past A24 projects that the film's cast and crew members worked on. Schreier helped conceive the trailer, which was released around the same time Pugh described the film as an "indie, A24-feeling assassin movie with Marvel superheroes".<ref name="A24TrailerVariety" /> Zosha Millman from ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]'' said Marvel had "film buffs in mind" when releasing the trailer through Letterboxd. Despite the creative pedigree touted by the trailer, she thought ''Thunderbolts*'' would still be "largely what you expect" of a Marvel film.<ref name="A24TrailerPolygon" /> A24 was not aware of the trailer before its release and responded on social media with a meme from its [[HBO]] drama series ''[[Euphoria (American TV series)|Euphoria]]'' (2019–2026).<ref name="A24TrailerVariety" /> Later in March, Marvel Studios announced that many MCU cast members would be reprising their roles for ''Doomsday'', including Stan, Russell, Pugh, Pullman, Harbour, and John-Kamen.<ref name="DoomsdayCastAnnouncementTHR" /> Commentators discussed whether this announcement, which seemingly revealed characters who survive the film's events and aligned with speculation that Taskmaster would die early in the story due to limited presence in trailers, was a [[Spoiler (media)|spoiler]].<ref name="DoomsdayCastSpoilerCB" /><ref name="DoomsdayCastSpoilerInverse" /> Michael Walsh at ''[[Nerdist]]'' criticized the announcement, feeling it had undermined one of the key reasons some people were interested in ''Thunderbolts*'', which was the feeling that any of the main cast could die. He compared this to spoiling who survives the events of the film ''[[The Dirty Dozen]]'' (1967), and said Marvel should have excluded the ''Thunderbolts*'' cast members from the ''Doomsday'' announcement.<ref name="DoomsdayCastSpoilerNerdist" /> Amelia Emberwing of ''[[IGN]]'' said "knowing who makes it out alive [does not matter] much in a comic book franchise" and also felt the announcement was not necessarily a spoiler because the returning actors could be playing versions of their characters from other universes within the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]].<ref name="DoomsdayCastSpoilerIGN" />

Various fan screenings occurred on April 22, 2025, in 10 major cities across the U.S.<ref name="FanScreeningPremieres" /> At the end of April, Marvel announced a collaboration with Wheaties to create a limited edition box of the cereal, mimicking the Wheaties-inspired poster for the film.<ref name="WheatiesBox" /> The film's marketing campaign was valued between $75–100&nbsp;million,<ref name="NewTitleDeadline" /><ref name="VarietyBOAnalysis" /> and featured partnerships with [[Harley-Davidson]], [[Tide (brand)|Tide]], Wheaties, [[Mike and Ike]], [[Cupra Kiro]] with [[Formula E]] racing, [[LinkedIn]], Perfect Sports, and [[Arizona Beverage Company]].<ref name="NewTitleDeadline" />

[[File:Marvel Studios' The New Avengers logo.png|thumb|upright=1.2|left|''The New Avengers'' logo]]
Following the film's opening weekend, Marvel Studios rebranded the film as ''The New Avengers''.<ref name="PearsonTHRMay2025" /> The studio released a video of the cast tearing away the ''Thunderbolts*'' logo from the poster to reveal ''The New Avengers'', billboards and theater marquees were also updated to ''The New Avengers'',<ref name="NewTitleVariety" /> and new posters were released.<ref name="NewTitleDeadline" /> Schreier explained that revealing and embracing the updated title felt like it was part of the studio's plan and had been built up to since the asterisk was first included in the film's title.<ref name="NewTitleVariety" /> Disney clarified that the film was not formally retitled, just that the asterisk was "spelled out",<ref name="NewTitleDeadline" /> and ticketing services still listed the film as ''Thunderbolts*'', despite featuring the updated posters with the ''New Avengers'' name. Sydney Bucksbaum of ''Entertainment Weekly'' felt this was the first instance a film "planned in advance to change its title a few days after its release for narrative purposes".<ref name="NewTitleEW" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Thunderbolts*'' grossed $190.3&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $192.2&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $382.4&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /> It is one of the lowest grossing MCU films. Rebecca Rubin at ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' reported that the film needed to earn an "unachievable" $425&nbsp;million worldwide to [[break-even]], based on "slightly cheaper" than usual costs for an MCU film: the production budget was $180&nbsp;million and the marketing budget was $100&nbsp;million.<ref name="VarietyBOAnalysis" /> Rubin and Brent Lang said ''Thunderbolts*'' and the other two 2025 MCU films, ''Brave New World'' and ''First Steps'', were all "commercial duds" and part of a "lousy streak" for Marvel Studios.<ref name="Variety2025BOScorecard" /> Feige attributed the low box office performance to audiences believing they needed to watch the MCU's Disney+ series in order to understand the film; he felt the studio should have better explained that this was not necessary.<ref name="FeigeConferenceJul2025THR" />

In mid-April 2025, ''Thunderbolts*'' was projected to have a $63–77&nbsp;million opening weekend in the United States, with the "target number" expected to be $70&nbsp;million.<ref name="AprBOTracking" /> By the end of the month, the film was projected to earn $70–75&nbsp;million domestically and $160–175&nbsp;million globally on its opening weekend,<ref name="AprBOTracking2" /> with some domestic projections going as high as $80–90&nbsp;million.<ref name="AprBOTracking3" /> The film earned $31.5&nbsp;million domestically in its opening day, including $11.5&nbsp;million from Thursday night previews. The film would gross $74.3&nbsp;million in its domestic opening weekend, while also earning $86.1&nbsp;million internationally for a total of $160.1&nbsp;million, including $20&nbsp;million from IMAX screenings.<ref name="NewTitleDeadline" /><ref name="OpeningBOTHR" /><ref name="OpeningBODeadline" /><ref name="BOMWknd1" /> In its second weekend the film made $33.1&nbsp;million, remaining atop the box office. The 55% drop was noted as good for an MCU film, compared to the 68% second weekend decrease by ''Brave New World'' earlier that year.<ref name="2ndWkndBO" /> It made $17&nbsp;million on its third weekend, a drop of 52%, and it dropped to second behind the new release of ''[[Final Destination Bloodlines]]''.<ref name="3rdWkdBO" />

=== Critical response ===
{{expand section|date=April 2026}}
[[File:Actress Florence Pugh - Thunderbolts - L.A. Premiere (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Picture of actress Florence Pugh promoting the film in April 2025|Florence Pugh received praise and accolades for her performance in the film.]]
<!-- Local consensus for MCU articles is to NOT use aggregate templates! Do NOT convert the prose here to them! -->
On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 88% of 381 critics gave ''Thunderbolts*'' a positive review, with an average rating of 7.1/10. The critics consensus reads, "Assembling a ragtag band of underdogs with Florence Pugh as their magnetic standout, ''Thunderbolts*'' refreshingly goes back to the tried-and-true blueprint of the MCU's best adventures."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=thunderbolts |type=m |title=Thunderbolts* |access-date=June 6, 2026}}{{cbignore}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 68 out of 100 based on 53 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=thunderbolts |type=movie |title=Thunderbolts* |access-date=March 2, 2026}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Critics called it a return to form for the MCU, highlighting the character development, visuals, action sequences, and performances. The handling of [[mental health issues]] and the performances of Pugh and Pullman were especially praised.<ref name="RTReviewRoundup" /> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A–" on an A+ to F scale, while those surveyed by [[PostTrak]] gave it an average of 4.5 out of 5 stars, with 74% saying they would definitely recommend the film.<ref name="OpeningBOTHR" /><ref name="OpeningBODeadline" />

Writing for [[NPR]], critic [[Bob Mondello]] gave ''Thunderbolts*'' a generally positive review, calling the film "a decently effective blend of misfit mercenaries and pop psychology".<ref name="NPR-Review" /> Jake Coyle of the [[Associated Press]] gave it 3/4 stars, writing, "All the assembled parts here, including an especially high-quality cast (even Wendell Pierce!) work together seamlessly in a way that Marvel hasn't in some time. Most of all, Pugh commands every bit of the movie."<ref name="APReview" /> [[Robbie Collin]] of ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' gave the film 2/5 stars, writing, "It's just dollop upon dollop of dourness, all the way to an end-credits scene that has the nerve to tease brighter, less narratively convoluted times ahead. Talk about creating a market."<ref name="DailyTelegraphReview" /> Nicholas Barber of [[BBC Culture]] gave ''Thunderbolts*'' a generally positive review, praising its character-driven approach and Pugh's performance. He noted that the film offers a more grounded and emotionally resonant narrative compared to recent Marvel releases, while still delivering the expected action and humor.<ref name="BBCReview" />

'''''The Marvels''''' is a 2023 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is the sequel to the film ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019), a continuation of the television miniseries ''[[Ms. Marvel (miniseries)|Ms. Marvel]]'' (2022), and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 33rd film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Nia DaCosta]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Megan McDonnell]] and Elissa Karasik. It stars [[Brie Larson]] as [[Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Carol Danvers / Captain Marvel]], [[Teyonah Parris]] as [[Monica Rambeau]], and [[Iman Vellani]] as [[Kamala Khan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kamala Khan / Ms. Marvel]], alongside [[Zawe Ashton]], [[Gary Lewis (actor)|Gary Lewis]], [[Park Seo-joon]], [[Zenobia Shroff]], [[Mohan Kapur]], [[Saagar Shaikh]], and [[Samuel L. Jackson]]. In the film, Danvers, Rambeau, and Kamala team up as "the Marvels" after they begin swapping places with each other every time they use their powers.

Marvel Studios confirmed plans to make a sequel to ''Captain Marvel'' in July 2019. Development began in January 2020 with McDonnell hired after working on the television miniseries ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021). Larson was set to return from the first film as Danvers, and DaCosta was hired to direct that August. In December, Parris was revealed to be reprising her role as Rambeau from ''WandaVision'' alongside Vellani returning as Kamala from ''Ms. Marvel''. [[Second unit]] filming began in mid-April 2021 in [[New Jersey]], and the title—referring to the three characters and their similar abilities—was revealed in early May. [[Principal photography]] began in July 2021 and concluded by mid-May 2022, taking place at [[Pinewood Studios]] in [[Buckinghamshire]] and [[Longcross Studios]] in [[Surrey]], England, as well as in Los Angeles and [[Tropea]], Italy. Karasik's involvement was revealed during post-production.

''The Marvels'' premiered in Las Vegas on November 7, 2023, and was released in the United States on November 10 as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU. It received mixed reviews from critics, with praise for its performances and action sequences but criticism for its script and tonal inconsistencies. The film <!-- Do NOT change. == Plot ==
[[Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Carol Danvers]] destroys the [[Supreme Intelligence (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Supreme Intelligence]], the [[artificial intelligence]] that leads the [[Kree (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kree]] empire.{{Efn|This is soon after the events of ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019)<ref name="ComplexEasterEggs" />}} This leads to a civil war on planet [[Hala (Marvel Comics location)|Hala]], the Kree home world, rendering the planet barren as it loses its air, water, and sunlight over the next 30 years. The Kree come to know Danvers as "the Annihilator".

The Kree's new leader [[Dar-Benn (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dar-Benn]] discovers one of the two legendary Quantum Bands, which were previously used to create [[Universal Neural Teleportation Network|a network]] that allows fast travel across space. She uses the Band to forcibly open a new jump point that connects to the network. The resulting anomaly impacts the entire network, including a jump point near Earth's [[S.A.B.E.R.]] space station run by [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]]. Captain [[Monica Rambeau (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Monica Rambeau]] investigates the jump point near S.A.B.E.R. while Danvers investigates the new one that Dar-Benn opened. When they touch their respective jump points, Rambeau is transported to Danvers's location, [[Kamala Khan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kamala Khan]]—who has the other Quantum Band on Earth—is transported to Rambeau's location, and Danvers is transported to Kamala's house. The three use their different light-based powers to fight off Kree enemies, leaving Kamala's home destroyed.

After the trio return to their original places, Fury and Rambeau visit Kamala on Earth. Rambeau surmises that their light-based powers are linked through [[quantum entanglement]] and that they switch places when any of the three use their powers simultaneously. They join up at a [[Skrulls (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Skrull]] refugee colony on planet Tarnax, which Danvers helped found and where a peace treaty is being negotiated with the Kree. When talks break down, Dar-Benn rips open another jump point which siphons the atmosphere from Tarnax to restore breathable air to Hala. After a hasty effort to evacuate the colony, Danvers, Rambeau, and Kamala form a team that Kamala names "the Marvels". Danvers explains that Dar-Benn's repeated rupturing of jump points is causing instability in the network and endangering the entire universe. They deduce that Dar-Benn is targeting planets that are meaningful to Danvers, whom Dar-Benn blames for the desolation of Hala.

The Marvels travel to the [[Ocean world|water planet]] Aladna—where people communicate in song—and warn Prince Yan, Danvers's husband through a diplomatic act. Dar-Benn arrives and tears open a jump point, drawing the planet's water to Hala. Her final plan is to use the energy from Earth's sun to restore that of Hala's sun. On S.A.B.E.R., [[Goose (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Goose]]—Danvers's cat-like pet [[Flerken]]—gives birth to a litter of kittens that can temporarily consume people, which Fury uses to evacuate the station's personnel. Dar-Benn steals Kamala's Band and attempts to use both Bands, but this destroys Dar-Benn, ends the Marvels' entanglement, and leaves behind a rupture between realities. Kamala reclaims the Bands and joins Danvers to energize Rambeau, allowing her to close the rupture but stranding her on the other side. Kamala returns to Earth and Danvers uses her power to restore Hala's sun.

Kamala's family helps Danvers move into Rambeau's house. Kamala's short-lived team-up with Danvers and Rambeau inspires her to seek out other young heroes and form a new group, starting with [[Kate Bishop (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kate Bishop]]. In a [[mid-credits scene]], Rambeau awakes in a parallel universe where she is greeted by [[Binary (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Binary]]—an alternate version of her mother [[Maria Rambeau|Maria]]—and the [[Mutant (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|mutant]] scientist [[Beast (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hank McCoy]].

== Production ==
=== Development ===
[[File:Nia DaCosta.png|thumb|upright|left|Director Nia DaCosta promoting the film in November 2023]]
Ahead of the release of ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019), star [[Brie Larson]] expressed interest in a sequel featuring the character [[Kamala Khan|Kamala Khan / Ms. Marvel]].<ref name="LarsonMsMarvel" /> Producer [[Kevin Feige]] previously said there were plans to introduce Kamala to the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) following the release of ''Captain Marvel'', because Kamala is inspired by [[Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Carol Danvers / Captain Marvel]];<ref name="FeigeMsMarvel" /> [[Iman Vellani]] was later cast as Kamala for the [[Disney+]] television miniseries ''[[Ms. Marvel (miniseries)|Ms. Marvel]]'' (2022).<ref name="VellaniMsMarvelCast" /> In March 2019, Feige said [[Marvel Studios]] had some "pretty amazing" ideas for a sequel,<ref name="FeigeMar2019" /> which could either be set in the 1990s, like the first film, or the present day.<ref name="FeigeMar2019ET" /> [[Lashana Lynch]] expressed interest in reprising her role of [[Maria Rambeau]] in a sequel, even if it was set in the present.<ref name="LynchSequel" />

At the 2019 [[San Diego Comic-Con]] in July, Feige confirmed plans for a ''Captain Marvel'' sequel.<ref name="CMSequelConfirmed" /> Official development began in January 2020, when [[Megan McDonnell]] entered negotiations to write the script after serving as a staff writer on Marvel Studios' Disney+ miniseries ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021).<ref name="McDonnell" /> Larson was confirmed to return as Danvers,<ref name="McDonnellLarson" /> but [[Anna Boden and Ryan Fleck]] were not expected to return after directing and co-writing the first film. The studio hoped to hire a female director to replace them. The film was expected to be set in the present day, and was aiming for a 2022 release.<ref name="McDonnell" /> In April 2020, [[Walt Disney Studios (division)|Disney]] scheduled the film for release on July 8, 2022, filling the July 2022 date that the studio had previously reserved for an untitled Marvel film.<ref name="July2022Date" />

[[Nia DaCosta]] was hired to direct the film in August 2020. ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]''{{'s}} Justin Kroll said this was another sign of Marvel Studios adding diversity to its films due to DaCosta being the first Black woman hired as a director by the studio. Kroll added that the film would likely break the record for the biggest-budgeted film directed by a Black woman.<ref name="DaCosta" /> The studio had also considered [[Olivia Wilde]] and [[Jamie Babbit]] as directors for the film,<ref name="DaCostaVariety" /> but DaCosta was said to have been the frontrunner for some time.<ref name="DaCosta" /> Richard Newby of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' said DaCosta's hiring could bring new energy to the MCU and Captain Marvel franchise, saying she "enjoys challenging preconceived notions about the relationship between characters and the lore behind stories". Newby also felt the film could explore Danvers's story from the perspective of Maria Rambeau's daughter [[Monica Rambeau (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Monica]], a Black woman in present-day America.<ref name="NewbyDaCosta" />

DaCosta, a self-professed comic book nerd, developed the film with ''WandaVision'' producer [[Mary Livanos]] who gave her "creative latitude" to make the film she wanted.<ref name="DaCostaAug2021Guardian" /><ref name="LivanosWandaVision" /> Larson said DaCosta was the best person to direct the film and praised her [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] presentation.<ref name="LarsonDaCosta" /> DaCosta's original pitch included [[Adam Warlock (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Adam Warlock]] and [[time travel]], but she was told that Warlock would be appearing in ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' (2023) and the [[Disney+]] series ''[[Loki (TV series)|Loki]]'' (2021–2023) already dealt with time travel stories.<ref name="AdamWarlockTimeTravelPitch" /> DaCosta also cited ''[[Final Fantasy VII: Advent Children]]'' (2005) in her pitch as a reference for the film, particularly for its fight scenes and ending sequence.<ref name="FinalFantasy" />

Feige announced ''Captain Marvel 2'' in December 2020, with a new release date of November 11, 2022. He confirmed DaCosta's involvement, revealed that [[Teyonah Parris]] would reprise her role as Monica Rambeau from ''WandaVision'', and said Vellani would reprise her role as Kamala.<ref name="DisneyInvestorDay" /> Parris was excited to work with DaCosta again after ''[[Candyman (2021 film)|Candyman]]'' (2021), and to further explore Monica's relationship with Danvers as teased in ''WandaVision''.<ref name="ParrisExcitements" /> Larson felt it made sense narratively to have Kamala and Rambeau introduced to the MCU in other projects before the three characters meet in this film, something she had discussed with Feige "from the beginning".<ref name="EWJuly2023" />

=== Pre-production ===
Pre-production work began by February 2021, when [[Zawe Ashton]] was cast as the film's villain.<ref name="Ashton" /> By that time, all of the scripts for ''Ms. Marvel'' had been written so ''The Marvels'' creative team was able to read those to know what happens to Kamala in that series.<ref name="AliMsMarvelTHRJun2022" /> [[Principal photography]] was expected to begin at the end of May,<ref name="May2021FilmingStartLocations" /> though some [[second unit]] filming began on April 9 in [[Jersey City, New Jersey]],<ref name="AprilSecondUnit" /><ref name="NJFilming" /> under the [[working title]] ''Goat Rodeo'',<ref name="AprilSecondUnit" /> to capture aerial footage, [[establishing shot]]s, and green screen plates.<ref name="AprilSecondUnit" /><ref name="AprilShotsNJ" /> In May, Marvel Studios revealed that the sequel would be titled ''The Marvels''.<ref name="TheMarvels" /> Ethan Anderton of ''[[/Film]]'' noted that the title referred to both Captain Marvel and Ms. Marvel, since the film's logo included the same stylized "S" from the ''Ms. Marvel'' series' logo.<ref name="/FilmMarvelsTitle" /> Graeme McMillian at ''The Hollywood Reporter'' acknowledged this explanation, but also wondered if there was a connection with the 1994 comics series ''[[Marvels]]''—which tells various Marvel Universe events from the perspective of a photographer—or with a project of the same name announced in 2020. He also wondered if "The Marvels" referred to a family of heroes, much like [[DC Comics]]' [[Marvel Family]] (now known as the Shazam Family).<ref name="THRTheMarvels" /> Later that month, pre-production work began in the United Kingdom.<ref name="UKPrep" />

[[Park Seo-joon]] was cast in an undisclosed role in mid-June, and was set to join the production after completing work on the film ''[[Concrete Utopia]]'' (2023).<ref name="Park" /> His role was reported as Prince Yan of the planet Aladna.<ref name="DoGTeaserTrailer" /><ref name="KoreaRelease" /> Larson and Parris began preparing for filming the next month.<ref name="LarsonFilmingPrep" /><ref name="ParrisFilmingPrep" /> DaCosta said ''The Marvels'' would deal with "specific, personal, [and] sometimes sad things",<ref name="DaCostaJuly2021" /> such as how people deal with pain and trauma,<ref name="DaCostaAug2021Variety" /> but would have a lighter story than her films ''[[Little Woods]]'' (2018) and ''Candyman''. She felt that she had more creative freedom on ''The Marvels'' than on her prior films.<ref name="DaCostaJuly2021" /> Feige said the dynamic between Danvers, Kamala, and Rambeau was the center of the film and likened their team-up to the formation of the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]] in ''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|The Avengers]]'' (2012). He revealed that ''The Marvels'' would have "fun cosmic elements", including some from [[Roy Thomas]]'s 1971 "[[Kree–Skrull War]]" comic book storyline, with the story directly picking up from the ending of ''Captain Marvel''. He described the film as tonally different from the MCU miniseries ''[[Secret Invasion (miniseries)|Secret Invasion]]'' (2023),<ref name="FeigeFeb2023" /> another ''Captain Marvel'' follow-up;<ref name="FeigeFeb2023" /> the series was believed to tie in with the film,<ref name="EWJuly2023" /> but ''The Marvels'' largely ignores the events of ''Secret Invasion''. Matt Webb Mitovich at ''[[TVLine]]'' speculated that Marvel intended for ''The Marvels'' to be set before ''Secret Invasion'', given that film had numerous previous release dates prior to ''Secret Invasion''{{'s}} June 2023 premiere, but that assumption "still leaves continuity issues {{em|all}} over the place".<ref name="MarvelsNoSIConnection" />

=== Filming ===
Principal photography was expected to begin on May 31, 2021,<ref name="May2021FilmingStartLocations" /> but began on July 26, 2021,<ref name="PressAdvance" /> at [[Pinewood Studios]] in [[Buckinghamshire]],<ref name="PressAdvance" /><ref name="May2021FilmingStartLocations" /><ref name="DaCostaJuly2021" /> and at [[Longcross Studios]] in [[Longcross]], [[Surrey, England]].<ref name="PressAdvance" /><ref name="LongcrossFilming" /> [[Sean Bobbitt]] was the cinematographer.<ref name="Bobbitt" /> Larson confirmed that she had started filming on August 10,<ref name="LarsonFilmingStart" /> and shortly after, [[Samuel L. Jackson]] revealed that he would reprise his MCU role as [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]],<ref name="Jackson" /> working on it in London at the same time as he was preparing to film ''Secret Invasion''.<ref name="Jackson3Projects" /> Filming for ''The Marvels'' took place in [[Tropea]], Italy, beginning on August 27, including on the coast of the [[Tyrrhenian Sea]].<ref name="TropeaFilmingLocations" /><ref name="TropeaFilming" /> On September 3, Park left for [[Los Angeles]] to begin filming.<ref name="ParkLA" /> Shortly after, [[Saagar Shaikh]], [[Zenobia Shroff]] and [[Mohan Kapur]] were revealed to be reprising their respective roles as Kamala's older brother [[Aamir Khan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Aamir]], mother [[Muneeba Khan|Muneeba]], and father [[Yusuf Khan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yusuf]], from ''Ms. Marvel''.<ref name="KhanFamily" /><ref name="VarietyTeaserTrailer" /> In October 2021, the film's release was delayed to February 17, 2023.<ref name="October2021Delay" /> Park shot his scenes for two months, and completed filming in England by November 2.<ref name="ParkWrap" /> Production designer Cara Brower said the film's scale and scope were massive, contrasting her work with DaCosta on ''Candyman''.<ref name="Brower" /> The production created 54 sets at Pinewood and Longcross Studios for five different planets (including Aladna) and spaceships, the S.A.B.E.R. space station, Maria Rambeau's house, and the Khan family's house. DaCosta wanted each planet to have a distinct design.<ref name="PressAdvance" /> In April 2022, the film's release was moved to July 28, 2023, swapping places with ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania]]'', as that film was further along in production.<ref name="Feb2023DateSwap" /><ref name="VarietyDateSwap" /> Filming of ''The Marvels'' [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] by the middle of the following month.<ref name="VellaniWrap" />

=== Post-production ===
Jackson revealed in mid-June 2022 that he would return to London in August to work on [[reshoot]]s for ''The Marvels'', before doing the same for ''Secret Invasion'',<ref name="JacksonReshoots" /> and Marvel was preparing for those reshoots by the end of July.<ref name="THRReshoots" /> That month, Larson made a [[cameo appearance]] in the last episode of ''Ms. Marvel'', "[[No Normal]]", through footage that DaCosta had filmed for ''The Marvels''. DaCosta did not know the footage would be used this way when she filmed it, and its inclusion in ''Ms. Marvel'' was a surprise to directors [[Adil El Arbi and Bilall Fallah]] as well.<ref name="LarsonMsMarvelCameo" /> Filming for ''The Marvels'' occurred in early August in [[Battery Park]] in New York City to capture visual effects plates.<ref name="BatteryParkFilming" /> DaCosta was revealed in January 2023 to have also worked on the film's script alongside Elissa Karasik and [[Zeb Wells]], who had respectively served as a writer on ''Loki'' and the MCU miniseries ''[[She-Hulk: Attorney at Law]]'' (2022),<ref name="AdditionalWritersJan2023" /> though only DaCosta, McDonnell, and Karasik were credited.<ref name="WritingCredits" /> In February 2023, the film's release was delayed to November 10, 2023, as Disney and Marvel Studios were re-evaluating their content output and costs.<ref name="Nov2023ReleaseChange" /> This allowed more time for post-production.<ref name="Nov2023ReleaseChange2" /><ref name="VarietyNov2023" /> Joanna Robinson of ''[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]'' reported in April that the film was undergoing a "massive overhaul" and reshoots,<ref name="RobinsonReshoots" /> which took place for four weeks to reportedly make the storyline coherent.<ref name="VarietyNov2023" /> Feige reportedly became more involved with the film than he had been previously following the poor box office reception to ''Quantumania''.<ref name="WSJMay2025" />

The first trailer, released in April 2023, revealed that Ashton was playing [[Dar-Benn (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dar-Benn]] and [[Daniel Ings]] had been cast as Ty-Rone.<ref name="DoGTeaserTrailer" /><ref name="MPATeaserTrailer" /> Lynch was then reported to be reprising her role as Maria Rambeau, alongside [[Cobie Smulders]] and [[Randall Park]] in their respective MCU roles as [[Maria Hill (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Maria Hill]] and [[Jimmy Woo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Jimmy Woo]],<ref name="ColliderCast" /> although Smulders denied her involvement in June.<ref name="SmuldersDeny" /> Also in June, [[Gary Lewis (actor)|Gary Lewis]] was revealed to have joined the cast,<ref name="ProjectProfile" /> portraying Emperor Dro'ge,<ref name="RTCast" /> and Marvel Studios held a public test screening of the film in Texas. This was an "uncharacteristic" move by the studio and the screening was reportedly met with mixed responses.<ref name="VarietyNov2023" /> [[Tessa Thompson]] was revealed to be reprising her MCU role of [[Valkyrie (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valkyrie]] in the film when the final trailer was released in early November.<ref name="Thompson" /> Marvel wanted to show a friendship between Danvers and Valkyrie after fans had "[[Shipping (fandom)|shipped]]" the two characters, and they thought it made sense for Thompson to cameo in the film due to her close relationship with DaCosta after starring in ''Little Woods''.<ref name="LivanosEWNov2023" /> When the film was released soon after, [[Hailee Steinfeld]] and [[Kelsey Grammer]] were revealed to be in the film, reprising their respective roles of [[Kate Bishop (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kate Bishop]] from the Disney+ miniseries ''[[Hawkeye (miniseries)|Hawkeye]]'' (2021) and [[Beast (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dr. Hank McCoy / Beast]] from [[20th Century Fox]]'s ''[[X-Men (film series)|X-Men]]'' films ''[[X-Men: The Last Stand]]'' (2006) and ''[[X-Men: Days of Future Past]]'' (2014).<ref name="CameosCreditsScene" /><ref name="EWEndingMidCredits" /> Kate Bishop was chosen for the film's end scene, where she is recruited by Kamala to join a new team of young heroes, because the characters live close together in the [[New York metropolitan area|New York tri-state area]]. Livanos was excited to start bringing together the MCU's younger heroes,<ref name="LivanosEWNov2023" /> many of whom had been introduced in various recent MCU projects,<ref name="EWEndingMidCredits" /> and said the scene would "lead to some pretty exciting things". For Grammer's cameo in the [[mid-credits scene]], Feige personally called him about returning, leveraging their relationship from when Feige worked on the ''X-Men'' film series.<ref name="LivanosEWNov2023" /> Grammer was excited to return to his role, which he described as "a real character of [[gravitas]] and importance in our culture", and hoped it would lead to more MCU appearances.<ref name="GrammerWrap" /> Also appearing in the scene is Lynch as an alternate version of Maria called [[Binary (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Binary]].<ref name="EWEndingMidCredits" />

Catrin Hedström and Evan Schiff edited the film. Hedström previously worked with DaCosta on ''Candyman''.<ref name="Hedstrom" /><ref name="PressAdvance" /> Ashton stated in February 2024 that they filmed an alternate ending where Danvers perished with Dar-Benn.<ref name="AshtonFeb2024" /> Visual effects for the film were created by [[Industrial Light & Magic]], [[Rise FX]], [[Rising Sun Pictures]], [[Sony Pictures Imageworks]], [[Trixter (company)|Trixter]], [[Wētā FX]], and Wylie Co. Tara DeMarco was the visual effects supervisor.<ref name="ArtOfVFX" />

== Music ==
{{Main|The Marvels (soundtrack){{!}}''The Marvels'' (soundtrack)}}
In January 2022, [[Laura Karpman]] was hired to compose the score for the film, after previously doing so for the [[What If...? season 1|first season]] of the MCU television series ''[[What If...? (TV series)|What If...?]]'' (2021) and ''Ms. Marvel''.<ref name="Karpman" /><ref name="KarpmanMsMarvel" /> DaCosta asked Karpman to write a new theme for the titular team rather than focusing on the individual characters, similar to how ''The Avengers'' focused on a team theme rather than individual character themes.<ref name="KarpmanDec2023" /><ref name="KarpmanNov2023" /> Karpman described the film's main theme, "Higher. Further. Faster. Together", as a [[chosen family]] theme that combines elements for Danvers, Rambeau, and Kamala. The theme ends with a choir chanting "higher, further, faster, together" in [[Latin]]. Dar-Benn's "slithery and jazzy" theme is mainly played on flutes and was inspired by the music of [[Herbie Hancock]].<ref name="KarpmanNov2023" /> [[John Ottman]]'s ''X-Men'' themes from ''[[X2 (film)|X2]]'' (2003) and ''X-Men: Days of Future Past'' are heard during the [[mid-credits scene]].<ref name="EWEndingMidCredits" />

A suite that Karpman wrote to take the main theme "through paces", titled "The Marvels Suite", was performed at the [[Philadelphia Orchestra]] on June 3, 2023.<ref name="Suite" /> "Higher. Further. Faster. Together." premiered at [[BBC Proms#Last Night of the Proms|the Last Night of the BBC Proms]] on September 9,<ref name="MainThemeBBCProms" /> and was released as a digital single on November 2.<ref name="MainThemeSingle" /> A soundtrack album for Karpman's score was released by [[Hollywood Records]] and [[Marvel Music (record label)|Marvel Music]] on November 8.<ref name="Soundtrack" />

''The Marvels'' premiered in Las Vegas on November 7, 2023.<ref name="VegasPremiere" /> It was released in South Korea on November 8,<ref name="KoreaRelease" /> and in the United States and China on November 10,<ref name="Nov2023ReleaseChange" /><ref name="ChinaRelease" /> in [[IMAX]],<ref name="IMAXConfirm" /> [[ScreenX]], and [[4DX]].<ref name="PremiumFormats" /> The film was initially not expected to have an IMAX release because ''[[Dune: Part Two]]'' was scheduled to be released on November 3 and would have access to all IMAX screens for five to six weeks.<ref name="NoIMAX" /> [[IMAX Corporation]] CEO [[Richard Gelfond]] said the company would pivot to showing ''The Marvels'' in IMAX should ''Dune: Part Two'' be delayed by the SAG-AFTRA strike,<ref name="NoIMAX" /> and this was confirmed in August when the film was moved to March 2024.<ref name="IMAXConfirm" /><ref name="Dune2Delay" /> Before it was set for the November 2023 release date, ''The Marvels'' was scheduled for July 8, 2022,<ref name="July2022Date" /> November 11, 2022,<ref name="TheMarvels" /> February 17, 2023,<ref name="October2021Delay" /> and July 28, 2023.<ref name="Feb2023DateSwap" /> It is part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFiveSDCC2022" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''The Marvels'' grossed $84.5&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $121.6&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $206.1&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /> It is the first [[box-office bomb]] and the lowest-grossing film of the MCU franchise,<ref name="Bomb" /><ref name="DHNetProfit" /><ref name="BO4thWknd" /><ref name="WSJMay2025" /> falling short of an estimated [[break-even point]] of $439.6&nbsp;million.<ref name="Early Budget" /> ''[[Puck News]]'' reported that the film needed to earn around $700&nbsp;million to become profitable.<ref name="PuckProfitable" /> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' calculated the net losses of the film to be $237{{nbsp}}million, when factoring together expenses and revenues.<ref name="DHNetProfit" /> Despite this, it surpassed ''[[A Wrinkle in Time (2018 film)|A Wrinkle in Time]]'' (2018) to become the highest-grossing film directed by a Black woman.<ref name="ForbesRecords" />

In the United States and Canada, ''The Marvels'' was originally projected to gross around $60&nbsp;million from 4,030 theaters in its opening weekend.<ref name="BOPreview" /> After making $21.3&nbsp;million on its first day (including $6.6&nbsp;million from Thursday night previews), estimates were lowered to $47–52&nbsp;million. It went on to debut to $46.1&nbsp;million, topping the box office and marking the best opening weekend for a Black female director. However, this also marked the lowest opening weekend for an MCU film, taking that record from ''[[The Incredible Hulk (film)|The Incredible Hulk]]'' (2008).<ref name="BOOpeningWknd" /> In its second weekend, the film made $10.12&nbsp;million and finished fourth behind newcomers ''[[The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes]]'', ''[[Trolls Band Together]]'', and ''[[Thanksgiving (2023 film)|Thanksgiving]]''.<ref name="BO2ndWknd" /> This was a 78% drop from the first weekend, which was the biggest second-weekend drop for an MCU film, a record previously held by ''Quantumania''.<ref name="BO2ndWkndDrop" /> ''The Marvels'' made $6.4&nbsp;million in its third weekend, finishing in sixth place.<ref name="BO3rdWknd" /> On December 3, during its fourth weekend, Disney described the film's box office as "winding down" and said the studio would no longer report its weekend box office grosses, despite the film remaining in theaters through the New Year's holiday. It earned an additional $2.4&nbsp;million in the fourth weekend, dropping out of the top 10 to 11th place.<ref name="BO4thWknd" />

Discussing the film's low box office performance, Anthony D'Alessandro of ''Deadline Hollywood'' rejected claims that it was impacted by general "superhero fatigue" and instead blamed lackluster marketing and the overexposure of MCU content on Disney+.<ref name="BOOpeningWknd" /> [[John Scalzi]], writing for ''[[Uncanny Magazine]]'', criticized the amount of "homework" required to understand the film considering its connections to multiple Disney+ series and prior MCU films.<ref name="IgerResponseUM" /> Disney CEO [[Bob Iger]] also acknowledged the large amount of MCU content produced for Disney+, but added that insufficient day-to-day supervision by Disney executives while filming took place during the COVID-19 pandemic was partially to blame for the film's failure.<ref name="IgerSupervision" /> Multiple commentators took issue with this statement, questioning how more executive oversight than Marvel is already known for could be an improvement. Some felt Iger was wrongfully blaming DaCosta for the film's performance,<ref name="IgerResponseGamer" /><ref name="IgerResponseCBR" /> and several characterized his statement as throwing the director "[[under the bus]]".<ref name="IgerResponseUM" /><ref name="IgerResponseBC" /><ref name="IgerResponseAVClub" /><ref name="IgerResponseGizmodo" /> This came amid a wider narrative in the Hollywood media, which some attributed to Disney, in which DaCosta appeared to be unfairly targeted.<ref name="IgerResponseGizmodo" /><ref name="IgerResponseVF" /><ref name="DaCostaUnfairForbes" /><ref name="DaCostaUnfairCBR" /> Kaitlyn Booth at ''[[Bleeding Cool]]'' and Rachel Ulatowski at ''[[The Mary Sue]]'' both noted that Iger did not suggest Disney's other 2023 box office failures required additional supervision, including ''Quantumania'' and ''[[Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny]]'' which were both directed by men,<ref name="IgerResponseBC" /><ref name="IgerResponseMarySue" /> and Ulatowski made it clear that she found Iger's statement to be sexist.<ref name="IgerResponseMarySue" /> In July 2025, Feige attributed the box office to audiences believing they needed to watch the Disney+ series to see the film. He felt the studio should have better explained that this was not necessary.<ref name="FeigeConferenceJul2025THR" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of 63% with an average score of 5.9/10, based on 377 reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "Funny, refreshingly brief, and elevated by the chemistry of its three leads, ''The Marvels'' is easy to enjoy in the moment despite its cluttered story and jumbled tonal shifts."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |title=The Marvels |id=the_marvels |type=m |access-date=February 4, 2025}}{{cbignore}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 50 out of 100 based on 57 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |title=The Marvels |id=the-marvels |type=movie |access-date=December 6, 2023}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on A+ to F scale, which at the time was tied with ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'' (2021) and ''Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania'' for the lowest score of the MCU, while those polled by [[PostTrak]] gave it a 73% positive score.<ref name="BOOpeningWknd" />

Writing for ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'', Lovia Gyarkye praised DaCosta's direction as "kinetic", and felt that the directing and the "intimate storytelling style" lets audiences see the main characters from "new and entertaining vantage points".<ref name="THRReviw" /> Abby Olcese, for ''[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]]'', rated the film an 8.5/10 and praised the film for playing with genre and pulling "aesthetics" from the ''Ms. Marvel'' streaming series; Olcese felt that "DaCosta's assured, efficient direction" was an example of what the MCU could have been if the franchise "hadn't gotten bogged down by gloopy effects and overblown lore".<ref name="PasteReview" /> In contrast, James Mottram of ''[[NME]]'' gave the film a 3/5 star rating and felt that the film "never musters the same level of engagement" as DaCosta's ''Candyman'' even with "a script that is chock full of good lines and a cast of willing participants".<ref name="NMEReview" /> [[Christy Lemire]] of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film a negative review with a 1.5 star rating, calling it "terrible" and opined that it is "the worst film yet in the Marvel Cinematic Universe" with only the musical moments as "an unexpected and much-needed delight".<ref name="RogerEbertReview" />

Despite mixed critical reception, the performances were praised. [[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' described the lead trio as "an entertaining intergalactic ensemble".<ref name="TheGuardianReview" /> [[Peter Travers]] of [[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]] similarly commended that "If there is such a thing as chemistry, Larson, Parris and Vellani have it".<ref name="ABCNewsReview" /> Amelia Emberwing of ''[[IGN]]'' declared that Vellani "predictably steals the show".<ref name="IGNReview" /> [[Helen O'Hara (journalist)|Helen O'Hara]] of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' praised the humor and emotional depth of Vellani's Kamala with the "two older heroes".<ref name="EmpireReview" /> Christian Holub of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' gave a lukewarm review of the film and opined that it was "a mixed bag that tries to juggle too many different characters and plotlines", but praised Vellani as a "shining star".<ref name="EWReview" />

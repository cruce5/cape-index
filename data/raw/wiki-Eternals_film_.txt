'''''Eternals''''' is a 2021 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[Marvel Comics]] race the [[Eternals (comics)|Eternals]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 26th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Chloé Zhao]], who wrote the screenplay with [[Patrick Burleigh]], Ryan Firpo, and [[Kaz Firpo]]. It stars an [[ensemble cast]] including [[Gemma Chan]], [[Richard Madden]], [[Kumail Nanjiani]], [[Lia McHugh]], [[Brian Tyree Henry]], [[Lauren Ridloff]], [[Barry Keoghan]], [[Ma Dong-seok|Don Lee]], [[Kit Harington]], [[Salma Hayek]], and [[Angelina Jolie]]. In the film, the Eternals are immortal beings who emerge from hiding after thousands of years to protect Earth from their ancient counterparts, the [[Deviants (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Deviants]].

In April 2018, Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]] announced that a film based on the Eternals was in development and would explore the group as [[ancient alien]]s living throughout human history. Ryan and Kaz Firpo were hired to write the script, and Zhao was set to direct in September. She was given significant creative freedom to rewrite the script, taking particular inspiration from [[Terrence Malick]]'s films, and to use more locations and natural lighting than previous MCU films had. Casting began in early 2019 and Burleigh joined to work on the script ahead of [[principal photography]], which took place from July 2019 to February 2020 at [[Pinewood Studios]] and on location in England and the [[Canary Islands]].

''Eternals'' premiered at the [[Dolby Theatre]] in [[Los Angeles]] on October 18, 2021, and was released in the United States on November 5 as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU. It grossed $402.1&nbsp;million worldwide and received several awards and nominations, but it also became the first MCU film to not receive generally positive reviews. Particular criticism went to the screenplay and pacing, with commentators feeling it did not live up to Zhao's previous work or to previous MCU films. The themes and visuals received some praise.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
In 5000 BC, ten superpowered beings known as [[Eternals (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Eternals]]—[[Ajak (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ajak]], [[Sersi (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sersi]], [[Ikaris (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ikaris]], [[Kingo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kingo]], [[Sprite (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sprite]], [[Phastos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Phastos]], [[Makkari (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Makkari]], [[Druig (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Druig]], [[Gilgamesh (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Gilgamesh]], and [[Thena (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thena]]—are sent to Earth by [[Arishem (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Arishem]], a [[Celestials (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Celestial]], on the [[starship]] ''[[Domo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Domo]]''. They are charged with exterminating invasive entities known as the [[Deviants (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Deviants]]. After the last Deviants are killed in 1521, the group's opinions differ over their continued relationship with humankind. Over the next 500 years, they mostly live apart, waiting for Arishem's orders.

In the present, Sersi and Sprite live together in [[London]]. After Sersi's partner Ikaris left her without explanation centuries earlier, she is now in a relationship with human [[Dane Whitman (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dane Whitman]] who works at the [[Natural History Museum, London|Natural History Museum]]. When the trio is attacked by the Deviant [[Kro (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kro]], Ikaris arrives and chases the creature away. The three Eternals travel to South Dakota to reunite with their leader, Ajak, only to find her dead. Sersi is posthumously chosen by Ajak as her successor, granting her the ability to communicate with Arishem.

Sersi learns that the Eternals' mission was actually to prepare Earth for the [[Emergence (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Emergence]] of a new Celestial. Arishem explains that, for millions of years, he has been planting Celestial seeds inside planets where the energy from large populations allows new Celestials to be born. The Deviants were sent to destroy the [[apex predator]]s of each planet to ensure the development of intelligent life, but when they evolved and began hunting the planets' native populations, Arishem created the Eternals to counter them. With the recent end of [[the Blip]] restoring Earth's population,{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019)}} humankind has reached the necessary size to allow the Celestial [[Tiamut (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tiamut]] to be born, which will result in Earth's destruction.

Hoping to delay the Emergence, the Eternals reunite. At Druig's residence in the [[Amazon rainforest]], they are attacked by the Deviants. They kill them all except for Kro, who kills Gilgamesh before fleeing. Phastos proposes they use the [[Uni-Mind (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Uni-Mind]], a connection between all the Eternals that would give Druig enough power to put Tiamut to sleep with his [[Brainwashing|mind-control]] abilities. However, Ikaris is loyal to Arishem and refuses to help stop the Emergence. He reveals that Ajak wanted to stop the Emergence and save humanity, so he led her to the Deviants and allowed them to kill her. Sprite joins Ikaris due to her unrequited love for him while Kingo chooses to leave, agreeing with Ikaris but not wanting to hurt his friends.

Makkari locates the site of the Emergence, an [[active volcano]] in the Indian Ocean, where Ikaris and Sprite attempt to stop them. Druig knocks out Sprite, and Phastos restrains Ikaris. Kro arrives and is killed by Thena. Druig is unable to put Tiamut to sleep, and Sersi instead attempts to turn him into stone. Ikaris breaks free of his restraints and goes to kill Sersi, but is unable to due to his love for her. Both he and Sprite join with the others in the Uni-Mind, and Sersi gains enough power to turn Tiamut into stone. Guilt-ridden, Ikaris flies into the Sun. Sersi uses the remaining Uni-Mind energy to turn Sprite into a human, ending her [[Puer aeternus|permanent childlike state]].

Thena, Druig, and Makkari depart on the ''Domo'' to warn Eternals on other planets of the Emergences. Whitman professes his love for Sersi and is about to reveal a secret about his family history when she, Phastos, and Kingo are lifted into space by Arishem. Displeased with their treason, Arishem says he will spare humanity if the Eternals' memories show that humans are worthy of living. He vows to return for judgment and takes the trio into a singularity.

In a [[mid-credits scene]], Thena, Makkari, and Druig meet the Eternal [[Eros (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Eros]] and his assistant [[Pip (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Pip the Troll]]. In a post-credits scene, an unseen person{{efn|Identified off-screen as [[Blade (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Blade]]<ref name="Blade" />}} questions whether Whitman is ready to wield the [[Ebony Blade (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ebony Blade]].

== Production ==
=== Development ===
In April 2015, [[Marvel Television]] was working with screenwriter [[John Ridley]] to craft a new television series for [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]], "reinventing" an existing [[Marvel Comics]] character or property.<ref name="RidleySeries" /> This was revealed years later to be the [[Eternals (comics)|Eternals]], a race of long-lived superheroes created by [[Jack Kirby]].<ref name="RidleyEternalsSeries" /> Ridley said he was trying to make a superhero series that viewers had not seen before, differentiating it from Marvel's other projects,<ref name="RidleyIGNJan2017" /> and said his approach would have been a "really weird story" that may not have been entertaining for everyone. It ultimately did not move forward with Ridley,<ref name="RidleyEternalsSeries" /> and Marvel Television was folded into [[Marvel Studios]] in December 2019.<ref name="MarvelTVFolded" />

Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]] said in April 2018 that the studio was actively developing a film based on the Eternals, to be released as part of their [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] slate of films. Marvel Studios had met with multiple screenwriters and was believed to be focusing on the character [[Sersi (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sersi]] in the film.<ref name="FeigeApr2018" /> Marvel set Ryan and [[Kaz Firpo]] to write the script a month later,<ref name="Firpos" /> with their outline including a love story between the characters Sersi and [[Ikaris (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ikaris]].<ref name="Zhao" /> The Firpos said the 1999 limited comic book series ''[[Earth X]]'' by [[Jim Krueger]] and [[John Paul Leon]], which is about a [[dystopian]] version of the [[Marvel Universe]], was a major inspiration for them.<ref name="EarthX" /> In June, Feige said Marvel was interested in exploring the "[[ancient alien]]s kind of sci-fi trope" by having the Eternals be the inspiration for myths and legends throughout the history of the MCU.<ref name="FeigeJun2018" />

[[File:Chloe Zhao by Gage Skidmore.jpg|thumb|upright|left|Director [[Chloé Zhao]] promoting ''Eternals'' at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con]]
During late August, Marvel's director search for ''Eternals'' narrowed to a shortlist that included [[Chloé Zhao]]—who had also been in the running to direct Marvel's ''[[Black Widow (2021 film)|Black Widow]]'' (2021)—[[Nicole Kassell]], [[Travis Knight]], and the pair of [[Cristina Gallego]] and [[Ciro Guerra]].<ref name="Zhao" /> Zhao approached Marvel Studios about making the film as she had been a fan of the MCU. She wanted to work with the studio to bring her own take and [[world-building]] to the franchise,<ref name="ZhaoDec2020" /> and impressed them with a presentation that Feige described as fascinating, with "reams of visuals".<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" /> Feige was also impressed with Zhao's passion for the project, since it was not a top priority for the studio.<ref name="ZhaoPlaylistJan2026" /> Zhao worked with producer [[Nate Moore (film producer)|Nate Moore]] to develop her [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]],<ref name="ZhaoApril2021" /> noting she became interested in the project after reading its [[Film treatment|treatment]] that read like a Greek play "where gods get to discuss everything about human nature based on their own relationship".<ref name="ZhaoPlaylistJan2026" /> The presentation left Marvel concerned that she might take on a different big-studio project instead of ''Eternals'', forcing them to move quickly to secure her, and Zhao was hired as director in September.<ref name="Zhao" /><ref name="Zhao2" /> This was ahead of her beginning production on her film ''[[Nomadland]]'' (2020), with Zhao beginning to work on the ''Eternals'' script during the filming of ''Nomadland''.<ref name="ZhaoPlaylistJan2026" /> Zhao hoped to push the scope of the film further than Marvel's ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019),<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" /> but also wanted it to have intimacy.<ref name="ZhaoWriterJan2021" /> Zhao described the film as a melting pot of influences, taking from Kirby's original ''Eternals'' comics, [[Neil Gaiman]]'s [[Eternals (comics)#Memory loss and the Dreaming Celestial|2006 comic book run]], previous MCU projects, and Zhao's love of science fiction and fantasy films and [[manga]].<ref name="ZhaoApril2021" /> Speaking specifically to her love of manga, she hoped those influences would create a "marriage of East and West".<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" /> Zhao cited the television series ''[[Ancient Aliens]]'' (2009–present), the ''[[Final Fantasy]]'' video game franchise, concepts from [[Yuval Noah Harari]]'s book ''[[Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind]]'' (2011), and the films of [[Terrence Malick]] as inspirations.<ref name="ZhaoNov2020" />

Marvel saw ''Eternals'' as an opportunity to create an [[ensemble film]] that is not a [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]], similar to ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014), and to introduce relatively unknown characters to audiences as they did with the [[Guardians of the Galaxy (2008 team)|Guardians]] and the [[Avengers (comics)|Avengers]].<ref name="FeigeFeb2019" /> The studio also considered ''Eternals'' to be a perfect transition into its next phase of films along with projects such as ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019),<ref name="Zhao2" /> allowing them to cast a diverse group of actors to portray the various Eternals.<ref name="Zhao" /> Moore's initial planning documents for the project included the swapping of genders, sexualities, and ethnicities of some characters, and Zhao further advocated for this approach.<ref name="FeigeApril2021" /> In February 2019, Feige reiterated that Marvel was interested in the Eternals due to Kirby's epic, century-spanning story,<ref name="FeigeFeb2019" /> with the film version covering 7,000 years and exploring humanity's place in the cosmos.<ref name="ZhaoApril2021" /> Zhao said the Eternals "have lived among us for so long, [they have] the same struggles like identity, purpose, faith, personal freedom versus greater good—all the duality and flaws that make us human". With a story that spans so many years, the characters' relationships evolve like a complex family unit where friends "turn into frenemies, and then turn into enemies, and then come back to friendship". Zhao considered the Earth to be an eleventh character in the film.<ref name="EWCoverStoryAug2021" />

=== Pre-production ===
Zhao continued working on a draft of the script throughout early 2019, which built upon the multiple drafts submitted by the Firpos. Approximately four months ahead of filming, the script was not finalized. Moore introduced Zhao to [[Patrick Burleigh]], who had been part of Marvel's writers program, in the hopes that Burleigh could assist with the writing. Zhao hired him to work on the screenplay, which he did for about three months ahead of filming. Burleigh believed Zhao needed to write on the film as well as direct to "process the film through her own filter", and said her draft showed that she was "trying to make the Terrence Malick version of a Marvel film"; Zhao cited Malick's film ''[[The Tree of Life (film)|The Tree of Life]]'' (2011) as a particular inspiration. Burleigh said his work on the script was mostly "structural" to find "the flow of the film", and likened the story to a [[road trip film]].<ref name="BurleighTHR" />

{{Quote box|width=33%|align=right|quote="I wanted it to reflect the world we live in. But also I wanted to put a cast together that feels like a group of misfits. I didn't want the jocks. I want you to walk away at the end of the movie not thinking, 'This person is this ethnicity, that person is that nationality.' No. I want you to walk away thinking, 'That's a family.' You don't think about what they represent."|source=—Zhao on the diverse cast of the film<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" />}}
[[Angelina Jolie]] joined the cast in March 2019, reportedly as Sersi,<ref name="Jolie" /><ref name="Nanjiani" /> with [[Kumail Nanjiani]] and [[Ma Dong-seok|Don Lee]] cast in undisclosed roles the next month.<ref name="Nanjiani" /><ref name="Ma" /> At that time, the film was expected to feature Marvel Studios' first gay superhero.<ref name="Ma2" /> In May, [[Richard Madden]] entered negotiations for the role of Ikaris,<ref name="MaddenTalks" /> and [[Salma Hayek]] had entered early negotiations for an undisclosed role the following month.<ref name="Hayek" /> In July, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' reported that the cast included Jolie, Madden, and [[Millie Bobby Brown]], but Brown denied that she had been cast.<ref name="Brown" /><ref name="NoBrown" />

At the 2019 [[San Diego Comic-Con]], Feige officially announced ''The Eternals'' with a release date of November 6, 2020. He officially announced the casting of Jolie as [[Thena (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thena]], Nanjiani as [[Kingo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kingo]], Lee as [[Gilgamesh (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Gilgamesh]], Madden as Ikaris, and Hayek as [[Ajak (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ajak]], along with [[Lauren Ridloff]] as [[Makkari (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Makkari]], [[Brian Tyree Henry]] as [[Phastos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Phastos]], and [[Lia McHugh]] as [[Sprite (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sprite]].<ref name="McHugh" /> Zhao looked for actors for each role that could "find a bit of themselves in their character".<ref name="EWCoverStoryAug2021" /> Feige added that one of these actors was portraying an LGBTQ character,<ref name="LGBTQ" /> with actor [[Haaz Sleiman]] later revealing that Phastos is depicted as gay in the film. Sleiman portrays the character's husband, and the pair have a child in the film. Sleiman felt it was important to depict "how loving and beautiful a queer family can be" rather than the "sexual or rebellious" depiction in some previous media.<ref name="SleimanFeb2020" /> Feige said the relationship was "always sort of inherent in the story" and he felt it was "extremely well done" in the film,<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" /> while Sleiman said it was a "thoughtful" depiction.<ref name="SleimanJan2021" />

''Eternals'' is set around eight months after the events of ''Avengers: Endgame'',<ref name="EternalsTimeline" /> and addresses why the Eternals did not interfere in any previous conflicts in the MCU. Both Feige and Moore said the film would have "major ripple effects" on the future of the MCU, and at times, it was "a challenge" to balance the grounded nature of the MCU with the "mythic grandeur" of the Eternals property.<ref name="EWCoverStoryAug2021" />

=== Filming ===
By the time of the film's official announcement in July 2019, [[principal photography]] had begun at [[Pinewood Studios]] in [[Buckinghamshire]], England.<ref name="FilmingStart" /><ref name="PinewoodStudios" /> [[Ben Davis (cinematographer)|Ben Davis]] served as director of photography, after doing so on several previous MCU films.<ref name="FeigeApril2021" /><ref name="Davis" /> Zhao said Marvel Studios allowed her creative freedom to shoot the film on location, "exactly the way [she] wanted to shoot" it. She was able to use a similar style to her previous films, including 360° shots and working with the same camera and rigs as were used for her film ''[[Nomadland]]'' (2020),<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" /> which Zhao was set to complete [[Back-to-back film production|back-to-back]] with ''Eternals''.<ref name="ZhaoApril2021" /> Zhao felt that she "got lucky in that Marvel wants to take risks and do something different".<ref name="ZhaoSept2020" /> Zhao cited ''[[The Revenant (2015 film)|The Revenant]]'' (2015) as a primary influence when composing the film's action sequences.<ref name="ZhaoRevenantInfluence" />

[[Gemma Chan]] and [[Barry Keoghan]] were in talks to join the cast in August 2019.<ref name="Chan" /><ref name="Keoghan" /> Chan previously portrayed [[Minn-Erva (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Minn-Erva]] in ''Captain Marvel'', but reports noted that she was potentially being looked at by Marvel to play a separate character in this film.<ref name="Chan" /> Since Minn-Erva dies in that film, Chan had felt that it was unlikely she would return to the MCU, but after working on the film she was told by Feige that the studio wanted to "make better use" of her in a future project. This led to Chan auditioning for Sersi, one of the last actresses to be considered for the role. Chan later described Sersi as the most difficult role for the film to cast.<ref name="ChanTHRDec2020" /> Chan and Keoghan were confirmed to be cast in the film at the [[D23 (Disney)|D23 Expo]] in August, in the roles of Sersi and [[Druig (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Druig]], respectively, along with [[Kit Harington]] as [[Dane Whitman (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dane Whitman]].<ref name="D232019" /> Chan said she and Marvel Studios were surprised by how soon after ''Captain Marvel'' her new MCU role came, with both assuming that it would have been a project further in the future.<ref name="ChanTHRDec2020" /> [[Harish Patel]] was cast at the end of August as [[Karun Patel|Karun]], Kingo's manager,<ref name="PatelNews18" /><ref name="PatelHindustanTimes" /> and filmed his role from September 2019 until January 2020.<ref name="PatelNews18" />

By early November, filming had taken place in the [[Canary Islands]]. The cast and crew, including Jolie and Madden, had to be evacuated from a shooting location on the island of [[Fuerteventura]] when an explosive device, thought to be a remnant armament from a Nazi base, was found there.<ref name="Canary" /> Later that month, [[Zain Al Rafeea]] joined the cast.<ref name="Rafeea" /><ref name="Rafeea2" /> In early January 2020, filming took place outside the [[Oxford University Museum of Natural History]] in [[Oxford]], England,<ref name="Oxford" /> as well as [[Hampstead Heath]] and in [[Camden Town|Camden]] in London,<ref name="London" /><ref name="Camden" /> under the [[working title]] ''Sack Lunch''.<ref name="Camden" /><ref name="WorkingTitle" /> Chan said the filming process felt very different from what she experienced on ''Captain Marvel'', explaining that ''Eternals'' shot more on location and used natural light while ''Captain Marvel'' had more studio work and bluescreens.<ref name="ChanTHRDec2020" /> Filming [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on February 4, 2020.<ref name="Wrap" />

=== Post-production ===
In March 2020, [[Scanline VFX]], one of the companies working on the film's visual effects, confirmed that they would be working remotely due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="ScanLineVFX" /> [[Industrial Light & Magic]], [[Luma Pictures]], [[Rise FX|RISE]], and [[Weta Digital]] also worked on the film's visual effects.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Frei |first=Vincent |date=October 25, 2021 |title=Eternals – The Art of VFX |url=https://www.artofvfx.com/eternals/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211109013833/https://www.artofvfx.com/eternals/ |archive-date=November 9, 2021 |access-date=July 7, 2022 |website=Art of VFX}}</ref> In early April, Disney shifted much of their Phase Four slate of films due to the pandemic, moving ''The Eternals''{{'}} release date to February 12, 2021.<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> In August, the film's title was officially shortened from ''The Eternals'' to ''Eternals'',<ref name="NoThe" /> and the next month, the release date was pushed back to November 5, 2021.<ref name="Phase4DelayAgain" /> Reshoots had taken place by mid-November 2020.<ref name="RidloffReshoots" />

Additional filming occurred in [[Los Angeles]] in early February, also under the working title ''Sack Lunch''.<ref name="Feb7LAFilming" /> [[Dylan Tichenor]] and [[Craig Wood (film editor)|Craig Wood]] serve as co-editors of the film.<ref name="ZhaoApril2021" /> Tichenor said Zhao usually edits her own films and has "strong opinions", but on ''Eternals'' she was relying on Tichenor and Wood due to the size of the production and the ongoing awards season for ''Nomadland''. Tichenor added that Zhao respected the pair's editing experience and point of view, and they made their first cut of the film without much input from her. They began adjusting the film based on Zhao's feedback and were still working on the editing in April 2021.<ref name="TichenorWood" /> At the end of the month, Zhao said editing for the film was in its "final stretch",<ref name="ZhaoApril2021" /> and Jashaun St. John was revealed to appear in the film, after previously starring in Zhao's film ''[[Songs My Brothers Taught Me]]'' (2015).<ref name="StJohn" />

At the end of May 2021, with the release of the first teaser trailer and poster for the film, the official writing credits were revealed: Zhao was credited as the screenwriter, both as a solo contributor and as part of a writing team with Burleigh, while Ryan and Kaz Firpo received story credit.<ref name="VarietyTeaser" /><ref name="PosterCredits" /> Additionally, [[Gil Birmingham]] was revealed to have been cast in the film,<ref name="Birmingham" /> although he was absent in the final film.{{citation needed|date=May 2023}} In July 2021, [[Writers Guild of America West]] presented the film's final writing credits, awarding Ryan and Kaz Firpo screenplay credit along with Zhao and the team of Zhao and Burleigh, in addition to their story credit.<ref name="WGA" /><ref name="BurleighTHR" /> Moore believed the studio had "bit off as much as we could chew" with the film, creating one that "felt urgent and present and had [a fast] pace, but also took time to reflect back over the centuries".<ref name="EWCoverStoryAug2021" />

At the film's world premiere, Matt Donnelly of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' [[tweeted]] that [[Harry Styles]] made an appearance in a [[post-credits scene]] as [[Eros (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Eros / Starfox]], [[Thanos]]'s brother in the comics, which was not shown in early press screenings of the film.<ref name="StylesEros" /> Zhao later revealed that she "kept tabs on [Styles]" since his appearance in the film ''[[Dunkirk (2017 film)|Dunkirk]]'' (2017), believing that he "makes me think of Eros as a character".<ref name="ZhaoCastingStyles" /> [[WWE]] wrestler [[Becky Lynch]] had a role in the film for a planned post-credits scene that was intended to set up a future project. The scene was removed for being reportedly "too depressing".<ref name="BeckyLynch" />

== Marketing ==
[[File:Eternals Futures exhibit at the Smithsonian Institution.jpg|thumb|250px|Select costumes from the film on display as part of the ''Futures'' exhibit at the [[Smithsonian Institution]]]]
The first footage of the film was released in May 2021, as part of a promotional video from Marvel Studios celebrating their films and a return to theaters in light of the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="VergeSizzleFootage" /><ref name="NerdistSizzleFootage" /> Though the footage was limited and "vague", Hoai-Tran Bui at ''[[/Film]]'' still felt it was "very exciting".<ref name="/FilmSizzleFootage" /> Chaim Gartenberg of ''[[The Verge]]'' felt one of the biggest moments of the footage was seeing Jolie wielding a sword made of light. He was also encouraged that ''Eternals'' appeared to be one of Marvel's first films to "deliver on [their] years-long promise of creating films with more diverse casts".<ref name="VergeSizzleFootage" /> ''[[Nerdist]]''{{'s}} Michael Arbeiter said the footage was quick, but felt that it "manages an air of wonderment".<ref name="NerdistSizzleFootage" />

The first teaser for the film was released on May 24, 2021. Gartenberg felt the teaser was light on plot elements, with it instead focusing on the "civilization-spanning scope of the superhero team and its members throughout human history". He also believed the film was "a big swing" from Marvel Studios to entice audiences with a lesser-known comics property, but believed that it would succeed due to its diverse cast of well-known actors and Zhao's status as "one of the most exciting directors around".<ref name="VergeTeaser" /> ''[[io9]]''{{'s}} Charlies Pulliam-Moore felt the trailer was a "multiple millennia-spanning recap of Earth's history", and said it was unclear how much the larger MCU would impact on the film outside of a brief reference to the Avengers at the end of the trailer.<ref name="io9Teaser" /> Writing for ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'', Nick Romano felt the teaser was a combination of "thrilling, goosebump-inducing moments" and some jokes.<ref name="EWTeaser" /> Erik Adams of ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' felt the spot teased some fresh angles for the MCU in a similar way to ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'' (2017) and ''Guardians of the Galaxy'' (2014), and he enjoyed the images of Kingo's Bollywood dance, Sprite performing karaoke, and the end scene of the Eternals eating together and joking about the Avengers.<ref name="AVClubTeaser" /> After seeing the teaser, ''Variety''{{'s}} Adam B. Vary described the film as "unquestionably a Chloé Zhao movie", but wished more action set pieces were featured in the teaser to see how Zhao would approach them in the film.<ref name="VarietyTeaser" /> Adele Ankers of ''[[IGN]]'' discussed the poster that was released at the same time as the trailer, highlighting how the Eternals appear in silhouette against a sunlit background which she described as "another taste of Chloé Zhao's signature filming style and the use of natural light to illuminate a frame" that would be seen in the film.<ref name="IGNTeaserPoster" /> Upon release, the teaser quickly became the number one trending video on [[YouTube]] and amassed 77 million global views in its first 24 hours.<ref name="TeaserViews" /> Disney's president of marketing Asad Ayaz said the teaser was designed to just be an introduction to the characters and tone and not give much of the film away, adding the marketing team would be "very judicious" on when more material would be revealed, while also using the releases of ''Black Widow'' and ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]'' before ''Eternals'' to help expose audiences to the characters.<ref name="AyazJun2021" />

The final trailer was released in August 2021, with Ethan Shanfeld and Manori Ravindran of ''Variety'' feeling it was "more serious in tone" than the teaser with the Eternals "grappling with the prospect of emerging after centuries living apart in order to help humans".<ref name="VarietyFinalTrailer" /> ''Entertainment Weekly''{{'s}} Devan Coggan felt that the trailer was the "best glimpse yet" of the film and gave a "sense of the vast scale and scope of the film", while answering "one important question" about the absence of the Eternals during the conflict against [[Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thanos]] in ''Avengers: Infinity War'' and ''Avengers: Endgame''.<ref name="EWFinalTrailer" /> Aaron Couch at ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' said the trailer "reveals much of [the film's] plot as well as its characters' powers, teasing the answers to key questions about who the Eternals answer to, their relationship to Earth and why they ultimately didn't intervene" in the conflict against Thanos.<ref name="THRFinalTrailer" />

In October 2021, [[Lexus]] released a commercial promoting the film and its [[Lexus IS#XE30|Lexus IS 500]] sports sedan starring Nanjiani as Kingo, with frequent MCU directors [[Joe and Anthony Russo]] guiding its development and [[Framestore]] working on the visual effects.<ref name="Lexus" /> Lexus created ten [[concept cars]] based on the ten Eternals characters from the film.<ref name="LexusConcept" /> The [[Lexus LS#XF50|LS 500h]] and [[Lexus NX#AZ10|NX]] are featured in the film.<ref name="Lexus" /> Beginning on November 20, 2021, select costumes from the film were part of the ''Futures'' exhibit at the [[Smithsonian Institution]]'s [[Arts and Industries Building]], which ran through July 2022. Behind-the-scenes content looking at the evolution of the film's visuals as well as an augmented reality experience were also included in the exhibit.<ref name="SmithsonianCostumes" />

== Release ==
===Theatrical===
''Eternals'' had its world premiere at the [[Dolby Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on October 18, 2021,<ref name="WorldPremiere" /> and was screened at the [[Rome Film Festival]] on October 24.<ref name="RomeFilmFest" /> The film was released theatrically in many European countries on November 3, and in the United States and the United Kingdom on November 5 in [[RealD 3D]] and [[4DX]].<ref name="RomeFilmFest" /> In September 2021, Disney announced that the film would have an exclusive theatrical release for a minimum of 45 days.<ref name="45DayTheatricalRelease" /> ''Eternals'' was previously set for release on November 6, 2020,<ref name="MarvelSDCC" /> before it was shifted to February 12, 2021,<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> and then to the November 2021 date, due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="Phase4DelayAgain" /> It is part of Phase Four of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFour" />

==== Censorship ====
In May 2021, a Chinese state media report excluded ''Eternals'', as well as ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'', from its list of upcoming MCU films releasing, which ''Variety'' noted "added to rumors" that the films would not be released in China, especially since Zhao had become "an unexpected ''[[persona non grata]]''" in the country after she was scrutinized by Chinese [[netizens]] over her remarks in a 2013 interview with ''[[Filmmaker (magazine)|Filmmaker]]'' magazine in which she described China as "a place where there are lies everywhere".<ref name="VarietyChinaReleaseMay2021" /><ref name="VarietyGoldenGlobesMarch2021" /><ref name="SouthChinaMorningPostApril2021" /> By September 2021, ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' reported it remained "an open question" if the Chinese government or Zhao would attempt to "rehabilitate" the situation, but it "seems likely" the film would not be released in China following the country's response to ''Nomadland'' as well as ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' likely not being released in the country either following backlash to comments made by star [[Simu Liu]] in 2017.<ref name="DeadlineChinaReleaseSep2021" />

The film was not released in [[Saudi Arabia]], [[Kuwait]], [[Qatar]], [[Bahrain]] and [[Oman]] due to the depiction of a gay couple. Theaters in the [[United Arab Emirates]], [[Jordan]], [[Lebanon]], and [[Egypt]] were set to show an edited version of the film which is missing all the love scenes.<ref name="MiddleEastBan" />  Angelina Jolie criticized the countries' decision to ban the film, saying: "I'm sad for [those audiences]. And I'm proud of Marvel for refusing to cut those scenes out. I still don't understand how we live in a world today where there's still [people who] would not see the family Phastos has and the beauty of that relationship and that love. How anybody is angry about it, threatened by it, doesn't approve or appreciate it is ignorant."<ref name="JolieGayScenes" /> Lebanese actor [[Haaz Sleiman]], who played Phastos' husband Ben in the film, also voiced the same reaction towards the matter: "They stood their ground and said, 'Nope, we are not going to compromise the integrity of our film.' It made these Arab countries {{sic|looks}} so ignorant and pathetic. I have no respect for those governments. They have displayed to the world that they are not only a disgrace to humanity, but to God. Hopefully this will inspire the Saudi people, the Kuwaiti people and the people in Qatar to fight back."<ref name="MiddleEast" /> Marvel later agreed to cut any kind of love scenes, including the gay couple kiss for the film's release in [[Indonesia]], which received praise from the Indonesian [[Film Censorship Board]] (LSF).<ref name="IndoCensor" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Eternals'' grossed $164.9{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, and $237.2{{nbsp}}million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $402.1{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="BOM" /> The film's opening weekend earned $162&nbsp;million globally, which was the second-largest worldwide opening weekend in the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] for a Hollywood film, of which [[IMAX]] contributed to over $13.6&nbsp;million.<ref name="EternalsPostTrak" />

==== Pre-sale tickets and projections ====
Advanced ticket sales for ''Eternals'' were estimated to be $2.6&nbsp;million in its first 24 hours, outpacing those for ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' ($1.4&nbsp;million) and ''Black Widow'' ($2&nbsp;million) during the same time frame, while [[AMC Theatres]] had the largest first-day sales of 2021 for the film.<ref name="DeadlineAdvanceTickets" /> In November, [[Fandango Media|Fandango]] reported that pre-sales for ''Eternals'' were the second-largest of 2021, behind ''Black Widow''.<ref name="FandangoProjections" /> In October, ''[[Boxoffice Pro]]'' initially projected that the film would earn $82–102&nbsp;million within its opening weekend, and around $210–280&nbsp;million in total domestic box office.<ref name="BoxofficeProProjections" /> At the end of the month, ''Boxoffice Pro'' modified their projections to $67–92&nbsp;million for the film's opening weekend, and around $165–215&nbsp;million in total domestic box office, due to mixed early critical reception.<ref name="BoxofficeProProjections2" /> According to ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'', ''Eternals'' was expected to earn $75&nbsp;million within its domestic opening weekend, and around $150&nbsp;million globally.<ref name="WorldwideOpening" />

==== Performance ====
In the United States and Canada, ''Eternals'' earned $30.7&nbsp;million on its opening day, which included $9.5&nbsp;million from Thursday night previews, marking the third-largest opening day of the pandemic, behind ''Black Widow'' and ''[[Venom: Let There Be Carnage]]''. Its opening weekend earned $71.3&nbsp;million from 5.5&nbsp;million theater admissions, making it the top film of the weekend.<ref name="BoxOfficeMojo" /> IMAX accounted for over $7.6&nbsp;million over the weekend. This marked the fourth-largest opening weekend of the pandemic. The domestic opening weekend gross was lower than various pre-release projections. ''Deadline'' attributed this to the film's overall mixed reception from critics and audiences.<ref name="EternalsPostTrak" /> In its second weekend, ''Eternals'' remained the top film, grossing over $27.5&nbsp;million.<ref name="BO2ndWknd" /> ''Eternals'' became the [[List of American films of 2021|sixth-highest-grossing film of 2021 in the United States]].<ref name="2021Boxoffice" />

Outside of North America, ''Eternals'' earned over $90.7&nbsp;million within its opening weekend from 46 markets. It was number one in nearly all of these markets. The international opening weekend gross had exceeded various pre-release projections. IMAX contributed to $6&nbsp;million of the opening gross, from 58 countries. The film scored the largest opening of the pandemic in Italy, Brazil, and Hong Kong. In South Korea, ''Eternals'' earned $14.4&nbsp;million, marking the largest debut for a Hollywood film amid the pandemic.<ref name="InternationalOpening" /> In Russia, the film earned an opening of $5.4&nbsp;million through six days. In its second weekend, ''Eternals'' earned $48&nbsp;million from 49 markets, a drop of 49%. It remained number one in many of these territories.<ref name="InternationalGross" /> The following weekend, the film earned $22.7&nbsp;million. It was the top film of the weekend internationally, remaining the top film in many territories, including all markets across Latin America, except for Mexico.<ref name="InternationalBO" /> As of November 28, 2021, the film's largest markets were South Korea ($26.4&nbsp;million), the United Kingdom ($18.7&nbsp;million), France ($14.9&nbsp;million), Mexico ($14.3&nbsp;million), and Brazil ($11.1&nbsp;million).<ref name="InternationalMarketGross" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reports an approval rating of {{RT data|score}} based on {{RT data|count}} reviews, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The website's critical consensus reads, "An ambitious superhero epic that soars as often as it strains, ''Eternals'' takes the MCU in intriguing—and occasionally confounding—new directions."<ref name="Rotten Tomatoes">{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=eternals |type=m |title=Eternals |access-date=February 15, 2023}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 52 out of 100, based on 62 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=eternals |type=movie |title=Eternals Reviews |access-date=January 14, 2022}}</ref> Upon release, it became the lowest-rated MCU film on both websites and the first installment to be classified as "rotten" on Rotten Tomatoes.<ref name="EternalsLowestRT" /> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale, the lowest grade for an MCU film at the time of its release, and [[PostTrak]] reported 78% of audience members gave it a positive score, with 60% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="EternalsPostTrak" />

Charlotte O'Sullivan of the ''[[Evening Standard]]'' described the film as "lavish" and "ambitious", and praised the fight sequences as "astounding, beautifully paced and crammed with detail".<ref name="EveningStandardReview" /> Robert Abele of ''[[TheWrap]]'' commended the cinematography and felt "an earnestness to the operatic stakes".<ref name="TheWrapReview" /> Oliver Jones of the ''[[Observer.com|Observer]]'' called it "full of wonder and romance and fueled by an agenda and audacity all its own".<ref name="ObserverReview" /> [[Owen Gleiberman]] was disappointed over Zhao's missing filmmaking style that shaped her films ''[[The Rider (film)|The Rider]]'' (2017) and ''[[Nomadland]]'' (2020) to "embrace the straight-up expository conventionality of Marvel filmmaking", but felt that the film was a "squarely fun and gratifying watch".<ref name="GleibermanVariety" /> Moira Macdonald of ''[[The Seattle Times]]'' thought "it's got some pretty slow spots midfilm and it's desperately in need of a bit more wit ... what it does have is a palpable, artful mood; this is a movie full of superheroes who spend time thinking and feeling, and of special effects that aren't just zippy but often delicately elegant".<ref name="TheSeattleTimesReview" />

Linda Marric of ''[[The Jewish Chronicle]]'' described it as "a mishmash of well-meaning, yet jarringly verbose and bafflingly incoherent nonsense which is only just about saved by some half decent performances";<ref name="TheJewishChronicleReview" /> Nicholas Barber of ''[[BBC Culture]]'' called Zhao's direction "workmanlike";<ref name="BBCReview" /> and Clarisse Loughrey of ''[[The Independent]]'' said that it "strives for the same expansive soulfulness" as ''Nomadland'' "but discovers there's room enough only for the occasional burst of it".<ref name="TheIndependentReview" /> Brian Truitt of ''[[USA Today]]'' praised Zhao's "penchant for naturalistic environments", but wrote that the narrative "struggles to juggle its many subplots and tries to do too much".<ref name="USATodayReview" /> Mark Kennedy of the [[Associated Press]] criticized the dialogue and fight scenes, but praised the visual effects and Nanjiani's performance.<ref name="AssociatedPressReview" /> Shirley Li of ''[[The Atlantic]]'' felt that "Zhao's delicate examination of her characters outshines ''Eternals''{{'}} duller and more convoluted moments".<ref name="TheAtlanticReview" />

[[Robbie Collin]] wrote the film was "constantly engaged in a kind of grit-toothed authenticity theatre, going out of its way to show you it's doing all the things proper cinema does, even though none of them bring any discernible benefit";<ref name="TheDailyTelegraphReview" /> [[Kevin Maher (writer)|Kevin Maher]] felt "a strange self-sabotaging energy at the heart";<ref name="TheTimesReview" /> and Steve Rose of ''[[The Guardian]]'' missed "the breezy wit of Marvel's best movies ... like coming into ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' cold without having seen any of the preceding installments".<ref name="TheGuardianReview" /> K. Austin Collins of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' felt that the film was "good at telling us where to look, at impressing us with its manufactured sense of grandeur", but lacked "any credible sense of what's actually worth seeing".<ref name="RollingStoneReview" /> [[Justin Chang]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' had a "depressing realization that you've just seen one of the more interesting movies Marvel will ever make, and hopefully the least interesting one Chloé Zhao will ever make".<ref name="LosAngelesTimesReview" /> Brian Lowry of [[CNN]] thought "the movie's structural flaws offset its stunning visuals and strong performances".<ref name="CNNReview" /> [[Kyle Smith (critic)|Kyle Smith]] of ''[[National Review]]'' was harsher, describing it as "one of the dumbest, cheesiest, most trite, and least human" of the MCU films.<ref name="SmithNationalReview" />

The mixed reception surprised some commentators, given the MCU's positive track record and the acclaim of Zhao's previous work. Richard Newby of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' wrote that "much of the criticism of Kirby's work", which was divisive for its subject matter and relationship to the wider [[Marvel Universe]], "has followed Zhao in her adaptation." He compared the divided response to that of Zack Snyder's [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU) films ''Man of Steel'' and ''[[Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice]]'' (2016), writing that they and ''Eternals'' share "a deconstructive approach to superheroes and force them to question their purpose in the world, through meditative and melancholy narrative beats, and a tragic yet hopeful ending." This approach, he felt, subverted critics' and audiences' expectations, which made them review the film more harshly.<ref name="THRDividing" /> Zhao addressed some of the criticisms of the film, believing some of the division came from everyone "having an existential crisis" because of the COVID-19 pandemic and the film being an "existential crisis, both for humanity and God". She also felt the reactions to how the film incorporated her filmmaking sensibilities with Marvel's were "a testament to how much we had merged with each other; how uncomfortable that might make people feel".<ref name="ZhaoCriticismResponse" />

== Future ==
In October 2021, Zhao said she was open to making a sequel.<ref name="ZhaoPlaylistOct2021" /> The next month, Kaz and Ryan Firpo expressed interest in making a prequel series for Disney+ that would focus on one Eternal in a different time period each episode, such as an episode about Thena in Ancient Greece and one about Kingo in 1890s Mumbai where he balances his life as a film star with [[Mahatma Gandhi]]'s [[Indian independence movement|campaign for India's independence]] from [[British Raj|British rule]]. They also felt there were opportunities to continue the film's story by further exploring Dane Whitman and showing the Eternals confronting the Celestials.<ref name="THRFirpoNov2021" /> Chan said in January 2022 that she would reprise the role of Sersi in the future.<ref name="ChanJan2022" /> Feige was asked about Styles's future in the MCU that July and said Marvel Studios was excited to further explore the characters Eros and Pip as part of the MCU's more cosmic stories.<ref name="FeigeHarryStylesJul2022" /> A month later, Oswalt said a sequel had been confirmed by Marvel Studios and Zhao was returning to direct. He hoped it would further explore Eros and Pip, and expressed interest in a spin-off film for the characters.<ref name="OswaltAug2022" /> In October, Moore said Marvel intended to bring back the Eternals characters in the future of the MCU.<ref name="MooreCharactersReturn" /> Nanjiani said the following month that he was unaware of a sequel and believed Oswalt was mistaken, but expressed interest in reprising his role as Kingo.<ref name="NanjianiUnclear" /> Don Lee's talent agency indicated in December that he would reprise his role as Gilgamesh in an ''Eternals'' sequel.<ref name="LeeDec2022" /> Oswalt clarified his statements in February 2023, saying a sequel was not in development at that time and that he had been misled.<ref name="OswaltNoSequel" /> In July 2024, Feige said there were no plans for a sequel at the time.<ref name="FeigeJul2024" /> In August 2025, Nanjiani said the film's poor reception "shattered [him]" and tempered his expectations of reprising the role for the next ten years.<ref name="NanjianiContract" />

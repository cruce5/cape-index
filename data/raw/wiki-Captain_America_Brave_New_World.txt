'''''Captain America: Brave New World''''' is a 2025 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the character [[Falcon (comics)|Sam Wilson / Captain America]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is the fourth installment in the [[Captain America (film series)|''Captain America'' film series]], a continuation of the television miniseries ''[[The Falcon and the Winter Soldier]]'' (2021), and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 35th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Julius Onah]] from a screenplay by [[Rob Edwards (screenwriter)|Rob Edwards]] and the writing teams of [[Malcolm Spellman]] & [[Dalan Musson]] and Onah & [[Peter Glanz (filmmaker)|Peter Glanz]]. It stars [[Anthony Mackie]] as [[Sam Wilson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sam Wilson / Captain America]] alongside [[Danny Ramirez]], [[Shira Haas]], [[Carl Lumbly]], [[Xosha Roquemore]], [[Giancarlo Esposito]], [[Liv Tyler]], [[Tim Blake Nelson]], and [[Harrison Ford]]. In the film, Wilson investigates a conspiracy involving [[President of the United States|U.S. president]] [[Thaddeus Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thaddeus Ross]] (Ford).

''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016) ended the ''Captain America'' trilogy starring [[Chris Evans (actor)|Chris Evans]] as [[Steve Rogers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Steve Rogers]], and Wilson becomes the new Captain America in ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''. Series writers Spellman and Musson were writing a new Captain America film by April 2021, and Mackie signed on that August. Onah joined in July 2022, when the title ''Captain America: New World Order'' was announced. Additional cast members joined later that year. Nelson and Tyler returned from the second MCU film, ''[[The Incredible Hulk (film)|The Incredible Hulk]]'' (2008), while Ford replaced [[William Hurt]] as Ross following Hurt's death in March 2022; Ross becomes the superpowered [[Red Hulk]] in the film. Filming took place from March to June 2023 at [[Trilith Studios]] in [[Atlanta]], Georgia, with additional filming in [[Washington, D.C.]] The subtitle was changed to ''Brave New World'' during filming. Matthew Orton joined in December 2023 to write for [[Pick-up (filmmaking)|reshoots]], which took place between May and November 2024, and added Esposito to the film. The involvement of Edwards and Glanz was revealed that December.

''Captain America: Brave New World'' premiered on February 11, 2025, at the [[TCL Chinese Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], and was released in the United States on February 14 as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU. It grossed $415&nbsp;million worldwide. <!-- CRITICAL RESPONSE WORDING DETERMINED BY TALK PAGE CONSENSUS. PLEASE DO NOT CHANGE IT WITHOUT NEW CONSENSUS. The performances, particularly those of Mackie and Ford, received praise.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per [[WP:FILMPLOT]], plot summaries for feature film articles should be between 400 and 700 words. Current count: 686 -->
Five months after [[Thaddeus Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thaddeus Ross]] is elected [[president of the United States]], he sends [[Sam Wilson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sam Wilson]] and [[Joaquin Torres (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Joaquin Torres]]—the new Captain America and Falcon, respectively—to [[Oaxaca]], Mexico, to stop the illegal sale of classified items stolen by [[Seth Voelker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sidewinder]] and his mercenary group [[Serpent Society (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Serpent]]. Wilson and Torres recover the items, but Sidewinder escapes. Torres is excited to be taking on Wilson's former mantle of Falcon, but Wilson is hesitant to involve Torres in dangerous missions due to the pair not having superpowers like former Captain America [[Steve Rogers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Steve Rogers]]. After the mission, Wilson and Torres train with [[Isaiah Bradley (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Isaiah Bradley]], a super soldier who was imprisoned and experimented on by the U.S. government.

Ross invites Wilson and Torres to a summit with world leaders at the [[White House]], and Wilson accepts on the condition that Bradley is also invited. Ross asks Wilson to help him reform the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]]. During the summit, Ross explains that a new metal, [[Adamantium (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|adamantium]], has been discovered on "Celestial Island", which was formed when the [[Celestials (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Celestial]] [[Tiamut (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tiamut]] emerged in the Indian Ocean.{{efn|As depicted in the film ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'' (2021)<ref name="EasterEggsMW" />}} The recovered items were the first refined samples of the metal and were stolen from a Japanese mining operation. To avoid an [[arms race]], Ross proposes a treaty to govern adamantium's mining and distribution. As he is talking, the song "[[Mr. Blue]]" plays and causes several men, including Bradley, to begin shooting at Ross and other dignitaries. Apprehended by Ross's head of security, former [[Black Widows (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Black Widow]] [[Ruth Bat-Seraph (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ruth Bat-Seraph]], the men regain their senses and deny any knowledge of the attack.

While investigating, Wilson is ambushed by Sidewinder and captures him. Torres tracks a call on Sidewinder's phone to a hidden black site in West Virginia called Camp Echo One. Ross tries to salvage the treaty, but the Japanese government blames him for the theft of their adamantium and the subsequent White House attack. Ross realizes that the mastermind behind these events is Dr. [[Samuel Sterns (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Samuel Sterns]], who gained advanced intelligence after being exposed to the blood of [[Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bruce Banner / Hulk]] during the [[Abomination (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Abomination]]'s rampage through [[Harlem]].{{efn|As depicted in the film ''[[The Incredible Hulk (film)|The Incredible Hulk]]'' (2008)<ref name="EasterEggsMW" />}} Ross imprisoned Sterns at Camp Echo One, publicly blamed him for the Abomination's actions, and promised to release him if Sterns helped advance him to the presidency. Wilson and Torres find Sterns and learn how he uses technology and the song "Mr. Blue" to control minds. He escapes while they are fighting mind-controlled soldiers.

Ross sends Bat-Seraph to secure Camp Echo One, where she finds and helps Wilson and Torres. The trio meet with Wilson's military friend [[Dennis Dunphy (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dennis Dunphy]], who has Sidewinder in custody. Wilson learns enough from Sidewinder to deduce Sterns's plan to destroy Ross's reputation. Wilson, Torres, and Bat-Seraph go to Celestial Island where Ross and Ozaki, the [[Prime Minister of Japan]], are racing to claim ownership of adamantium. Ross tells Wilson that he is dying of [[heart failure]] and had Sterns develop pills that have prolonged his life; Ross refused to release Sterns for fear that he would no longer make the pills, leading to Sterns's anger and plans for revenge. Sterns takes control of the minds of two American pilots and makes them attack the Japanese fleet. Wilson and Torres intercept the planes and convince the Japanese to stand down, but Torres is injured in the fight.

Wilson is consoled by his friend [[Bucky Barnes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bucky Barnes]]. Sterns kills Dunphy, who learned that the pills have been adding [[gamma radiation]] to Ross's body, and surrenders himself to Wilson. Sterns's arrest and connection to Ross are publicly revealed while the latter is at a press conference. Ross loses control of his emotions and transforms into [[Red Hulk|a red Hulk]], destroying part of the White House. Wilson injures Ross enough that he can be reasoned with, and reminds Ross of visits to [[Washington, D.C.]]'s [[cherry blossom]] trees with his estranged daughter [[Betty Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Betty]]. Ross reverts to normal. After the incident, Bradley is exonerated, Wilson invites a recovering Torres to join the Avengers, and the treaty is ratified. Ross resigns and has himself incarcerated at the [[Raft (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Raft]], where Wilson and Betty visit him. In a [[post-credits scene]], an imprisoned Sterns warns Wilson of a coming attack from other worlds.{{efn|Identified off-screen as pertaining to the alternate universes of the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]]<ref name="MultiversePostCredits" />}}

== Production ==
=== Development ===
{{See also|The Falcon and the Winter Soldier{{!}}''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''}}
[[Marvel Studios]] president [[Kevin Feige]] said in October 2015 that ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016) was the conclusion of the ''Captain America'' trilogy starring [[Chris Evans (actor)|Chris Evans]] as [[Steve Rogers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Steve Rogers / Captain America]], after ''[[Captain America: The First Avenger]]'' (2011) and ''[[Captain America: The Winter Soldier]]'' (2014).<ref name="Trilogy" /> ''Civil War'' was Evans's last contracted standalone [[Captain America]] film,<ref name="EvansCapContract" /> but he was open to extending his contract beyond the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]] films ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018) and ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019).<ref name="EvansContract" /> In January 2021, Evans was reportedly close to signing a deal to return as Rogers in at least one project. Evans's involvement was said to be similar to how fellow MCU star [[Robert Downey Jr.]] had supporting roles as [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark / Iron Man]] in other MCU films following the ''Iron Man'' trilogy.<ref name="EvansJan2021" /> Evans said the report was "news to [him]".<ref name="EvansJan2021Deny" />

[[File:Anthony Mackie by Gage Skidmore 2.jpg|right|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Anthony Mackie speaking at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con in California|Anthony Mackie at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con announcing the series ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'', in which his character Sam Wilson accepts the mantle of Captain America; this film is a continuation of the series starring Mackie]]
By October 2018, Marvel Studios was developing a limited series for [[Disney+]] starring [[Anthony Mackie]]'s [[Sam Wilson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sam Wilson / Falcon]] and [[Sebastian Stan]]'s [[Bucky Barnes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bucky Barnes / Winter Soldier]] from [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the MCU films]]. [[Malcolm Spellman]] was hired as [[head writer]] of the series,<ref name="FalconWinterSoldier" /><ref name="FalconWinterSoldier2" /> which was officially announced as ''[[The Falcon and the Winter Soldier]]'' in April 2019.<ref name="MarvelDisneyInvestorDay" /> After Rogers bequeaths his [[Captain America's shield|shield]] and the mantle of Captain America to Wilson in ''Endgame'',<ref name="SkoglandF&WS" /> the series shows Wilson accepting the mantle and coming to terms with the implications of that as a Black man. Before the series premiered, Mackie said there had been no discussions regarding a second season and he was not sure when he would next appear in an MCU film because of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="MackieVariety" /> Series director [[Kari Skogland]] was also unsure whether there would be a second season, saying she told the story she wanted to with the first season but there were more stories and characters to explore if a second was made.<ref name="SkoglandSeason2" /> Executive producer [[Nate Moore (film producer)|Nate Moore]] said the series had "evergreen" topics that lent themselves to further exploration and set up ideas for a potential second season.<ref name="MooreS2" /> Feige said there were ideas for what "another one" could be, but Marvel intended for the series to lead into an MCU film first as they did with fellow Disney+ miniseries ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021).<ref name="PressConferenceRecap" />

After the [[series finale]] of ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'', "[[One World, One People]]", was released in April 2021, Spellman and series writer [[Dalan Musson]] were revealed to be writing a fourth Captain America film that continues Wilson's story.<ref name="Cap4THR" /> [[Rob Edwards (screenwriter)|Rob Edwards]] said he was the original writer for the film.<ref name="EdwardsOriginal" /> Evans's reported return as Rogers was expected to come in a different project.<ref name="Cap4Deadline" /> Mackie said he was unaware of any plans for a film or second season, but was excited to see what happened next and said it would be monumental for him to headline an MCU film, particularly as a Black actor.<ref name="MackieEW" /><ref name="MackieUSAToday" /> Mackie negotiated a deal to star in the film over the next few months,<ref name="MackieCap4Negotiations" /> and officially signed on in August.<ref name="MackieCap4" /> He later said he had been excited to make a second season of ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'' with Stan and their co-star [[Daniel Brühl]]—who portrays [[Helmut Zemo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Helmut Zemo]]—and was disappointed by the pivot to starring in a film without them.<ref name="MackiePreferS2" />

=== Pre-production ===
[[Julius Onah]] was hired to direct the film in July 2022,<ref name="Onah" /><ref name="PhaseFiveDirectorsConfirm" /> after several meetings with Moore and co-producer Kyana Davidson in which they discussed character, themes, and tone.<ref name="OnahBreakdownPlaylist" /> Onah was championed to Feige and Mackie by actress [[Octavia Spencer]] who starred in his film ''[[Luce (film)|Luce]]'' (2019); Spencer is included in the "special thanks" section of the film's credits.<ref name="Spencer" /> Key elements were set by the time Onah joined. He said it was always going to be "a Sam Wilson story",<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> and Wilson would not be conflicted about being Captain America: "That question has been resolved. Sam is our Captain America now."<ref name="OnahVFFeb2025" /> The characters [[Samuel Sterns (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Samuel Sterns]] and [[Thaddeus Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thaddeus Ross]] who were first introduced in the second MCU film, ''[[The Incredible Hulk (film)|The Incredible Hulk]]'' (2008), were also set to appear.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> The inclusion of Sterns was one of the reasons Onah was interested in the film, feeling that someone who operates from intellect would be a good adversary for Wilson.<ref name="OnahLeaderExcitement" /> Onah was drawn to the relationships between Wilson and Ross, and between Wilson and [[Joaquin Torres (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Joaquin Torres / Falcon]] who was introduced in ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''. As Onah further developed the film with Spellman and Musson, they also leaned into Wilson's relationship with another ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'' character, [[Isaiah Bradley (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Isaiah Bradley]]. Onah felt these three relationships were integral to Wilson's journey in the film, connecting to his past and future.<ref name="OnahBreakdownTHR" /><ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Isaiah's grandson [[Eli Bradley (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Eli]] was included in early versions of the script, but was removed due to the large number of characters in the film and the focus on other relationships.<ref name="NoEli" /> Onah said the combination of Wilson, Ross, and Sterns organically led to the film's [[Thriller (genre)|thriller]] tone, with elements of [[paranoid thriller]]s, [[political thriller]]s, and [[action thriller]]s. It was described as more grounded than some recent MCU films, closer to the tone of ''The Winter Soldier''.<ref name="OnahBreakdownPlaylist" /><ref name="MackieEWApr2024" /><ref name="CinemaCon2024" />

The filmmakers always intended to bring back Stan for a scene with Barnes and Wilson together, but this was kept secret until the film's release. Onah said there was "no way we were gonna completely divorce these two characters from each other" after ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''. Several different places in the film were considered before they settled on a moment where Barnes provides emotional support for Wilson later in the film.<ref name="OnahBreakdownPlaylist" /><ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Another element planned before Onah joined was the [[Serpent Society (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Serpent Society]], a group of snake-themed villains from the comics. This paid off a joke Marvel made years earlier when announcing the third ''Captain America'' film as ''Captain America: Serpent Society'', before revealing its true subtitle to be ''Civil War''. Moore acknowledged that the team can come across as "goofy", but he considered them to be part of Captain America's "all-time Hall of Fame villain groups" and wanted to treat them seriously.<ref name="SerpentSocietyEW" /> Onah said their challenge in developing the film was trying to fit the different elements into the intended thriller tone. He added that despite the connections to ''The Incredible Hulk'' and ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'', they wanted the film to work on its own.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Mackie said viewers did not need to have seen ''The Incredible Hulk'' to watch the film, calling it a "reset" of the MCU that establishes new themes and antagonists for the universe moving forward.<ref name="MackieEWApr2024" /> Onah said it was important to call back to ''The Incredible Hulk'' as part of Ross's story, but he wanted these elements to be revealed through Wilson's point of view.<ref name="OnahBreakdownVariety" />

Later in July 2022 at [[San Diego Comic-Con]], the film's title was revealed to be ''Captain America: New World Order'' and it was given a release date of May 3, 2024, making it part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five|Phase Five]] of the MCU.<ref name="SDCC2022" /> The subtitle, which is also the name of [[New World Order (The Falcon and the Winter Soldier)|the first episode]] of ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'',<ref name="NWOSubtitleMS" /> was noted for its use in [[New world order (politics)|politics]], [[New World Order (professional wrestling)|professional wrestling]], and various [[New World Order (conspiracy theory)|conspiracy theories]] along with [[Antisemitism|antisemitic]] rhetoric,<ref name="NWOSubtitleGR" /><ref name="BNWSubtitle/Film" /><ref name="BNWSubtitleCollider" /> and was deemed to be a controversial choice.<ref name="BNWSubtitle/Film" /> Several cast members were announced at the [[D23 Expo]] that September: [[Danny Ramirez]] as Joaquin Torres / Falcon and [[Carl Lumbly]] as Isaiah Bradley, both reprising their roles from ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier''; [[Tim Blake Nelson]] as Samuel Sterns, returning from ''The Incredible Hulk''; and Israeli actress [[Shira Haas]] as the comic book character [[Sabra (character)|Sabra]].<ref name="VarietyD23Panel" /> Onah said filming would begin in early 2023.<ref name="OnahFilming" />

The announcement that Sabra, an Israeli superheroine, would be adapted for the film led to criticisms from some people who believed this would lead to negative stereotypical portrayals of Palestinians and Arabs; in the comics, Sabra is a member of the [[Mossad]] and some of the Arab characters she interacts with were perceived to be misogynistic, antisemitic, and violent.<ref name="SabraCNN" /> The name "[[wikt:Sabra|Sabra]]" also has different meanings to Israeli Jews and Palestinians.<ref name="SabraTHRJul2024" /><ref name="SabraNYTimes" /><ref name="SabraVariety" /> The [[Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel]] criticized the use of the character and called the comic book version's "valorization of Mossad" and perceived [[Anti-Palestinianism|racism against Palestinians]] "sickening".<ref name="SabraTHRJul2024" /> [[Yousef Munayyer]], a Palestinian-American writer and analyst, felt the character could not have a positive role in the film based on her depiction in the comics and said turning Israeli spies into heroes was "insensitive and disgraceful". In response, Marvel Studios said the film was taking a new approach to the character, just as other comics characters had been re-imagined for modern audiences when brought into the MCU.<ref name="SabraCNN" /> This led to concerns from some other groups that Marvel was "erasing" the character's Israeli background, which the [[American Jewish Committee]] (AJC) compared to "making Captain America Canadian".<ref name="SabraTheWrapJul2024" /> In the film, the character is a former [[Black Widows (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Black Widow]] and high-ranking U.S. government official named [[Ruth Bat-Seraph (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ruth Bat-Seraph]]. The name Sabra is not used, nor is she a [[Mutant (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|mutant]] or Mossad agent, but the character is depicted as a first-generation Israeli, has an Israeli accent, and Moore said she has the "attitude" and the "essence" of the comics character. He reiterated that the character's backstory was not changed in response to public concerns, and explained that using the Black Widow program to adapt characters who Marvel Studios "didn't want to translate as one-to-one" from the comics was a general idea that came up during the making of the film ''[[Black Widow (2021 film)|Black Widow]]'' (2021).<ref name="NationalNewsFeb2025" /> The character's inclusion prompted some calls to boycott the film, in part due to the [[Gaza war]]; some supporters of Israel took issue with the character having allegiance to the U.S. government, while some who are critical of Israel protested the film's use of the character due to her depiction in the comics.<ref name="SabraTHRJul2024" /> With confirmation that the character would still be depicted as Israeli in the film, the AJC and the [[Anti-Defamation League]] (ADL) both praised the decision.<ref name="SabraTheWrapJul2024" />

[[File:Harrison Ford 2017 crop.jpg|left|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Harrison Ford speaking at the 2017 San Diego Comic-Con in California|Harrison Ford plays new U.S. president Thaddeus Ross in the film, replacing William Hurt who portrayed Ross in the MCU until his death in March 2022.]]
In October 2022, Moore was revealed to be producing ''New World Order'' alongside Feige.<ref name="HaasMarvel" /><ref name="MooreOct2022" /> [[Harrison Ford]] was cast as Thaddeus Ross that month, replacing [[William Hurt]] who portrayed the character in the MCU until his death in March 2022. Ford was reported to be appearing as Ross in this film as well as the following MCU film ''[[Thunderbolts*]]'' (2025).<ref name="FordConfirm" /><ref name="SneiderFordRossNWO" /> Marvel Studios planned for Ross to transform into [[Red Hulk]] in the film. Referencing the character's history of hunting for [[Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bruce Banner / Hulk]], Moore said: "Turning a guy who hunted Hulks into a Hulk himself makes him more than an antagonist; it makes him a tragic character."<ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /> The studio informed Hurt of their plans to have Ross become Red Hulk and he had been eager to portray that aspect of the character.<ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /> They considered changing course with Ross's role in the film following Hurt's death, believing no actor would want to take over from him.<ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /> During that time, Ford reached out to Feige about possibly joining the MCU and agreed to take on the role without seeing a script.<ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /><ref name="FordJoinNoScript" /> Ford saw playing Ross as an opportunity to honor what Hurt had done with the character and also continue with a [[character arc]] that he had enjoyed from the previous films. In the film, Ross becomes the [[president of the United States]] and finds himself at odds with Wilson amidst a global conspiracy. Moore drew comparisons to the 1974 comic book storyline ''[[Secret Empire (1974 comic)|Secret Empire]]''. He noted that Wilson wears a costume featuring the colors of the American flag while facing off with the U.S. president, and said the film's story was about "the notion of a guy who believes an ideal, realizing that ideal is not always held up to the same standards by other people, and feeling really betrayed by where the American government [has] gone".<ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /> Onah drew comparisons to Ford's film ''[[Clear and Present Danger (film)|Clear and Present Danger]]'' (1994), which features a similar conspiracy involving the president. He wanted to feature themes of bringing people together and focusing on shared experiences, believing these were central to Captain America and Sam Wilson's character, which he thought had "political resonance" but was more about humanity than politics. Marvel Studios was supportive of this, despite Feige joking about Onah trying to "save America" with the film.<ref name="OnahVFFeb2025" />

Pre-production work at [[Trilith Studios]] in [[Atlanta]], Georgia, began on November 7 ahead of a planned filming start in March 2023.<ref name="AJCFilming" /> Onah directed cinematographer [[Kramer Morgenthau]], production designer Ramsey Avery, and costume designer Gersha Phillips to be specific about how they used the color red in the film, avoiding it in many scenes to make the appearance of Red Hulk at the end of the film more impactful. When red is used in earlier scenes, it is to signify "chaos" while blue tones represent "peacefulness and a sense of things being under control".<ref name="ColorRed" /> Avery, who worked on the MCU films ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2]]'' (2017) and ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017),<ref name="Avery" /> reiterated that their intent was for the film to be more grounded in reality than recent MCU films.<ref name="AveryGrounded" /> He said it was important to Onah and Feige that the film feel like it was taking place in "the current space... a very real and honest world".<ref name="AveryFxguide" /> Mackie's Captain America costume was redesigned from the one seen in ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'', which was based on the mostly-white costume that Wilson is known for wearing as Captain America in the comics. The new costume for ''Brave New World'' is mostly dark blue, with red and white accents, closer to the costumes Evans wore in ''The Winter Soldier'' and other films.<ref name="NewCostume" /> The cowl that Mackie wore for ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'' was removed at his request, as he struggled with "the heat, the sweat, the fogged-up glasses" and said it was his "worst nightmare".<ref name="NoCowl" /> [[Ryan Meinerding]], Marvel Studios' head of visual development, worked on the costume.<ref name="ComicCoversFeb2025" />

[[Xosha Roquemore]] joined the cast in January 2023.<ref name="Roquemore" /> Mackie expected to begin work on March 1.<ref name="MackieFilmingStart" /> That month, [[Washington, D.C.]]'s Office of Cable Television, Film, Music and Entertainment said Marvel Studios would be filming in the city in June.<ref name="DCOffice" /> Also in early 2023, Onah reached out to [[Liv Tyler]] about reprising her role as Ross's daughter [[Betty Ross (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Betty]] from ''The Incredible Hulk'';<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Tyler was confirmed to be returning soon after filming began.<ref name="Tyler" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began on March 21, 2023, at Trilith Studios in Atlanta, Georgia,<ref name="AJCFilming" /><ref name="FilmingStart" /> with Kramer Morgenthau as cinematographer.<ref name="Morgenthau" /> The film was made under the [[working title]] ''Rochelle Rochelle'', a reference to the television series ''[[Seinfeld]]'' (1989–1998) that was suggested by Davidson.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Morgenthau, who worked on the MCU film ''[[Thor: The Dark World]]'' (2013), said they wanted to pay homage to the visuals of thriller films from the 1970s such as ''[[Klute]]'' (1971), ''[[The Day of the Jackal (film)|The Day of the Jackal]]'' (1973), ''[[The Parallax View]]'' (1974), and ''[[All the President's Men (film)|All the President's Men]]'' (1976).<ref name="MorgenthauCredits" /> Wanting a look as close to [[photographic film]] as could be achieved with digital cameras, they tested with vintage [[anamorphic]] lenses and made a set of rules for camera movement, color, composition, and texture.<ref name="AveryFxguide" /> Early in filming, there was a week of character-focused scenes featuring Mackie, Ramirez, and Ford;<ref name="OnahBreakdownTHR" /> Ramirez's first scene was the film's final moment between Wilson and Torres.<ref name="RamirezInterview" /> At the end of March, [[Julia Louis-Dreyfus]] was reported to be reprising her MCU role as [[Valentina Allegra de Fontaine (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valentina Allegra de Fontaine]] in the film.<ref name="JLDExpected" />

Onah wanted the action scenes to be "grounded and tactile", and give Wilson things to do that were not seen in his previous appearances.<ref name="OnahFilming" /> While Wilson is at "peak human performance" and aided by technology, Onah kept in mind that the character does not have superpowers.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> The start of the [[2023 Writers Guild of America strike]] in May was not expected to impact the film's production, with Marvel Studios reportedly planning to shoot what they could during principal photography and make any necessary writing adjustments during the film's already scheduled [[Pick-up (filmmaking)|reshoots]].<ref name="WGAStrikeImpact" /> Later in May, set photos revealed that [[Seth Rollins]] was part of the cast and led to speculation that he was portraying a member of the Serpent Society.<ref name="Rollins" /> In June, Marvel Studios announced the new title ''Captain America: Brave New World''.<ref name="BraveNewWorld" /> Jeremy Mathai at ''[[/Film]]'' praised the change, saying it "strikes a far more optimistic perspective [than ''New World Order''], doubling as a commentary on the future of the famous superhero in the Marvel Cinematic Universe {{em|and}} as a statement on some very overdue representation".<ref name="BNWSubtitle/Film" /> The change was also deemed wise by ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'s}} Hilary Remley, who noted that the original title could have been interpreted as having antisemitic connotations.<ref name="BNWSubtitleCollider" /> Moore explained that Marvel Studios felt ''New World Order'' was "really interesting, and sort of moody and scary" for a subtitle and was not intended to represent real-world issues. The studio decided to change the title after receiving feedback about how the phrase "New World Order" had been "co-opted in the real world in a way that made people uncomfortable".<ref name="MooreMackieEmpireDec2024" /> At the time of the new subtitle's announcement, Onah was revealed to have co-written the script.<ref name="BraveNewWorld" /> The film's release date was soon delayed to July 26, 2024.<ref name="Jul2024Release" />

[[File:Washington monument and cherry blossoms - Washington DC - 2014-04-10 (13772901163).jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Cherry blossom trees surrounding the Washington Monument|Location filming took place in Washington, D.C., and the city's cherry blossom trees are seen throughout the film.]]
Onah said Washington, D.C., was an important location for the film because it is where Ross is based as president of the U.S., but also because it is where Wilson was first introduced to the MCU in ''The Winter Soldier'', and it is close to Isaiah's home in [[Baltimore]], Maryland.<ref name="OnahBreakdownVariety" /> The [[cherry blossom]] trees that are planted around D.C. are featured throughout the film,<ref name="OnahVFFeb2025" /> including during Wilson's climactic fight with Red Hulk.<ref name="EWTeaser" /><ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /> Onah said he had vivid memories of the cherry blossoms from the time he lived near D.C. in [[Arlington, Virginia]], as a child, and to him they aligned with the film's themes of empathy since they were gifts to the U.S. from Japan.<ref name="OnahVFFeb2025" /> Several D.C. locations were recreated by the production at Trilith Studios, including the [[Oval Office]], the [[White House Rose Garden]], the [[White House bunker]], and [[Hains Point]]. Filming also took place at a replica of the [[White House]] at [[Tyler Perry Studios]], also in Atlanta. The recreated locations included a mixture of practical and digital cherry blossom trees.<ref name="AveryFxguide" /> At the end of June, filming moved to D.C. for a week of location shooting.<ref name="DCFilming" /><ref name="AveryFxguide" /> Mackie said this brought his MCU experience "full circle", with the production staying at the same hotel and filming in the same areas as they did for ''The Winter Soldier''.<ref name="MackieDCFullCiricle" /> Filming for ''Brave New World'' [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on June 30.<ref name="Wrap" />

=== Post-production ===
By October 2023, there was potential for ''Brave New World'' to return to its May 2024 release date because it was further along in production than Marvel Studios' ''[[Deadpool & Wolverine]]'' (2024), which had taken over that date. However, this was considered unlikely,<ref name="PossibleReleaseMoveMay2024" /> and ''Brave New World''{{'s}} release was instead delayed to February 14, 2025, following the [[2023 SAG-AFTRA strike]]'s conclusion in November 2023.<ref name="Feb2025Release" /> Journalist [[Jeff Sneider]] reported that the film was not received well in an early test screening, that three major sequences were being cut, and that extensive reshoots were planned from January 2024 until that May or June.<ref name="SneiderReshoots" /> In December, {{vanchor|Matthew Orton}} was hired to write additional material for the reshoots,<ref name="OrtonReshoots" /> which were then scheduled to last from early-to-mid-2024. Orton previously worked on the Marvel Studios miniseries ''[[Moon Knight (miniseries)|Moon Knight]]'' (2022). The same day, [[Rosa Salazar]] was revealed to have been cast in the film,<ref name="OrtonReshoots" /> and was later reported to be portraying [[Rachel Leighton (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Diamondback]],<ref name="SalazarDiamondback" /> a member of the Serpent Society. This confirmed speculation that she was playing the character based on her appearance in set photos.<ref name="SalazarDiamondbackSetPhoto" />

[[Mark Ruffalo]] stated in February 2024 that he was reprising his MCU role as Bruce Banner / Hulk in the film.<ref name="Ruffalo" /> Adam B. Vary of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' soon reported that Ruffalo was not appearing in the film, explaining that the actor had misspoken when he was asked about the film and had just intended to express enthusiasm for its upcoming release. Vary noted that there had been ongoing speculation about Ruffalo potentially appearing in the film due to the number of characters from ''The Incredible Hulk'' already included and Ross's transformation into Red Hulk.<ref name="Ruffalo" /><ref name="NoRuffalo" /><ref name="PeopleFordSDCC" /> Onah said they considered including Ruffalo, but felt this would take away from Wilson's story.<ref name="OnahBreakdownPlaylist" />

[[File:Giancarlo Esposito by Gage Skidmore 3.jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|left|alt=Giancarlo Esposito talking at the 2024 Phoenix Fan Fusion event|Giancarlo Esposito joined the film as Sidewinder, leader of the Serpent Society, during reshoots that began in May 2024. His casting was part of efforts to refine the film's political thriller tone and also bring more gravitas to the Serpent Society team.]]
[[Giancarlo Esposito]] revealed in May 2024 that he had been cast in an MCU role and would be appearing "sooner rather than later",<ref name="Esposito" /> with his character being teased in a film before a bigger role in an MCU television series.<ref name="Esposito2" /> He was confirmed to be appearing in ''Brave New World'' at the end of the month as part of the reshoots, which began at that time in Atlanta and were scheduled to last for 22 days. These were expected to be less extensive than the reshoots for the MCU films ''[[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness]]'' (2022) and ''[[The Marvels]]'' (2023), focusing on new action sequences and integrating Esposito.<ref name="EspositoReshoots" /> ''Brave New World'' was also expected to be much less expensive than ''The Marvels'', which cost more than $300&nbsp;million;<ref name="EspositoReshoots" /><ref name="TheMarvelsBudget" /> the budget for ''Brave New World'' was reported to be $180&nbsp;million.<ref name="THRProjection" /> Nelson returned to Atlanta for reshoots in June,<ref name="NelsonSRJun2024" /> and said they redid much of his original work.<ref name="BlakeNelsonTHRSep2024" /> Mackie characterized the reshoots as just adding a few additional scenes to enhance the film and said there was no "retooling or remaking".<ref name="MooreMackieEmpireDec2024" /> Moore said the reshoots were focused on creating the "perfect political thriller tone", and the casting of Esposito was part of this. He believed the actor made the film more grounded despite his "heightened" material, similar to his role in the television series ''[[The Mandalorian]]'' (2019–2023).<ref name="NationalNewsFeb2025" /> Ford and [[Takehiro Hira]] also participated in the reshoots.<ref name="HiraFordReshoots" /> The film's [[post-credits scene]], in which Wilson is warned about coming threats from the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]],<ref name="MultiversePostCredits" /> was filmed during reshoots. Onah explained that they waited until writing on upcoming MCU films had restarted following the writers' strike before they determined an appropriate tease for future projects.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" />

Esposito announced in July 2024 that he was portraying [[Seth Voelker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Seth Voelker / Sidewinder]], leader of the Serpent Society,<ref name="SDCC2024Esposito" /> and said he would return for additional reshoots in mid-November.<ref name="EspositoReshootsNov2024" /> Moore originally told Esposito that he would play [[Cobra (Marvel Comics)|King Cobra]], and the actor enjoyed the notion of an African-American man being called "King", but the filmmakers ultimately decided that he would portray Sidewinder instead,<ref name="EspositoKingCobra" /> in part because Sidewinder is the founder of the Serpent Society in the comics.<ref name="OnahBreakdownWrap" /> Some of King Cobra's characteristics from the comics were given to Sidewinder for the film.<ref name="EspositoKingCobra" /> The remains of [[Tiamut]]—the [[Celestials (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Celestial]] whose emergence on Earth was halted by the [[Eternals (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Eternals]] in the MCU film ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'' (2021)—were also revealed to be a focus of the film's story,<ref name="TiamutPolygon" /><ref name="FeigeTiamut" /> allowing the introduction of the fictional metal [[Adamantium (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|adamantium]] to the MCU.<ref name="SDCC2024" /> Moore said Marvel Studios had been waiting to address Tiamut's remains in a story that could make the "best use" of them.<ref name="NationalNewsFeb2025" /> Onah said the prior ''Captain America'' films had been used to establish aspects of the MCU, and they felt it was interesting to introduce adamantium as a "geopolitical football".<ref name="OnahBreakdownVariety" /> The film originally ended with a climactic action scene on Tiamut's remains, referred to as "Celestial Island", but this was reworked.<ref name="VFXReworked" /><ref name="OnahBreakdownWrap" /> A [[dogfight]] near the island featuring multiple countries was also simplified to just feature the United States and Japan. Onah originally filmed the sequence in a thriller-inspired style like other scenes, but they found the action needed more camera movement and intensity.<ref name="VFXReworked" /> [[Jóhannes Haukur Jóhannesson]] was revealed to be part of the cast in December, when Marvel credited the film's story to Spellman & Musson and Edwards, with the screenplay credited to Onah & [[Peter Glanz (filmmaker)|Peter Glanz]] and Orton.<ref name="TheatricalPoster" /> The final writing credits were revealed the next month: Edwards received screenplay credit alongside Spellman & Musson and Onah & Glanz, with the story credited to the former three. Additional off-screen literary material was attributed to Orton.<ref name="WGACredits" />

Also in January 2025, Rollins said he would no longer be appearing in the film following the reshoots.<ref name="NoRollins" /> Salazar's scenes as Diamondback were also cut.<ref name="EasterEggsMW" /> Onah said the film was "taking a swing" with its depiction of the Serpent Society and they felt this aspect needed to be "refined" to align better with the film's thriller tone. He said they decided to move away from the more comic-accurate versions of Serpent Society members portrayed by Rollins and Salazar, and the film's reshoot dates also clashed with a different project Salazar was working on. Both actors were replaced by Esposito, who serves the same purpose in the film's story but with a more grounded tone.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Onah felt Esposito's inclusion was a "no-brainer" once he became available, and thought the actor brought the right level of gravitas to the film and the Serpent Society team,<ref name="OnahNoRollins" /> which is just called "Serpent" in the film.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /><ref name="OnahBreakdownVariety" /> Jóhannesson plays another member of the group, [[Copperhead (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Copperhead]], alongside Esposito.<ref name="EasterEggsMW" /> Moore compared the group, described as a "violent special-ops team", to the more grounded version of the comic book character [[Batroc the Leaper]] that is introduced in ''The Winter Soldier'' portrayed by [[Georges St-Pierre]]. Onah said Esposito's role was "great setup" for further appearances of the Serpent Society in the MCU.<ref name="SerpentSocietyEW" /> Addressing public interest in the changes made to the film during reshoots, Onah said they were a natural part of the film's development and he pushed back against speculation that came from leaks and reports that he said were inaccurate.<ref name="OnahBreakdownWrap" /> An example of this was speculation that Ross was originally supposed to die in the film, based on set photos and early trailer footage showing a funeral scene that was cut from the film.<ref name="RossDiesSpeculation" /> Onah said there was "never a version where he explicitly died", but there were versions of the film in which his fate was left unknown to the public. Onah added that Red Hulk is a "fan favorite character" from the comics, and said it is "not very easy for a Hulk to perish".<ref name="OnahBreakdownVariety" /> 

[[File:The Leader make-up comparison in Captain America Brave New World.png|thumb|upright=0.9|The original prosthetic design for Samuel Sterns by Blue Whale Studios (L) and the character's appearance in the final film (R). This design was one of the many changes to the film during its reshoots.]]  
[[Matthew Schmidt]] and Madeleine Gavin edited the film;<ref name="THRReview" /> Schmidt previously worked on ''The Winter Soldier'' and ''Civil War''.<ref name="SchmidtReturn" /> ''Brave New World'' is the first MCU film to not feature an [[Marvel Studios#Logo|animated Marvel Studios opening logo]], instead presenting the studio's logo in "no-nonsense black-and-white" without a traditional musical fanfare.<ref name="NoLogo" /> [[Bill Westenhofer]] was the film's initial visual effects supervisor, but left the project to work on the film ''[[Moana (2026 film)|Moana]]'' (2026) and was replaced with Alessandro Ongaro.<ref name="VFXReworked" /> Visual effects were provided by [[Wētā FX]], [[Digital Domain]], [[Luma Pictures]], Barnstorm VFX, UPP, OPSIS, and Outpost VFX.<ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> Digital Domain worked on the Celestial Island sequence. The same digital model for Tiamut from ''Eternals'' was used, but they "scaled it down a little".<ref name="VFXReworked" />

Wētā FX designed Red Hulk with Meinerding. Wētā visual effects supervisor Dan Cox said they did not want the character to just look like a color-swapped version of the Hulk. They hoped to make him more intimidating than the Hulk, with a more upright, athletic posture. Onah wanted the character to feel more tactical and methodical due to Ross's military background, and referenced a bear for poses where the character is on all fours. Ford's facial features were incorporated into the design based on scans of his face. The climactic confrontation between Red Hulk and Captain America in D.C. has more than 300 visual effects shots.<ref name="EWRedHulkDec2024" /> Ford and a stunt double provided [[motion capture]] for the character.<ref name="FordRedHulkMocap" /><ref name="VFXReworked" /> Ongaro said Ross's transformation into Red Hulk was originally meant to look more "disgusting. You could see the skin tearing and breaking on his hand, bones popping off", but they decided to pull back on that aspect. They also planned to show the full transition from Red Hulk back to Ross, but Onah felt it broke the "peacefulness" of the moment and instead chose to focus on his shadow during the transition.<ref name="VFXReworked" /> For Sterns, who also features a monstrous look, Onah said they always intended to use visual effects to augment the practical effects that were used on set.<ref name="OnahBreakdownCollider" /> Ongaro said the practical effects for Sterns were replaced with an "all-<nowiki>[</nowiki>[[Computer-generated imagery|computer-generated]]<nowiki>]</nowiki> approach" by Luma.<ref name="VFXReworked" />

== Music ==
{{Main|Captain America: Brave New World (soundtrack){{!}}''Captain America: Brave New World'' (soundtrack)}}
[[Laura Karpman]] was announced as the film's composer in August 2024. She previously composed the music for the MCU series ''[[What If...? (TV series)|What If...?]]'' (2021–2024) and ''[[Ms. Marvel (miniseries)|Ms. Marvel]]'' (2022), and the film ''[[The Marvels]]''.<ref name="Karpman" /><ref name="Karpman2" /> Karpman wrote a new heroic theme that represents Wilson, Torres, and Bradley, and an [[Aaron Copland]]-inspired piano theme for Ross. The latter evolves into a "[[Godzilla]] monster thing" when Ross transforms into Red Hulk. The film's thriller aspects are scored with "conspiracy music" that Karpman compared to the ''[[Mission: Impossible]]'' franchise. She also included a subtle reference to [[Alan Silvestri]]'s "Captain America March" from the previous films.<ref name="KarpmanSR" /> Nora Kroll-Rosenbaum, who worked with Karpman on ''What If...?'', provided additional music for the film. A soundtrack album featuring their music was released by [[Hollywood Records]] and [[Marvel Music (record label)|Marvel Music]] on February 12, 2025.<ref name="Soundtrack" /> At that time, Mackie said the film's "title-song" had been provided by [[Kendrick Lamar]], which was interpreted by some as Lamar having written a new song for the film.<ref name="Lamar" /> However, it is his 2014 song "[[I (Kendrick Lamar song)|I]]" that is played over the film's end credits.<ref name="SongsSR" /> Additionally, the 1959 song "[[Mr. Blue]]" by [[the Fleetwoods]] is heard throughout the film as a trigger for characters being mind-controlled by Sterns; this is a reference to his "Mr. Blue" alias in ''The Incredible Hulk''.<ref name="EasterEggsMW" />

== Marketing ==
Feige and Mackie revealed the first footage from the film, focusing on Wilson meeting with Thaddeus Ross, at [[CinemaCon]] in April 2024.<ref name="MackieEWApr2024" /> The following month, [[McDonald's]] began selling [[Happy Meal]]s with toys based on characters from the film, including Red Hulk and Diamondback. Stephanie Kaloi at ''[[TheWrap]]'' noted that these characters had not been confirmed for the film by Marvel at that time, and the early reveal was likely due to the film's release being delayed but the marketing partnership with McDonald's not being pushed back to match.<ref name="McDonaldsMay2024" /> More footage from the film was shown at a [[CineEurope]] panel in June teasing Disney's theatrical slate.<ref name="CineEurope" />

A teaser trailer was released in July which drew comparisons to ''The Winter Soldier''{{'s}} tone,<ref name="THRTeaser" /><ref name="TheWrapTeaser" /><ref name="TheVergeTeaser" /> with Jennifer Ouellette of ''[[Ars Technica]]'' describing it as "half-superhero movie, half-political thriller".<ref name="ArsTechnicaTeaser" /> The appearance of Red Hulk and the character's potential true identity were both widely discussed;<ref name="TeaserPremise" /><ref name="VarietyTeaser" /><ref name="DeadlineTeaser" /><ref name="/FilmTeaser" /> Adam Chitwood at ''TheWrap'' wondered if rumors of Thaddeus Ross becoming Red Hulk were a [[red herring]].<ref name="TheWrapTeaser" /> ''[[The Verge]]''{{'s}} Charles Pulliam-Moore questioned if the film being a continuation of ''The Falcon and the Winter Soldier'' would be difficult for general audiences to follow.<ref name="TheVergeTeaser" /> Conversely, Sandy Schaefer and Chris Evangelista from ''[[/Film]]'' felt the film would not rely much on the series to have wider audience appeal. They called the trailer "pretty good" considering the film's recently completed reshoots, noting that it largely contains action rather than plot details.<ref name="/FilmTeaser" /> Commentators also noted the reveal that the remains of Tiamut would be featured in the film, after the [[Emergence (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Emergence]] had largely been ignored in MCU projects since ''Eternals''.<ref name="GizmodoTeaser13Things" /><ref name="TiamutPolygon" /><ref name="TiamutComicBook" /><ref name="TiamutIGN" /> Michael McWhertor at ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]'' noted that the trailer shows the United States and other countries meeting at a Global Unity Summit to discuss the Celestial, with a stylized version of Tiamut's hand being used as a logo for the summit.<ref name="TiamutPolygon" /> Later in July, Marvel released an updated version of the teaser for distribution in theaters that removed Isaiah Bradley's assassination attempt on Thaddeus Ross. This came following the [[attempted assassination of Donald Trump in Pennsylvania]] that month.<ref name="TeaserEdit" />

Also in July 2024, Feige and members of the cast promoted the film at San Diego Comic-Con. More footage was shown, Esposito's role was announced, and Thaddeus Ross was confirmed to be the character who transforms into Red Hulk.<ref name="SDCC2024" /> Similar footage was also shown at the 2024 D23 Expo.<ref name="D232024Footage" /> Following online leaks of the San Diego Comic-Con and D23 footage, Marvel released an official look at Red Hulk within their video celebrating the company's 85th anniversary in August.<ref name="85thOfficialLook" /> A full trailer for the film debuted at [[D23 (Disney)#D23 Brazil: A Disney Experience|D23 Brasil]] in November and was also released online.<ref name="DeadlineTrailer" /> After the teases of Red Hulk in previously released footage, the trailer features Ross's transformation into Red Hulk and shots of the character fighting Wilson.<ref name="CinemaBlendTrailer" /><ref name="GR+Trailer" /> Several commentators noted the trailer's unique style, featuring "creepy black lines", [[Split screen (video production)|split-screen shots]], and a flickering text effect. Comparisons were drawn to political thriller films such as ''The Day of the Jackal'', ''The Parallax View'', and ''All the President's Men'', as well as ''[[The Candidate (1972 film)|The Candidate]]'' (1972), ''[[JFK (film)|JFK]]'' (1991), and ''[[The Constant Gardener (film)|The Constant Gardener]]'' (2005).<ref name="EmpireTrailer" /><ref name="CinemaBlendTrailer2" /><ref name="AVClubTrailer" /> Jordan King at ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' questioned whether ''Brave New World'' would live up to these comparisons, but said it was a promising sign that the film was "looking to place itself in conversation" with those previous films.<ref name="EmpireTrailer" />

Marvel Studios partnered with [[Tide (brand)|Tide]]'s "Collateral Stains" promotional campaign to release a series of commercials for the film in January 2025. These feature [[Gregg Turkington]] reprising his role of [[Baskin-Robbins]] store manager Dale from the MCU films ''[[Ant-Man (film)|Ant-Man]]'' (2015) and ''[[Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania]]'' (2023).<ref name="TurkingtonTideCommercial" /> For the three weeks leading up to the film's premiere, Disney was said to be making a "final and biggest marketing" push for it.<ref name="THRProjection" /> At a press event in Italy at the end of January, Mackie said Captain America represented values like "honor, dignity and integrity" rather than America itself. He received backlash from some online commenters who labeled the film "anti-American" and [[woke]]. Mackie soon added, "I'm a proud American and taking on the shield of a hero like Cap is the honor of a lifetime... Cap has universal characteristics that people all over the world can relate to." [[James Hibberd (writer)|James Hibberd]] of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' said Evans made similar statements when playing the character and the actors were likely directed by Marvel to focus on Captain America's "universal qualities" to avoid any [[jingoistic]] connotations while promoting internationally. Hibberd also discussed the film's other controversies—similarities between Thaddeus Ross and the newly re-elected President [[Donald Trump]], the original ''New World Order'' subtitle, opposing views on the inclusion and portrayal of Ruth Bat-Seraph, and "ugly" criticisms related to Mackie being Black—but said these did not appear to have impacted the film's box office performance based on early projections.<ref name="THRCultureWars" /> Coinciding with the film's release in February, [[concept art]] by Meinderding of the film's Captain America costume and design for Red Hulk were used as variant covers for [[Marvel Comics]]' ''Sam Wilson, Captain America'' #2 and ''Red Hulk'' #1.<ref name="ComicCoversFeb2025" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Captain America: Brave New World'' grossed $200.5&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $214.6&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $415.1&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' reported that the film needed to earn $425&nbsp;million worldwide to [[break even]], taking into account the reported production budget of $180&nbsp;million as well as marketing costs.<ref name="DeadlineBOOpeningWeekend" /> Feige attributed the film's low box-office performance to the absence of Steve Rogers actor Chris Evans, who had headlined the three previous ''Captain America'' films.<ref name="FeigeConferenceJul2025Deadline" />

In the United States and Canada, the film was released alongside ''[[Paddington in Peru]]'' and was projected to gross $80–94&nbsp;million from 4,105 theaters in its four-day opening weekend.<ref name="DeadlineBOPreview" /> Tracking service Quorum reported early box office estimates for the film in mid-January 2025, projecting that it would earn $86–$95&nbsp;million during its opening weekend and more than $100&nbsp;million across the four-day weekend from [[Valentine's Day]] to [[Presidents' Day]]. This was similar to ''The Winter Soldier''{{'s}} $95&nbsp;million opening weekend in 2014.<ref name="DeadlineProjection" /> Further tracking, reported later in January, similarly indicated an opening of at least $90&nbsp;million.<ref name="THRProjection" /> The film grossed $40{{nbsp}}million on its opening day, including an estimated $12&nbsp;million from Thursday previews.<ref name="DeadlineBOOpeningWeekend" /> It debuted to $88.8&nbsp;million, and a total of $100&nbsp;million over the four-day frame, topping the box office.<ref name="DeadlineBOOpeningWeekend" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=Domestic 2025 Weekend 7 |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2025W07/?ref_=bo_hm_rw |url-status=live Mojo}}</ref> In its [[Second weekend in box office performance|second weekend]], it retained the top spot at the box office and grossed $28.2{{nbsp}}million, a 68% drop that ranked as the third-worst === Critical response ===
<!-- CRITICAL RESPONSE WORDING DETERMINED BY TALK PAGE CONSENSUS. PLEASE DO NOT CHANGE IT WITHOUT NEW CONSENSUS. SEE THE "CRITICAL RESPONSE WORDING" TALK PAGE DISCUSSION FOR MORE. -->''Captain America: Brave New World'' received mixed reviews from critics,<ref name="RTRoundup" /><ref name="WrapBOOpeningWeekend" /><ref name="NoLogo2" /> who were divided on the film's story, connections to other MCU projects, and visual effects.<ref name="CBRoundup" /> The overall reception was also described as unfavorable,<ref name="VarietyBOOpeningWeekend" /> negative,<ref name="VarietyBOSecondWeekend" /> and mixed-to-negative.<ref name="EWBOSecondWeekend" /> The performances, particularly those of Mackie and Ford, received praise.<ref name="RTRoundup" /><ref name="CBRoundup" /> On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 46% of 367 critics gave ''Brave New World'' a positive review, with an average rating of 5.4/10. The critics consensus reads, "Anthony Mackie capably takes up Cap's mantle and shield, but ''Brave New World'' is too routine and overstuffed with uninteresting [[Easter egg (media)|Easter eggs]] to feel like a worthy standalone adventure for this new Avengers leader."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=captain_america_brave_new_world |type=m |access-date=May 7, 2026}}{{cbignore}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] summarized the critical response as "mixed or average", based on a weighted average score of 42 out of 100 from 56 critics.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=captain-america-brave-new-world |type=movie |access-date=March 15, 2025}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B–" on an A+ to F scale, the lowest grade for an MCU film to date, and [[PostTrak]] reported a "mixed bag" average rating of three stars out of five.<ref name="DeadlineBOOpeningWeekend" />

The political subject matter was a subject of critical commentary. Frank Scheck at ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' remarked that the scene where Ross transformed into Red Hulk and caused havoc across Washington, D.C. seemed like a fantasy of real-world United States president [[Donald Trump]];<ref name="THRReview" /> [[David Ehrlich]] of ''[[IndieWire]]'' agreed with parallels between Ross and Trump's "[[Make America Great Again]]" rhetoric, citing both being gruff old men elevated to office based on the promise of facilitating unity based on dealing with an "alien" threat.<ref name="IndieWireReview" /> ''[[The Independent]]''{{'s}} Adam White said that while Ross / Red Hulk could be interpreted as a Trump [[pastiche]], this was ultimately unlikely as the film refused to establish a political stance, with subplots regarding politics remaining underused.<ref name="IndependentReview" /> ''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'s}} David Fear joked that Ross was "somehow still only the second-most destructive, rage-filled commander in chief to ever hold office",<ref name="RollingStoneReview" /> and ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' writer Maureen Lee Lenker joked that "the most unbelievable part is a U.S. president with empathy, a sense of responsibility for his actions, and a capacity for change".<ref name="EWReview" /> Brandon Yu of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote that "the only wisps of tangible political intrigue to be found" were either unintended, or from allusions to previous subject matter in ''Falcon and the Winter Soldier''.<ref name="NYTimesReview" /> Other commentary concerned the controversial Bat-Seraph character, with PJ Grisar at ''[[The Forward]]'' writing that the controversy about the character relating to Israel and Gaza did not matter because the film overall was "honestly such a [[wikt:dog's breakfast|dog's breakfast]]" in terms of politics and presentation,<ref name="SabraResponseTheForward" /> while ''[[/Film]]''{{'s}} Devin Meenan opined that the character's debut could not have been timed more poorly because of the divisiveness of public opinion inherent in the ongoing conflict.<ref name="SabraResponse/Film" />

The characterization and narrative were criticized. Molly Freeman, writing for ''[[Screen Rant]]'', claimed that the only purpose of Sam's suit switch from white to blue was [[toyetic|to sell more toys]], pointing this out as an example of the film featuring elements without "flare" nor concern for furthering its story or characters;<ref name="ScreenRantReview" /> Yu likewise wrote that it amounted to the genre of the "action-figure commercial".<ref name="NYTimesReview" /> ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]''{{'s}} Bilge Ebiri wrote that the film marked Marvel's turn into a "giant slop machine", opining that the film's bid to create sincerity came off as phony and that the jokes to cut this up were "lame",<ref name="VultureReview" /> while Scheck agreed that humor was "in severely short supply" and that Mackie's "reliable" charisma was lacking.<ref name="THRReview" /> ''[[TheWrap]]''{{'s}} William Bibbiani wrote that the film was unoriginal and without emotional depth, even in the "few" moments where Wilson made an emotional connection to the film, as these moments only "rehashed" the plot of ''Falcon and the Winter Soldier'' "briefly" and without impact. It said that ''Brave New World''{{'s}} "steadfast refusal to engage with its own ideas, either by artistic design or corporate mandate, reeks of timidity".<ref name="TheWrapReview" /> Writing for ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', Robert Daniels said Wilson was turned into the [[Magical Negro]] for the film, and that "in an effort to soothe white America's anger and hurt", the film "also asks its hero to grin and figuratively tap dance off screen".<ref name="RogerEbertReview" />

''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'s}} Aidan Kelley said the dialogue was among the worst present in the whole of the MCU, and criticized the boringness of Wilson, Sidewinder, and Bat-Seraph, though it praised Ford's empathy. They pointed out the non-cohesive nature of the film, mentioning the reshoots the film underwent as resulting in this.<ref name="ColliderReview" /> In agreement on the topic of Ford, Lee said Ford was the main attraction, lauding his gravitas and "twinkle in his eye and a merry warmth".<ref name="EWReview" /> White called the character development "an afterthought", feeling Mackie had "little to play with" emotionally, and criticized his relegation to "a functional, blandly heroic punching machine" despite his acting talent.<ref name="IndependentReview" /> ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]''{{'s}} [[Kyle Smith (critic)|Kyle Smith]] said that the film is "competently executed, without resorting to played-out gimmickry such as skipping across the multiverse", adding that it "gives the audience plenty of analogues for real-world problems". He also wrote that the MCU "draws on ''[[The Manchurian Candidate]]'' to produce one of its better recent movies",<ref name="WSJReview" /> while Katie Walsh of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' described the film as "a decent political thriller with something culturally resonant to say that exceeds mere comic-book particulars".<ref name="LATReview" />

Critics were polarized on the visuals. For the ''[[New York Post]]'', Johnny Oleksinski felt that the animation of the White House fight scene was "so cheap and cheesy" that it was unable to be taken seriously, and the reveal of the mutated Sterns caused a "hearty guffaw" because of the poor makeup and prosthetics.<ref name="NYPostReview" /> Kelley wrote that the CGI in the film was worse than ''[[The Incredible Hulk (film)|The Incredible Hulk]]'' (2008), released nearly two decades earlier, and concluded it was unconvincing.<ref name="ColliderReview" /> Ebiri wrote that the "VFX-heavy sequences are so lifeless and tiresome that I felt my eyes drifting closed a couple of times".<ref name="VultureReview" /> Daniels and Freeman both praised the VFX on Red Hulk, though Freeman felt the cherry blossoms were "a green screen mess".<ref name="RogerEbertReview" /><ref name="ScreenRantReview" /> Yu wrote that the visuals were "garish" and "slapdash",<ref name="NYTimesReview" /> while Bibbiani called the visuals "drab" and unfinished, criticizing Wilson's CGI wings as a "[[proof of concept]] that proves nothing"; they wrote that the film looked "so clean it looks completely fake".<ref name="TheWrapReview" /> Scheck wrote the CGI was "underwhelming" and at other times even worse, but were amused from recognizing Ford's features in Red Hulk.<ref name="THRReview" /> [[Richard Roeper]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film three of four stars and called it a "far better work than recent [MCU] missteps".<ref name="ChicagoSTReview" />

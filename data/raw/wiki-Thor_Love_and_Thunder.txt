'''''Thor: Love and Thunder''''' is a 2022 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the character [[Thor (Marvel Comics)|Thor]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is the sequel to ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'' (2017) and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 29th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Taika Waititi]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Jennifer Kaytin Robinson]], and stars [[Chris Hemsworth]] as [[Thor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thor]] alongside [[Christian Bale]], [[Tessa Thompson]], [[Jaimie Alexander]], Waititi, [[Russell Crowe]], and [[Natalie Portman]]. In the film, Thor tries to find inner peace, but must return to action and recruit [[Valkyrie (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valkyrie]] (Thompson), [[Korg (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Korg]] (Waititi), and [[Jane Foster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Jane Foster]] (Portman)—who is now the Mighty Thor—to stop [[Gorr (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Gorr the God Butcher]] (Bale) from eliminating all gods.

Hemsworth and Waititi had discussed plans for a sequel to ''Ragnarok'' by January 2018. ''Love and Thunder'' was announced in July 2019, with Hemsworth, Waititi, and Thompson all set to return, as well as Portman who did not appear in ''Ragnarok''. Waititi wanted to differentiate ''Love and Thunder'' from ''Ragnarok'', seeking to make a [[romance film]] and 1980s-inspired adventure. He adapted elements from [[Jason Aaron]]'s run on ''Thor'' comic books during the 2010s, which similarly debuted Gorr and saw Foster take on the mantle and powers of Thor whilst under treatment for cancer. Robinson joined to contribute to the script in February 2020, and further casting was revealed later that year including the appearance of the [[Guardians of the Galaxy (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]. Production was expected to begin in late 2020 but was delayed by the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. Filming ultimately began in January 2021 in Sydney, Australia with [[Baz Idoine|Barry Idoine]] as the director of photography, and concluded at the beginning of June.

''Thor: Love and Thunder'' premiered at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on June 23, 2022, and was released in the United States on July 8, as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU. The film received mixed reviews from critics, who praised the performances of Hemsworth, Bale and Portman, but criticized its visual effects and tonal inconsistencies.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Leite |first=Marcelo |date=July 7, 2022 |title=Why Thor: Love & Thunder's Reviews Are So Divided |url=https://screenrant.com/thor-love-thunder-reviews-mixed-rotten-tomatoes/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230116174655/https://screenrant.com/thor-love-thunder-reviews-mixed-rotten-tomatoes/ |archive-date=January 16, 2023 |access-date=January 16, 2023 |website=[[Screen Rant]] |language=en}}</ref> The film grossed $760.9 million worldwide, making it the [[2022 in film#Highest-grossing films|eighth-highest-grossing film of 2022]], as well as the second-highest-grossing film in the ''Thor'' franchise.

== Plot ==
[[Gorr (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Gorr]] and his daughter, [[Love (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Love]], the last of their race, struggle in a barren desert. Despite their prayers to their god, Rapu, Love dies. The god-killing [[Necrosword (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Necrosword]] calls to Gorr, leading him to Rapu's lush realm. After Rapu cruelly mocks and dismisses Gorr's plight, he renounces the god, causing Rapu to start strangling him. The Necrosword offers itself to Gorr, who kills Rapu with it and vows to [[Deicide|kill all gods]]. Gorr is granted the ability to manipulate shadows and produce monsters but is cursed with impending death and corruption under the sword's influence.

[[Thor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thor]] has joined the [[Guardians of the Galaxy (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Guardians of the Galaxy]] after the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]]' [[Battle of Earth (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|battle]] against [[Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thanos]].{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019)}} He learns of a distress signal from [[Sif (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sif]], so he parts ways with the team. He finds an injured Sif, who warns that Gorr's next target is [[New Asgard]]. Meanwhile, Dr. [[Jane Foster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Jane Foster]], Thor's ex-girlfriend, has been diagnosed with stage four [[terminal cancer]]. With [[Cancer treatment|medical treatment]] proving ineffective, she travels to New Asgard hoping that Thor's hammer [[Mjolnir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mjolnir]], which was previously fractured by [[Hela (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hela]],{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'' (2017)}} might heal her. Due to an enchantment Thor unknowingly placed on it years earlier to protect Foster, Mjolnir reforges and bonds itself to her.

Thor arrives in New Asgard just as Gorr's attack begins. Thor is surprised to find Foster wielding Mjolnir, but teams up with her, [[Valkyrie (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valkyrie]], and [[Korg (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Korg]] to fight Gorr. The group thwarts Gorr, but he escapes, kidnapping numerous Asgardian children and imprisoning them in the Shadow Realm. The group travels to [[Omnipotence City (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Omnipotence City]] to warn the other gods and ask for their help in creating an army. The leader of the gods, [[Zeus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Zeus]], is unwilling to help, thinking they can remain safe and hidden from Gorr in the city; he also believes that Gorr will not be able to achieve his stated goal of visiting the realm of [[Eternity (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Eternity]], where he will be granted one wish, presumably to destroy all gods. As a security precaution, Zeus orders the group's capture to prevent them from exposing the city's location to Gorr. When Zeus injures Korg, Thor impales Zeus with his thunderbolt, which Valkyrie steals before they escape to confront Gorr in the Shadow Realm. On the way, Thor learns of Foster's cancer diagnosis.

The group arrives at the Shadow Realm but are unable to locate the children. Foster sees ancient drawings that depict Thor's battle-axe [[Mjolnir and Stormbreaker|Stormbreaker]] as a way to summon the [[Bifrost]] to enter Eternity, and deduces the trap laid out by Gorr. She throws away Stormbreaker to prevent Gorr from accessing it. However, Gorr overpowers the group and threatens to kill Foster, forcing Thor to summon it back. Gorr steals Stormbreaker and injures Valkyrie before a weakened Foster collapses.

Upon traveling back to New Asgard, Thor discovers that Foster's Thor form is not allowing her body to naturally fight the cancer. Due to this, Thor travels to Eternity's altar alone and using Zeus's thunderbolt, imbues the children with his power to fight Gorr's monsters while he battles Gorr. When Foster senses that Gorr is about to kill Thor, she joins the battle with Mjolnir to save him. They destroy the Necrosword, freeing Gorr from its influence, but the three are brought into Eternity's realm. With Gorr poised to make his wish, Thor implores Gorr to revive his daughter instead of destroying the gods. Thor then leaves Gorr to make his decision and attends to Foster, who succumbs to her illness and dies in his arms. Moved by their display, Gorr wishes for Eternity to revive Love, which it grants. As Gorr dies from the curse, he requests Thor to take care of Love.

In the aftermath, the children return to New Asgard, where Valkyrie and Sif commence their training, and a monument is erected in Foster's memory. Thor adopts Love, who accompanies him in his heroics, with Thor wielding Mjolnir and Love wielding Stormbreaker. In a [[mid-credits scene]], back in Omnipotence City, a recuperating Zeus sends his son [[Hercules (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hercules]] to kill Thor as revenge. In a post-credits scene, Foster arrives at the gates of [[Valhalla (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valhalla]], where [[Heimdall (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Heimdall]] welcomes her.

== Production ==
=== Development ===
Shortly after the release of the third ''Thor'' film, ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'', in November 2017, that film's director [[Taika Waititi]] and executives from [[Marvel Studios]] met to discuss ideas for another film, which was [[green-lit]] following the positive responses to ''Ragnarok''.<ref name="WaititiGreenlit" /> [[Chris Hemsworth]] indicated in January 2018 that he was interested in continuing to play [[Thor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thor]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU), despite his contract with Marvel Studios being set to end with his role in the MCU film ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019).<ref name="HemsworthFutureIGN" /> By then, Hemsworth and Waititi had discussed what they would want in a potential fourth ''Thor'' film,<ref name="RagnarokSequel" /> and Hemsworth said a month later that he would consider returning if there was "another great script".<ref name="DigitalSpyFeb2018" /> [[Tessa Thompson]], who plays [[Valkyrie (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Valkyrie]] in the [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|MCU films]], believed in April 2019 that a [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] had been made for a ''Ragnarok'' sequel that involved Waititi returning.<ref name="ThompsonFuture" /> Hemsworth said then that he would continue to play Thor for as long as possible, crediting Waititi with revitalizing his interest in the role after he became exhausted and underwhelmed with it prior to making ''Ragnarok''.<ref name="HemsworthApril2019Yahoo" />

In July 2019, Waititi officially signed on to write and direct a fourth ''Thor'' film, with Hemsworth expected to reprise his role.<ref name="WaititiThor4" /> Waititi was not interested in repeating what they did with ''Ragnarok'', instead wanting to do "something more interesting for myself to keep the whole thing ignited and to make sure that I'm feeling creatively stimulated".<ref name="EWMay2022" /> Later that month, at [[San Diego Comic-Con]], [[Marvel Studios]] President [[Kevin Feige]] announced the film as ''Thor: Love and Thunder'', with a release date of November 5, 2021. Hemsworth and Thompson were confirmed to be returning along with [[Natalie Portman]], reprising her role of [[Jane Foster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Jane Foster]] from the MCU films ''[[Thor (film)|Thor]]'' (2011) and ''[[Thor: The Dark World]]'' (2013).<ref name="DeadlineSDCC" /> Hemsworth, who would also serve as an executive producer,<ref name="HemsworthEP" /> was paid $20{{nbsp}}million upfront for the film,<ref name="HemsworthPayAug2021" /> an increase from the $15{{nbsp}}million upfront salary he earned for each of his appearances in ''Ragnarok'', the film ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018), and ''Endgame''.<ref name="HemsworthPayOct2018" /> Portman agreed to return to the franchise, after her character had not been included in ''Ragnarok'', following a single meeting with Waititi, who was given creative freedom from Marvel Studios to reimagine her role in the film, in which he offered to portray the character in a different and fresh way, with a "license to be adventurous and fun and funny".<ref name="FeigeCNNJuly2019" /><ref name="WaititiPortmanAug2019" /><ref name="GizmodoPortman" /> Thompson and Feige added that Valkyrie's bisexuality would be addressed in the sequel, [[Retroactive continuity|retroactively]] making her Marvel Studios' first [[LGBTQ]] superhero.<ref name="ThompsonLGBTQ" /><ref name="FeigeLGBTQ" /> Marvel Studios executive [[Brad Winderbaum]] was producing the film alongside Feige.<ref name="VarietyTeaser" />

[[Don Harwin]], the [[Minister for the Public Service and Employee Relations, Aboriginal Affairs, and the Arts|Minister for the Arts]] for the Australian state of [[New South Wales]], announced at the end of July that ''Thor: Love and Thunder'' would be filmed at [[Fox Studios Australia]] in Sydney back-to-back with the MCU film ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]'' (2021), with work set to begin on ''Love and Thunder'' there in March 2020 ahead of an August 2020 filming start. The production was set to receive over AU$24 million (US${{To USD|24|AUS|round=yes}} million) of subsidies from the Australian and New South Wales governments. Marvel Studios vice president David Grant said shooting the two films back-to-back would provide continuous employment for local crews, with ''Love and Thunder'' expected to generate over AU$178 million (US${{To USD|178|AUS|round=yes}} million) for the local economy. He added that the studio would work with "local educational institutions in creating internship opportunities".<ref name="NSWThorShangChi" /> [[Jeff Goldblum]] said in August 2019 that there was a chance he could reprise his ''Ragnarok'' role of the [[Grandmaster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Grandmaster]] in the sequel,<ref name="GoldblumMaybe" /> and Waititi confirmed in October that he would be reprising his own role of [[Korg (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Korg]] from ''Ragnarok'' and ''Endgame''.<ref name="Korg" /> [[Christian Bale]] entered talks to join the cast in January 2020,<ref name="Bale" /> with [[pre-production]] expected to begin in April.<ref name="PreProductionStart" /> Thompson confirmed a month later that Bale would star as the villain in the film,<ref name="BaleCast" /> while [[Vin Diesel]], who voices [[Groot (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Groot]] in the MCU films, said he had discussed the film with Waititi and had been told the [[Guardians of the Galaxy (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Guardians of the Galaxy]] would be appearing in it.<ref name="Diesel" /> In early April, Disney shifted much of their [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] slate of films due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], moving ''Thor: Love and Thunder''{{'s}} release date to February 18, 2022.<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> Pre-production on the film was delayed due to the pandemic, with Waititi unsure when production would continue.<ref name="WaititiInstagramDetails" /> At the end of the month, Disney moved the release date up to February 11, 2022.<ref name="SonyPhase4Delay" /> By July, filming was set to start in early 2021.<ref name="2021Filming" />

==== Writing ====
[[File:Taika Waititi by Gage Skidmore 2.jpg|thumb|upright|Co-writer and director [[Taika Waititi]] at the 2019 [[San Diego Comic-Con]]]]
When announcing the film in July 2019, Waititi said it would adapt elements from [[Jason Aaron]]'s ''Mighty Thor'' comic books by having Foster become a hero called the Mighty Thor.<ref name="DeadlineSDCC" /><ref name="MightyThor" /> Feige said that the film would include a lot of elements from Marvel comics and that the story line of Foster becoming the Mighty Thor would be an important part,<ref name="FeigeCNNJuly2019" /><ref name="MightyThor" /> explaining that Marvel considered the ''Mighty Thor'' run to be one of the best recent comic books. Waititi read the comic while working on ''Ragnarok'', and when he agreed to direct another ''Thor'' film, he told Marvel that he wanted to include Foster as the Mighty Thor.<ref name="FeigeCNNJuly2019" /> The studio agreed to adapt Aaron's storyline and include Portman following discussions with Waititi about where the story could lead.<ref name="EmpireMay2022" /> Waititi said they stuck close to Foster's storyline from those comics and tried to adapt its best parts. He also explained that Thor having his axe [[Stormbreaker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stormbreaker]] and then seeing his hammer [[Mjolnir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mjolnir]] return "in the hands of someone else" and "no longer [being] his hammer" would explore "the idea that someone's taking his place", which he felt some fans assumed would occur, but did not believe this as such.<ref name="WaititiTotalFilm" /> Aaron served as a consultant on the film.<ref name="AaronConsultant" /> [[A. C. Bradley (screenwriter)|A. C. Bradley]], the creator and [[head writer]] of the animated series ''[[What If...? (TV series)|What If...?]]'' (2021–2024), pitched an episode of that series in which Foster would have become Thor, but the idea was rejected because the storyline was already being used for ''Love and Thunder''.<ref name="BradleyWhatIfIdea" />

In August, Waititi was reported to have completed a script for the film,<ref name="ScriptCompleteAug2019" /> but he denied this later that month.<ref name="WaititiNoScriptAug2019" /> While promoting his film ''[[Jojo Rabbit]]'' (2019) in October, Waititi said he had completed the first draft of the screenplay,<ref name="WaititiOct2019Yahoo" /> but the story would change throughout filming and editing. He was unsure if the film would include the storyline from Aaron's run in which Foster suffers from [[breast cancer]], which he said was a powerful part of the comics.<ref name="WaititiOct2019Variety" /> Portman later said Foster's treatment from cancer would be explored in the film.<ref name="PortmanOct2020Yahoo" /> Waititi added that Marvel was still discussing how much time would pass between ''Endgame'' and ''Love and Thunder'', and that this would affect whether Thor is still carrying the extra weight he is depicted with in ''Endgame'', which earned him the nicknames "Fat Thor" and "Bro Thor". Waititi did note that he wanted to "keep changing it up with Thor. He's so interesting when he's changing all the time."<ref name="WaititiOct2019Yahoo" /> The film is set eight years after Thor and Foster broke up, around 2024.<ref name="Timeline8Years" /> Waititi also acknowledged fans "[[shipping (fandom)|shipping]]" Valkyrie with [[Brie Larson]]'s [[Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Carol Danvers / Captain Marvel]] but said he did not intend to include a romantic relationship between the pair because he would rather surprise fans than do something by popular demand.<ref name="WaititiInterviewWIRED" /> [[Jennifer Kaytin Robinson]] was hired to work on the film's screenplay with Waititi in February 2020.<ref name="RobinsonVariety" /> Waititi received sole screenplay credit on the film's theatrical poster, with Robinson credited with him for the story,<ref name="OfficialTrailerTHR" /> before the final credits from the [[Writers Guild of America]] credited both Waititi and Robinson as the film's writers.<ref name="WGAWritersCredits" />

''Thor: Love and Thunder'' follows Thor as he attempts to find inner peace, but must return to action and recruit Valkyrie, Korg, and Foster to stop [[Gorr (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Gorr the God Butcher]] from eliminating all gods.<ref name="VarietyTeaser" /> Four or five drafts of the screenplay had been completed by mid-April 2020,<ref name="WaititiInstagramDetails" /> when Waititi said the sequel was "so over the top now in the very best way" and would make ''Ragnarok'' look like a "run of the mill, very safe film" by doubling down on its crazier aspects.<ref name="WaititiInstagramDetails" /><ref name="WaititiInterviewWIRED" /> He wanted to up the ante and make the film as if "10-year-olds told us what should be in a movie and we said yes to every single thing." Waititi added that the film would explore more of Korg's [[Kronan (comics)|Kronan]] culture and indicated that it would include the [[Space Sharks]] alien race from the comics. He expressed interest in featuring the character [[Beta Ray Bill]], but was unsure at that point if he would.<ref name="WaititiInstagramDetails" />

At the end of July, Waititi said they had been writing the script off and on for over a year and he was taking another pass on it that week. He said it was very romantic, and explained that he wanted to make a [[romance film]] because he wanted to make something that he had not done before,<ref name="2021Filming" /><ref name="WaititiBBC" /> and, as such, wrote the film as a love story.<ref name="WaititiWiredMay2022" /> He later said the film was "about love, with superheroes and outer space", and that he wanted to "embrace this thing that I was always a bit dismissive of, and explore this idea of love, and show characters who do believe in love".<ref name="WaititiEmpireMay2022" /> That October, Hemsworth said Waititi was still writing the script and expressed his excitement for doing something drastically different with his character, as he did in his previous three MCU films.<ref name="Jan2021Filming" /> Waititi later described ''Love and Thunder'' as the craziest film he had ever made and explained that each element was intended to not make sense. He said it would have its own "distinct flavour" from ''Ragnarok'',<ref name="WaititiJuly2021Empire" /> which he called a 1970s space opera with a festive, party-like tone. He saw this film as a 1980s adventure, taking inspiration from posters for films such as ''[[Conan the Barbarian (1982 film)|Conan the Barbarian]]'' (1982) and ''[[The Beastmaster]]'' (1982) as well as art seen on vans in [[Venice, Los Angeles|Venice Beach]],<ref name="EWMay2022" /> with the [[Jack Kirby bibliography|works of]] [[Jack Kirby]] and the covers of "old" [[Mills & Boon]] romance novels also serving as "visual touchstones" for the film.<ref name="WaititiWiredMay2022" /> He said it was not serious or dramatic, but it did explore themes such as love, loss, and "our place in the world" while making the characters ask themselves the questions: "What is your purpose? What is the reason that you're a hero, and what do you do when you have these powers?"<ref name="EWMay2022" /> The film's ending involving Love underwent multiple revisions.<ref name="DifferentEndings" />

[[James Gunn]], the writer and director of the three ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film series)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' films, consulted on how the Guardians characters were used in ''Love and Thunder'',<ref name="JamesGunnConsult" /> with Gunn and Waititi discussing where the characters were going before Waititi began writing. Waititi read Gunn's script for ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' (2023), which is set after ''Love and Thunder'', and then Gunn read Waititi's script and shared his thoughts,<ref name="JamesGunnConsult2" /> asking for some adjustments to be made.<ref name="GunnAdjustments" /> In January 2021, Gunn said the Guardians were "in great hands" with Waititi,<ref name="JamesGunnConsult" /> and he later praised Waititi's work on the script.<ref name="GunnInput" /> [[Chris Pratt]] said ''Love and Thunder'' would continue the rivalry between Guardians leader [[Peter Quill (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Quill / Star-Lord]] and Thor that was established in ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame'',<ref name="PrattJune2021Yahoo" /> while [[Karen Gillan]] said Waititi brought out the "bonkers side" of [[Nebula (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nebula]] through her "pure aggression".<ref name="GillanJune2021Collider" />

=== Pre-production ===
Pre-production began in Australia by October 2020.<ref name="PortmanOct2020Yahoo" /> A month later, Chris Pratt was revealed to be reprising his role as Guardians of the Galaxy leader Peter Quill / Star-Lord.<ref name="PrattCast" /> Given the ensemble nature of the cast, industry insiders described the film as feeling like "''Avengers 5''".<ref name="Avengers5Feel" /> In December, Feige revealed that the film's release was delayed again, this time to May 6, 2022, and he announced that Bale would portray Gorr the God Butcher in the film.<ref name="DisneyInvestorDay" /> [[Jaimie Alexander]] was also revealed to be reprising her MCU role in ''Love and Thunder'', returning as [[Sif (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Sif]] from the first two ''Thor'' films after not appearing in ''Ragnarok'',<ref name="Alexander" /><ref name="AlexanderRagnarok" /><ref name="AlexanderRagnarok2" /> alongside other members and allies of the Guardians of the Galaxy: [[Pom Klementieff]] as [[Mantis (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mantis]],<ref name="KlementieffSydney" /> [[Dave Bautista]] as [[Drax (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Drax the Destroyer]], Karen Gillan as Nebula,<ref name="BautistaGillan" /> and [[Sean Gunn]] as [[Kraglin Obfonteri]]; Gunn also provides on set reference for Guardians member [[Rocket (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Rocket]].<ref name="BautistaGillan" /><ref name="GunnKraglin" /> [[Matt Damon]] was set to appear, after making a cameo appearance in ''Ragnarok'' as an Asgardian actor playing [[Loki (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Loki]].<ref name="BautistaGillan" /><ref name="Damon" /> He received special permission to enter Australia, despite the country's strict [[COVID-19 pandemic in Australia#Travel restrictions|travel restrictions during the pandemic]], since ''Love and Thunder'' was providing jobs for Australians.<ref name="DamonGQ" /> This prompted criticism as it was perceived that Damon, who traveled with his family, received preferential treatment to enter when many Australian nationals abroad were unable to return to the country.<ref name="Damon" /> [[Sam Neill]]—who appeared alongside Damon in ''Ragnarok'' as an actor playing [[Odin (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Odin]]—said there was a good chance he would be in the film as well, as long as he could travel from New Zealand to Australia during the pandemic.<ref name="NeillPotential" />

[[Mayes C. Rubeo]] served as the film's costume designer, after doing so for ''Ragnarok''.<ref name="RubeoDeadline" /><ref name="RubeoWWD" /> For ''Love and Thunder'', Rubeo focused on making clothes that reflect faithfully to the comics and fit Thor's mythology.<ref name="RubeoDeadline" /> Making of Foster's costume required 10 [[set construction]]s.<ref name="RubeoMPA" /> Rubeo created Foster's costume "based on the old comics" while maintaining her heroic attributes, with advanced technology that was "flexible and versatile". As Portman was [[vegan]], it was important to Rubeo that she make Foster's costume out of non-[[animal material]]s such as plastic and other [[Synthetic polymers|synthetic]] materials. To differentiate Foster from Thor, Rubeo added "silver metallic and deeper red hues" to Foster's costume since Thor's one used "new colors", as well as her winged helmet.<ref name="RubeoDeadline" /><ref name="RubeoWWD" /> Rubeo created 25 costumes for Hemsworth. These included many types of Thor's typical uniform which Rubeo integrated "more vibrant colors, like reds, blues and metallic golds to match the exaggerated aesthetic of the film."<ref name="RubeoDeadline" /><ref name="RubeoWWD" /> Thor's 1980s costume was influenced by [[Kurt Russell]]'s character Jack Burton in the film ''[[Big Trouble in Little China]]'' (1986),<ref name="RubeoNYT" /> and his other costumes made more "flashier" compared to Foster's. Gorr's costume—unlike those of most of the characters in ''Love and Thunder'', which were taken directly from the comics—was redesigned for the film, with Rubeo using [[classical sculpture]]s as a source of inspiration for his outfit during the [[Drapery|draping]] process. For a "great contrast to vibrant colors" used by other characters, Rubeo applied white to his costume rather than black, reflecting his supernatural nature.<ref name="RubeoDeadline" /><ref name="RubeoWWD" /> Rubeo bought {{convert|50|yd}} of linen to hide the costume's [[wire rigger]] and the [[heating, ventilation, and air conditioning]] system; this prevented Bale from overheating. Leather masters Augusto and Giampaolo Grassi were hired to make a breastplate for [[Zeus (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Zeus]]' costume using [[gold leaf]].<ref name="RubeoNYT" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began on January 26, 2021,<ref name="FilmingStart" /> at Fox Studios Australia in Sydney,<ref name="NSWThorShangChi" /> under the [[working title]] ''The Big Salad''.<ref name="WorkingTitle" /> [[Baz Idoine|Barry "Baz" Idoine]] serves as the cinematographer,<ref name="THRReview" /> after previously working with Waititi on the [[Disney+]] ''[[Star Wars]]'' series  ''[[The Mandalorian]]'' (2019–2023).<ref name="Idoine" /> Filming was delayed from an initial August 2020 start date due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="NSWThorShangChi" /><ref name="2021Filming" /> [[Industrial Light & Magic]] provided the same [[StageCraft]] virtual production technology used on ''The Mandalorian'' which Waititi directed an episode of, creating a custom volume space at Fox Studios Australia. It featured more LED panels and offered a higher resolution than the original volume created for ''The Mandalorian''.<ref name="ILM" /> Waititi also used Satellite Lab's PlateLight technology (after previously using their Dynamiclight technology on ''Ragnarok''), which is a special rig designed to capture multiple lighting set-ups simultaneously within a single shot at a high frame rate, allowing Waititi to select which lighting set up he would like in post-production.<ref name="WaititiWired" /> Waititi believed he was able to shoot ''Love and Thunder'' more efficiently than ''Ragnarok'' due to already having experience working on a Marvel Studios film.<ref name="EWMay2022" />

Filming occurred at [[Centennial Park (Sydney)|Centennial Park]] in Sydney in early February,<ref name="CentennialPark" /> with Klementieff, Gillan, Bautista, and Pratt completing their scenes shortly afterward.<ref name="KlementieffGillanFilmingWrap" /><ref name="BautistaFilmingWrap" /><ref name="PrattFilmingWrap" /> In early March, set photos revealed Damon and Neill were reprising their roles as the Asgardian actors playing Loki and Odin, along with [[Luke Hemsworth]] in his ''Ragnarok'' role as the actor playing Thor. [[Melissa McCarthy]] was also revealed to be portraying an actress playing [[Hela (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hela]] alongside her husband [[Ben Falcone]] in an undisclosed role; these cameos were for a sequence where actors reenact scenes from ''Ragnarok''.<ref name="March2021Actors" /> Damon filmed his part over two days.<ref name="DamonGQ" /> Alexander completed filming her scenes by the end of the month,<ref name="AlexanderFilmingWrap" /> while Goldblum was confirmed to appear as the Grandmaster,<ref name="Goldblum" /> and [[Russell Crowe]] was revealed to be appearing in the film in what was described as a "fun [[Cameo appearance|cameo]]".<ref name="Crowe" /> Crowe finished filming his scenes in late April, around which time it was confirmed that he would play Zeus in the film.<ref name="CroweZeus" /> Crowe was originally set to play [[Satan]] in the film before his character was changed to Zeus.<ref name="CroweSatan" /> Septimus Caton would later be cast as Satan and had makeup and prosthetic tests done, but the character does not appear in the film.<ref name="SeptimusCaton" /> At the beginning of May, Waititi said there were four weeks left of shooting,<ref name="FilmingEnding" /> while Idoine said he would continue work on the film through the end of 2021.<ref name="Idoine" /> Filming [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on June 1, 2021,<ref name="FilmingWrap" /> by which point Neill had revealed that singer [[Jenny Morris (musician)|Jenny Morris]] would appear in the film,<ref name="NeillMorris" /> making a cameo as a [[New Asgard]] citizen.<ref == Marketing ==
[[Lego]] sets and [[Hasbro]] figures based on the film were revealed in February 2022,<ref name="LegoHasbro" /> with additional Lego sets and [[Funko Pops]] revealed that April.<ref name="LegoFunko" /> The teaser trailer was released on April 18,<ref name="VarietyTeaser" /> and commentators discussed its ending, which debuted Portman's Foster in the Mighty Thor costume and wielding a restored version of Thor's hammer Mjolnir. Marco Vito Oddo of ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]'' and Ryan Parker from ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' highlighted the use of "Sweet Child o' Mine", which Oddo felt indicated Waititi would "keep the hard rock aesthetics that helped ''Thor: Ragnarok'' become a huge success", while Parker called it a "bright, stylish, fun trailer [that] sets a tone for the film in true Taika Waititi form".<ref name="USATodayTeaser" /><ref name="ColliderTeaser" /><ref name="THRTeaser" /> Justin Harp of ''[[Digital Spy]]'' felt the humor from ''Ragnarok'' was "clearly back in this film too",<ref name="DigitalSpyTeaser" /> while Tom Power of ''[[TechRadar]]'' felt the trailer was a "superhero, intergalactic glam rock feast for the senses" and contained intriguing footage that did a good job of teasing the film.<ref name="TechRadarTeaser" /> Both ''[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]''{{'s}} Daniel Chin and ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]''{{'s}} Sophie Butcher highlighted the trailer's focus on Thor's journey of self-discovery as well as the lack of footage of Bale's Gorr. He thought the trailer was a "far cry from the ''Thor'' we witnessed over a decade ago, as the franchise has transformed into a full-blown space comedy". Butcher felt the trailer's introspection was not surprising due to the film being set after ''Endgame'', and said the trailer was brief but exciting.<ref name="TheRingerTeaser" /><ref name="EmpireTeaser" /> The trailer had 209 million global views in its first 24 hours, becoming the seventh most viewed trailer in that time period.<ref name="TeaserTrailerViews" />

A second trailer was released on May 23, during Game 4 of the [[2022 NBA playoffs#Eastern Conference finals|2022 NBA Eastern Conference finals]].<ref name="OfficialTrailerVariety" /> Many commentators highlighted Bale's appearance as Gorr the God Butcher in the trailer.<ref name="OfficialTrailerVariety" /><ref name="OfficialTrailerInverse" /><ref name="OfficialTrailerArcsTechnica" /> Eric Francisco of ''[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]]'' noted the similar comedic tone from ''Ragnarok'' while adding "some darker shades" with the inclusion of Gorr. He said Bale brought an "absolutely {{em|terrifying}} vision of Gorr the God Butcher to the screen", adding that it appears Hemsworth was ready to pass the mantle of Thor to Portman.<ref name="OfficialTrailerInverse" /> Jennifer Ouellette of ''[[Ars Technica]]'' noted that Gorr's scenes featured a different color palette, being shown in "primarily gray tones", which she felt made the contrast between other characters "more stark".<ref name="OfficialTrailerArcsTechnica" /> Zach Seemayer from ''[[Entertainment Tonight]]'' felt that it "gives fans everything they've been hoping for from the brief flashes of important moments from the first trailer in April", citing scenes featuring Portman's the Mighty Thor, Thor "living up to his 'Space Viking' title", and Gorr's footage.<ref name="OfficialTrailerET" /> Similarly, ''[[The A.V. Club]]''{{'s}} Sam Barsanti and William Hughes also highlighted Portman and Hemsworth's Thor, Gorr's appearance, and other comedic moments in the trailer, such as when Thor is nude after Crowe's Zeus exposes his disguise.<ref name="OfficialTrailerAVClub" /> Scott Mendelson of ''[[Forbes]]'' called the trailer "the real deal" compared to the teaser, and noted similarities with the ''[[Masters of the Universe]]'' franchise. He speculated that Thor and Foster would be "pining" for each other despite it being 11 years after their first encounter, and called it "a little depressing", as he hoped the two "A+ dating specimens would have moved on". He also highlighted how Gorr's scenes played with color and contrast, and the "interplay" between Foster and Valkyrie.<ref name="OfficialTrailerForbes" /> Footage from the film was also featured in the 2022 [[CineEurope]] presentation.<ref name="CineEurope" />

[[Ulta Beauty]] partnered with Marvel Studios to create a makeup collection based on the film.<ref name="UltaBeauty" /> On July 9, 2022, Waititi and Thompson appeared on ''[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]''{{'s}} ''Notes on a Scene'' series, analysing the "Taste the Rainbow" scene.<ref name="WaititiThompsonVF" /> The video was met with criticism for what some viewers perceived as Waititi and Thompson "mocking" the visual effects, on the same day that multiple VFX artists who worked for Marvel had spoken out against being "underpaid" and "overworked" by the studio's productions.<ref name="VFXControversy" />

== Release ==
=== Theatrical ===
''Thor: Love and Thunder'' premiered at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on June 23, 2022.<ref name="Premiere" /><ref name="ElCapitan" /> The film was theatrically released in Australia on July 6, 2022,<ref name="AustraliaNZReleaze" /> in the United Kingdom, Ireland, and New Zealand on July 7,<ref name="UKRelease" /><ref name="AustraliaNZReleaze" /> and in the United States on July 8, 2022,<ref name="DisneyPhase4Delay" /> in [[4DX]], [[RealD 3D]], [[IMAX]], [[ScreenX]], and [[Dolby Cinema]].<ref name="Format" /> It was previously set for release on November 5, 2021,<ref name="DeadlineSDCC" /> but was delayed to February 18, 2022, due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> It was then moved up a week to February 11 once ''[[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness]]'' was rescheduled from November 2021 to March 2022,<ref name="SonyPhase4Delay" /> and delayed once again to May 6, 2022, in December 2020,<ref name="DisneyInvestorDay" /><ref name="SonyPhase4Delay" /> before shifting to the July 2022 date in October 2021.<ref name="DisneyPhase4Delay" /> It is part of Phase Four of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFour" /> The El Capitan Theatre hosted two sensory inclusive screenings of the film in July 2022, featuring expedited check in, a "calming" lobby, reduced capacity, and gift bags for guests with items to aid with anxiety.<ref name="SensoryScreenings" />

The film was not released in Malaysia,<ref name="Malaysia" /> Brunei,<ref name="Brunei" /> Kuwait,<ref name="Kuwait" /> Bahrain,<ref name="Bahrain" /> Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Oman, Qatar,<ref name="Arab countries" /> and Jordan<ref name="Jordan" /> due to containing LGBT elements. ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' reported that the Malaysian release did not happen because Disney refused to make cuts to the film requested by the [[Film Censorship Board of Malaysia]].<ref name="Malaysia" /> The Malaysian government confirmed that this was due to [[LGBT]] elements not being cut from the film.<ref name="MalaysiaLGBT" /> However, the film became available on Disney+ in the Middle East region on September 8, 2022.<ref name="MiddleEastD+Release" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Thor: Love and Thunder'' grossed $343.3{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, and $417.7{{nbsp}}million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $760.9{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="BOM" /><ref name="NUM" /> It is the [[2022 in film#Highest-grossing films|eighth-highest-grossing film of 2022]].<ref name="Highest2022" /> The film's opening weekend earned $303.3 million globally, of which [[IMAX]] contributed $23 million, making it the third-largest July debut of all-time for the format.<ref name="DeadlineInternationalOpeningWeekend">{{Cite web |last=Tartaglione |first=Nancy |date=July 10, 2022 |title='Thor: Love And Thunder' Nails $302M Global Debut; 'Minions' Gru-ving To $400M WW – International Box Office |url=https://deadline.com/2022/07/thor-love-and-thunder-marvel-opening-weekend-minions-top-gun-maverick-global-international-box-officeinternational-box-office-1235061061/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220711211442/https://deadline.com/2022/07/thor-love-and-thunder-marvel-opening-weekend-minions-top-gun-maverick-global-international-box-officeinternational-box-office-1235061061/ |archive-date=July 11, 2022 |access-date=July 11, 2022 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' calculated the film's net profit as $103{{nbsp}}million, accounting for production budgets, marketing, talent participations, and other costs; box office grosses and home media revenues placed it ninth on their list of 2022's "Most Valuable Blockbusters".<ref name="DeadlineProfit" />

In the United States and Canada, the film was projected to gross $140–167 million from 4,375 theatres in its opening weekend, along with a total gross of $345–420 million.<ref name="BoxOfficeProJulProjection" /><ref name="DeadlineProjection" /> The film earned $69.5 million on its first day, which included $29 million from Thursday night previews. This marked the second-highest opening day and preview night of 2022, behind fellow MCU film ''[[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness]]'', as well as the fifth-highest opening day ever for an MCU film.<ref name="VarietyProjection" /> Its opening weekend earned $144.2 million, which was the largest debut of the ''Thor'' franchise, the twelfth-largest for the MCU, and the third-largest for 2022, behind ''Multiverse of Madness'' ($187.4 million) and ''[[Jurassic World Dominion]]'' ($145 million). Of the $144.2 million figure, over $13.8 million came from IMAX, making it the fifth-highest July debut of all-time for the format domestically. ''Love and Thunder'' was also the sixth film in the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] to earn over 10 million theater admissions in its opening weekend.<ref name="DeadlineThursPV" /> Its second weekend grossed $46.6 million, a decline of nearly 68%, representing one of the MCU's largest sophomore weekend drops. ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' attributed this to its CinemaScore grade and generally mixed reception from critics and audiences.<ref>{{Cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=July 17, 2022 |title='Thor: Love And Thunder' Drops −68% In Weekend 2; 'Crawdads' Sings $17M Tune – Sunday AM Box Office Update |url=https://deadline.com/2022/07/box-office-thor-love-and-thunder-paws-of-fury-where-the-crawdads-sing-2-1235064783/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220717001915/https://deadline.com/2022/07/box-office-thor-love-and-thunder-paws-of-fury-where-the-crawdads-sing-2-1235064783/ |archive-date=July 17, 2022 |access-date=July 17, 2022 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> In its third weekend the film made $22.6 million, finishing second behind newcomer ''[[Nope (film)|Nope]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Domestic 2022 Weekend 29 |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W29/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221019031035/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W29/ |archive-date=October 19, 2022 |access-date=July 28, 2022 |website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref>

Outside of North America, ''Love and Thunder'' grossed $15.7{{nbsp}}million from 17 markets on its opening day, surpassing the first-day overall results of ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'' (2017) by 39%.<ref name="DeadlineInternationalOpeningDay" /> Its weekend gross was $159.2 million from 47 markets, making it the MCU's ninth-largest overseas debut ever and the third-largest for any [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]] film released in the COVID-19 pandemic. It surpassed ''Ragnarok''<nowiki/>'s opening by 19%. IMAX contributed $9.2 million to the weekend, becoming the third-largest July debut ever overseas. It also set several pandemic box office milestones in its opening weekend, marking the second-highest pandemic opening in Australia, New Zealand and the Philippines, while earning the third-highest in numerous territories, including Brazil, Italy, Thailand, Singapore and Argentina.<ref name="DeadlineInternationalOpeningWeekend" /> As of July 24, 2022, the film's largest markets are the United Kingdom ($31.5 million), Australia ($25.5 million), Mexico ($23.2 million), South Korea ($22.3 million), and Brazil ($17.2 million).<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tartaglione |first=Nancy |date=July 24, 2022 |title=''Minions'' Mints $640M Global As ''Thor'' Closes In On $600M; ''Top Gun: Maverick'' Soaring To $1.3B WW This Week – International Box Office |url=https://deadline.com/2022/07/thor-love-and-thunder-minions-rise-of-gru-top-gun-maverick-jurassic-world-dominion-global-international-box-office-1235076356/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221120091138/https://deadline.com/2022/07/thor-love-and-thunder-minions-rise-of-gru-top-gun-maverick-jurassic-world-dominion-global-international-box-office-1235076356/ |archive-date=November 20, 2022 |access-date=July 24, 2022 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of {{RT data|score}}, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}, based on {{RT data|count}} reviews. The site's critics consensus reads: "In some ways, ''Thor: Love and Thunder'' feels like ''Ragnarok'' [[Redux (literary term)|redux]] – but overall, it offers enough fast-paced fun to make this a worthy addition to the MCU."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=thor_love_and_thunder |type=movie |title=Thor: Love and Thunder |access-date=January 10, 2023}}{{cbignore}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 57 out of 100, based on 64 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=thor-love-and-thunder |type=movie |title=Thor: Love and Thunder |access-date=July 14, 2022}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale, and [[PostTrak]] reported 77% of audience members gave it a positive score, with 63% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="DeadlineThursPV" />

In early reactions to the film, ''[[Screen Rant]]'' noted that the performances from Christian Bale and Natalie Portman were praised.<ref name="EarlyReceptionScreenRant" /> [[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' said there are "many words to describe [''Thor: Love and Thunder'']", stating "comedy is all part of the package" that made ''Ragnarok'' "such a genuine Marvel wildflower." Gleiberman wrote that the film "sustains its freshness" to the end, although it started with a "more awkward" first act with the Guardians of the Galaxy. He also praised Gorr's characterization–a "power[ful introduction]"–and concluded the review by saying he felt "moved" by the end.<ref name="GleibermanVariety" /> David Ehrlich of ''[[IndieWire]]'' praised the film for its light tone and humorous moments, writing that "[Taika] Waititi continues to brighten up the Thor movies with his own flavor of wackiness, which is as welcome here as it was grating in ''[[Jojo Rabbit]]''." However, he noted that the film "is clouded by its uncertain place in the universe from the moment it starts".<ref name="IndiewireReview" /> Nick Allen at ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film 3 out of 4 stars, stating that it was "more or less a victory lap for all that [Waititi] achieved with his previous Marvel film, the often hilarious, rousing, and plainly refreshing ''Thor: Ragnarok''. And while it has too many familiar flourishes and jokes, this entertaining sequel is still a force for good, with enough visual ambition and heart in front of and behind the camera to stand on its own." He praised Natalie Portman's performance that "conveys why it's great to see Jane again" and Christian Bale's "striking" role that he considered as the "closest we'll get to seeing him play [[Pennywise the Clown]], with a dash of [[Voldemort]], but tethered to the same humility [he] brings to his most human, humbled characters."<ref name="RogerEbertReview" />

Todd Gilchrist at ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' graded the film 'B', feeling that it did "[revisit] the cheeky, sentimental tone of the nearly universally beloved ''Thor: Ragnarok'', and propels its eponymous hero into new adventures that bring full circle a journey that started with the very first, much-less-beloved ''Thor'' back in 2011." He praised Portman and Bale's performances, with the latter's character being considered "the most interesting and sympathetic Marvel adversary since [[Michael B. Jordan]]'s Killmonger in ''[[Black Panther (film)|Black Panther]]''", and Waititi's "distinctive imagery [in the film] that may disturb and upset some viewers", outpacing [[Sam Raimi]]'s accomplishments in ''[[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness]]'' (2022).<ref name="AVClubReview" /> Leah Greenblatt at ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' also gave a similar grade, feeling that although "its cheerful melee of starry cameos, in-jokes, and Cliffs-Notes mythology, feels a lot like franchise fatigue, it also has frequent moments of gonzo charm, thanks largely to the Technicolor lunacy of writer-director [Waititi] and a cast that seems inordinately game to follow his lead."<ref name="EWReview" />

David Rooney of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' stated, "The movie feels weightless, flippant, instantly forgettable, sparking neither love nor thunder."<ref name="THRReview" /> David Sims of ''[[The Atlantic]]'' felt the film delivered a "usual lightning-streaked action and tossed-off gags, but this time, there's not enough heft behind the flashiness." He found it a "hasty-feeling mess of a movie" and "disappointing given that it's directed by [Waititi]", although he praised Bale's and Russell Crowe's performances.<ref name="TheAtlanticReview" /> David Fear of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' felt the film was "oddly unengaging; even the love and death aspects often feel like cold transmissions from distant sources." He saw it as a mess due to the "collision of competing tones, subplots, conceptual big swings and chaos masquerading as pathos".<ref name="RollingStoneReview" /> Eric Francisco at ''[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]]'' found Chris Hemsworth's Thor in the film becoming "meandering, left in the purgatory of a roundabout tale that makes some sense on paper but in execution fails to maintain viscosity", feeling that Marvel "doesn't know what to do with [the character] anymore." He felt that despite "striv[ing] for an emotional story about loss and love, the movie gets in its own way with tiresome relentless comedy, distracted plotting, and uneven tone."<ref name="InverseReview" /> [[Richard Roeper]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film 2 out of 4 stars, calling it "one of the goofiest and least consequential sagas in MCU history—an allegedly wild and wacky but ultimately disappointing and disjointed chapter in the ongoing story of the God of Thunder, who seems to get more clueless with each passing movie."<ref name="ChicagoSunTimesReview" />

At the London premiere Q&A, in response to the question "How gay will the film be?" Waititi exclaimed that it would be "super gay"; critics and moviegoers who saw the film expressed disappointment that this statement was false and that Marvel [[Queerbaiting|queerbaited]] the audience.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Murphy |first=Chris |date=July 8, 2022 |title=So, How Gay Is 'Thor: Love and Thunder,' Really? |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/07/so-how-gay-is-thor-love-and-thunder-really |magazine=Vanity Fair |access-date=August 1, 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Cheung |first=Kylie |date=July 8, 2022 |title='Thor: Love and Thunder' Could Have Been Gayer, Honestly |url=https://jezebel.com/thor-love-and-thunder-could-have-been-gayer-honestl-1849157300 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220722104818/https://jezebel.com/thor-love-and-thunder-could-have-been-gayer-honestl-1849157300 |archive-date=July 22, 2022 |access-date=August 1, 2022 |website=Jezebel}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Jaworski |first=Michelle |date=July 8, 2022 |title=Taika Waititi and Natalie Portman called out for over-hyping the queerness in 'Thor: Love and Thunder' |url=https://www.dailydot.com/unclick/thor-love-and-thunder-gay-natalie-portman/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220710084349/https://www.dailydot.com/unclick/thor-love-and-thunder-gay-natalie-portman/ |archive-date=July 10, 2022 |access-date=August 2, 2022 |website=The Daily Dot}}</ref> Jade King of ''TheGamer'' felt expectations from queer audiences for prominent "LGBTQ+ characters and themes" in ''Thor: Love and Thunder'' were not met, writing that "the film's creators have been out there talking up the LGBTQ+ representation when it isn't really a massive part of the experience. [...] it's time to temper your expectations and stop letting Marvel continue to queerbait us into disappointment."<ref>{{Cite news |last=King |first=Jade |date=July 8, 2022 |title=We Need To Stop Letting Ourselves Be Queerbaited By The MCU |work=TheGamer |url=https://www.thegamer.com/marvel-queerbaiting-thor-love-and-thunder/ |url-status=live |access-date=July 11, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220709064735/https://www.thegamer.com/marvel-queerbaiting-thor-love-and-thunder/ |archive-date=July 9, 2022}}</ref>

In June 2023, Hemsworth commented on the film's response, stating: "I think we just had too much fun. It just became too silly. It's always hard being in the center of it and having any real perspective [...] you just don't know how people are going to respond."<ref name="HemsworthGQJun2023" /> He later reflected on his own performance, believing he had become "a parody of [himself]" with the improv and "wackiness" of the film, and that he "didn’t stick the landing".<ref name="HemsworthVFApr2024" />

== Future ==
Waititi was unaware that the tagline "Thor will return" was added to the end of the film. Waititi was open to directing another ''Thor'' film if Hemsworth was also involved, stating the story would need to be "something surprising and unexpected", suggesting a low-budget [[Road movie|road trip film]] with no fight scenes, similar to ''[[Nebraska (film)|Nebraska]]'' (2013).<ref name="WaititiFuture" /> Hemsworth hoped that a future installment would have a "drastically different tone" that would "close the book" on the character.<ref name="HemsworthFutureVF" /> In June 2023, Hemsworth said informal conversations had discussed potential ideas for future appearances for the character. He also explained that he would want to return to Thor if all creatives were able to find another way to reinvent the character again to keep the experience "a little unpredictable" in order to not become stale with audiences.<ref name="HemsworthEWJun2023" /> Waititi noted in [[Titan Books]]' ''Thor: Love and Thunder – The Official Movie Special Book'', which was published in August 2023, that any additional ''Thor'' film would need to continue the character's evolution "but still in a very fun way and still giving him things to come up against" and would need to have a villain more powerful than [[Hela (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hela]], who is featured in ''Ragnarok''.<ref name="WaititiTitanBook" /> In November 2023, Waititi confirmed that he would not be involved with a fifth ''Thor'' film.<ref name="WaititiNov2023" />

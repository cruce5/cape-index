'''''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings''''' is a 2021 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the character [[Shang-Chi]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 25th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Destin Daniel Cretton]] from a screenplay he wrote with [[Dave Callaham]] and [[Andrew Lanham]], and stars [[Simu Liu]] as [[Shang-Chi (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Shang-Chi]] alongside [[Awkwafina]], [[Meng'er Zhang]], [[Fala Chen]], [[Florian Munteanu]], [[Benedict Wong]], [[Yuen Wah]], [[Michelle Yeoh]], [[Ben Kingsley]], and [[Tony Leung Chiu-wai|Tony Leung]]. In the film, Shang-Chi is forced to confront his past when his father [[Wenwu]] (Leung), the leader of the [[Ten Rings (organization)|Ten Rings]] terrorist organization, draws Shang-Chi and his sister [[Xialing]] (Zhang) into a search for a mythical village.

A film based on Shang-Chi entered development in 2001, but work did not begin in earnest until December 2018 when Callaham was hired. Cretton joined in March 2019 and the film's title and primary cast were announced that July. This revealed the film's connection to the Ten Rings organization, which previously appeared throughout the MCU, and its leader Wenwu who was adapted from the problematic comic book characters [[Zheng Zu|Fu Manchu]] and [[Mandarin (character)|the Mandarin]]. ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' is the first Marvel Studios film with an Asian director and a predominantly Asian cast. Filming began in Sydney in February 2020 but was put on hold in March due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. Production resumed in August and ended in October, with additional filming in San Francisco. [[Brad Allan]] and other members of the [[Jackie Chan Stunt Team]] coordinated the fight sequences.

''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' premiered in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on August 16, 2021, and was released in the United States on September 3 as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU. It grossed over $432&nbsp;million worldwide, making it the [[2021 in film|ninth-highest-grossing film of 2021]]. It set several box office records and received positive reviews from critics, many of whom praised the exploration and representation of [[Asian culture]] which differentiated the film from the rest of the MCU, as well as the action sequences and Leung's performance. The film received various accolades, including a nomination for [[Academy Award for Best Visual Effects|Best Visual Effects]] at the [[94th Academy Awards]]. A sequel is in development.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
A thousand years ago, [[Xu Wenwu]] discovers the mystical [[Ten Rings (object)|ten rings]] which grant godly powers including [[immortality]]. He establishes the [[Ten Rings (organization)|Ten Rings]] organization, conquering kingdoms and toppling governments throughout history. In 1996, Wenwu searches for [[Ta Lo]], a village said to harbor mythical beasts. He travels through a magical forest to the village entrance but is stopped by a guardian, [[Ying Li (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ying Li]]. The two soon fall in love, but the Ta Lo villagers reject Wenwu so Li chooses to leave with him. They marry and have two children, [[Shang-Chi (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Shang-Chi]] and [[Xu Xialing|Xialing]]. Wenwu abandons his organization and locks away the ten rings.

When Shang-Chi is seven years old, Li is murdered by Wenwu's enemies, the Iron Gang. Wenwu dons the rings once again, massacres the Iron Gang, and resumes leadership of his organization. He makes Shang-Chi undergo brutal training in [[martial arts]] but does not allow Xialing to train, prompting her to secretly teach herself. When Shang-Chi is 14, Wenwu sends him to assassinate the Iron Gang's leader. After completing his mission, a traumatized Shang-Chi runs away to San Francisco and adopts the name "Shaun".

In the present day, Shang-Chi works as a parking valet with his best friend [[Katy (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Katy]]. They are attacked on a bus by the Ten Rings organization who steal a pendant that Li gave to Shang-Chi. Shang-Chi travels to [[Macau]] to find Xialing, fearing that the Ten Rings will go after her matching pendant. He reveals his past to Katy who insists on helping him. They find Xialing at a secret fight club which she founded after escaping from Wenwu at 16. The Ten Rings attack the fight club and Wenwu arrives to capture Shang-Chi, Katy, Xialing, and her pendant.

At the Ten Rings' compound, Wenwu uses the pendants to reveal a mystical map leading to Ta Lo. Wenwu explains that he has heard Li calling and believes she has been held captive in Ta Lo behind a sealed gate. He plans to destroy the village unless they release her. When his children and Katy object, he imprisons them. They meet former actor [[Trevor Slattery]], whom the Ten Rings imprisoned for impersonating Wenwu,{{efn|As depicted in  ''[[Iron Man 3]]'' (2013) and the [[Marvel One-Shot]] short film ''[[All Hail the King]]'' (2014)}} and his [[hundun]] companion [[Morris (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Morris]]. They escape and Morris guides them to Ta Lo, which exists in a separate dimension with various [[Chinese mythological creatures]].

The group meet Li's sister [[Ying Nan]] who explains the history of Ta Lo: thousands of years ago, the village's universe was attacked by the soul-consuming [[Dweller-in-Darkness (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dweller-in-Darkness]] and its soul eaters, but was saved by a [[Chinese dragon]] called the [[Great Protector]] who helped seal the Dark Gate to the Dweller's world. According to Nan, the Dweller-in-Darkness has been impersonating Li so Wenwu will use the ten rings to open the Gate. Shang-Chi, Xialing, Katy, Slattery, and Morris join the villagers in training and preparing for Wenwu's arrival, using outfits and weapons crafted from dragon scales that are effective against the soul eaters.

Wenwu and the Ten Rings arrive and attack. Wenwu overpowers Shang-Chi, pushes him into the nearby lake, and attacks the Gate with the rings. This allows some of the Dweller's soul eaters to escape, with the Ten Rings allying with the villagers to effectively fight them. The Great Protector revives Shang-Chi and bears him from the lake to battle the soul eaters. Wenwu and Shang-Chi fight once more, and Shang-Chi gains the upper hand but chooses to spare Wenwu. The Dweller-in-Darkness escapes from the weakened Gate and attacks Shang-Chi. Wenwu saves Shang-Chi but is killed by the Dweller-in-Darkness; he bequeaths the rings to Shang-Chi before he dies. Shang-Chi, the Great Protector, Xialing, and Katy slay the Dweller-in-Darkness. Afterward, Shang-Chi and Katy return to San Francisco where the sorcerer [[Wong (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wong]] summons them to [[Kamar-Taj (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kamar-Taj]].

In a [[mid-credits scene]], Wong introduces Shang-Chi and Katy to [[Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bruce Banner]] and [[Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Carol Danvers]] and the group discusses the origins of the rings. They discover that the rings are acting as a beacon to something. In a post-credits scene, Xialing becomes the new leader of the Ten Rings, despite telling Shang-Chi that she would disband the organization, and begins training women alongside men.

== Production ==
=== Development ===
According to [[Margaret Loesch]], former president and CEO of [[Marvel Productions]], [[Stan Lee]] discussed a potential film or television series based on the [[Marvel Comics]] character [[Shang-Chi]] with actor [[Brandon Lee]] and his mother [[Linda Lee Cadwell|Linda Lee]] during the 1980s, with the intention of having Brandon Lee star as the character.<ref name="BrandonLee" /> Brandon's father, martial arts legend [[Bruce Lee]], was the visual inspiration for artist [[Paul Gulacy]] when drawing Shang-Chi during his tenure on the ''[[Master of Kung Fu (comics)|Master of Kung Fu]]'' comic book in the 1970s.<ref name="CBA7" /> In 2001, [[Stephen Norrington]] signed on to direct a Shang-Chi film entitled ''The Hands of Shang-Chi''.<ref name="BloodyDisgusting" /><ref name="NorringtonLee" /> By 2003, it was in development at [[DreamWorks Pictures]] with [[Yuen Woo-Ping]] replacing Norrington as director and [[Bruce C. McKenna]] writing the screenplay.<ref name="Dreamworks" /> [[Ang Lee]] joined the project as a producer in 2004, but it did not materialize and the rights to the character reverted to Marvel.<ref name="NorringtonLee" /> In September 2005, Marvel chairman and CEO [[Avi Arad]] announced Shang-Chi as one of ten properties being developed as films by the newly formed [[Marvel Studios]],<ref name="IGNSept05" /> after the company received financing to produce the slate with [[Paramount Pictures]] as distributor.<ref name="10Films" /> Marvel thought the character could make a great film, despite being relatively unknown, because he has a "very [[Disney]] story" in the comic books.<ref name="LiuMensHealth" />

The [[Ten Rings (organization)|Ten Rings]] organization was introduced in Marvel Studios' first [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) film, ''[[Iron Man (2008 film)|Iron Man]]'' (2008), without its leader the [[Mandarin (character)|Mandarin]]. Marvel Studios President [[Kevin Feige]] felt they could not do the character justice in the ''Iron Man'' films because they focused on [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark / Iron Man]], and planned to introduce him in a later film instead.<ref name="FeigeInterviewRT" /> According to Chris Fenton, former president of the Chinese-based film production company [[DMG Entertainment]] that was in talks with Marvel Studios to co-produce their films, Marvel offered to create a teaser featuring either Shang-Chi or the Mandarin for the Chinese market that would be featured at the end of ''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|The Avengers]]'' (2012). DMG balked at the offer since the Mandarin's negative stereotypical portrayal in the comics could potentially prevent the film from being released in China and risk DMG being shut down. The Mandarin would eventually appear in the DMG co-produced film ''[[Iron Man 3]]'' (2013) portrayed by [[Ben Kingsley]], but he is revealed to be an actor named [[Trevor Slattery]] who was hired to pose as the Mandarin.<ref name="DMG" /> Feige felt this did not necessarily mean a more faithful version of the character did not exist in the MCU,<ref name="FeigeInterviewRT" /> and this was confirmed in the [[Marvel One-Shot]] short film ''[[All Hail the King]]'' (2014) in which Kingsley reprised his role as Slattery.<ref name="KingsleyAHtK" /><ref name="ShaneBlackAHtK" />

[[File:Destin Daniel Cretton by Gage Skidmore.jpg|thumb|upright|left|Destin Daniel Cretton promoting the film at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con]]
By December 2018, Marvel had fast-tracked development of a Shang-Chi film with the intent of making it their first film with an Asian lead. Marvel hired Chinese-American writer [[Dave Callaham]] to write the screenplay, and began looking at Asian and Asian-American filmmakers to potentially direct the film. The studios' goal was to have Asian and Asian-American filmmakers explore themes related to their culture, as they had done for African and African-American culture with the MCU film ''[[Black Panther (film)|Black Panther]]'' earlier in 2018.<ref name="Callaham" /> Development of the film also came following the success of the film ''[[Crazy Rich Asians (film)|Crazy Rich Asians]]'' that was likewise released earlier in 2018 and led to several other Asian-led properties being developed by [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]] studios.<ref name="Callaham2" /> Callaham's script was expected to modernize elements of the comic book story, which was first written in the 1970s, to avoid what modern audiences would consider to be negative stereotypes.<ref name="Callaham" /> When Callaham began work on the script, he became emotional realizing it was the first project where he was asked to write "from my own experience, from my own perspective".<ref name="EWCoverStoryJul2021" /> [[Jessica Gao]], who would later become the head writer of the Marvel Studios television series ''[[She-Hulk: Attorney at Law]]'' (2022), also presented a [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] for the film.<ref name="/FilmGaoAug2022" /> Richard Newby of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' said the film could "break out in a way similar to ''Black Panther''" by bringing a new perspective to the character. Newby felt Shang-Chi could have worked well as a television series, and said it "speaks volumes" that Marvel would decide to make a feature film about the character instead. Newby concluded that the film was an opportunity to avoid stereotypes about Asian martial artists and be "more than Marvel's Bruce Lee".<ref name="THRNewbyDec2018" />

Marvel Studios hired Japanese-American filmmaker [[Destin Daniel Cretton]] to direct the film in March 2019. [[Deborah Chow]]—who previously directed episodes of [[Marvel Television]]'s [[Marvel's Netflix television series|Netflix series]] ''[[Iron Fist (TV series)|Iron Fist]]'' (2017–2018) and ''[[Jessica Jones (TV series)|Jessica Jones]]'' (2015–2019)—[[Justin Tipping]], and [[Alan Yang]] were also considered.<ref name="THRCrettonMarch2019" /> Cretton had previously not been interested in directing a superhero film, but was drawn to the project to help create a world and character that Asian children could look up to and see themselves in.<ref name="EWApril2021" /> His pitch included visual inspiration from [[Cinema of China|Chinese]], [[Cinema of South Korea|South Korean]], [[Cinema of Japan|Japanese]], and other [[Cinema of Asia|Asian cinema]], including [[anime]], to highlight a tone that showed "the drama and the pain of life, but also show[ed] the humor of life".<ref name="GeekCulture" /> In April, Marvel Studios and Australian [[Minister for the Arts (Australia)|Arts Minister]] [[Mitch Fifield]] announced that an upcoming Marvel film, believed to be ''Shang-Chi'', would be filmed at [[Fox Studios Australia]] in [[Sydney]] and on location throughout the state of [[New South Wales]]. The production received AU$24&nbsp;million (US${{To USD|24|AUS|round=yes}} million) in one-off funding from the Australian government, as well as backing from the AU$10&nbsp;million (US${{To USD|10|AUS|round=yes}} million) "Made in NSW" state fund. The production was expected to generate AU$150&nbsp;million (US${{To USD|150|AUS|round=yes}} million) for the Australian economy as well as 4,700 new jobs, while taking advantage of around 1,200 local businesses.<ref name="Sydney" /> [[Don Harwin]], the [[Minister for the Public Service and Employee Relations, Aboriginal Affairs, and the Arts|New South Wales Arts Minister]], confirmed in July that this film was ''Shang-Chi'' and it would be produced [[Back-to-back film production|back-to-back]] with Marvel Studios' ''[[Thor: Love and Thunder]]'' (2022).<ref name="NSWThorShangChi" /> Production on ''Shang-Chi'' was set to begin in early 2020 and be completed later that year.<ref name="NSWThorShangChi" /><ref name="CrettonOct2019" />

=== Pre-production ===
==== Casting ====
Marvel Studios began testing actors in their 20s for the role of Shang-Chi in mid-July 2019,<ref name="KrollCasting" /> including [[Lewis Tan]] and [[Simu Liu]];<ref name="ComicCon2019" /><ref name="Tan" /> Tan previously portrayed [[Zhou Cheng (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Zhou Cheng]] in ''Iron Fist''.<ref name="Tan" /> The studio was adamant that actors be of Chinese descent to audition for the character.<ref name="KrollCasting" /> Liu was considered earlier in the audition process and was brought back in for a second audition when the creatives were finding it difficult to cast the role.<ref name="FeigeInterviewRT" /> He tested again on July 14 and was officially cast on July 16.<ref name="ComicCon2019" /> [[Awkwafina]], who was the first actor cast for the film, had chemistry tests with the potential actors and said "it was apparent that [Liu] was Shang-Chi from the jump".<ref name="AwkwafinaTHR" /> Liu and Awkwafina's castings were announced by Cretton and producer Feige at Marvel Studios' [[San Diego Comic-Con]] panel on July 20, where the film's full title was announced to be ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings''. Feige noted the connection between this title and the Ten Rings organization that had appeared throughout the MCU. He said the group's leader, the Mandarin, would be introduced in this film and [[Tony Leung Chiu-wai|Tony Leung]] had been cast in the role.<ref name="ComicCon2019" /> Cretton had wanted to cast Leung but was doubtful he would accept the part. Despite this hesitation, producer [[Jonathan Schwartz (film producer)|Jonathan Schwartz]] decided to contact Leung and he accepted the role after a meeting with Cretton.<ref name="LeungGQ" />

In December, Feige said the film would feature a predominantly Asian cast.<ref name="FeigeAsianCast" /> A month later, [[Michelle Yeoh]] entered talks for a role in the film. This was for a different character than [[Aleta Ogord (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Aleta Ogord]], who Yeoh had briefly portrayed in the MCU film ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2]]'' (2017).<ref name="Yeoh" /> [[Jessica Henwick]], who previously portrayed [[Colleen Wing (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Colleen Wing]] in Marvel's Netflix series, was asked to audition for the role of Xialing in the film,<ref name="HenwickTHR" /> but was concurrently offered the role of Bugs in the film ''[[The Matrix Resurrections]]'' (2021). After being given an ultimatum to select one of the roles by the respective studios, Henwick chose the ''Resurrections'' part.<ref name="HenwickPassedRole" /> This was partially because she hoped to reprise the role of Wing in a future MCU production.<ref name="HenwickTHR" /> Newcomer [[Meng'er Zhang]] was eventually cast in the role.<ref name="SRCast" /> Kingsley reprises his role as Slattery in the film.<ref name="Kingsley" /> The character's inclusion was planned early on to help fully explore the "context of who the Mandarin is in the MCU".<ref name="SlatteryEW" /> Schwartz called Slattery a "secret weapon" whose introduction part-way through the film moves it in a different direction and provides comic relief.<ref name="KingsleyMarvel" />

==== Writing ====
In addition to Callaham, Cretton and [[Andrew Lanham]] also contributed to the screenplay for ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' from a story by Callaham and Cretton.<ref name="VarietyReview" /><ref name="ColliderTeaser" /> The film was described as a "sweeping superhero epic that combines emotional family drama with gravity-defying martial arts action". Schwartz said much of Shang-Chi's arc within Marvel Comics is a family drama, and Cretton wanted to focus on that element for the film, exploring Shang-Chi's broken and abusive family background. Liu noted that the comic book backstory for Shang-Chi is not widely known like those for comic book characters such as [[Batman]] or [[Spider-Man]], and that gave the film's writers freedom to take more creative liberties with the story. Cretton and Callaham were cognizant of some of the racial stereotypes surrounding the character in the comics, with Liu saying everyone involved was "very sensitive to not have it go into stereotypical territory". Cretton believed the resulting script was a "really beautiful update" to the character from what began in the comics.<ref name="EWApril2021" />

Cretton felt the film told an authentic story about Asian identity.<ref name="EWApril2021" /> Callaham said there is "no single Asian American voice", and he and Cretton contemplated how the film could speak to "the wider Asian diaspora" and would be generally entertaining but also "personal to all these people".<ref name="EWCoverStoryJul2021" /> Cast members Liu, Leung, and Zhang contributed their own experiences coming from Canada, Hong Kong, and mainland China, respectively, to add to the authenticity of the film. Some aspects that were discussed regarding each scene were whether characters should be speaking in [[Mandarin Chinese]] or [[English language|English]] and the type of food served in the different households to ensure it felt authentic to whose house it was. The film's opening begins with narration entirely in Mandarin, which [[Nancy Wang Yuen]], writing for ''[[io9]]'', said was a striking decision for an MCU film to "begin in a language other than English and continue to do so for an extended period". Regarding the use of Mandarin in the film, Cretton said the choice of which language to use when was "always rooted in just the logic of the characters and who would naturally be speaking what language". Zhang, whose first language is Mandarin, served as a dialect coach for the other actors. ''Shang-Chi'' further deals with some of the negative characterization around Asian-Americans and languages by portraying its characters as having varied knowledge of Asian languages, as demonstrated by an exchange with Katy and the character [[Jon Jon]] when Jon Jon says he speaks "ABC" ([[American-born Chinese]]). Another such moment is when Shang-Chi teaches Katy how to pronounce his name, which serves as a meta moment to also aid the audience on learning the proper pronunciation of "Shang-Chi".<ref name="Crettonio9" />

Cretton felt it was important to have "many strong women" around Shang-Chi that were "kicking his butt into gear" in contrast to the relationship between Shang-Chi and his father [[Wenwu]] (the film's version of the Mandarin). These supporting characters also have their own narrative arcs.<ref name="CrettonWomenio9" /> Cretton's three sisters and his wife helped inspire the characters Katy, Xialing, Ying Nan, and Ying Li.<ref name="MarvelWomen" /> Speaking to the relationship between Shang-Chi and Katy, Cretton enjoyed being able to show a strong, "deeply caring" friendship that is rarely seen in superhero films, adding that it would have felt forced to have the two characters end up in a romantic relationship together.<ref name="CrettonRomance" /> Yeoh requested a scene be added between her character Ying Nan and Zhang's Xialing that would empower Xialing to "step out of the shadows for the first time"; this scene became an important part of Xialing's journey in the film and was one of the many scenes that also addressed the sexism presented in the story.<ref name="CrettonWomenio9" /> Cretton felt seeing Xialing become the new head of the Ten Rings organization in the film's post-credits scene was reflective of her beginning to take control of her life. Various versions of the scene existed throughout the production before it was moved to be after the credits since they thought it was "a juicy idea for where the story might go in the future".<ref name="PostCredits" />

==== Design ====
Sue Chan served as production designer for the film. The first location that she and the crew scouted was San Francisco, where Chan previously lived. She suggested Clement Street, "the old Chinatown of San Francisco", as the characters' home because it does not have the expected Chinatown cliches. The film's San Francisco apartment set was one of Chan's favorites due to its accuracy to real Asian-American houses. Another favorite was a restaurant set where Wenwu kills his enemies, for which Chan took inspiration from Hong Kong gangster films. Cretton wanted Xialing's fight club to be fun and show her character rather than be scary and dangerous. Chan noted that Xialing's office combines imagery of skulls with flowers and bold colors, and the sets incorporate the character's feminist views. The fighting arena is an octagon because the number eight is significant in Asian cultures.<ref name="ChanAD" />

For Ta Lo, Chan researched ancient Chinese culture and designs and took elements from many eras since the village is not from a specific time in Chinese history. These elements includes symbolism, styles, and colors from the [[Tang dynasty|Tang]] and [[Song dynasty|Song dynasties]]. Key symbols for the village were the five [[Chinese elements]] of wood, water, fire, metal, and earth, with metal being replaced with [[Chinese dragon|dragon]] scales for the film. These elements were used as the basis of the village set, along with traditional construction elements such as bamboo (both real and artificial bamboo was used). The village itself was built in an open area by a reservoir outside of Sydney and all of the buildings in the village were planned, built, and dressed as they would be used by the inhabitants. These include a pottery shed, a cooking shed, and a schoolroom, all built around the central temple which is open on all sides and contains the history of Ta Lo.<ref name="ChanAD" /><ref name="ChanVariety" /> Ta Lo's universe is inhabited by various [[Chinese mythological creatures]], including dragons, [[fenghuang]], [[Chinese guardian lions|shishi]], [[hundun]], [[jiuwei hu]], and [[qilin]].<ref name="TaLoCreatures" /><ref name="TaLoCreatures2" /> [[Andy Park (artist)|Andy Park]] led Marvel Studios' visual development team for the film, and specifically designed Shang-Chi's hero costume. The costume combines elements of his own style with those from both his parents: the jacket is made from the Ta Lo dragon scales and is a gift from his mother, he receives the ten rings from his father at the end of the film, and he wears his own American shoes.<ref name="Park" /> Costume designer [[Kym Barrett]] also researched ancient Chinese designs and then adapted these to suit the film's stunt needs.<ref name="Barrett" />

The Ten Rings logo that was originally designed for the MCU featured a circle of ten interlocked rings with characters from the Mongolian language. The logo's appearance in ''Iron Man 3'' drew ire from the Mongolian government who felt the Mongolian scripts "offensively tied the country's intangible cultural heritage to a terrorist group". The logo was updated for ''Shang-Chi'' to use "inoffensive" Chinese characters instead, including synonyms for strength or power written in ancient seal script. Oyungerel Tsedevdamba, Mongolia's former minister of culture, sports and tourism, believed the change was more to appease the Chinese market than the Mongolian government.<ref name="NewTenRingsLogo" /> The actual ten rings are portrayed as bangles worn on the wrist rather than as finger rings like they are in the comics. Schwartz said multiple approaches to the rings were discussed, and the comic-accurate design looked "a little goofy in practice" and was too similar to the [[Infinity Stones (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Infinity Stones]] that appear throughout the MCU.<ref name="TenRingsInfinityStones" /> The Ten Rings compound goes through four stages in the film that reflect Wenwu's character: it begins as a "cold" fortress that prominently features the Ten Rings logo; the compound then becomes a family home with bright colors, gardens, design elements from the Ta Lo village, and a mosaic of a [[Phoenix (mythology)|phoenix]] replacing the Ten Rings logo; the color was then stripped from the sets to make it a "desolate shell" as Wenwu mourns the death of his wife; and finally elements of Xialing's fight club are introduced in the post-credits scene as she takes over the compound and makes it her own.<ref name="ChanAD" /><ref name="ChanVariety" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began in February 2020,<ref name="FeigeLATimesMarch2020" /><ref name="CrettonCoronavirus" /> shooting at Fox Studios Australia in Sydney and on location throughout the state of New South Wales,<ref name="Sydney" /> under the [[working title]] ''Steamboat''.<ref name="SanFranFilming" /> [[Bill Pope|William Pope]] served as cinematographer for the film, shooting on the [[Arri Alexa#Alexa LF|Arri Alexa LF]].<ref name="FXGuide" /> Cretton chose Pope because he felt the cinematographer's style could be both naturalistic and heightened, and because of Pope's work on the film ''[[The Matrix]]'' (1999) which Cretton believed had the right tone for an MCU film focused on Asian and Asian-American characters.<ref name="CrettonOct2019" /> Pope already wanted to work with Cretton after being impressed by his film ''[[Short Term 12]]'' (2013).<ref name="PopeBtL" /> Cretton was inspired for his direction by [[Jackie Chan filmography|Jackie Chan's filmography]], the [[Ip Man (film series)|''Ip Man'' series]], ''[[Tai Chi Master (film)|Tai Chi Master]]'' (1993), and ''[[Kung Fu Hustle]]'' (2004) among others in the martial arts and kung-fu genres,<ref name="CrettonFandango" /> as well as anime and video games.<ref name="Crettonio9" />

On March 12, after studios had started halting production on films due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], Cretton decided to have himself [[COVID-19 testing|tested]] for [[Coronavirus disease 2019|coronavirus]] after working closely with people who had potentially been exposed to it.<ref name="CrettonCoronavirus" /><ref name="FirstUnitSuspended" /> This was a precaution due to Cretton having a newborn baby, and he self-isolated while awaiting these results;<ref name="FirstUnitSuspended" /> the test later came back negative.<ref name="CrettonCoronavirus" /> While Cretton was self-isolating, Marvel suspended first unit production for the film but intended for other aspects such as second unit to continue as normal.<ref name="FirstUnitSuspended" /> On March 13, the rest of the film's production was paused as Disney halted filming on most of its projects.<ref name="AllUnitsSuspended" /> Before the shut down, [[Ronny Chieng]] joined the cast in an undisclosed role.<ref name="ChiengCast" /> In early April, Disney shifted much of the MCU's [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] slate of films due to the pandemic, moving ''Shang-Chi''{{'s}} release date to May 7, 2021.<ref name="Phase4Delay" />

Work building sets for the film resumed at the end of July 2020, and by August 2 all cast and crew members had arrived to begin shooting "in the coming days".<ref name="FilmingRestartCBR" /> Any cast and crew returning to Australia had to be quarantined for two weeks upon arrival before returning to work, according to [[COVID-19 pandemic in Australia#Preventive measures|Australia's COVID-19 guidelines]].<ref name="FilmingRestart" /> Later in August, Yeoh was confirmed to be appearing in the film.<ref name="YeohVariety" /> The next month, the film's release date was pushed back to July 9, 2021, after the MCU film ''[[Black Widow (2021 film)|Black Widow]]'' was shifted to the May 2021 date.<ref name="Phase4DelayAgain" /> In October, filming took place in San Francisco.<ref name="SanFranFilming" /> Due to the pandemic, crew in Australia could not travel to San Francisco for this filming.<ref name="ChanAD" /> Shooting locations included the [[Russian Hill, San Francisco|Russian Hill]], [[Noe Valley, San Francisco|Noe Valley]], and [[Nob Hill, San Francisco|Nob Hill]] neighborhoods, as well as [[Fisherman's Wharf, San Francisco|Fisherman's Wharf]] and [[Ghirardelli Square]].<ref name="SanFranFilming2" /><ref name="SanFranFilming3" /> Filming [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on October 24, 2020.<ref name="FilmingWrap" />

==== Choreography ====
[[File:Bus VFX sequence in Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings.jpg|thumb|Original plate from filming in Sydney (top) and a completed shot (bottom) from the bus fight sequence]]
Cretton wanted to use a range of different fighting styles in the film due to Shang-Chi being trained in different types of [[martial arts]]. These include the "elegant, almost ethereal wushu style" from the film ''[[Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon]]'' (2000) and the "kinetic" fights of [[Jackie Chan]]'s films. Supervising stunt coordinator [[Brad Allan]] along with other members of the [[Jackie Chan Stunt Team]] became responsible for making the different styles feel consistent,<ref name="EWApril2021" /> as well as containing elements of [[Hong Kong action cinema]].<ref name="GeekCulture" /> Schwartz said there was a meaning for each fighting style in the film, and they helped to tell the story visually.<ref name="EWApril2021" /> ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' is dedicated to Allan, who died in August 2021.<ref name="EmpireReview" /><ref name="AllanDeath" />

The bus fight sequence was part of Cretton's initial pitch for the film. He called it a "what-if scenario" that helped explain the kind of fight sequences he enjoyed, "ones where the stakes just keep rising as the fight continues". Once it was decided to include the sequence in the film and planning for it began, Cretton credited Allan for bringing the "[[Buster Keaton]]-like physical comedy [to the fight], mixed with setups and payoffs, and stakes rising ... to almost ridiculous levels".<ref name="CrettonTHRSep2021" /> The stunt team added a specific reference to Jackie Chan, who is known for this style of fighting, in which Shang-Chi takes off his jacket, spins it around while fighting, and puts it back on.<ref name="SanFranFilming3" /> Fight coordinator [[Andy Cheng]] said the whole sequence took over a year to plan, going through as many as 20 different iterations, with most of the differences pertaining to the fighting within the bus. He said figuring out how the bus would be sliced in half and choreographing a fight around that was the most difficult part.<ref name="ChengVarietyBusFight" /> The stunt team put together 10 to 15 different "stuntviz" edits planning out the sequence.<ref name="FXGuide" />

Two buses were shipped to Sydney for filming the sequence's interior shots. These buses were taken apart and re-assembled on a {{convert|15|ft|m}} high [[Gimbal#Film and video|gimbal]] and a {{convert|3.3|ft|m}} high gimbal, with the windows and seats removed most of the time for safety.<ref name="ChanAD" /><ref name="ChengVarietyBusFight" /> The sequence was partially completed when the production shut down for COVID, requiring those involved to "retune" once production resumed.<ref name="ChengVarietyBusFight" /> Filming for the interior shots took around six weeks.<ref name="FXGuide" /> The exterior shots were filmed in San Francisco, with a different crew due to the pandemic.<ref name="ChanAD" /> The visual effects team planned out the shoot remotely so the unit could get the footage that was needed.<ref name="FXGuide" /> Four more buses were used for this portion of the sequence, including one that was filmed crashing down a hill into several cars.<ref name="SanFranFilming3" /> The entire sequence was shot in [[48 fps]] to help with tracking and making adjustments, and was then converted back to [[24 fps]]. The shots in slow-motion were shot at 250 fps using a [[Phantom (high-speed camera brand)|Phantom Camera]].<ref name="FXGuide" /> The sequence ends at a real intersection in San Francisco that was partially filmed in Sydney and combined with footage from San Francisco by the visual effects team.<ref name="ChanAD" /> Cretton said there were some "fudges" in how accurate the sequence was to San Francisco—"you can't go downhill for six minutes straight"—but was mostly based on a real route,<ref name="SanFranFilming3" /> suggested by Chan.<ref name="ChanAD" /> The bus is meant to be a [[San Francisco Municipal Railway|Muni]] bus but different branding was used due to that company not wanting to be associated with a crashing bus.<ref name="SanFranFilming3" />

Cretton initially told Leung that he would not need to fight due to Wenwu's use of the ten rings, but the actor did ultimately have several fight sequences and only had two weeks to prepare for them. He said this was not difficult due to his experience acting in action films.<ref name="LeungTime" /> The character's first action scene, where he falls in love with Ying Li, took specific inspiration from the [[wuxia]]-style of films such as ''[[Hero (2002 film)|Hero]]'' (2002) which Leung starred in,<ref name="LeungTime" /><ref name="PopeBtL" /><ref name="EmpireJune2021" /> and he said filming the scene reminded him of his early career.<ref name="LeungTime" /> The fight in Macau on scaffolding outside Xialing's fightclub was filmed against [[Chroma key|bluescreen]] on several sets of scaffolding, including one that was built on a 45-degree gimbal to achieve a specific shot with Xialing.<ref name="FXGuide" /> The [[Kamehameha (Dragon Ball)|Kamehameha]] attack from the anime series ''[[Dragon Ball Z]]'' was an inspiration for the final fight between Shang-Chi and Wenwu, which Cretton also included in his pitch.<ref name="GeekCulture" />

=== Post-production ===
[[Nat Sanders]] and Elísabet Ronaldsdóttir served as co-editors on the film,<ref name="FilmEditors" /> alongside [[Harry Yoon]].<ref name="Yoon" /> In December 2020, Marvel revealed roles for several cast members, including Awkwafina as Shang-Chi's friend Katy, Yeoh as Jiang Nan, and Chieng as Jon Jon. They also announced the casting of Zhang as Xialing, [[Fala Chen]] as Jiang Li, and [[Florian Munteanu]] as [[Razor Fist (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Razor Fist]];<ref name="Dec2020Castings" /> Munteanu was cast after Marvel Studios was impressed with his role in the film ''[[Creed II]]'' (2018).<ref name="MunteanuImpress" /> In March 2021, the film's release date was pushed back once again to September 3, 2021, when ''Black Widow'' was shifted to the July 2021 date,<ref name="SeptemberRelease" /> and [[Dallas Liu]] was revealed to be appearing.<ref name="DallasLiu" />

The film's official trailer in June 2021 revealed that [[Benedict Wong]] would reprise his MCU role of [[Wong (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wong]], and the character [[Emil Blonsky (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Emil Blonsky / Abomination]] would be appearing alongside him;<ref name="FeigeRT" /><ref name="AbominationWongTrailer" /> the Abomination first appeared in the MCU in the film ''[[The Incredible Hulk (film)|The Incredible Hulk]]'' (2008), portrayed by [[Tim Roth]],<ref name="AbominationTrailer" /> and Roth returned to provide uncredited vocals for the character in ''Shang-Chi''.<ref name="RothAbominationVocals" /><ref name="CrettonTHRSep2021" /> Feige enjoyed returning to the Abomination after over a decade since his last MCU appearance, especially with the fans recognizing and embracing the reference in the trailer.<ref name="FeigeRT" /> The characters were added to the film after Gao created a storyline for them in ''She-Hulk: Attorney at Law'', so the audience could be reminded of Abomination and get excited for their upcoming appearances in the series.<ref name="AbominationWongSheHulk" /><ref name="GaoAbominationShangChi" /> Wong was thrilled to be part of the film and its Asian cast, expressing excitement to be "sat at a table of Asian excellence".<ref name="WongSyFy" /><ref name="WongCollider" />

The film's mid-credits scene, which features [[Mark Ruffalo]] as [[Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bruce Banner]] and [[Brie Larson]] as [[Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Carol Danvers]], was conceived late in production by Cretton to tease the origins of the ten rings. Several origins were written for the rings, but Callaham said none of these made a difference to the film's story so they decided to leave the origin ambiguous which meant it could be addressed in a later MCU project.<ref name="PostCredits" /> Cretton hoped they could feature Wong in the scene and have him go to karaoke with Shang-Chi and Katy to sing "[[Hotel California]]", but was unsure which additional Avengers characters would appear until late in post-production.<ref name="PostCredits2" /> Banner and Danvers were chosen since they each represent the science and space aspects of the MCU, respectively,<ref name="PostCredits" /> and because their appearances align with other events in the MCU happening around the time of the scene.<ref name="PostCredits2" /> Cretton also jokingly acknowledged that Larson's appearance continued her streak of appearing in his films after the pair worked together on ''Short Term 12'', ''[[The Glass Castle (2017 film)|The Glass Castle]]'' (2017), and ''[[Just Mercy]]'' (2019).<ref name="CrettonTHRSep2021" /><ref name="PostCredits" /> Ruffalo and Larson filmed their roles in early 2021 during the film's additional photography.<ref name="PostCredits2" /> Feige said the scene indicated how important Shang-Chi is to the MCU, likening it to [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]]'s appearance in the post-credits scene of ''Iron Man''.<ref name="ComicBookAug2021" />

==== Visual effects ====
[[Christopher Townsend]] was hired to be the visual effects supervisor for the film. He worked with eleven different vendors, and also hired the Australia-based Joe Farrell to serve as an additional supervisor working with another five vendors. They created more than 1,700 visual effects shots for the film. Farrell supervised the bus sequence in Sydney (and remotely for the San Francisco portion of shooting) and said it was one of the most complicated sequences he and Townsend had worked on. The scene featured 168 shots, with 40 to 50 of them being mostly digital. The environment required digital pieces for the bus, buildings, and people. Farrell said the sequence's motion made editing difficult, especially in regards to the nine passengers. They were mapped out to know where they were at all times and sometimes were digitally moved around. Because the Macau fight takes place on scaffolding outside a glass building, [[Rodeo FX]] had to [[Rotoscoping|rotoscope]] around the actors and replace the entire background. A digital version of downtown Macau was created based on reference filming that Farrell again supervised remotely.<ref name="FXGuide" />

{{external media|float=left|video1=[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y0606tpMVx4 Marvel Studios' Shang-Chi and The Legend of The Ten Rings End Credits Main On End Title Sequence] presents the film's main-on-end title sequence, [[YouTube]] video from [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]]'s channel}}
The map to Ta Lo went through many iterations to determine how that information should be conveyed. Cretton felt the completed scene, which uses water effects, connected well to the story and was "visually beautiful".<ref name="CrettonTHRSep2021" /> The film's main-on-end title sequence, designed by [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]], also focuses on the movement of water.<ref name="Perception" /> [[Rising Sun Pictures]] contributed more than 300 visual effects shots to the film and was primarily responsible for creating Ta Lo. They based the geography of the forest on different locations in [[Southeast Asia]], and integrated this digital environment with some practical elements from material shot in Sydney. The team also worked with the [[Australian Institute for Machine Learning]] to develop an advanced technique for facial replacement, powered by [[artificial intelligence]], which was used to replace the stunt double's faces with those of the principal actors for the action scenes.<ref name="RSPTaLoVFX" /> The hundun [[Morris (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Morris]] was inspired by Cretton's family dog, a 15-year-old [[dachshund]]. Images of hunduns, which do not have faces, were included during early development as potential inspiration for the film and Cretton wanted to feature one in the film in some way after seeing them. Morris was a green screen "blob" during filming, with Cretton crediting Kingsley for helping to "breathe life into him", making it feel as if Morris was a real character. Multiple different designs were tested for the character, including one that had him looking "like a plucked chicken", but the creatives wanted to ensure Morris remained cute, which was challenging since a character's eyes and face help convey their emotion. As such, they relied on the look of his fur and feathers.<ref name="MorrisMarvel" />

[[Wētā FX]] contributed more than 305 visual effects shots to the film, including creating the ten rings. Wētā visual effects supervisor Sean Walker said creating realistic-looking rings digitally was not complicated, but getting the correct movements for each character was; Shang-Chi uses the rings defensively while Wenwu uses them in an aggressive manner. Feige initially wanted the energy of each ring to have its own color as a reference to the comic book rings, which each have a different ability, but they found this to be too distracting.<ref name="WetaVFXInterview" /> Instead, the energy of the rings is based on who is using them, with warmer orange energy for Shang-Chi and a cooler blue color for Wenwu.<ref name="TenRingsColors" /> Wētā also created the [[Great Protector]] and the [[Dweller-in-Darkness (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dweller-in-Darkness]] for the final action sequence. The Great Protector's scales were initially based on [[quartz]], porcelain, and shells before the team found images of albino lizards, alligators, and snakes that they thought were the perfect reference for the creature. The dragon's hair was then simulated on top. In contrast, the Dweller-in-Darkness was based on rhino and elephant hides and armored lizards. The souls that the Dweller-in-Darkness and its soul eaters consume were intended to be colorful visualizations of [[chakra]]s but this was ultimately toned down to a more subtle effect.<ref name="WetaVFXInterview" /><ref name="WetaDragonDesign" />

== Music ==
{{Main|Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings (soundtrack){{!}}''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' (soundtrack)}}
[[Joel P. West]], who scored Cretton's four previous films, returned to compose the score for ''Shang-Chi'';<ref name="West" /> he acknowledged that this was the biggest film he had ever worked on. West began working on the film when Cretton was hired and developed his ideas throughout the filmmaking process. He wrote "ominous" and "dominating" music for Wenwu and more "ethereal" music for Ying Li, and then combined these to create Shang-Chi's music since the story is about him accepting both sides of his family. West spent months researching Chinese music so he could incorporate traditional Chinese elements into the score, primarily for Ying Li and Ta Lo.<ref name="WestVariety" /> Recording took place with a 70-piece London orchestra at [[Abbey Road Studios]] in London by June 2021,<ref name="West" /><ref name="WestVariety" /> with the [[UK Chinese Ensemble]] also providing traditional Chinese instruments and performances for the score.<ref name="WestVariety" />

An album featuring the film's score was released digitally by [[Marvel Music (record label)|Marvel Music]] and [[Hollywood Records]] on September 1, 2021.<ref name="Score" /> Marvel Music, Hollywood Records, and [[Interscope Records]] also released four separate [[single (music)|singles]] inspired by the film ahead of its release: "Lazy Susan" by [[21 Savage]] and [[Rich Brian]],<ref name="LazySusan" /> "Every Summertime" by [[Niki (singer)|Niki]],<ref name="EverySummertime" /> "[[Run It (DJ Snake song)|Run It]]" by [[DJ Snake]], [[Rick Ross]], and Rich Brian,<ref name="RunIt" /> and "In the Dark" by [[Swae Lee]] & [[Jhené Aiko]].<ref name="InTheDark" /> A soundtrack album was released on September 3 containing these in addition to songs by [[JJ Lin]], [[Saweetie]], [[Anderson .Paak]], and other members of the "next generation of Asian artists". This was produced by Sean Miyashiro and [[88rising]], who worked with Cretton to tell a story in the album about the Asian-American experience with particular inspiration from San Francisco because that city is featured in the film.<ref name="Soundtrack" /><ref name="SoundtrackMarvel" />

== Marketing ==
On April 19, 2021, which was Liu's birthday, he shared the first teaser poster for the film. This was shortly followed by Marvel releasing the first teaser trailer.<ref name="ColliderTeaser" /> Adam B. Vary of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' felt it was "gratifying to finally see Liu in action as Shang-Chi" and highlighted how the teaser provided further insight into details such as how the film would depict the Mandarin wearing the ten rings.<ref name="VarietyTeaser" /> Cole Delbyck at ''[[HuffPost]]'' said the "eye-popping" action was unlike anything seen in past MCU films.<ref name="HuffPostTeaser" /> Writing for ''io9'', Rob Bricken felt the teaser did not disappoint with its action, but the family drama was what made the film look compelling to him.<ref name="io9Teaser" /> ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'s}} Adam Chitwood called the teaser "pretty fantastic", comparing its story and tone to ''Black Panther'' and saying ''Shang-Chi'' looked to be "an exciting, fresh, and new Marvel Cinematic Universe experience" based on the teaser.<ref name="ColliderTeaser" /> Reactions to the poster and trailer in Chinese speaking regions in East Asia were more critical, with commentators believing both presented a stereotypical view of Chinese people and culture.<ref name="VarietyChinaReleaseMay2021" />

The film's first full trailer was released on June 24, 2021, during [[ESPN]]'s ''[[NBA Countdown]]''. Sean Keane at ''[[CNET]]'' enjoyed seeing more of Leung in the trailer and called the fight sequences "super-impressive". He was surprised by the inclusion of Abomination at the end of the trailer and noted that the character looked more like his design from the comics than when he appeared in ''The Incredible Hulk''.<ref name="CNETTrailer" /> ''[[Digital Spy]]''{{'s}} Gabriella Geisinger felt the Abomination's role in the film would just be a cameo appearance to set up the character's story in ''She-Hulk: Attorney at Law'' but also felt it could have "wide-reaching implications" for the MCU.<ref name="DigitalSpyTrailer" /> Germain Lussier of ''io9'', Susana Polo of ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]'', and Jennifer Ouellette of ''[[Ars Technica]]'' all felt the trailer was a better showcase for Shang-Chi than the teaser was,<ref name="io9Trailer" /><ref name="PolygonTrailer" /><ref name="ArsTechnicaTrailer" /> with Ouellette highlighting the different narration for the trailer that expanded on Shang-Chi's family background.<ref name="ArsTechnicaTrailer" /> Lussier also noted a lot of new visual effects in the trailer that were not in the teaser, and felt Shang-Chi would soon become a big star despite not being a well-known character.<ref name="io9Trailer" />

On August 15, 2021, Ron Han created a [[GoFundMe]] drive to raise money for Asian American Pacific Islander (API) children at the [[Boys & Girls Clubs of America|Boys & Girls Club]] in [[San Gabriel Valley]] to see ''Shang-Chi'', as well as a larger "Shang-Chi challenge" for other people to create similar drives for their communities; the challenge was inspired by a similar one created for ''Black Panther''.<ref name="ShangChiChallenge" /><ref name="GoldOpenFund" /> By the end of the month, API nonprofit organization Gold House partnered with GoFundMe to create the ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' Gold Open Community Fund to raise money for private screenings of the film for the API community and non-profit groups.<ref name="GoldOpenFund" /> An episode of the series ''[[Marvel Studios: Legends]]'' was released on September 1, exploring the Ten Rings organization using footage from its previous MCU appearances.<ref name="LegendsEp" /> Beginning September 3, Shang-Chi and Death Dealer began appearing in [[Disneyland]]'s [[Avengers Campus]].<ref name="AvengersCampus" /> Promotional partners for the film included Sanzo beverages, which released a limited-edition version of lychee flavor; [[Microsoft]];<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" /> [[BMW]], which acted as the film's global car sponsor and had the [[BMW iX3 (G08)|BMW iX3]] and [[BMW M8]] appear in the film;<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" /><ref name="BMWPress" /> [[Old Spice]];<ref name="OldSpice" /> and international sponsors including [[Visa Inc.|Visa]], [[Virgin Plus]], [[Gruppo TIM]], [[Mikron Group]], and BGF.<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" />

== Release ==
=== Theatrical ===
''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' had its world premiere at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] and [[TCL Chinese Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on August 16, 2021,<ref name="WorldPremierePermit" /><ref name="WorldPremiere" /> and was screened at [[CinemaCon]] on August 25.<ref name="CinemaCon2021" /> The film was released in international markets beginning on September 1, and had been released in 66% of its markets by the end of its first weekend.<ref name="DeadlineBOProjections" /> The film was released in the United States on September 3,<ref name="SeptemberReleaseExclusiveWindow" /> in over 4,200 theaters, of which 400 were [[IMAX]], over 850 were premium large format, 1,500 were [[3D film|3D]], and an additional 275 were the [[D-Box]], [[4DX]], and [[ScreenX]] formats.<ref name="DeadlineBOProjections" /> ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' is part of Phase Four of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFour" />

''Shang-Chi'' was previously scheduled to be released on February 12, 2021,<ref name="ComicCon2019" /> the first day of the [[Chinese New Year]],<ref name="SCMPReaction" /> before it was shifted to May 7,<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> and then to July 9, delayed due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="Phase4DelayAgain" /> The film shifted once again in March 2021 to the September 2021 date after ''Black Widow'' was moved to the July 9 release date.<ref name="SeptemberRelease" /> In Australia, ''Shang-Chi'' was released on September 2, with a planned release in New South Wales, [[Victoria (Australia)|Victoria]], and [[Australian Capital Territory]] on September 16 because of [[Timeline of the COVID-19 pandemic in Australia (July–December 2021)|COVID-19-related state lockdowns]];<ref name="AustraliaRelease" /> it was ultimately released in New South Wales on October 11 when theaters in the state reopened.<ref name="NSWRelease" />

The film had a 45-day exclusive theatrical release, rather than being released [[Simultaneous release|simultaneously]] in theaters and on [[Disney+]] with [[Disney+ Premier Access|Premier Access]] like ''Black Widow'' was.<ref name="SeptemberReleaseExclusiveWindow" /> In August 2021, with the increase of [[COVID-19 Delta variant]] cases, Disney CEO [[Bob Chapek]] explained that the film would stay theatrical-only for its 45-day window due to logisitical reasons and said this window was an interesting experiment for the company to learn more on how consumers wished to view their films;<ref name="45DayExperimentCollider" /><ref name="45DayExperimentVariety" /> Liu took issue with Chapek calling the film an experiment, but Feige later stated that Liu's response appeared to be based on a misunderstanding of Chapek's intention.<ref name="LiuFeigeExperiment" />

In May 2021, a Chinese state media report excluded ''Shang-Chi'', as well as Marvel's ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'', from its list of upcoming MCU films being released. ''Variety'' noted that this added to rumors that the films would not be released in China.<ref name="VarietyChinaReleaseMay2021" /> In September 2021, ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' reported that a theatrical release in China was unlikely due to comments Liu made in an interview with the [[Canadian Broadcasting Corporation|CBC]] in 2017 which referenced his parents' negative experiences living in China.<ref name="DeadlineChinaReleaseSept2021" /> Disney still listed the film's release in China as "to be determined" in October,<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd5" /> but it ultimately did not receive a theatrical release in the country.<ref name="DeFactoChinaBan" />

=== Home media ===
The film was released on [[Digital distribution|digital download]] on November 12, 2021,<ref name="HomeMedia" /> as well as on Disney+ as part of the service's "[[Disney+ Day]]" celebration.<ref name="Disney+Release" /> Disney+ also received the IMAX Enhanced version of the film.<ref name="IMAXEnhanced" /> It was released on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[DVD-Video|DVD]] on November 30. The home media of the film includes audio commentary, deleted scenes, a gag reel, and various behind-the-scenes featurettes.<ref name="HomeMedia" />

Following the release of ''Shang-Chi'' on Disney+, viewer tracking application [[Samba TV]] reported that over 1.7&nbsp;million U.S. households watched the film within its first weekend of availability. Samba TV also reported viewership numbers for the United Kingdom (250,000), Germany (85,000), and Australia (17,000),<ref name="Shang-ChiStreaming" /> while [[TV Time]] reported that it was the most-streamed film in the United States during that same time frame.<ref name="TVTimeStreaming" /> [[Nielsen Corporation|Nielsen]] stated that it was the second-most-streamed film, behind [[Netflix]]'s ''[[Red Notice (film)|Red Notice]]'', with a viewership of 1.072{{nbsp}}billion minutes in its first week. Asian Americans made up 10% of the audience, the highest percentage for any title on the chart.<ref name="NielsenStreaming" /> The film remained in the top five on Nielsen and TV Time's charts in the following weeks,<ref name="NielsenStreamingWknd2" /><ref name="TVTimeStreamingWknd2" /> and TV Time stated that it was the sixth most-streamed-film globally in 2021.<ref name="TVTimeStreaming2021" /> The film was number one on the [[The NPD Group|NPD]] Videoscan First Alert chart, which tracks combined sales of DVDs and Blu-rays, and the dedicated Blu-ray sales chart for two weeks.<ref name="HomeMediaChartsWknd1" /><ref name="HomeMediaChartsWknd2" /> It was the third most-selling title on disc for December 2021 according to the NPD Group.<ref name="HomeMediaChartsDec2021" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' grossed $224.5&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $207.7&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $432.2&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /> It became the highest-grossing film ever released on [[Labor Day]] weekend,<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" /> and earned $13.2&nbsp;million worldwide from IMAX, another Labor Day weekend record.<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd1" />

''Shang-Chi'' earned $29.6&nbsp;million on its opening day (which included $8.8&nbsp;million from its Thursday night previews), the third-best opening day since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic in March 2020. The Thursday night previews gross was the second-highest of the pandemic, behind ''Black Widow'' ($13.2&nbsp;million). It grossed $75.5&nbsp;million over its three-day opening weekend, the second-largest of the pandemic behind ''Black Widow'' ($80.3&nbsp;million). IMAX contributed $8&nbsp;million, which was a record for a Labor Day weekend release. The film earned $94.67&nbsp;million over the four-day Labor Day weekend, surpassing ''[[Halloween (2007 film)|Halloween]]''{{'s}} (2007) $30.6&nbsp;million to become the largest Labor Day weekend opening ever.<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" /> The film surpassed $100&nbsp;million in five days, the fastest film to reach that milestone since ''[[Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker]]'' (2019) did it in its opening weekend.<ref name="100Million" /> ''Shang-Chi'' remained the number one film in its second weekend, earning $35.8&nbsp;million which was the largest second weekend gross of the pandemic,<ref name="THRBOWknd2" /> and in its third weekend when it earned $21.7&nbsp;million, the second-largest third weekend gross for a September release behind ''[[It (2017 film)|It]]'' (2017).<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd3" /> It was the third MCU film following ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014) and ''Black Panther'' to remain the top film for four consecutive weeks,<ref name="TopFilm4Weeks" /> and was the first film of the pandemic era to surpassed $200&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada when it did so on September 30.<ref name="200Million" /> It was the third-highest-grossing film in its fifth weekend,<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd5" /> the fourth-highest in its sixth weekend,<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd6" /> and remained in the top ten at the box office for two additional weekends.<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd7" /><ref name="DeadlineBOWknd8" /> The film ended its run at the United States and Canada box office as the [[List of American films of 2021|second highest-grossing film of 2021]] behind ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]''.<ref name="AmericanFilms" />

The film earned $56.2&nbsp;million from 41 markets in its opening weekend, opening number one in many.<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd1" /> The United Kingdom release had the largest opening day of the pandemic,<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" /> and the largest three-day opening weekend of the pandemic with $7.7&nbsp;million. In Hong Kong, the film produced the largest September opening weekend, as well as the second-best opening during the pandemic. Nancy Tartaglione of ''Deadline Hollywood'' noted South Korea's $6.5&nbsp;million opening was an underperformance for the market and an MCU film, though it was the first Hollywood film to open at number one there in several weeks.<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd1" /> In its second weekend, the film earned $35.2&nbsp;million from 42 markets, remaining number one in several.<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd2" /> In its third weekend, the film earned an additional $20.3&nbsp;million from 43 markets and was still the top film in territories such as Australia, Brazil, Mexico, and the UK.<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd3" /> The film remained at number one in all of the territories in its fourth weekend, when it opened in Indonesia. It was number one there as well with $1.2&nbsp;million.<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd4" /> ''Shang-Chi'' was the top film in Australia for nine weekends, with its highest-grossing day coming on October 31, 2021.<ref name="DeadlineAustraliaBO" /> As of October 2021, the film's largest markets are the United Kingdom ($27.1&nbsp;million), South Korea ($14.9&nbsp;million), and France ($11.5&nbsp;million).<ref name="DeadlineBOIntWknd5" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of 92%, with an average score of 7.5/10, based on 344 reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'' isn't entirely free of Marvel's familiar formula, but this exciting origin story expands the MCU in more ways than one."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=shang_chi_and_the_legend_of_the_ten_rings |type=m |title=Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings |access-date=August 14, 2023}}{{cbignore}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 71 out of 100, based on 52 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=shang-chi-and-the-legend-of-the-ten-rings |type=movie |title=Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings Reviews |access-date=August 14, 2023}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Critics commonly praised the film's representation of Asian culture which differentiated it from other MCU films, as well as the performances and action sequences.<ref name="MashableReviewRoundUp" /> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale, while [[PostTrak]] reported 91% of audience members gave it a positive score and 78% said they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="DeadlineBOWknd1" />

Peter Debruge at ''Variety'' said the decision to give the "second-tier" Shang-Chi the same "over-the-top treatment" as major Marvel heroes had broadened Marvel Studios' cultural representation and would empower audiences of Asian descent in a similar way to what ''Black Panther'' did for audiences of African descent.<ref name="VarietyReview" /> Angie Han of ''The Hollywood Reporter'' felt the film did not combine its martial-arts, fantasy elements, and exploration of Chinese and Asian-American culture "as smoothly as [it] should", but did find it to be a fresh and fun superhero film.<ref name="THRReview" /> Giving the film 3.5 out of 4 stars, ''[[RogerEbert.com]]''{{'s}} Nick Allen said the film fit within Marvel's formula but had a soulfulness that other MCU films, as well as superhero and action films in general, did not have. He concluded that ''Shang-Chi'' was not an experiment for Marvel and Disney but rather "a promising template for how they can get it right again".<ref name="RogerEbertReview" /> Writing for ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'', Laura Sirikul said the film was "a winning blend" of Chinese culture and the Marvel formula that avoided Asian stereotypes and called out some of Marvel's past "racial errors".<ref name="EmpireReview" /> Francesca Rivera at ''[[IGN]]'' felt the film had successfully balanced the "real-life complicated conflict between first-gen Asian American children and the wishes of their immigrant parents" with the needs of an MCU film.<ref name="IGNReview" />

Alexis Nedd said Liu was "flawless" as Shang-Chi and compared his casting to that of [[Robert Downey Jr.]] as Tony Stark / Iron Man and [[Chris Evans (actor)|Chris Evans]] as [[Steve Rogers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Steve Rogers / Captain America]] in the MCU.<ref name="MashableReview" /> Jake Cole of ''[[Slant Magazine]]'' was more critical, giving the film 1.5 out of 4 stars and criticizing Liu's performance as being "curiously affectless". Cole also criticized the film's flashbacks which he felt slowed the overall story down, but praised Leung as "effortlessly convey[ing] the calm malice with which Wenwu asserts his absolute power as well as the anguish that the man feels over the loss of his wife".<ref name="SlantReview" /> Allen said Leung was the film's most brilliant casting,<ref name="RogerEbertReview" /> and Han praised his performance for bringing sincerity to Wenwu which made him a "supervillain with a soul".<ref name="THRReview" /> Katie Rife at ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' said Leung "exudes the type of movie-star charisma critics sometimes complain is on the decline". Rife felt Leung was not being challenged by the material in the film but was still able to bring emotional depth to Wenwu.<ref name="AVClubReview" /> Sirikul praised the performances of Liu, Awkwafina, and Leung, but felt some of the character arcs were undermined by the film's convoluted story and rushed ending.<ref name="EmpireReview" /> ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'s}} Leah Greenblatt highlighted Zhang, who she felt deserved her own spin-off, and Awkwafina.<ref name="EWReview" />

Rivera praised the film's action sequences as the best of the MCU so far, focusing on the choreography and cinematography and particularly highlighting the wuxia-style fight between Wenwu and Li at the start of the film, but criticized the digital backgrounds in Ta Lo.<ref name="IGNReview" /> Wenlei Ma at ''[[News.com.au]]'' also said the film's action had some of the best choreography of the MCU.<ref name="NewsAUReview" /> Greenblatt praised the bus fight, fight club, and skyscraper fight sequences as "astonishing set pieces",<ref name="EWReview" /> while Matt Goldberg at ''Collider'' also praised the action set pieces for combining classic kung-fu movie influences with MCU visual effects.<ref name="ColliderReview" /> Peter Hartlaub of the ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]'' said the bus fight was "one of the best car chases in San Francisco movie history".<ref name="SFCReview" /> Allen enjoyed the various fight sequences, saying Cretton changed the height, light, reflections, and staging for each. He described the film's final act as "such an over-the-top, giddy, rollercoaster ride that you can't help but root for it".<ref name="RogerEbertReview" /> In contrast, Cole took issue with the amount of visual effects in the action sequences and said the final act "devolves into loud and chaotic visual nonsense".<ref name="SlantReview" /> At ''[[/Film]]'', Hoai-Tran Bui also criticized the visual effects-heavy third act but said the film had "one of the more forgivable ones, if only because it verges on full fantasia", and praised the wuxia moments.<ref name="/FilmReview" />

== Documentary special ==
{{Main|Marvel Studios: Assembled{{!}}''Marvel Studios: Assembled''}}
In February 2021, the documentary series ''[[Marvel Studios: Assembled]]'' was announced. The specials go behind the scenes of the MCU films and television series with cast members and additional creatives.<ref name="Assembled" /> A special for this film, "[[The Making of Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings|The Making of ''Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings'']]", goes behind the scenes of the making of the film and was released on Disney+ on November 12, 2021.<ref name="AssembledRelease" />

== Sequel ==
By December 2021, development on a sequel had begun with Cretton returning to write and direct.<ref name="SequelConfirmed" /> Liu was also expected to return as Shang-Chi by the following month. He wanted the sequel to explore what his character would do with the "newfound power" of the ten rings, as well as how he fits into the larger MCU.<ref name="LiuSequel" /> Liu said in July 2023 that he was unsure when the film would be made.<ref name="LiuJul2023SequelUpdate" /> By that November, Cretton stepped down as director of the planned film ''[[Avengers: The Kang Dynasty]]'', which was eventually reworked into ''[[Avengers: Doomsday]]'' (2026), to focus on his commitments to other MCU projects, including the ''Shang-Chi'' sequel.<ref name="KangDynastyCrettonExit" /> In September 2024, Cretton signed on to direct ''[[Spider-Man: Brand New Day]]'' (2026), which was a higher priority for Marvel Studios than the ''Shang-Chi'' sequel.<ref name="CrettonSpideyTalksVariety" /> In January 2026, Liu reiterated that the sequel was still in development.<ref name="LiuJan2026" />

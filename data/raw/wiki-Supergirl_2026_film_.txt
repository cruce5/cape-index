'''''Supergirl''''' is a 2026 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[DC Comics]] superheroine [[Supergirl (Kara Zor-El)|Kara Zor-El / Supergirl]]. Directed by [[Craig Gillespie]] and written by [[Ana Nogueira]], it is the second film in the [[DC Universe (franchise)|DC Universe]] (DCU). [[Milly Alcock]] stars in the [[title role]], alongside [[Matthias Schoenaerts]], [[Eve Ridley]], [[David Krumholtz]], [[Emily Beecham]], [[David Corenswet]], and [[Jason Momoa]]. In the film, Kara embarks on a journey across the galaxy to hunt down an adversary who has harmed her pet dog [[Krypto (DC Universe)|Krypto]]. The film is produced by [[Peter Safran]] and [[James Gunn]] of [[DC Studios]].

A film featuring Supergirl entered development as part of the [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU) franchise in August 2018, and the character was introduced in the 2023 film ''[[The Flash (film)|The Flash]]'', portrayed by [[Sasha Calle]]. Plans for the standalone project were altered when Gunn and Safran became co-CEOs of DC Studios in October 2022, and they began work on the DCU as a reboot of the DCEU. A new Supergirl film was announced in January 2023 as an adaptation of the 2021–22 comic book miniseries ''[[Supergirl: Woman of Tomorrow]]'' by [[Tom King (writer)|Tom King]] and [[Bilquis Evely]]. Nogueira was hired by November 2023; Alcock was cast in January 2024 to debut in the first DCU film, ''[[Superman (2025 film)|Superman]]'' (2025); and Gillespie joined that May. Filming took place from January to May 2025 at [[Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden]] and London in England, and in [[Scotland]].

''Supergirl'' premiered at [[Brooklyn Cultural District|the Plaza at 300 Ashland]] in Brooklyn on June 22, 2026, and was theatrically released by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] in the United States on June 26. It is part of the DCU's [[Chapter One: Gods and Monsters]]. The film received mixed reviews from critics and was a [[box-office bomb]], grossing $126.4&nbsp;million against a production budget of $170–186&nbsp;million.<ref>{{cite news |last=Coyle |first=Jake |title=In blow to DC Studios, 'Supergirl' is no match for 'Toy Story 5' at box office |url=https://apnews.com/article/supergirl-toy-story-5-box-office-49830636c7ab8d0ee53bae541100ce2e |publisher=[[Associated Press]] |date=June 28, 2026 |access-date=July 30, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Nolfi |first=Joey |title=DC Studios boss addresses Supergirl's poor box office performance |url=https://ew.com/dc-reacts-supergirl-box-office-flop-12009000 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=June 29, 2026 |access-date=July 30, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Child |first=Ben |title=Supergirl is a box office catastrophe. == Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700 words. -->
During the destruction of the planet [[Krypton (DC Universe)|Krypton]], scientist [[Zor-El (DC Universe)|Zor-El]] and his wife [[Alura In-Ze (DC Universe)|Alura]] evacuate along with many Kryptonians to [[Argo City]], where Zor-El activates a force field system that separates a chunk containing the city from Krypton, preventing it from being destroyed when the planet's core explodes. Eight years later, Alura gives birth to her and Zor-El's daughter, [[Kara Zor-El (DC Universe)|Kara]]. Eventually, it is discovered that the separation of Argo City exposed a mineral known as "[[Kryptonite (DC Universe)|Kryptonite]]", which is slowly killing the citizens via radiation. After Alura succumbs to Kryptonite poisoning, Zor-El decides to send Kara and her dog [[Krypto (DC Universe)|Krypto]] on a ship headed to Earth. Upon landing, they are greeted by Kara's cousin [[Clark Kent (DC Universe)|Kal-El]], who is living a double life as Clark Kent and Superman. Kara later tries to adjust to her new life on Earth, while also adopting the moniker "Supergirl".

In the present, Kara travels with Krypto to several planets and parties in celebration of her 23rd birthday. Superman often calls her, concerned that she is constantly off-world and unable to settle on Earth. While stopping at the planet Holzherr, Kara encounters [[Ruthye Marye Knoll (DC Universe)|Ruthye Marye Knoll]], the last survivor of her family who were murdered by [[Krem (DC Universe)|Krem of the Yellow Hills]], leader of a ruthless bandit group known as the Brigands. Ruthye requests Kara's assistance in her quest for vengeance, but is denied. Krem hijacks Kara's ship alongside several Brigands, and shoots Krypto with a poisonous dart when he tries to stop them. Upon learning that Krypto has only three days left to live, Kara decides to pursue Krem to retrieve an antidote that he keeps on his person, while also reluctantly taking on Ruthye as her companion. 

After a brief skirmish with the [[Sklarian Raiders]], a group of space pirates, Kara interrogates them and learns of Krem's location on the planet Bilquis. There, Kara and Ruthye learn from a local couple, Bomar and Mareck, that the Brigands have been kidnapping young women to force them to be their brides. Mareck attempts to drug Kara to trade her and Ruthye to the Brigands in exchange for their daughter Sarna, but Kara resists and fights the Brigands, and is assisted by the bounty hunter [[Lobo (DC Universe)|Lobo]], who is targeting the Brigands' lieutenant Drom Baxton. Amidst the battle, Kara stops Ruthye from killing Krem, allowing him to escape after killing Bomar and his family. A guilt-ridden Kara decides to pursue Krem alone, and is escorted by several local aliens to the Brigands' location on another planet, Barenton. However, Kara is weakened by the planet's green sun, and is tended to by Ruthye, who secretly followed her.

Ruthye is later kidnapped by the Brigands and taken to their ship, where Lobo is also imprisoned. Ruthye manages to escape her cell and free Lobo, but is caught by Krem as she makes it topside. She is then saved by Kara, who has managed to recover after the planet rotates to a yellow sun and has donned her Supergirl suit. With Lobo's assistance, Supergirl defeats the Brigands and frees the captive women on board. She pleads with Ruthye not to kill Krem, convincing her that it would not ease her pain. As Ruthye walks away, Supergirl picks up her sword and uses it to kill Krem herself. She recovers the antidote and returns to Krypto, saving him in time. Later, as Supergirl bids farewell to Ruthye, she invites her to join her in celebrating the rest of her birthday week.

Some time later, Supergirl reunites with Superman on Earth, where she expresses a desire to stay for the long term, reaffirming that the planet is now her home.

== Production ==
=== Background ===
[[Oren Uziel]] was hired by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] in August 2018 to write a film based on the [[DC Comics]] character [[Supergirl]]. This came as Warner Bros. and [[DC Films]] were looking for new approaches to their [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU) franchise following several critical and commercial disappointments. Development on the film was in the early stages,<ref name="Uziel" /> but Warner Bros. was already being "very vocal" about wanting to hire a female director.<ref name="FemaleDirector" /> The studio's shift to focusing on a Supergirl film was given as one of the reasons that [[Henry Cavill]] was no longer expected to portray Supergirl's cousin [[Superman (DC Extended Universe)|Clark Kent / Superman]] in future DCEU films after portraying the role since ''[[Man of Steel (film)|Man of Steel]]'' (2013);<ref name="CavillSep2018" /> Cavill later indicated that he still wanted to return to the role.<ref name="CavillNov2019" /> Production on ''Supergirl'' was tentatively scheduled to begin in early 2020,<ref name="ProductionStart2020" /> but was put on hold due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="ProductionOnHold" />

In February 2021, [[Sasha Calle]] was cast as [[Kara Zor-El (DC Extended Universe)|Supergirl]] for the DCEU film ''[[The Flash (film)|The Flash]]'' (2023) and signed a multi-film contract for the DCEU.<ref name="CalleOct2024" /><ref name="CalleSupergirl" /> ''Supergirl'' was included on DC's slate of films planned for release in 2022 or 2023.<ref name="THRApr2021" /> In April 2022, [[Discovery, Inc.]] and Warner Bros.' parent company [[WarnerMedia]] merged to become [[Warner Bros. Discovery]] (WBD), led by president and CEO [[David Zaslav]]. The new company was expected to restructure [[DC Entertainment]] while Zaslav began searching for an equivalent to [[Marvel Studios]] president [[Kevin Feige]] to lead the new subsidiary.<ref name="ZaslavApr2022" /> Tatiana Siegel of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' reported in August that ''Supergirl'' was unlikely to move forward under Zaslav,<ref name="SupergirlCanceled" /> and it was quietly canceled around that time.<ref name="AlcockVariety" /> Writer/director [[James Gunn]] and producer [[Peter Safran]] were announced as the co-chairs and co-CEOs of the newly formed [[DC Studios]] at the end of October.<ref name="GunnSafran" /> A week after starting their new roles, the pair had begun working with a group of writers to develop an eight-to-ten-year plan for a new [[DC Universe (franchise)|DC Universe]] (DCU) that would be a "[[Soft reboot (fiction)|soft reboot]]" of the DCEU.<ref name="GunnSafranTownHall" /><ref name="DCU" /><ref name="DCUSlateTHR6Qs" />

=== Development ===
[[File:Ana Nogueira at 2019 Pacific Playwrights Festival.jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Picture of writer Ana Nogueira in 2019|[[Ana Nogueira]] was hired to write a Supergirl film for the DCU by November 2023, after working on a planned Supergirl film for the DCEU.]]
On January 31, 2023, Gunn and Safran unveiled the first projects from their DCU slate, which begins with [[Chapter One: Gods and Monsters]]. One of the films in the slate was an adaptation of the comic book miniseries ''[[Supergirl: Woman of Tomorrow]]'' (2021–22) by [[Tom King (writer)|Tom King]] and [[Bilquis Evely]].<ref name="DCUSlateVariety" /><ref name="DCUSlateDeadline" /> Gunn described the project as "a big science fiction epic film" and stated it would explore a more "hardcore" version of [[Kara Zor-El (DC Universe)|Kara Zor-El / Supergirl]] than had been previously seen on screen.<ref name="DCUSlateCBR" /><ref name="DCUSlateDeadline" /> King was also revealed to be one of the writers working on the overall story for the DCU.<ref name="DCUSlateVariety" /> Within days of the film's announcement, copies of the ''Supergirl: Woman of Tomorrow'' comic book had sold out on [[Amazon (company)|Amazon]] and at many different publishing houses and comic book shops. Gunn said he was working with DC Comics publisher and [[chief creative officer]] (CCO) [[Jim Lee]] to ensure more copies were printed soon.<ref name="ComicsSoldOut" />

While promoting the release of ''The Flash'' in June 2023, Calle expressed her love for the ''Woman of Tomorrow'' comic book and said she hoped to reprise her role in the film, but this was not guaranteed.<ref name="CalleJun2023" /> She had met with Safran to discuss her future as the character by then,<ref name="CalleMeeting" /> but Gunn eventually decided to move in a different direction with the character.<ref name="SupergirlScreenTest2" /> Calle said she was heartbroken and frustrated with the decision because she had filmed a different ending of ''The Flash'' that was intended to set up future appearances for her character. She was proud of her work in the role.<ref name="CalleOct2024" /> Following rumors that the DCEU's [[Arthur Curry (DC Extended Universe)|Aquaman]] actor, [[Jason Momoa]], would be recast as the character [[Lobo (character)|Lobo]] in the DCU,<ref name="NoWW3" /> he was reported in October 2023 to have had discussions with DC Studios about playing that character in the first DCU film, ''[[Superman (2025 film)|Superman]]'' (2025), or a standalone film.<ref name="MomoaLoboTalks" />

In November 2023, [[Ana Nogueira]] was revealed to be writing the screenplay. She was previously attached to write a Supergirl film in 2022 when it was being developed as a spin-off from ''The Flash''. Gunn and Safran enjoyed her work so much that they had her return for their own take, and she signed an overall writing deal with DC.<ref name="Nogueira" /> Gunn confirmed Nogueira's involvement and called the film a "beautiful, star-spanning tale".<ref name="NogueiraGunn" /> He chose to move forward with it as the second DCU film after ''Superman'', which was not originally planned, based on the strength of Nogueira's script.<ref name="GunnExplainSecondFilm" /> The film was reported to include the super-powered dog [[Krypto (DC Universe)|Krypto]].<ref name="NogueiraVariety" /> Nogueira revealed that changes from the comic ''Woman of Tomorrow'' were already included in her initial pitch, such as Supergirl being the one to kill Krem instead of [[Ruthye Marye Knoll]].<ref name="NogueiraComicChanges" />

=== Pre-production ===
[[File:Milly alcock 2026 1.jpg|thumb|left|upright=0.75|alt=Picture of actress Milly Alcock during an interview promoting Supergirl in June 2026|[[Milly Alcock]] was first envisioned for the role of Supergirl by producer [[James Gunn]] over a year before she was cast in January 2024.]]
By January 2024, [[Milly Alcock]], [[Emilia Jones]], [[Cailee Spaeny]], and [[Meg Donnelly]] were being considered to portray Supergirl. Donnelly had voiced the character in the animated films ''[[Legion of Super-Heroes (film)|Legion of Super-Heroes]]'' (2023) and ''[[Justice League: Crisis on Infinite Earths]]'' (2024).<ref name="SupergirlScreenTest2" /><ref name="SupergirlScreenTest" /> Jones did not read for the role.<ref name="NoJones" /> Alcock had a general meeting with Safran and was asked to make a [[self-tape]] a few weeks later.<ref name="AlcockVarietyJun2025" /> Alcock was conflicted about pursuing the role, wanting to "do things people watch" but also not be "pigeonholed into one part"; she was convinced after researching the role and realizing it emotionally resonated with her.<ref name="AlcockVarietyJun2025" /><ref name="AlcockMay2026" /> Alcock and Donnelly had auditions and screen tests in costume on the ''Superman'' set in [[Atlanta]] later in January.<ref name="AlcockVariety" /><ref name="SupergirlScreenTest2" /> Alcock was officially cast at the end of the month. Gunn said she embodied how King, Evely, and Nogueira envisioned the character,<ref name="AlcockTHR" /><ref name="Alcock" /> and he had first mentioned Alcock for the role to Safran over a year earlier after seeing her performance in the television series ''[[House of the Dragon]]'' (2022–present). Gunn believed she had the "edge, grace [and] authenticity" for this interpretation of Supergirl, which he wanted to distance from the more earnest [[Kara Danvers|portrayal of the character]] by [[Melissa Benoist]] in the [[Arrowverse]] series ''[[Supergirl (TV series)|Supergirl]]'' (2015–2021).<ref name="AlcockGunnFirst" /> Alcock learned of her casting approximately 10 days after the audition when Gunn sent her an online article about the news.<ref name="AlcockMay2026" /><ref name="AlcockVarietyJun2025" /> The studio was hoping to hire a director in the coming weeks,<ref name="AlcockVariety" /> but prioritized casting Supergirl because the character was set to debut in another DCU project, reported to be ''Superman''.<ref name="SupergirlScreenTest2" /><ref name="SupergirlScreenTest" /><ref name="Alcock" />

When Alcock was cast, filming was expected to begin in late 2024.<ref name="AlcockTHR" /><ref name="AlcockGunnFirst" /> She spent approximately two months during pre-production doing stunt rehearsals at [[Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden]].<ref name="AlcockMay2026" /> Zaslav confirmed in February that the script had been written and further casting was underway.<ref name="ZaslavScriptCasting" /> In April, [[Craig Gillespie]] entered talks to direct the film and DC Studios EVP [[Chantal Nong]] was revealed to be an executive producer.<ref name="GillespieTalks" /> Gillespie said he was immediately interested in directing the filming after reading the script and seeing Alcock's performance, as he understood the "tone and what to do with it".<ref name="SupergirlAntihero" /> His pitch to Gunn and Safran was heavily based on Nogueria's script, in addition to also providing 120 images as to what he felt the film should look like.<ref name="GillespieIWJun2026" /> He also enjoyed receiving creative freedom from Gunn, which they felt was necessary in maintaining the script's tone.<ref name="SupergirlAntihero" /> He was confirmed as director the next month, when the film was given a release date of June 26, 2026.<ref name="ReleaseDate" /> In September, [[Matthias Schoenaerts]] joined the cast as the main villain, [[Krem (DC Universe)|Krem of the Yellow Hills]],<ref name="Schoenaerts" /><ref name="MomoaLobo" /> and filming was expected to begin in January 2025 in the United Kingdom.<ref name="Schoenaerts" /> The next month, Gunn said the film would follow a [[three-act structure]] rather than the structure of the ''Woman of Tomorrow'' comic book, but it would retain the major characters and themes from the comic. He had reviewed screen tests for the character [[Ruthye Marye Knoll (DC Universe)|Ruthye Marye Knoll]] by then,<ref name="NYCC2024THR" /> and [[Eve Ridley]] was cast in that role at the end of the month.<ref name="Ridley" /> Gillespie included more scenes with Krypto during the opening credits, which were not originally written in the script, as he felt it would better develop Kara's relationships with Krypto while also establishing Krypto as "the only physical, tangible thing she has" from her life in Krypton.<ref name="GillespieIWJun2026" />

In December, Momoa was reported to be portraying [[Lobo (DC Universe)|Lobo]] in the film. He confirmed the casting by referencing an interview from a year earlier in which he expressed interest in the role.<ref name="MomoaLobo" /> While the character does not appear in the ''Woman of Tomorrow'' comic book, King's original [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] for the story was a team-up between Supergirl and Lobo in which they would take on similar roles to the characters Mattie Ross and [[Rooster Cogburn (character)|Rooster Cogburn]], respectively, from the novel ''[[True Grit (novel)|True Grit]]'' (1968).<ref name="KingLoboPitch" /> Gunn said Lobo was added to the film to help with adapting it to a three-act structure. He felt Lobo was "the biggest comic book character that's never been in a film".<ref name="GunnJun2025" /> Momoa's role was reported to be a [[cameo appearance]].<ref name="KrumholtzBeecham" /> [[Anna B. Sheppard]] and Neil Lamont were the film's costume designer and production designer, respectively.<ref name="BleedingCoolPersonnel" /> The costume team based Krem's character design off of ''Woman of Tomorrow''{{'s}} descriptions of him as a Viking and on further historical research they did regarding piercings and studs. For Lobo, the team claimed they were heavily influenced by his comic book aesthetics in the 1990s and had opted for simplicity and faithfulness to his character.<ref name="GillespieIWJun2026" /> During pre-production, Gillespie and Momoa reviewed different designs for Lobo, which were criticized by some who believed they were [[Artificial intelligence art|created with artificial intelligence]] (AI).<ref name="LoboAIDesign" /> The cape of Alcock's costume was designed from 16 meters of the same material used in the cape of [[Christopher Reeve]]'s [[Superman (1978 film series character)|Superman]] costume.<ref name="ReevesCape" /> 

Gillespie noted how ''Woman of Tomorrow'' itself was inspired by ''True Grit'', and incorporated that into the script while also citing ''[[Logan (film)|Logan]]'' (2017) and ''[[John Wick]]'' (2014) as inspirations.<ref name="GillespieEWMar2026" /><ref name="Gillespie/FilmDec2025" /> Earlier versions of the script featured Supergirl wearing her costume earlier in the film. However, Gillespie chose to postpone it as much as possible because he felt it was thematically consistent with Kara embracing her responsibilities as a hero whenever she is ready for it.<ref name="SupergirlMovieMaker" /> Meanwhile, Alcock felt Kara's choice to embrace Supergirl occurs after she understands it is about selflessly helping others.<ref name="AlcockMay2026" /> He also described ''Supergirl'' as being a "road movie" and as having a darker tone than ''Superman'' because Nogueira's scripts candidly dealt with the trauma Kara Zor-El had gone through during her childhood.<ref name="Gillespie/FilmDec2025" /> It centers around Kara Zor-El's journey in accepting her heroism, and takes place in outer space,<ref name="SupergirlAntihero" /> with Gillespie estimating that nine distinct worlds were featured, each with their own languages. He cited the 1997 film ''[[The Fifth Element]]'' for creating a world with a variety of distinct characters.<ref name="GillespieEWMar2026" />

=== Post-production ===
[[Tatiana S. Riegel]], a frequent collaborator of Gillespie's and an additional editor on Gunn's ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' (2023), edited the film,<ref name="BleedingCoolPersonnel" /> alongside [[Fred Raskin]], a frequent collaborator of Gunn's.<ref name="THRBehindScenes" /> Geoffrey Baumann is the visual effects supervisor,<ref name="BleedingCoolPersonnel" /> with visual effects provided by [[Digital Domain]], [[Framestore]], and [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM).<ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> In June 2025, Gunn announced that the film's title had been shortened to simply ''Supergirl''.<ref name="NoSubtitle" /> By October, a [[test screening]] had quietly occurred at the [[Warner Bros. Studios Burbank]] lot with some Warner Bros. and DC Studios executives and personnel.<ref name="StudioLotScreening" /> The film was reported at the start of December to have a $200&nbsp;million budget,<ref name="ForbesBudget" /> which Gunn disputed;<ref name="GunnBudgetResponse" /> it was subsequently reported to have a net budget of $170&nbsp;million.<ref name="Budget" /> Journalist [[Jeff Sneider]] reported that another test screening had occurred during the week of December 15, 2025, and that it had received praise for Alcock's performance and featured several [[Needle drop (audio)|needle drops]] like Gunn's ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film series)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014–2023) films for Marvel Studios. However, Sneider said the action sequences and Schoenaerts's Krem elicited more mixed responses;<ref name="SneiderRogenTestScreening" /> ''The Hollywood Reporter'' would later report that after this screening, DC Studios took control of the post-production process, enlisting the help of writer [[Jeremy Slater]].<ref name="THRBehindScenes" /> Between November 2025 and January 2026, additional actors were revealed to be appearing in the film: [[Ferdinand Kingsley]] as Ruthye's father, Elias Knoll;<ref name="Kingsley" /> [[David Corenswet]] reprising his DCU role as [[Clark Kent (DC Universe)|Kal-El / Clark Kent / Superman]];<ref name="CorenswetSuperman" /> and [[Diarmaid Murtagh]] as Krem's second-in-command, Drom Baxton.<ref name="Murtagh" /> Sneider also reported that [[Seth Rogen]] had a voice cameo,<ref name="SneiderRogenTestScreening" /> later revealed to be an alien space bus passenger. Rogen previously worked with Gillespie on the television series ''[[Pam & Tommy]]'' (2022) and the film ''[[Dumb Money]]'' (2023).<ref name="RogenCameoEW" /> By early 2026, the studio decided to test screen two different versions of the film; Gillespie's cut which was 11 minutes longer and included more of the villain Krem, scored strongly on song choices, pacing and villain; the studio went with their own cut after test screenings showed it scored slightly above Gillespie's.<ref name="THRBehindScenes" />

== Music ==
{{Main|Supergirl (soundtrack){{!}}''Supergirl'' (soundtrack)}}
[[Ramin Djawadi]] was revealed to be composing the ''Supergirl'' score in December 2025, after scoring several comic book films and Gillespie's 2011 film ''[[Fright Night (2011 film)|Fright Night]]''.<ref name="Djawadi" /><ref name="DjawadiPriorWorks" /> He was revealed to have been replaced by frequent DCEU film composer [[Tom Holkenborg]] in February 2026,<ref name="Holkenborg" /> before [[Claudia Sarne]] took over as composer by the following month.<ref name="Sarne" /> The soundtrack was released by [[WaterTower Music]] on June 26, 2026.<ref name="Soundtrack" />

Songs featured in the film that are not featured on the soundtrack album include: "This Summer" by [[Sleigh Bells (band)|Sleigh Bells]], "Digital Tattoo" by Werner Neidermeier, "[[Tous les garçons et les filles (album)|Le Temps de l'amour]]" by [[Françoise Hardy]], "[[Catch These Fists]]" by [[Wet Leg]], "[[Silver Lining (Rilo Kiley song)|Silver Lining]]" by [[Rilo Kiley]], "Satin in a Coffin" by [[Modest Mouse]], "Smile" by [[Wolf Alice]], "(I've Got) Trouble in Mind" by [[The Limiñanas]], "What Are We" by Winnie Ama, "[[The Girl from Ipanema]]" by [[Antônio Carlos Jobim]], "[[Cheek to Cheek]]" by [[Ella Fitzgerald]], "Safeword" by [[Halsey (singer)|Halsey]], "Care" by Hana Vu, "Roforofo Fight" by [[Fela Kuti]], "Don't Speak (I Came to Make a Bang!)" by [[Eagles of Death Metal]], "[[The Middle (Jimmy Eat World song)|The Middle]]" covered by Kelty Greye and KidMotel, and the "[[Superman March|Original Superman Theme]]" by [[John Williams]].<ref name="SongsTheWrap" /><ref name="SongsNME" /> "The Middle", which plays over the film's final major action sequence, was chosen after Gunn personally selected it out of approximately 45 songs that were in contention.<ref name="GunnSongIGN" /><ref name="GunnSongRS" /> The original plan was to have a cover version of "[[Girls Just Wanna Have Fun]]" by [[Cyndi Lauper]], also chosen by Gunn, but this was scrapped.<ref name="THRBehindScenes" />

== Marketing ==
Following Supergirl's introduction in the final scene of ''Superman'',<ref name="SupermanSetUp" /> Gunn released the first poster for ''Supergirl'' in July 2025. It shows her leaning against the ''Superman'' logo with that film's tagline, "Look Up", graffitied to say "Look Out". Commentators highlighted the change in tone from ''Superman'' suggested by the poster, as well as Supergirl's coat, which is similar to one she wears in the ''Woman of Tomorrow'' comic book.<ref name="PosterTF" /><ref name="PosterTR" /><ref name="PosterBC" /> Aaron Perine at ''[[/Film]]'' wrote that the graffitied tag line was a succinct way to indicate ''Supergirl''{{'s}} different direction and tone, and said the "whole rebellious angle makes all the sense [in] the world" following her cameo appearance in ''Superman''.<ref name="Poster/Film" /> At a [[CCXP]] panel in Brazil in early December 2025, the film's logo and Supergirl suit were showcased.<ref name="LogoCCXPOmelete" /><ref name="SuitCCXPOmelete" /> The logo's design was reminiscent of Corenswet's Superman emblem, rather than an earlier design used during this film's production that featured a yellow italicized typeface and a blue background, which Taimur Dur of ''[[ComicsBeat]]'' had determined was a placeholder design.<ref name="OldLogoComicsBeat" /><ref name="SuitCCXPSHH" /> ''[[Omelete]]''{{'s}} Guilherme Jacobs noted that the Supergirl suit design showed more wear and tear than that of Superman's, which was inherent from Supergirl's less careful adventuring;<ref name="LogoCCXPOmelete" /> Matthew Aguilar, writing for ''[[ComicBook.com]]'', felt the enlarged S symbol, modified golden belt, and reveal of the cape made for a "sleeker" presentation than that of the design from ''Superman''.<ref name="SuitCCXPComicBook" />

Later in December, two short teaser previews and a poster featuring the tagline "Truth, Justice, Whatever"—a spoof on the catchphrase "Truth, Justice, and the American Way" that is commonly associated with Superman—were released online, while the first full trailer was screened at a private press conference in [[Manhattan]], New York City–hosted by Gunn, Safran, Gillespie, and Alcock–days before its December 11 release.<ref name="FirstTeaserTechRadar" /><ref name="SecondTeaser+SecondPosterTechRadar" /><ref name="FullTeaserVariety" /> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]''{{'s}} Antonio Ferme and Jordan Moreau felt that the trailer, set to "[[Call Me (Blondie song)|Call Me]]" by [[Blondie (band)|Blondie]], and the earlier footage "made a clear case for how Alcock's Kara Zor-El will stand apart from her more optimistic cousin Superman".<ref name="FullTeaserVariety" /> James Grebey of ''[[Men's Journal]]'' noted similarities between scenes in the second preview of Kara wearing headphones and listening to music and [[Peter Quill (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Star-Lord]] from Gunn's [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) film ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014),<ref name="SecondTeaserMensJournal" /> while Aguilar felt the poster and footage "just radiate the aura of cool and confidence" of the character from the comics.<ref name="PosterComicBook" /> ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]''{{'s}} Jake Kleinman felt between the use of "Blondie", the outer space setting, and a space group that "look suspiciously like the [[Ravagers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ravagers]]", "''Supergirl'' looks way more like ''Guardians [of the Galaxy] Vol. 4'' than a ''Superman'' follow-up" which was "concerning". He was hopeful upon release ''Supergirl'' would be "its own distinct take on the superhero genre" and not Gunn "fall[ing] back on his signature style".<ref name="SecondTeaserPolygon" /> Rafael Motamayor of ''/Film'' speculated that seeing Jason Momoa's Lobo in the trailer was Gunn being "misdirecting" for marketing purposes, guessing that Lobo would replace a bounty hunter who Supergirl defeats early in the comic, and that his role in the film would be small to not infringe on the adaptation of the comic yet enough to "gauge interest in more Lobo appearances";<ref name="SecondTeaserLoboSlashFilm" /> ''[[Kotaku]]''{{'s}} Ethan Gach praised Kara's line "[Superman] sees the goodness in everyone, I see the truth", wondered if Momoa could perform a distinctive characterization for Lobo as opposed to just being Aquaman again, and felt the visuals in the trailer were bland.<ref name="SecondTeaserKotaku" /> Alcock, Momoa, and Safran discussed the film and presented an action sequence during the 2026 Warner Bros. [[CinemaCon]] panel.<ref name="2026CinemaCon" /> Warner Bros. and DC Studios collaborated with over 80 brands for a $100&nbsp;million promotional campaign for the film.<ref name="100MPromo" /> The marketing budget was reported to be $120&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOLossesVariety" />

== Reception ==
=== Box-office performance ===
''Supergirl'' grossed $72.4&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $54&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $126.4 million.<ref name="NUM" /><ref name="BOM" /> With a net production budget of $170–186&nbsp;million, the film reportedly needed to gross around $315&nbsp;million worldwide to [[break-even|break even]].<ref name="VarietyProjections" /><ref name="BOFirstWeekend" /> Following its opening weekend, it was reported that the film was projected to lose the studio $85–125&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOLossesTheWrap" /><ref name="BOLossesDeadline" /><ref name="BOLossesVariety" /><!-- DO NOT LABEL AS A BOX OFFICE BOMB YET PER [[WP:EXCEPTIONAL]] AND [[MOS:ACCLAIMED]]. SEE TALK PAGE. -->

In the United States and Canada, ''Supergirl'' was projected to earn between $40–55&nbsp;million during its opening weekend from 3,600 theaters.<ref name="LowerProjections" /><ref name="BOTrackingDeadline" /><ref name="VarietyProjections" /> Estimates were lowered after the film made $13&nbsp;million on its first day (including $7.8&nbsp;million from previews). It ended up debuting to just $38&nbsp;million domestically and $68&nbsp;million worldwide.<ref name="BOFirstWeekend" /> In its second weekend, the film dropped 76% to $9.6&nbsp;million domestically and reached $100.5&nbsp;million worldwide.<ref name="BOSecondWeekend" />

=== Critical response ===
On the [[review aggregation]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 52% of 371 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 5.5/10. The website's critical consensus reads: "Milly Alcock brings a swagger to Kara Zor-El that'd make Krypton proud in this otherwise familiar origin story, dawning a promising new hero in the DCU who's still waiting for an adventure that matches her vigor."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=supergirl_2026 |type=movie |access-date=August 21, 2026}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 50 out of 100, based on 56 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=supergirl-2026 |type=movie |access-date=June 29, 2026}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B−" on an A+ to F scale, while 52% of those surveyed by [[PostTrak]] said they would definitely recommend the film.<ref name="BOFirstWeekend" />

David Rooney of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' gave the film a mixed review, writing: "[...] Gillespie barrels through a pedestrian mainframe narrative that's basically ''[[True Grit (2010 film)|True Grit]]'' meets ''[[John Wick (film)|John Wick]]'' meets ''[[Mad Max: Fury Road]]'' with the usual random assortment of needle drops," also noting: "Given [Australian director] Gillespie's history with films about spirited, rule-breaking women, like ''[[I, Tonya]]'' and ''[[Cruella (film)|Cruella]]'', the failure to find emotional depth in the sisterhood of Kara and Ruthye is notable."<ref name="THRreview" /> Pete Hammond of ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' gave the film a mixed review, writing: "The special effects and relentlessly dark tone often get in the way of making this group rock despite the best efforts of Alcock who is a bit too quirky, playing Zor-El as somewhat reckless, headstrong, and not always put together."<ref name="DeadlineReview" /> [[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' gave the film a negative review, writing: "Kill Krem! Save the dog! Those are the motivations driving the entire not-even-interesting-enough-to-be-convoluted plot of ''Supergirl''. Maybe that's why the movie is full of action yet numbingly flat."<ref name="VarietyReview" />

In a one-and-a-half-star-out-of-four review, Tomris Laffly of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' called the film a "shallow blockbuster that never takes flight", as well as writing: "In the end, you can't help but wonder why the emotions of these characters don't pack as much of a punch."<ref name="RogerEbert.com"/> Clarisse Loughley of ''[[The Independent]]'' awarded the film two stars out of five, writing: "The mandate here appears to be to keep things as distinctly Gunn-esque as possible. In return, Superman's moodier, more cynical relative has been robbed of the chance to speak with her own voice. It's the superhero equivalent of a Vegas impersonator."<ref name="TheIndependentReview" /> [[Amy Nicholson]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' criticized the film, labeling it a "hot mess" with a "too-terrestrial plot", and that it "thrives on chaos but settles for convention."<ref name="LATimesReview" />

In a positive review for ''[[IndieWire]]'', Kate Erbland wrote: "What makes ''Supergirl'' stand out [...] is its interest in staying small while asking some very big questions indeed."<ref name="IWReview" /> [[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film three stars out of five, calling it "sprightly and sparkling superhero yarn", while also writing that ''Supergirl'' "isn't a perfect movie by any means", but said that "there are moments when you'll believe this franchise can fly."<ref name="GuardianReview" /> David Fear of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' gave the film a positive review, noting that it "can go deep", and wrote, "This movie proves that you can take something that might seem esoteric or strictly-for-the-heads and make mass entertainment out of it without selling out completely."<ref name="RollingStoneReview" /> Leila Latif of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' gave the film three stars out of five, writing: "Milly Alcock's hungover hero is delightful, even if the film never truly cuts loose."<ref name="EmpireReview" />

== Future ==
In May 2026, Safran said that Alcock's Supergirl would have a major role in the future of the DCU beyond her return in the ''Superman'' follow-up film ''[[Man of Tomorrow (film)|Man of Tomorrow]]'' (2027).<ref name="AlcockMay2026" />

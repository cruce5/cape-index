'''''The Suicide Squad''''' is a 2021 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[DC Comics]] team [[Suicide Squad]]. Written and directed by [[James Gunn]], it is a sequel to ''[[Suicide Squad (2016 film)|Suicide Squad]]'' (2016) and the tenth film in the [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU). The film stars an [[ensemble cast]] including <!--Order based on title credits per talk page consensus-->[[Margot Robbie]], [[Idris Elba]], [[John Cena]], [[Joel Kinnaman]], [[David Dastmalchian]], [[Daniela Melchior]], [[Michael Rooker]], [[Jai Courtney]], [[Peter Capaldi]], [[Alice Braga]], [[Pete Davidson]], [[Sylvester Stallone]], and [[Viola Davis]].<!--Order based on title credits per talk page consensus--> In the film, a group of convicts join a task force known as the "Suicide Squad" in exchange for lighter sentences. They are sent to the [[South America]]n island nation of [[Corto Maltese (DC Comics)|Corto Maltese]] to destroy all traces of the giant alien starfish [[Starro the Conqueror]] before it falls into the local government's control.

[[Warner Bros. Pictures]] originally hired [[Gavin O'Connor (filmmaker)|Gavin O'Connor]] to direct. However, O'Connor left due to creative differences, and Gunn was hired to write and direct the film after being temporarily fired by [[Disney]] and [[Marvel Studios]] as director of ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' (2023). He drew inspiration from [[war film]]s and [[John Ostrander]]'s 1980s ''Suicide Squad'' comics. Filming took place from September 2019 to February 2020, primarily in [[Atlanta, Georgia]], as well as in [[Colón, Panama]], and [[Porto, Portugal]]. The film had the biggest sets ever built for a Warner Bros. production.

''The Suicide Squad'' premiered theatrically in the United Kingdom on July 30, 2021, and was released in the United States on August 5, simultaneously in theaters and on the streaming service [[HBO Max]]. Unlike its predecessor, the film received positive reviews from critics and became the most-streamed DCEU film on HBO Max. ''The Suicide Squad'' grossed $169{{nbsp}}million against a $185{{nbsp}}million production budget. Its box office performance was attributed to factors such as the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], the film's availability on HBO Max, and its relationship to the first ''Suicide Squad''. It was followed by a spin-off television series, ''[[Peacemaker (TV series)|Peacemaker]]'', in 2022, and an animated series, ''[[Creature Commandos (TV series)|Creature Commandos]]'', in 2024. A further television series, ''[[Waller (TV series)|Waller]]'', is in development.

==Plot==
Intelligence officer [[Amanda Waller (DC Extended Universe)|Amanda Waller]] assembles two [[Task Force X]] teams, colloquially known as the "[[Suicide Squad]]", which consists of [[Belle Reve (DC Comics)|Belle Reve]] penitentiary inmates who agree to carry out missions in exchange for commuted sentences. The teams are sent to the South American island nation of [[Corto Maltese (DC Comics)|Corto Maltese]] after an anti-American regime overthrows its government and are tasked with destroying the [[Nazi Germany|Nazi]]-era laboratory Jötunheim, which houses the secret experiment "Project Starfish".

Upon making landfall, most of the first team is slaughtered by the Corto Maltese military, who capture [[Harley Quinn (DC Extended Universe)|Harley Quinn]] while team leader [[Rick Flag (DC Extended Universe)|Rick Flag Jr.]] escapes. Elsewhere, the second team{{snd}}led by assassin [[Characters of the DC Extended Universe#Robert DuBois / Bloodsport|Bloodsport]], whom Waller blackmailed in exchange for not sending his estranged daughter Tyla to prison, and consisting of [[Characters of the DC Extended Universe#Christopher Smith / Peacemaker|Peacemaker]], [[Characters of the DC Extended Universe#King Shark|King Shark]], [[Abner Krill (DC Extended Universe)|Polka-Dot Man]], [[Cleo Cazo|Ratcatcher 2]], and her pet rat Sebastian{{snd}}enters the country undetected, revealing that the other team was a deliberate decoy. Waller orders the squad to find Flag, who is being held at a rebel base. The team massacres the rebel soldiers, only to learn that rebellion leader Sol Soria had saved Flag. Despite the group's actions, Soria agrees to help them infiltrate the capital, where they capture Project Starfish's lead scientist, the [[Thinker (DC Comics)|Thinker]]. Harley is taken to Corto Maltese's new dictator, Silvio Luna, who wishes to marry her. Harley is initially enchanted by this proposition, but after learning of Luna's plans to use Project Starfish on political dissidents, she kills him and escapes. She joins the others, who use the Thinker to break into Jötunheim, in exchange for sparing his life, and begin rigging it with explosives.

Flag and Ratcatcher 2 enter the Thinker's laboratory and find [[Starro|Starro the Conqueror]], a giant starfish-like alien that creates smaller versions of itself to kill people and control their bodies. The Thinker explains that Starro was brought to Earth by American astronauts, that the U.S. government has [[Black budget|secretly funded]] experiments on it for decades using Corto Maltesean citizens and political enemies of the prior dictatorship as test subjects, that said regime used Project Starfish to strengthen its rule, and that Waller sent them to cover up American ties to the project. An enraged Flag decides to leak a hard drive containing evidence of this, but is killed by Peacemaker, who is under orders from Waller to ensure the coverup. Meanwhile, a skirmish between the rest of the team and the military leads to Polka-Dot Man accidentally setting off the explosives prematurely. As Jötunheim crumbles, the drive falls into Ratcatcher 2's possession. Peacemaker attempts to kill her before she can escape with it, but Bloodsport shoots him and takes it.

Starro escapes the destroyed Jötunheim, kills the Thinker and much of the military, and begins taking control of the island's population. Waller tells the squad that their mission is complete and orders them to leave, but Bloodsport leads the team in fighting Starro. Waller, wanting to keep the squad's secrecy from the public, attempts to execute them for this, but her subordinates knock her out. Polka-Dot Man weakens Starro, but is killed. Harley pierces a hole in Starro's eye, allowing Ratcatcher 2 to summon the city's rats to chew it to death from the inside. With the military diverted, Soria takes control of the government, promising [[Democracy|democratic elections]]. Bloodsport blackmails Waller into releasing him and the surviving squad members and dropping Tyla's charges in exchange for keeping the contents of the drive confidential. Waller reluctantly agrees and the remaining squad members are airlifted out of Corto Maltese; Harley proposes she and Bloodsport become friends with each other, in honor of their fallen common friend Flag.

In a [[mid-credits scene]], it is revealed that [[Weasel (DC Extended Universe)|Weasel]], a member of the first team, has survived and runs off into the jungle. In a [[post-credits scene]], Waller punishes two of her subordinates, [[Emilia Harcourt (DC Extended Universe)|Emilia Harcourt]] and [[John Economos (DC Extended Universe)|John Economos]], by assigning them to a new mission with Peacemaker, who is still alive and recovering in a hospital.{{efn|name="PeacemakerPostCredits"|This scene sets up the events of the [[Peacemaker season 1|first season]] of the [[Spinoff (media)|spin-off]] television series ''[[Peacemaker (TV series)|Peacemaker]]'' (2022).<ref name="THRHollandJan2022" />}}

==Production==
===Development===
====Original plans====
[[File:David Ayer by Gage Skidmore 2.jpg|thumb|upright|left|[[David Ayer]] was initially set to return as director from the first ''Suicide Squad'', but chose to work on other projects.]]
Before the first ''[[Suicide Squad (2016 film)|Suicide Squad]]'' film was released in August 2016, director [[David Ayer]] and star [[Will Smith]] were expected to return for a [[sequel]]. Filming was planned to begin in 2017 after the pair completed their work on the film ''[[Bright (film)|Bright]]'' (2017).<ref name="VarietySequelMar2016" /> [[Karen Fukuhara]] expressed her interest in fleshing out [[Tatsu Yamashiro (DC Extended Universe)|Tatsu Yamashiro / Katana]]'s backstory in a sequel, though she also noted that if not, that could be in a spin-off film of her own.<ref name="KarenFukuharasDashedHopes" /> In September, Ayer said the first film had been rated [[PG-13 (Motion Picture Association)|PG-13]] because it had originally been envisioned that way by the studio and an [[Motion Picture Association film rating system#Ratings|R-rating]] needed to be planned from the start of a production. He said that it would be worth "lobbying" to make a sequel R-rated, as he felt the first film had the "edge" and "attitude" of an R-rated film already.<ref name="AyerRApr2016" /> ''Suicide Squad'' was released to a polarized response and was reported to have a difficult production, but the film grossed enough for [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] to fast-track development of a sequel.<ref name="AyerGCSDec2016" /><ref name="GibsonFeb2017THR" /> Several spin-off films were also in development by December, including one featuring Smith as [[Deadshot]]. The project that was furthest along was ''[[Gotham City Sirens (film)|Gotham City Sirens]]'', an all-female team-up film with [[Margot Robbie]] planned to reprise her role as [[Harley Quinn (DC Extended Universe)|Harley Quinn]]. Ayer was set to direct and produce that film rather than ''Suicide Squad 2''.<ref name="AyerGCSDec2016" />

Warner Bros. began searching for a new director for ''Suicide Squad 2'' and was courting [[Mel Gibson]] by mid-February 2017.<ref name="GibsonFeb2017THR" /> [[Ruben Fleischer]], [[Daniel Espinosa]], [[Jonathan Levine]] and [[David S. Goyer]] were also considered.<ref name="DirectorsFeb2017" /><ref name="GoyerConsidered" /> [[Adam Cozad]] entered negotiations to write the film a month later, at which point there was still no director attached to the project. It was described as being a priority for Warner Bros.<ref name="Cozad" /> Script delays led to the planned filming start being pushed to mid-2018, at which point Gibson moved on from the project. [[Jaume Collet-Serra]] became the new frontrunner to direct in early July,<ref name="Collet-SerraJul2017Deadline" /> by which time both Smith and Robbie were set to reprise their roles.<ref name="Collet-SerraJul2017Deadline" /> [[Zak Penn]] had [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitched]] a new story treatment for the film to Warner Bros.,<ref name="Collet-SerraJul2017THR" /> and wrote a draft of the script as a favor to the studio.<ref name="Collet-SerraJul2017THR" /><ref name="PennFavor" /> Later in July, Collet-Serra was hired to direct [[The Walt Disney Company]]'s ''[[Jungle Cruise (film)|Jungle Cruise]]'' (2021) and withdrew from directing ''Suicide Squad 2'' after deciding he would rather originate a new story than continue an existing franchise;<ref name="Collet-SerraExited" /> Collet-Serra went on to direct the [[DC Extended Universe]] (DCEU) film ''[[Black Adam (film)|Black Adam]]'' (2022).<ref name="Collet-SerraBA" />

In August, [[Jared Leto]] was expected to reprise his role as the [[Joker (DC Extended Universe)|Joker]] from the first film,<ref name="LetoAug2017" /> while production was not expected to begin until Smith completed his work on ''[[Aladdin (2019 film)|Aladdin]]'' (2019) and ''[[Gemini Man (film)|Gemini Man]]'' (2019) in late 2018.<ref name="KrollAug2017" /> The next month, [[Gavin O'Connor (filmmaker)|Gavin O'Connor]] was chosen to direct the film and co-write it with his writing partner Anthony Tambakis, based on his own vision.<ref name="OConnorSep2017Deadline" /><ref name="OConnorSep2017Variety" /> The character [[Black Adam]] was reportedly the main villain of O'Connor's script, with [[Dwayne Johnson]] already attached to that role for [[DC Films]].<ref name="BlackAdamNov2017" /><ref name="BlackAdamOct2020" /> [[Michael De Luca]] joined the film as a producer in January 2018, working with the first film's producer [[Charles Roven]].<ref name="DeLuca" /> David Bar Katz and [[Todd Stashwick]] were co-writing the film with O'Connor in June,<ref name="KatzStashwick" /> and they had completed their draft by that September.<ref name="StashwickSep2018" /> O'Connor left the film by early October to focus on ''[[The Way Back (2020 film)|The Way Back]]'' (2020). This was reportedly out of frustration that Warner Bros. was already moving ahead with ''[[Birds of Prey (2020 film)|Birds of Prey]]'' (2020), a new Harley Quinn spin-off film with a similar story to the one he wrote for ''Suicide Squad 2''.<ref name="OConnorGoneOct2018" /> Years later while promoting ''[[The Accountant 2]]'' (2025), O'Connor clarified that he had written three-quarters of a screenplay that focused on a "father-daughter story" between Deadshot and his daughter Zoe when a new regime was established in DC and the filmmakers he was working with were replaced. Upon meeting new president [[Walter Hamada]], he and his writing partner told him to wait until they finished the script to show it to him, but once they did a couple of weeks later, Hamada asked them to make it a comedy, which they declined because that was not what O'Connor agreed to do for the studio, so he ultimately left.<ref name="OConnorsActualReasonAndStory" /> During the development process, the character [[Deathstroke]] appeared in four or five different versions of the script to fight Deadshot. [[Joe Manganiello]] was attached to reprise his role as Deathstroke from ''[[Justice League (film)|Justice League]]'' (2017) and the experience left him feeling that he would never portray the character again until he was asked to return for ''[[Zack Snyder's Justice League]]'' (2021).<ref name="ManganielloMarch2021" /><ref name="DeathstrokeVsDeadshot" />

====James Gunn====
[[File:James Gunn (28557194032) (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|right|[[James Gunn]], writer and director of ''The Suicide Squad'']]
In October 2018, [[James Gunn]] was hired to write and possibly direct the next ''Suicide Squad'' film.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Verhoeven |first=Umberto Gonzalez and Beatrice |date=October 9, 2018 |title=James Gunn in Talks to Write 'Suicide Squad 2' for DC; May Also Direct (Exclusive) |url=https://www.thewrap.com/james-gunn-suicide-squad-2-for-dc/ |access-date=July 27, 2024 |magazine=TheWrap}}</ref><ref name="GunnHired" /> His deal with Warner Bros. closed following the completion of his exit settlement with the Walt Disney Company, after he was dismissed by Disney and [[Marvel Studios]] as director of the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) film ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' (2023) in July 2018 when conservative commentators, such as [[Mike Cernovich]], circulated controversial [[Twitter]] posts he made involving [[rape]], [[pedophilia]], [[AIDS]], the [[Holocaust]], [[bestiality]] and the [[September 11 attacks]].<ref name="GunnDisneyFiring" /> Warner Bros. had been interested in recruiting Gunn for the DCEU immediately after his dismissal by Disney<ref name="WBGunnInterest" /> and asked him to make a new [[Superman]] film with an offer to recast [[Henry Cavill]] in the role.<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021" /><ref name="GunnSupermanOffer" /><ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://ew.com/superman-new-man-of-steel-leads-dcu-of-tomorrow-cover-story-11751060 | title=How 'Superman' shepherds the DC universe of tomorrow (Exclusive) | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] }}</ref> Gunn was uncertain if he wanted to take on Superman, so Warner Bros. told him that he could adapt any DC property he liked. He chose to make a Suicide Squad film, recalling that it was the one DC property he dreamed of adapting and had been jealous when Ayer's ''Suicide Squad'' film performed well.<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021" /> Ayer supported Gunn's hiring as a "brave and smart move" and called him "the right man for the job". [[Dave Bautista]], who starred in the ''Guardians of the Galaxy'' films for Gunn, expressed interest in appearing in his Suicide Squad film.<ref name="AyerBautista" />

One of the first things Gunn did when discussing the film with Warner Bros. was watch ''Suicide Squad'' for the first time. The studio said he could take or leave any elements he wanted from the first film, but they hoped that he would bring back Robbie as Harley Quinn.<ref name="NYTJul2021" /> The day after he was hired, Disney decided to reinstate Gunn as director of ''Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3''. He discussed his new DCEU commitment with Marvel Studios President [[Kevin Feige]], who encouraged him to "make a great movie" and agreed to delay production on ''Guardians'' until Gunn had completed work on the ''Suicide Squad'' sequel.<ref name="GunnDisneyRehiredSooner" /> In January 2019, the film was officially titled ''The Suicide Squad'' and was scheduled for release on August 6, 2021.<ref name="THRReleaseJan2019" /> The title was suggested by Gunn as a joke, but executives at Warner Bros. liked it.<ref name="TitleJoke" /> At that time, Gunn was in negotiations to direct the film which was described as a relaunch rather than a direct sequel that would take the franchise in a new direction and feature a largely new cast. Roven and [[Peter Safran]] were set as producers, with [[Zack Snyder]] and [[Deborah Snyder]] as executive producers.<ref name="GunnDirectingJan2019" /> Safran, who produced the DCEU films ''[[Aquaman (film)|Aquaman]]'' (2018) and ''[[Shazam! (film)|Shazam!]]'' (2019), had pushed Gunn to take on the project and felt there was no better director to "bring together a disparate group of outsiders on a mission".<ref name="ColliderJul2021Sequel" />

===Writing===
{{Quote box |width=33% |align=left |quote=We really wanted to make sure that this stands on its own two feet. So... it's not a sequel, but there are some characters that were in the first movie, right? So it's not really a full reboot either. So we just call it James Gunn's ''The Suicide Squad''. |source=—Producer Peter Safran on the relationship between ''The Suicide Squad'' and the first ''Suicide Squad'' film<ref name="ColliderJul2021Sequel" />}}
Gunn wrote a treatment that was a few pages long,<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> and then quickly produced several drafts of the script that Warner Bros. was "extremely high" on.<ref name="Elba" /> He said the film was "its own thing", rather than a sequel or [[reboot (fiction)|reboot]];<ref name="EmpireOct2020" /> it does not explicitly address the events of the first film or ''Birds of Prey'',<ref name="StandaloneStatus" /> but it also does not contradict them.<ref name="EmpireOct2020" /> Warner Bros. asked Gunn to make the film PG-13, but he insisted on it being R-rated if he was going to direct it; the studio felt Gunn's involvement was "worth the trade-off" for the higher rating.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" /> He took inspiration from [[John Ostrander]]'s original 1980s run on the Suicide Squad comic books,<ref name="DCFanDome/Film" /> which he was drawn to because it was about a "bunch of loser, B-grade supervillains".<ref name="BloodsportCCXP" /> Safran described ''The Suicide Squad'' as a "gritty 1970s war movie combined with the brilliance of James Gunn's characters and comedy",<ref name="DCFanDomeYahoo" /> and Gunn was influenced by war films such as ''[[The Great Escape (film)|The Great Escape]]'' (1963), ''[[The Dirty Dozen]]'' (1967), ''[[Where Eagles Dare]]'' (1968) and ''[[Kelly's Heroes]]'' (1970).<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021" /><ref name="DCFanDome/Film" /><ref name="ColliderJul2021Influences" />

Already knowing the kind of story that he wanted to tell, Gunn's first step in writing the film was picking the characters for the Suicide Squad roster. He spent days going through the library of existing DC Comics characters and deciding which group would work well together. He wanted to create a blend of characters by having each feel like they were part of a different film genre.<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> The first new character that Gunn added to the roster was [[King Shark]],<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021KingShark" /> who was intended to appear in the first film before being replaced by [[Killer Croc]].<ref name="KingSharkSS1" /> Gunn sought to feature an animal on the team and chose King Shark because he enjoyed the concept of a man-eating fish-human hybrid.<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021KingShark" /> Other new characters that Gunn added include [[Polka-Dot Man]], [[Peacemaker (character)|Peacemaker]] and a female version of [[Ratcatcher (comics)|Ratcatcher]].<ref name="ColliderCharacters" /> He said one of the main differences between this film and the ''Guardians of the Galaxy'' films was that it would be unclear whether some Suicide Squad members would turn out to be good or bad, unlike the [[Guardians of the Galaxy]] who are portrayed as heroes despite their flaws.<ref name="EmpireOct2020" /> Characters that were considered but ultimately rejected include [[Sportsmaster]],<ref name="GunnSportmaster" /> [[Dogwelder]],<ref name="GunnDogwelder" /> [[Bat-Mite]],<ref name="DCFanDome/Film" /> [[Livewire (DC Comics)|Livewire]], [[Punch and Jewelee]], [[Black Spider (comics)|Black Spider]], Deathstroke, [[Man-Bat]], [[Plastique (character)|Plastique]], [[Chemo (character)|Chemo]], [[KGBeast]], [[Solomon Grundy (character)|Solomon Grundy]], [[List of Batman family enemies#Enemies of lesser renown|Rainbow Creature]], Gunhawk, [[Knockout (DC Comics)|Knockout]], [[Killer Frost]], [[Mr. Freeze]],<ref name="UnusedVillains1" /><ref name="UnusedVillains2" /><ref name="UnusedVillains3" /> and [[Yahya Abdul-Mateen II]]'s [[Black Manta]] from ''Aquaman''.<ref name="BlackManta" /> He did not consider using the Joker because he felt Amanda Waller would not have any use for the character,<ref name="GunnNoJoker" /> and chose not to use [[Kite Man]] because he felt the character was already a punchline in the comics and would not feel fresh for the film.<ref name="GunnKiteMan" /> Gunn also avoided characters like [[Bronze Tiger]] and [[Katana (DC Comics)|Katana]] since they are [[antihero]]es rather than villains.<ref name="BronzeTigerKatana" />

Gunn felt he was in a position, and had the responsibility, to take risks with the property and try make "an all-out entertaining film from start to finish that didn't play by the rules". This included "[[post-modern]]" aesthetic decisions such as creating sequences that showed the perspectives of Harley Quinn and Polka-Dot Man, adding an extended subplot where Harley Quinn is on her own away from the action and adding title cards that segue between sequences.<ref name="GunnFandomJul2021" /> These elements were written into the script,<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> including what Gunn referred to as "Harley-vision" where gory murders are depicted through Harley's "starry-eyed way of looking at life" by mixing "hearts and beautiful little things" with the blood that is coming out of the people Harley is killing. The "Harley-vision" visuals were inspired by ''[[Lollipop Chainsaw]]'' (2012), a video game that Gunn helped develop.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" />

The main villains of the film are [[Amanda Waller]] and the U.S. government, who are trying to cover up their involvement in illegal experiments on the island of [[Corto Maltese (DC Comics)|Corto Maltese]]. Gunn did not feel that this storyline was specifically critical of the U.S. government, just realistic given the real-world history of the U.S. interfering in other countries. He also wanted to use this to make dramatic points rather than political ones, with the different stances on the government's actions that the film's main characters take leading to dramatic conflict.<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> For the antagonist that Waller sends the Suicide Squad to fight, Gunn initially considered using Superman before deciding to feature [[Starro|Starro the Conqueror]] instead.<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> He found the character to be both ridiculous and terrifying, which reflected what he was trying to accomplish with the film,<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021" /> and he felt Starro was the only major DC Comics villain that was unlikely to appear in another live-action film or who would not be adapted faithfully by another filmmaker if it was. He also wanted to avoid potential questions regarding who should be portraying Superman in the DCEU and what that would mean for the film's connections to the wider shared universe.<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> DC allowed Gunn to kill any character,<ref name="EmpireOct2020" /> including Harley Quinn,<ref name="GunnRSFeb2022" /> and he ignored the potential backlash from doing this to prioritize the natural evolution of the story.<ref name="DoGGunnApr2021" /> He did not want it to be like other films which might kill off some characters early on but then not kill any of the main characters at the end, so he knew that one of the Suicide Squad members would die during the final battle. He originally intended for this to be Ratcatcher, but ultimately felt that this would be too heartless. He chose Polka-Dot Man instead because he thought the character had the most complete [[story arc]] at that point in the film.<ref name="GunnScriptApart" />

===Design===
Production designer Beth Mickle said Gunn wanted the film to be "grey and drab and monotone" until the characters arrive in Corto Maltese, which he wanted to "explode with color" like [[Panama]] and [[Cuba]].<ref name="TotalFilmSetVisit" /> The design team took inspiration from photos of [[Havana]], Cuba and [[São Paulo]], [[Brazil]].<ref name="DailyDemocratAug2021" /> Gunn said the film featured the biggest sets ever built for a Warner Bros. film,<ref name="TotalFilmSetVisit" /> with Mickle building a set the size of three football fields for the outside of Jötunheim where the final battle was filmed,<ref name="ColliderJul2021Jotunheim" /> and an even bigger beach set that had real waves and a palm tree forest which Gunn called "the greatest set [he had] ever been on".<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" /> The jungle featured a bamboo cage that was {{Convert|8|ft|m}} deep.<ref name="ColliderJul2021Influences" /> Gunn said building a whole beach was expensive, but it was more cost-effective than using a real beach due to changing tides and the limited amount of time available for filming in a real environment.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" />

Because of the mixture of characters that Gunn selected for the film's Suicide Squad roster, there are "dorky comics costumes" alongside "modern, scary, grimdark" costumes.<ref name="EmpireJul2021" /> One of the biggest costume challenges for Gunn was creating Bloodsport's suit, from which the character can form various weapons.<ref name="FSRCommentary" /> Robbie wears a new costume that features Harley's traditional red and black color palette, with Gunn taking inspiration from the character's costume in the video game ''[[Batman: Arkham City]]'' (2011). He wanted her jacket to have "motorcycle gang style"-writing on the back and chose "Live fast, die clown" over other potential options "Clown AF" and "World's Best Grandpa".<ref name="GunnTrailerBreakdown" /> Gunn also removed Harley's "Rotten" facial tattoo that she had in previous DCEU films because both he and Robbie disliked it.<ref name="HarleyRotten" /> Flag wears a yellow T-shirt with a cartoon character called "Ultra Bunny" on it. Gunn drew the character and added the text "obstacles are opportunities" which is his slogan when directing on set.<ref name="FSRCommentary" /> Gunn initially used a [[hammerhead shark]] design from [[the New 52]] comics for King Shark, but found that it would be awkward to film the character with other actors due to his eyes being on the sides of his head.<ref name="GunnKSHammerhead" /> Gunn settled on a [[great white shark]] design that is similar to the one seen in the ''[[Harley Quinn (TV series)|Harley Quinn]]'' animated series, though this was a coincidence as that series was released after filming for ''The Suicide Squad'' began.<ref name="GunnKSHarley" /> Gunn gave King Shark a [[dad bod]] to make him look less like a mammal,<ref name="GunnKSDadBod" /> as well as small eyes, a big mouth and a small head to avoid the "cute anthropomorphic beast" design seen in popular characters like [[Baby Groot (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Baby Groot]] from ''Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2'' and [[Grogu]] from ''[[The Mandalorian]]'' (2019–present).<ref name="GunnKSCute" />

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] began on September 20, 2019, at [[Pinewood Atlanta Studios]] in [[Fayette County, Georgia]],<ref name="CapaldiDavidson" /><ref name="FilmingBegin" /> under the working title ''El Dorado''.<ref name="WorkingTitle" /> [[Henry Braham]] served as cinematographer, after previously doing so for Gunn's ''Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2''.<ref name="Braham" /> Feige and Marvel Studios co-president [[Louis D'Esposito]] visited the set during filming.<ref name="MarvelSetVisit" /> Gunn took several precautions to try to avoid details about the film leaking, such as referring to Elba's character in the script and on set as "[[Vigilante (character)|Vigilante]]" to prevent his actual role from being revealed and also not giving some of the actors script pages from after their characters' deaths.<ref name="GunnLeakPrecautions" />

Gunn extensively [[storyboard]]s his films, but the opening shot of ''The Suicide Squad''—in which the camera rotates out of a puddle's reflection on the ground to reveal Savant bouncing a ball—was a spontaneous decision that he made on set when he saw the reflections on the ground. For a scene where Bloodsport smokes a cigarette, Elba—who has asthma and does not smoke himself—was left with tears in his eyes that had to be removed digitally since Gunn otherwise liked the shot.<ref name="FSRCommentary" /> Gunn said the film used more practical effects than any other blockbuster comic book film, with on-set special effects provided by [[Dan Sudick]] and prosthetics created by [[Legacy Effects]].<ref name="GunnPracticalEffects" /> Gunn highlighted a shot in the film where King Shark, who is created with visual effects, rips a person in half, which was done practically using special effects and prosthetics.<ref name="GunnKSRealPerson" /> Stunt supervisor [[Guy Norris]] served as [[second unit]] director for the car scenes in the film.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" /> Gunn explained that he rarely uses second unit directors on his films and has never liked working with them, but he enjoyed the experience of working with Norris as he was not comfortable shooting with cars and had already known Norris for a long time.<ref name="Norris2ndUnit" /><ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" />

Filming in Atlanta was expected to last three months before moving to Panama for a month,<ref name="AtlantaPanamaFilming" /> with [[Colón, Panama]] standing in for the streets of Corto Maltese. Gunn said the city was "beautiful but falling-apart... we were able to use all the colors but to keep the grime" as well.<ref name="DailyDemocratAug2021" /> The cast and crew rescued several animals from the streets of Panama.<ref name="FSRCommentary" /> Filming also took place in [[Porto]], [[Portugal]],<ref name="PortoFilming" /> and [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on February 28, 2020.<ref name="FilmingWrap" />

===Editing===
[[Fred Raskin]] and [[Christian Wagner]] served as editors on the film.<ref name="GunnScriptApart" /> Raskin previously collaborated alongside Gunn on ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' (2014) and ''Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2''.<ref name="Raskin" /> By April 2020, Gunn was editing the film at his home due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], which he said had not affected post-production or the release schedule at that time.<ref name="GunnPost" /> The initial assembly cut for the film was around two hours and forty minutes long and around twenty minutes of that was soon cut down during editing.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" /> Gunn felt the film was still too long and removed some elements such as part of Harley Quinn's separate storyline that he felt was "pushing audiences away a little bit too much",<ref name="THRGunnGrounded" /> as well as a sequence with Ratcatcher, King Shark, Polka-Dot Man and the Thinker when they exit a Corto Maltese bar. Gunn felt the latter was "pretty dynamite stuff" but it was slowing down the film and at the wrong time.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" /> A smaller scene that was cut involved Waller's aide Flo Crawley being arrested for knocking Waller out during the final battle; Gunn explained that this was a mostly unnecessary detail which made the audience feel uncomfortable since it felt less like the villain, Waller, had lost, but he included the moment in a recap of the film that begins the spin-off series ''[[Peacemaker (TV series)|Peacemaker]]'' to explain why Crawley is not in that series.<ref name="CrawleySceneCut" /> In December, Gunn said editing for the film's final cut had been completed and work on the remaining visual effects, sound and score was continuing.<ref name="GunnCCXPProduction" /> The final runtime is two hours and twelve minutes.<ref name="Runtime" />

===Visual effects===
Kelvin McIlwain was the visual effects supervisor for the film, with visual effects vendors including [[Framestore]], [[Weta Digital]], [[Trixter (company)|Trixter]], [[Scanline VFX]] and [[Cantina Creative]].<ref name="ArtofVFX" /> Gunn said the visual effects for King Shark, primarily handled by Framestore and Weta Digital, were particularly difficult to get right.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" />

===Music===
{{Main|The Suicide Squad (soundtrack)|l1=''The Suicide Squad'' (soundtrack)}}
In May 2020, [[John Murphy (composer)|John Murphy]] was set as the composer for the film.<ref name="MurphyScore" /> [[Tyler Bates]], who scored all of Gunn's previous films, was originally attached to compose the score for ''The Suicide Squad'' but eventually left the project. During pre-production, Bates wrote music for Gunn to use on set as he had previously done for Gunn on the ''Guardians of the Galaxy'' films.<ref name="MurphyScore2" /> The single "Rain" by [[Grandson (musician)|grandson]] and [[Jessie Reyez]] from the film's soundtrack album was released on June 22, 2021, with the artists also expected to contribute songs individually to the album.<ref name="RainSingle" /> A single from Murphy's score, "So This Is The Famous Suicide Squad", was made available on July 8.<ref name="MurphySingle" /> On August 6, two full albums for the film were released by [[WaterTower Music]]: one featuring Murphy's score and a soundtrack album featuring songs from the film, including "Rain".<ref name="ScoreAlbum" /><ref name="SoundtrackAlbum" />

===Completion===
In January 2021, at the beginning of filming for ''Peacemaker'' in [[Vancouver, Canada]], Gunn filmed a [[post-credits scene]] for ''The Suicide Squad'' that sets up the series.<ref name="THRHollandJan2022" /> He had not planned an alternative post-credits scene before deciding to make ''Peacemaker''.<ref name="GunnColliderAug2021" /> A month later, the film was fully finished. Gunn said Warner Bros. had not interfered with his vision for the film and had only given a few minor notes.<ref name="GunnFinishedFeb2021" /> He added that ''The Suicide Squad'' was the most fun he had making a film, which he attributed to prioritizing creativity over perfectionism, being in the best place mentally and emotionally of his career, having a "stupendous" cast and crew and supportive studio and feeling that he was at the height of his directing abilities with the film.<ref name="GunnMostFun" /> It came in under budget and had no days of additional photography.<ref name="THRReflectOnScript" /> In July, the film's release date was moved up by a day to August 5.<ref name="ReleaseDateCollider" />

==Marketing==
A behind-the-scenes featurette was released on August 22, 2020, during the virtual [[DC FanDome]] event.<ref name="FirstLook" /> A panel for the film was held during [[Comic Con Experience]]'s digital event CCXP Worlds on December 6, with James Gunn and members of the cast in attendance. The cast members discussed their characters, and a design for Elba's costume as Bloodsport was revealed.<ref name="BloodsportCCXP" /> Cena decided to wear the Peacemaker costume for interviews and other promotional events as a way to familiarize the audience with the lesser-known character, which was a tactic that he had previously used when he was a professional wrestler.<ref name="CenaPeopleFeb2022" />

The film's first trailer was released on March 26, 2021, with Alex McLevy of ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' praising its jokes and action. He felt everything about the trailer perfectly captured the Suicide Squad's inherent "outlandish fun" from the comics.<ref name="AVClubTrailer" /> Anthony D'Alessandro at ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' felt the trailer had all the trademarks of Gunn's ''Guardians of the Galaxy'' films, noting the use of a "retro hit single" in [[Steely Dan]]'s "[[Dirty Work (Steely Dan song)|Dirty Work]]",<ref name="DeadlineTrailer" /> with ''[[The Verge]]''{{'s}} Jay Peters also getting "some serious ''Guardians of the Galaxy'' vibes" from the trailer.<ref name="VergeTrailer" /> D'Alessandro and Peters both compared it to the recently released DC film ''Zack Snyder's Justice League'' and noted how the trailer was much more colorful and humorous than that film.<ref name="VergeTrailer" /><ref name="DeadlineTrailer" /> McLevy, Jennifer Bisset and Sean Keane of ''[[CNET]]'', and ''[[CinemaBlend]]''{{'s}} Sean O'Connell all highlighted King Shark and his R-rated scenes.<ref name="AVClubTrailer" /><ref name="CNETTrailer" /><ref name="CinemaBlendTrailer" /> The trailer was viewed 150 million times within a week of its release, breaking the record for [[red band]] trailers that was established earlier in the year by the trailer for ''[[Mortal Kombat (2021 film)|Mortal Kombat]]'' (2021).<ref name="RedBandTrailerRecord" />

A [[green band]] trailer was released online on [[April Fool's Day]], after debuting in front of theatrical screenings of ''[[Godzilla vs. Kong]]'' (2021). It features new footage because Gunn did not want to use an edited version of the red band trailer as is usually done. Jennifer Ouellette at ''[[Ars Technica]]'' noted that the new trailer had a darker, more ominous tone than the red band version, without the jokes and King Shark scenes and with added character beats.<ref name="GreenBandTrailer" /> Another trailer was released on June 22 by the main cast of the film in a marketing stunt where they pretended that they were leaking the trailer early. Gunn and Warner Bros. also joked about the trailer's "early release".<ref name="VarietyTrailerLeak" /> The trailer features the song "Rain" by grandson and Jessie Reyez from the film's soundtrack album.<ref name="RainSingle" /> Footage from the film is included in the music video for "Rain", which was released the next month.<ref name="RainVideo" />

==Release==
===Theatrical and streaming===
''The Suicide Squad'' premiered theatrically in the United Kingdom on July 30, 2021.<ref name="UKRelease" /> It was scheduled to debut in the United States on August 6,<ref name="THRReleaseJan2019" /> but was moved up a day to August 5, for early preview screenings.<ref name="ReleaseDateCollider" /> The film was also made available on the streaming service [[HBO Max]] on August 5, for a one-month premiere streaming window. This was done as part of Warner Bros.'s joint theatrical and streaming release strategy for 2021 films that was announced in December 2020 amid the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="DualTheaterStreamingRelease" /> Reflecting on this release strategy in January 2022, Gunn acknowledged suggestions that it negatively impacted on the film's box office performance and said the approach was not ideal for the film. He added that the film was widely pirated following its streaming debut,<ref name="GunnTHRTop5" /> with a high quality version of the film appearing on piracy websites almost immediately after it was released on HBO Max.<ref name="Piracy" /> Internet technology company [[Akamai]] reported, based on global unlicensed streaming and torrent data, that ''The Suicide Squad'' was the sixth most pirated film between January and September 2021.<ref name="AkamaiPiracy" /> Despite this, Gunn was grateful that people had been able to see the film online during the pandemic and that it had otherwise been well received.<ref name="GunnTHRTop5" />

=== Home media ===
The film was made available for digital purchase and rental on September 17, 2021, after leaving its HBO Max same day premiere window. ''The Suicide Squad'' was then released on [[DVD]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]] on October 26, with special features including a commentary by Gunn, deleted and extended scenes, a gag reel, three [[retro style]] trailers, breakdowns of key scenes from the film, and several making-of featurettes.<ref name="HomeMedia" /> The film was the top-seller for home media sales in the United States for two weeks after its release, on both the [[The NPD Group|NPD]] Videoscan First Alert chart (which tracks combined Blu-ray and DVD sales) and the dedicated Blu-ray sales chart.<ref name="HMSales" /><ref name="HMSales2" /> According to ''[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]]'', it sold a combined 146,752 Blu-ray and DVD units in its first week for a total of $3.8{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="NUM" /> It was the fourth highest-selling title for the month of October on the VideoScan First Alert chart.<ref name="HMSalesOct2021" />

==Reception==
===Box office===
''The Suicide Squad'' grossed $55.8{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, and $112.9{{nbsp}}million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $168.7{{nbsp}}million, against a production budget of $185{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="BOM" /><ref name="NUM" />

In the United States and Canada, the film made $12.2{{nbsp}}million on its first day, including $4.1{{nbsp}}million from Thursday night previews, which was the biggest first day for an R-rated film during the pandemic. That contributed to $26.2{{nbsp}}million (47.0% of total gross) across the film's opening weekend, which was also the biggest for an R-rated film during the pandemic.<ref name="DeadlineOpening" /> The film dropped 72% in its second weekend, finishing fifth with just $7.5{{nbsp}}million. This drop was similar to those seen in the second weekends of other films released simultaneously in theaters and on HBO Max, such as ''[[Space Jam: A New Legacy]]'' (2021).<ref name="DeadlineWknd2" /> It dropped 55% in its third weekend, grossing $3.4{{nbsp}}million,<ref name="DeadlineWknd3" /> before crossing $50{{nbsp}}million in its fourth weekend with an additional $2{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="DeadlineWknd4" />

The week prior to its domestic release, the film made $6.7{{nbsp}}million from five countries, including $4.5{{nbsp}}million in the United Kingdom and $1.6{{nbsp}}million in France.<ref name="DeadlineIOpening" /><ref name="DeadlineIWknd2" /> ''The Suicide Squad'' expanded to 69 countries, covering all major markets except Japan, in its second week of international release. It made $35{{nbsp}}million, finishing first in Russia ($4.2{{nbsp}}million), Mexico ($2{{nbsp}}million), Brazil ($1.4{{nbsp}}million), Spain ($1.3{{nbsp}}million), and Italy ($1{{nbsp}}million), and only dropped 25% in markets that it opened in the previous week.<ref name="DeadlineIWknd2" /> The film opened at number one in Japan the following week with $1.9{{nbsp}}million, retained first position in Brazil, and moved to first in Australia as well. Overall, the weekend total was $17{{nbsp}}million, down 50% from the previous week and behind new release ''[[Free Guy]]'' in many markets.<ref name="DeadlineIWknd3" /> The film added $8.7{{nbsp}}million in its fourth international weekend,<ref name="DeadlineIWknd4" /> before passing $100{{nbsp}}million internationally the next week with an additional $4.6{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="DeadlineIWknd5" /> The United Kingdom, Russia, France, Germany, and Mexico were the leading markets for the film outside of the U.S. and Canada.<ref name="DeadlineIWknd4" />

The film's box office underperformance was generally attributed to its release during the COVID-19 pandemic, the fact that it was also available for streaming on HBO Max at the same time as its theatrical release, piracy allowed by the streaming release, the R-rating which limited younger audiences, the poor reception to the first ''Suicide Squad'', and confusion from general audiences over its relationship to the first film caused by their similar titles.<ref name="DeadlineOpening" /><ref name="DeadlineIWknd2" /><ref name="VarietyOpening" /><ref name="ForbesOpening" /><ref name="CNNOpening" /> Anthony D'Alessandro of ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' felt the HBO Max availability and feelings about the first film outweighed the impacts of the pandemic, and noted that other films had made significantly more money at the box office in recent weeks.<ref name="DeadlineOpening" /> Writing for ''[[Forbes]]'', Scott Mendelson felt that general audiences would not be compelled by Gunn's involvement and instead would see the film as a sequel without the main draws of the first film, such as Will Smith. Mendelson added that the film's $185{{nbsp}}million budget was an "absurd amount to spend" on an R-rated film and was significantly more than what was spent on previous R-rated superhero films. He said the film required a "best-case-scenario box office" just to break even with that budget, and that did not turn out to be the case.<ref name="ForbesOpening" />

===Streaming viewership===
HBO Max reported that ''The Suicide Squad'' had the second-biggest opening weekend of all the films that premiered simultaneously on the streaming service and in theaters. The service did not provide further details or data,<ref name="VarietyOpening" /> but analytics company [[Samba TV]], which gathers viewership data from certain [[Smart TV]]s and content providers, reported that 2.8{{nbsp}}million U.S. households watched the film over its first four days of release on HBO Max, which placed it only behind ''[[Mortal Kombat (2021 film)|Mortal Kombat]]'' (3.8{{nbsp}}million) for simultaneous theater and streaming releases. Also according to Samba TV, ''The Suicide Squad'' had the biggest HBO Max debut for a DC film, ahead of both ''[[Wonder Woman 1984]]'' (2.2{{nbsp}}million) and ''Zack Snyder's Justice League'' (1.8{{nbsp}}million).<ref name="SambaTVOpening" /> While discussing [[Nielsen Media Research]]'s streaming ratings—which HBO Max does not participate in—for the week of the film's release, ''Forbes''{{'}}s Scott Mendelson estimated that around 369 million minutes of the film had been viewed based on Samba TV's figures, which would have placed it third on Nielsen's list of top streaming films for the week behind [[Netflix]]'s ''[[Vivo (film)|Vivo]]'' (493{{nbsp}}million minutes) and [[Disney+]]'s ''[[Jungle Cruise (film)|Jungle Cruise]]'' (407{{nbsp}}million minutes).<ref name="ForbesNielsen" />

Samba TV later reported that 3.5{{nbsp}}million American households had watched the film within its first week of release.<ref name="Week1HBOMax" /> This grew to 4.7{{nbsp}}million for the first 17 days, which placed it third after ''Mortal Kombat'' (5.5{{nbsp}}million) and ''[[Godzilla vs. Kong]]'' (5.1{{nbsp}}million).<ref name="SambaTV17Days" /> These rankings remained the same for Samba TV's 30-day figures, which reported 5.1{{nbsp}}million households having viewed ''The Suicide Squad'' in that time.<ref name="SambaTV30Days" /> According to [[Whip Media]], who track viewership data for the 19 million worldwide users of their [[TV Time]] app, ''The Suicide Squad'' was the seventh-most-streamed-film of 2021 and the most-streamed-project of the year for HBO Max.<ref name="TVTime2021" />

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''The Suicide Squad'' holds an approval rating of {{RT data|score}} based on {{RT data|count}} reviews, with an average score of {{RT data|average}}. The site's critical consensus reads, "Enlivened by writer-director James Gunn's singularly skewed vision, ''The Suicide Squad'' marks a funny, fast-paced rebound that plays to the source material's violent, anarchic strengths."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id={{RT data|rtid|noprefix=y}} |type=m |title= The Suicide Squad (2021) |access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 72 out of 100, based on 55 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=the-suicide-squad |type=movie |title=The Suicide Squad Reviews |access-date=January 17, 2022}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale, the same score that the first film and ''Birds of Prey'' received, while [[PostTrak]] reported that 83% of audience members gave it a positive score compared to 73% for the first film. 62% told PostTrak that they would definitely recommend the film.<ref name="DeadlineOpening" />

Reviewing for ''[[The A.V. Club]]'', Katie Rife gave the film a "B+" grade and said, "Now that superhero movies have gone from disreputable entertainment for children to global events ushered in with awed reverence, it was time for someone to come along and pop the balloon. Pulpy and outrageous, irreverent and ultraviolent, ''The Suicide Squad'' does so with a smile."<ref name="AVClubReview" /> Writing for ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', [[Owen Gleiberman]] called the film what "the first ''Suicide Squad'' should have been" and said that "''The Suicide Squad'' comes sizzlingly alive as all-cylinders comic-book moviemaking. There's a frowsy destructive joy to the staging".<ref name="VarietyReview" /> Clarisse Loughrey of ''[[The Independent]]'' also praised the film and considered it an improvement over ''Suicide Squad'', writing "Gunn's distinct and self-assured vision, which he's said was left untouched and unbothered by studio interference, puts ''The Suicide Squad'' alongside the very best of modern comic-book filmmaking. His film, which now comes with an all-important ''<nowiki>'The'</nowiki>'' at the beginning of its title, functions both as a sequel and a fresh start."<ref name="IndependentReview" /> Brian Truitt of ''[[USA Today]]'' gave the film 3.5 stars out of 4 and said "''The Suicide Squad'' shifts superhero movies into a hilarious, gory and exceedingly bonkers new direction but writer/director James Gunn still makes time to show one goofy supervillain making sure another's buckled up for a plane ride headed for certain doom" and also said that the film "is a bloody marvel that blows up the superhero genre."<ref name="USATodayReview" />

[[Justin Chang]] from ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' stated: "After 2016's ugly, bludgeoning ''Suicide Squad'', I couldn't imagine liking—and could barely stomach the idea of seeing—another movie called ''Suicide Squad''. I'm delighted to be proven wrong", and called the film "redemption for James Gunn and DC".<ref name="LATReview" /> Richard Trenholm of ''[[CNET]]'' gave the film a positive review and praised it for its themes, writing "This irreverent comic book movie takes shots at very big and serious themes, raging against [[Imperialism|Western imperialism]], [[American foreign policy]] and government deception as it indicts interference in foreign countries. Representing this chilling bureaucratic evil, Amanda Waller emerges as perhaps the most hateful villain in the DC universe — certainly the coldest".<ref name="CNETReview" /> [[Alonso Duralde]] of the ''[[TheWrap]]'' wrote: "''The Suicide Squad'' is by no means perfect, but like the ''[[Deadpool (film)|Deadpool]]'' movies, it's a showcase for what can happen when a superhero movie is allowed to be sprightly, self-aware, and sardonic while also indulging in hard-R violence, gore, and language. Gunn's latest creation is not without moments that drag, but when it pops, it pops brilliantly".<ref name="WrapReview" /> ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'}}s Leah Greenblatt gave the film a C+ grade and wrote: "The script, accordingly, herks and jerks along with a sort of forced-festive glee, its mounting body count buffeted by goofball banter and pounding soundtrack cues. A good half of the jokes don't land, but unlike his predecessor's joyless slog, Gunn's version at least celebrates the nonsense."<ref name="EWReview" /> [[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film 3 out of 5 stars, and called it "a long, loud, often enjoyable and amusing film that blitzes your eyeballs and eardrums and covers all the bases".<ref name="GuardianReview" /> [[Eric Kohn]] of ''[[IndieWire]]'' described the film as a reminder of the United States' [[United States involvement in regime change in Latin America|historical interventions in Latin America]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Kohn |first1=Eric |title='The Suicide Squad' Is a Surprising Response to Latin American History — and America's Messy Relationship to It |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/the-suicide-squad-panama-history-1234656479/ |work=[[IndieWire]] |date=August 7, 2021}}</ref>

===Themes and analysis===
Some critics have interpreted ''The Suicide Squad'' as a critique and even a parody of [[Foreign policy of the United States|American foreign policy]] and noted some [[anti-imperialist]] themes, which was arguably done in greater extent than the film's predecessor. Noah Berlatsky of ''[[Foreign Policy]]'' compares the rationale of the Suicide Squad's intervention in the Corto Maltese civil war to the [[2003 invasion of Iraq]] on the grounds of an anti-American regime purportedly gaining access to and weaponizing a destructive force such as Starro. He also notes that American interference in Corto Maltese is "as every bit as disastrous as the 2003 Iraq invasion," also commenting that ''The Suicide Squad'' depicts the U.S. government as "bumbling" and "corrupt", citing the squad's accidental slaughter of the very fighters they were supposed to protect and Amanda Waller's desire to cover up America's role in Project Starfish and ambivalence towards Starro's rampage.<ref name="FPThemes" /> Reuben Baron of ''[[Comic Book Resources]]'' compares the torture and experimentation of Corto Maltese civilians in Project Starfish to the [[Abu Ghraib torture and prisoner abuse]] scandal and allegations of prisoner torture at [[Guantanamo Bay detention camp]]. He opines that the evil actions of Silvio Luna's regime and Starro, while heinous, are "just symptomatic of the ultimate villain of ''The Suicide Squad'': the United States government," describing Peacemaker's betrayal of the team as "misguided patriotism", and that the "heroes in this movie are those who are willing to defy the orders given to them by the government".<ref name="CBRThemes" /> Lucy Nichols of ''Counter Fire'' writes that the film "bizarrely, openly critiques American imperialism in [[Latin America]]", and like Baron, comments that ''The Suicide Squad'' is unusual among superhero films with its direct criticism of the U.S. government and military.<ref name="CFThemes" />

==Franchise==
===Spin-offs===
Gunn began working on a spin-off series featuring Peacemaker while completing work on ''The Suicide Squad''.<ref name="GunnPeacemakerSeries" /> In January 2021, Gunn said he had ideas for more television spin-offs beyond ''Peacemaker''.<ref name="GunnMoreSpinOffs" /> DC Films president [[Walter Hamada]] said a month later that they had plans for more projects with Gunn,<ref name="THRReflectOnScript" /> and Safran said there was potential for more spin-offs based on Suicide Squad members such as Ratcatcher and Bloodsport if a filmmaker had a unique vision for them.<ref name="SafranSpinOffs" /> Gunn said in February that he did not necessarily intend to write and direct every ''The Suicide Squad'' spin-off, like he did for ''Peacemaker''.<ref name="GunnVarietyFeb2022" /> In November 2022, Stallone said he would reprise his role as King Shark in the future.<ref name="StalloneReprisal" />

====''Peacemaker'' season 1====
{{Main|Peacemaker (TV series){{!}}''Peacemaker'' (TV series)}}{{See also|Peacemaker season 1}}
While completing work on ''The Suicide Squad'' during the COVID-19 pandemic, Gunn began writing a spin-off television series centered around Peacemaker.<ref name="GunnPeacemakerSeries" /> In September 2020, HBO Max ordered ''Peacemaker'' straight-to-series, with Gunn writing all eight episodes and directing five of them. Cena stars as Peacemaker,<ref name="PeacemakerSeriesDeadline" /> with Agee, Holland, and Davis also reprising their respective film roles.<ref name="AgeePeacemaker" /><ref name="PeacemakerCast" /><ref name="DavisPeacemaker" /> Gunn and Safran executive produce the series,<ref name="PeacemakerSeriesDeadline" /> which premiered in January 2022.<ref name="PeacemakerRelease" />

===Unproduced sequel===
In July 2021, Gunn said he had ideas for a sequel film that would go in a different direction from just assembling a new Suicide Squad team.<ref name="EWJul2021" /> In June 2022, Gunn said there had been discussions about a sequel to ''The Suicide Squad'' and he was considering it for his next feature film but his focus was on television for the foreseeable future following his positive experience making ''Peacemaker''.<ref name="GunnJun2022" /> The following June, Gunn said there would not be a sequel.<ref name="GunnNoSequel" />

=== DC Universe ===
{{Main|DC Universe (franchise)}}
In October 2022, Gunn and co-producer [[Peter Safran]] were announced to be assuming roles as the dual CEOs and chairmen of the newly established [[DC Studios]], placing them in supervision over all DC Comics-based media between film, television, animation and video games.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last1=Couch |first1=Aaron |last2=Kit |first2=Borys |date=October 25, 2022 |title=DC Shocker: James Gunn, Peter Safran to Lead Film, TV and Animation Division (Exclusive) |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/dc-movies-james-gunn-peter-safran-to-lead-film-tv-division-1235248438/ |access-date=December 23, 2024 |magazine=The Hollywood Reporter}}</ref> Following the assumption of their new posts in November, the pair began devising an eight-to-ten year plan for a new interconnected [[DC Universe (franchise)|DC Universe]] (DCU) franchise that would succeed and reboot the DCEU following the December 2023 release of ''[[Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom]]''.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Couch |first=Aaron |date=November 10, 2022 |title=James Gunn, Peter Safran Are Mapping Out "Eight- to 10-Year Plan" for DC |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/dc-movies-james-gunn-peter-safran-1235259792/ |access-date=December 23, 2024 |magazine=The Hollywood Reporter}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Dominguez |first=Noah |date=October 26, 2022 |title=The 'DCEU' Is Dead -

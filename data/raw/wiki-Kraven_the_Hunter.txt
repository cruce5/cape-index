'''Kraven the Hunter''' ('''Sergei Nikolaevich Kravinoff'''; [[Russian language|Russian]]: Сергей Николаевич Кравинов) is a [[supervillain]] appearing in [[American comic book]]s published by [[Marvel Comics]]. Created by writer [[Stan Lee]] and artist [[Steve Ditko]], the character [[First appearance|first appeared]] in ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man]]'' #15 (August 1964) as an adversary for the [[superhero]] [[Spider-Man]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Harn|first=Darby|date=June 2, 2021|title=Kraven The Hunter: 10 Things Only Comic Book Fans Know About The Spider-Man Villain|url=https://screenrant.com/kraven-the-hunter-comic-book-facts-spider-man/|access-date=2024-05-13|website=[[Screen Rant]]|language=en}}</ref> He has since endured as one of the web-slinger's most formidable foes, and is part of the collective of adversaries that make up [[List of Spider-Man enemies|Spider-Man's rogues' gallery]]. Kraven has also come into conflict with other heroes, such as [[Black Panther (character)|Black Panther]] and [[Tigra]]. He is the half-brother of the [[Chameleon (character)|Chameleon]]<ref>{{Cite web|last=Iacobucci|first=Jordan|date=June 23, 2023|title=Who is Kraven the Hunter And What are His Powers?|url=https://www.cbr.com/guide-kraven-hunter-marvel-comics-powers-abilities-history/|access-date=May 13, 2024|website=[[Comic Book Resources]]}}</ref> and a founding member of the [[Sinister Six]].<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Ghosh|first1=Artie|last2=Raul|first2=Danilo|date=November 12, 2023|title=Explained: Who is Kraven the Hunter?|url=https://movieweb.com/kraven-the-hunter-explained/|access-date=May 13, 2024|website=[[MovieWeb]]}}</ref>

In Kraven's first appearance, he calls Spider-Man "the most dangerous game", a direct reference to the 1924 [[The Most Dangerous Game|short story of the same name]], in which General Zaroff, a Russian [[big-game hunter]] (and a primary inspiration for the character), hunts people for sport.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Garcia|first=Mayra|date=May 26, 2023|title=10 Things You Didn't Know About Kraven The Hunter|url=https://www.cbr.com/kraven-the-hunter-trivia/|access-date=2024-05-13|website=[[Comic Book Resources]]|language=en}}</ref>

Kraven is typically portrayed as a renowned big-game hunter whose goal in life is to beat Spider-Man to prove himself as the world's greatest hunter.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Richard|first=Nate|date=April 27, 2024|title=Kraven the Hunter: Everything We Know So Far About the Spider-Man Spinoff|url=https://collider.com/kraven-the-hunter-movie/|access-date=2024-05-13|website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|language=en}}</ref> Though he is often overconfident in his own abilities, about which he likes to boast, he possesses a great sense of honor, and treats his adversaries as equals until proven otherwise. Because of this, Kraven has sometimes been depicted as an [[antihero]] and has teamed up with other heroes, including Spider-Man, the [[Avengers 1959|Avengers of 1959]], and [[Squirrel Girl]], in ''[[The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl]]'' (2015–2019). The character is widely regarded as one of Spider-Man's most formidable enemies, and has gained considerable attention from storylines such as 1987's "[[Kraven's Last Hunt]]," 2010's "[[Grim Hunt]]" and 2019's "[[Hunted (comics)|Hunted]]". The mantle of Kraven the Hunter has also been assumed by Kraven's sons '''[[Alyosha Kravinoff|Alyosha]]''' and '''[[#Last Son of Kraven|Sergei Kravinoff II]]''', and his daughter '''[[Ana Kravinoff]]'''.

Since his original introduction in comics, the character has been featured in various other Marvel-licensed products, including [[video games]], [[animated]] [[television series]], and merchandise. [[Aaron Taylor-Johnson]] portrayed Kraven the Hunter in the [[Sony's Spider-Man Universe]] (SSU) live-action film ''[[Kraven the Hunter (film)|Kraven the Hunter]]'' (2024).

==Publication history==
[[File:Kraven the Hunter.png|thumb|Kraven the Hunter on the cover of ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man]]'' #47 (April 1967). Art by [[John Romita Sr.]]]]
The character first appeared in ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man]]'' issue #15 (August 1964), and was created by writer [[Stan Lee]] and artist [[Steve Ditko]].<ref>{{cite book|last1=Manning|first1=Matthew K.|last2= Gilbert|first2= Laura|chapter= 1960s|title=Spider-Man Chronicle Celebrating 50 Years of Web-Slinging|publisher=[[Dorling Kindersley]]|year=2012|page=26|isbn=978-0756692360|quote= [Stan] Lee and [Steve] Ditko's newest villain, Kraven the Hunter, debuted in this issue.}}</ref> Though he would occasionally appear in other characters' titles, he was most frequently used as a Spider-Man foe. He is killed off in the acclaimed 1987 storyline by [[J. M. DeMatteis]], [[Mike Zeck]], and [[Bob McLeod (comics)|Bob McLeod]], "[[Kraven's Last Hunt]]". Although generally considered to be the definitive Kraven the Hunter story, the story originally had a completely different character in Kraven's role. Writer DeMatteis recounted:

{{Blockquote|As I was getting ready to work on the story, I was sitting in my office and looking through a [[Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe|''Marvel Universe Handbook'']] and came across the entry for Kraven the Hunter. To this day, I don't know if this was something that was established in [[Continuity (fiction)|continuity]], or if whoever was writing the Handbook entry made it up, but they mentioned that Kraven was [[Russians|Russian]]. For me, a total [[Dostoyevsky]] fanatic, the idea that Kraven was Russian and had the same tortured, Russian soul that the great Dostoyevsky characters had, unlocked this door in my head and suddenly I had a new understanding of this character. I thought about Kraven and the forces that had driven him to be who and what he was. This was a character I had never had any interest in whatsoever. I always thought he was one of the stupidest Spider-Man villains ever, but suddenly I had this whole new take on the character.<ref name="Back35">{{cite journal|last= Johnson|first= Dan|date= August 2009|title= In Our Sights: Kraven's Last Hunt|journal= [[Back Issue!]]|issue= #35|pages= 3–9|publisher= [[TwoMorrows Publishing]]}}</ref>}}

Despite Kraven the Hunter being one of Spider-Man's most long-standing arch-enemies, DeMatteis claims that none of Marvel's editorial staff objected to the proposal to have him killed in the story.<ref name="Back35"/> Kraven later returns as a ghost in the graphic novel ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man: Soul of the Hunter]]'', also by DeMatteis, Zeck, and McLeod. DeMatteis also wrote "Kraven's First Hunt" for ''The Sensational Spider-Man'' Annual '96, a retelling of ''The Amazing Spider-Man'' #15 which incorporated several DeMatteis [[retcon]]s, such as that Kraven was [[Relational aggression|abusive]] towards the [[Chameleon (character)|Chameleon]].

==Fictional character biography==
Sergei Kravinoff (Сергей Кравинов) is a Russian immigrant, the son of the [[aristocrat]] [[Nikolai Kravinoff]] who fled to the United States with his family in 1917 after the [[Murder of the Romanov family|executions of Russian nobility by the]] [[February Revolution]] and subsequent collapse of the reign of Tsar [[Nicholas II of Russia|Nicholas II]].

Kraven is a maniacal [[big-game hunter]] who seeks to defeat [[Spider-Man]] to prove that he is the greatest hunter in the world.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Brevoort|first1=Tom|authorlink1=Tom Brevoort|last2=DeFalco|first2=Tom|authorlink2=Tom DeFalco|last3=Manning|first3=Matthew K.|last4=Sanderson|first4=Peter|authorlink4=Peter Sanderson|last5=Wiacek|first5=Win|title=Marvel Year By Year: A Visual History|date=2017|publisher=DK Publishing|isbn=978-1465455505|page=101}}</ref> Unlike other hunters, he typically disdains the use of guns or [[archery|bow and arrows]], preferring to take down large dangerous animals with his bare hands. He also lives by a code of honour of sorts, choosing to hunt his game fairly.<ref>{{cite book |last=Rovin |first=Jeff |title=The Encyclopedia of Super-Villains |date=1987 |publisher=Facts on File |isbn=0-8160-1356-X |location=New York |page=182 |author-link=Jeff Rovin|title-link=The Encyclopedia of Super-Villains}}[https://archive.org/details/encyclopediaofsu0000rovi_h5r9/page/182/mode/2up]</ref> He consumes a mystical serum to give himself enhanced strength and dramatically slow the [[aging]] process. Kraven was trained as a hunter largely by a mysterious man named Gregor, a mercenary who once battled [[Ka-Zar (Kevin Plunder)|Ka-Zar]].<ref>{{Cite comic|Writer=Mark Waid|Penciller=Andy Kubert|Inker=Jesse Delperdang|Story=The Thrilling Return of Ka-Zar, Lord of the Savage Land!|Title=Ka-Zar|Volume=|Issue=#1|Date=May 1997|Publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref> Kraven was, at one point, the lover of the [[wikt:voodoo|voodoo]] priestess [[Calypso (comics)|Calypso]].<ref>{{cite comic| title=Daredevil|issue=310|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=1992}}</ref>

He is contacted by his half-brother the [[Chameleon (character)|Chameleon]] to defeat Spider-Man. He leads Spider-Man to [[Central Park]] with the help of the Chameleon, who disguises himself as Kraven to use himself as a decoy while the real Kraven ambushes the hero. However, despite Kraven having weakened Spider-Man with a poisonous dart, Spider-Man prevails in the end. Spider-Man proves a frustrating quarry because Kraven continually underestimates the superhero's resourcefulness.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|issue=15|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=1964}}</ref>

Kraven becomes a founding member of the [[Sinister Six]] when he accepts [[Doctor Octopus]]'s offer to form a team to fight Spider-Man. They capture [[Aunt May]] and [[Betty Brant]]. He attacks Spider-Man in [[Central Park]] with three tigers. Spider-Man fights off the attackers and secures the next clue to where Aunt May and Betty Brant are being held. After Aunt May and Betty Brant are rescued, Kraven the Hunter and the rest of the Sinister Six are arrested by the police.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man Annual|volume=1|issue=1|date=1964|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

While in the [[Savage Land]], Kraven the Hunter finds [[Gog (Marvel Comics)|Gog]] in a spaceship that he stumbles upon. Realizing how useful Gog can be, Kraven decides to use him in a plot to conquer the Savage Land. After kidnapping the visiting [[Gwen Stacy]] from a camp in the Savage Land, Kraven and Gog battle the heroes Ka-Zar and Spider-Man.<ref>{{Cite comic|Writer=Roy Thomas|Penciller=Gil Kane|Inker=Frank Giacoia and Tony Mortellaro|Story=Walk the Savage Land!/Gog|Title=The Amazing Spider-Man|Volume=|Issue=#103|Date=December 1971|Publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref> While Ka-Zar deals with Kraven, Spider-Man defeats Gog by luring him into a patch of quicksand, which he sinks to the bottom of.<ref>{{Cite comic|Writer=Roy Thomas|Penciller=Gil Kane|Inker=Frank Giacoia|Story=The Beauty and the Brute|Title=The Amazing Spider-Man|Volume=|Issue=#104|Date=January 1972|Publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

===''Kraven's Last Hunt''===
{{main|Kraven's Last Hunt}}

Determined to end his life as he becomes older, frustrated with his failing health and continuing failure to defeat Spider-Man, Kraven sets out a final hunt for Spider-Man. After capturing Spider-Man, he shoots him with a coma-inducing drug and buries him alive on his estate.<ref>{{cite comic|title=Web of Spider-Man|issue=31|date=1987|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref> To complete his victory, he attempts to become Spider-Man's clear superior by impersonating him in a brutal vigilante campaign and capturing [[Vermin (character)|Vermin]], the one foe Spider-Man had never been able to defeat on his own (Spider-Man's last battle with Vermin required [[Captain America]]'s assistance).<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|issue=293|date=1987|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref><ref>{{cite comic|title=The Spectacular Spider-Man|date=1987|issue=131|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

After Spider-Man emerges from his grave two weeks later, Kraven explains his actions to him and sets Vermin free. Spider-Man goes after Vermin to prevent his killing again. Kraven leaves a final [[Confession (law)|confession]] of his crimes against Spider-Man and then commits suicide.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|issue=294|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=1987}}</ref> Because of his suicide, his soul is unable to find rest until Spider-Man confronts his risen corpse on Kraven's behalf.<ref>{{Cite comic|Writer=J. M. Dematteis|Penciller=Mike Zeck|Inker=Bob McLeod|Story=Soul of the Hunter!|Title=The Amazing Spider-Man: Soul of the Hunter|Volume=|Issue= #1|Date=1992|Publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

===''The Gauntlet and Grim Hunt''===
{{main|The Gauntlet and Grim Hunt}}

Across "[[The Gauntlet and Grim Hunt#The Gauntlet|The Gauntlet]]" and "[[The Gauntlet and Grim Hunt#Grim Hunt|Grim Hunt]]", Kraven is resurrected by his ex-wife Sasha Kravinoff and their children with a [[dark magic]] ritual using the blood of Spider-Man.<ref name=AS635>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=1|issue=635|story=The Grim Hunt Chapter 2|writer=Joe Kelly|artist=Micheal Lark|coartists=Stefano Gaudiano, Matt Southworth|letterer=Joe Caramagna|colorist=Matt Hollongsworth|editor=Stephen Wacker, Tom Brennen, Tom Brevoort|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=June 23, 2010}}</ref> Thoroughly unhappy and enraged with having been bestowed an unwanted revival, Sergei is shown to now be explosively [[psychopath]]ic and cold towards his family, beating his son Vladimir and daughter Ana and expressing little attention towards his wife. After being stabbed by his daughter Ana, Sergei recovers, stating that Sasha restored him with corrupted blood which he calls "unlife". They pull off the mask of the corpse of Spider-Man hung in their mantel and discover that [[Kaine Parker|Kaine]] had impersonated Peter in Spider-Man's costume. Kaine's nature as a clone indicates his unnatural existence has muddied the properties of the ritual.<ref name=AS636>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=1|issue=636|story=The Grim Hunt Chapter 3|writer=Joe Kelly|cowriters=Zeb Wells|artist=Marco Checchetto|coartists=Micheal Lark, Stefano Gaudiano|letterer=Joe Caramagna|colorist=Matt Hollongsworth|editor=Stephen Wacker, Tom Brennen, Tom Brevoort|date=July 8, 2010|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref> Spider-Man, in his black costume, confronts Kraven and the rest of the Kravinoff family. Spider-Man is tempted to kill Kraven, who eagerly and desperately attempts to goad him into it. Beating him brutally, Spider-Man takes Kraven's spear in hand and prepares to grant his enemy his wish but he refuses to do so when he is reminded by [[Julia Carpenter]] (who has inherited the powers of [[Madame Web]] after her death) that committing murder is not in his moral code, and furthermore, by going through with it, damns himself to a future where from that point he takes on a more brutal approach to crime-fighting, including killing suspects, which will lead him to die in a standoff against the police (a scenario previously glimpsed in an earlier issue). Following their defeat by Spider-Man, Kraven and his family escape to the Savage Land. While there, Kraven kills Sasha and Vladimir. Alyosha flees in disgust of what his dad did to his stepmom and half-brother afterwards. Kraven and Ana discuss rebuilding the Kravinoff family, which leads to Ana running off to hunt Alyosha to prove herself worthy.<ref name=AS637>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=1|issue=637|story=The Grim Hunt Conclusion|writer=Joe Kelly|artist=Marco Checchetto|coartists=Micheal Lark, Stefano Gaudiano, Matt Southworth, Brian Thies|letterer=Joe Caramagna|colorist=Matt Hollongsworth|editor=Stephen Wacker, Tom Brennen, Tom Brevoort|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=July 14, 2010}}</ref>

It was later revealed that in the 1950s, Kraven was a member of [[Nick Fury]]'s [[Avengers 1959|Avengers]] alongside [[Dominic Fortune]], [[Dum Dum Dugan]], [[Namora]], [[Silver Sable]], [[Sabretooth (character)|Sabretooth]], and [[Ulysses Bloodstone]], tracking a stolen version of the Super-Soldier Serum combined with the Infinity Formula that had been stolen by a group of Nazis attempting to create their own Captain America.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The New Avengers|volume=2|issue=10| publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2011}}</ref>

===''The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl''===
{{main|The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl}}

Kraven, still claiming to be cursed, next battles [[Squirrel Girl]] at [[Empire State University (comics)|Empire State University]], having earned her ire by lashing out at the local squirrels, including [[Tippy-Toe]]. Kraven departs when Squirrel Girl informs him of the existence of sea monsters like [[Giganto#Giganto (Atlantean beasts)|Giganto]], playing on Kraven's obsessions by challenging him to hunt worthier prey.<ref>{{Cite comic|writer=Ryan North|penciller=Erica Henderson|inker=Erica Henderson|title=The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl|volume=1|issue=#1|date=7 January 2015|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref> Squirrel Girl has helped Kraven reform, teaming up with him to fight other villains, even establishing a friendship with him. She helps Kraven realize that Spider-Man is not even close to being the most dangerous prey in the Marvel Universe.<ref>{{cite comic |title=The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl |volume=2 |issue=32–35 |date=2018 |publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

===''Hunted''===
In the "[[Hunted (comics)|Hunted]]" storyline, Kraven the Hunter has 87 [[Cloning|clones]] of him created, trained as the Sons of Kraven, and sent out to prove themselves by being hunted by each other. The surviving clone is labeled as the [[#Last Son of Kraven|Last Son of Kraven]]. With help from [[Taskmaster (character)|Taskmaster]], [[Black Ant (comics)|Black Ant]], and [[Arcade (Marvel Comics)|Arcade]], Kraven captures various animal-themed villains, traps them in [[Central Park]], and has a group of Hunter-Bots hunt them down for sport.<ref name="Amazing Spider-Man #16">{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=5|issue=16|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2019}}</ref> It is revealed that Kraven's secret motive is to force Spider-Man into killing him again so that he can be free from his curse of immortality.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=5|issue=21|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2019}}</ref> Kraven releases all the surviving hunting targets and dons a copy of Spider-Man's black costume. Mistaking Kraven for Spider-Man, the Last Son of Kraven kills him, freeing him of his curse.<ref name="Amazing Spider-Man #22">{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=5|issue=22|publisher=Marvel Comics|date= 2019}}</ref>

==Powers and abilities==
By regularly ingesting the potion made from various unnamed jungle herbs, Kraven grants himself superhuman physical abilities,<ref>{{Cite web|last=Harn|first=Darby|date=October 7, 2022|title=15 Most Powerful Black Panther Villains|url=https://screenrant.com/most-powerful-villains-black-panther|access-date=2024-05-13|website=[[Screen Rant]]|language=en}}</ref> making him a physical threat to Spider-Man, although these powers are not as developed as those possessed by Spider-Man. Kraven's body is also more durable,<ref>{{Cite web|last=Domingues|first=Camila|date=June 20, 2023|title=Is Kraven the Hunter a villain? (What is Kraven the Hunter's origin story?)|url=https://bamsmackpow.com/2023/06/20/kraven-the-hunter-a-villain-origin-story/|access-date=May 13, 2024|website=[[FanSided]]}}</ref> and more resistant to certain forms of injury than the body of a normal human. He can withstand great impact forces, such as falling from several stories or being repeatedly struck by a superhumanly strong opponent, that would severely injure or kill a normal human, with little to no injury to himself. The effects of the potion have dramatically altered Kraven's aging process to the point that he has aged little, if at all, over several decades. Despite being over 70 years of age, he has the physical appearance of a man in his 30's.

The potion enhances Kraven's sight, hearing, and smell to superhuman levels, adding to his already impressive tracking skills. He can see farther, and with much greater clarity, than a normal human. His hearing is similarly enhanced, enabling him to detect sounds which an ordinary human cannot, or sounds that a normal human could detect, but at much greater distances. Kraven can use his sense of smell to track a target by scent, much as some animals do, even if the scent has been somewhat eroded by natural factors.

Even without his superhuman powers, Kraven is an athlete, tactician, hunter, and hand-to-hand combatant. He also has great knowledge of [[pressure point]]s, both in the anatomy of humans and in many animals. He can strike at these nerve clusters with pinpoint accuracy, allowing him to incapacitate more powerful opponents or animals. He is familiar with many exotic poisons and [[tranquilizer]]s, which he often uses during his hunts.

Due to a flaw in the ceremony that resurrected him, Kraven was given "the ''unlife''... the eternal curse", as he discovered when his daughter stabbed him through the heart. According to Kraven, he can now only die by Spider-Man's hand. However, this curse was apparently broken when Kaine temporarily killed Kraven by stopping his heart and then restarting it with the same move.

===Grim Hunter===
'''Vladimir Kravinoff''' is the son of Sergei Kravinoff, the infamous adversary of [[Spider-Man]] known as Kraven the Hunter. Unlike his illegitimate half-brother [[#Alyosha Kravinoff|Alyosha Kravinoff]] who was raised in shame in [[Africa]], Vladimir grew up in Russia benefiting from his father's wealth and luxuries. Their father often neglected both sons while he pursued his various international quarries. Unaware of his siblings, Vladimir was raised by Sergei's servant Gregor. Vladimir Kravinoff took up the name "'''Grim Hunter'''" and decided to hunt down Spider-Man and several of his foes. He only had one battle with Spider-Man, and his methods were nearly the same as his father's. After getting out of jail, Vladimir attempts to track down Spider-Man, but is killed by [[Kaine Parker]].<ref>{{cite comic |title=Spider-Man|issue=55|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=1967}}</ref>

During "[[The Gauntlet and Grim Hunt]]" storyline, Sasha Kravinoff sacrifices [[Mattie Franklin]] as part of a revival ritual that resurrects Vladimir as a lion-like creature.<ref name="ReferenceA">{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|issue=634|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2010}}</ref> Vladimir assists [[#Ana Kravinoff|Ana Kravinoff]] and Alyosha Kravinoff with capturing [[Anya Corazon]] as well as a second attempt to capture [[Julia Carpenter|Arachne]].<ref name="AS635"/> After Spider-Man defeats the Kravinoff family, they escape to the [[Savage Land]], where Sergei kills Sasha and Vladimir.<ref name="AS637"/>

===Alyosha Kravinoff===
'''Alyosha Kravinoff''' is a [[Mutant (Marvel Comics)|mutant]] who is the second Kraven the Hunter through an unnamed female mutant some time after the death of Vladimir.

Unlike Vladimir, Alyosha was separated from his family and raised in an African jungle. After Kraven's suicide, Alyosha comes to [[New York City]] to investigate his father. However, Calypso poisons Alyosha and Spider-Man and uses her hypnotic powers to force the two to fight to the death. Spider-Man resists and injured Calypso, while Alyosha subdued her with his lion. Despite asking for her forgiveness, Alyosha kills Calypso and all the tribesmen with her.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Spectacular Spider-Man|issue=243|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=1997}}</ref>

Alyosha begins dating Timber Hughes, an aspiring actress who worked as a waitress at an all-villain bar. Alyosha sought to help Timber's career in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles, California|Hollywood]] by becoming a director. Despite celebrity connections, Alyosha's efforts were stonewalled by arrogance, greed, and corruption within the Hollywood elite. Forced out of Hollywood by the powerful Rothstein brothers, Alyosha is beaten while Timber is raped. Alyosha and Timber leave Hollywood to pursue heroics in New York City.<ref name="Spider-Man's Get Kraven #1-6">{{cite comic|title=Spider-Man's Get Kraven|issue=1–6|date=2002–2003|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

During "[[The Gauntlet and Grim Hunt|The Gauntlet]]" storyline, Alyosha returned to New York to help his half-sister Ana and stepmother Sasha in the Grim Hunt by hunting [[Kaine Parker|Kaine]]. They then witness Spider-Man's fight with the [[Lizard (character)|Lizard]].<ref name="ReferenceB">{{cite comic |title=The Amazing Spider-Man |issue=631 |publisher=Marvel Comics |date=2010}}</ref> He was present at the ritual where Sasha sacrificed [[Mattie Franklin]] as part of a revival ritual that resurrects his half-brother [[#Grim Hunter|Vladimir]] as a humanoid lion-like creature.<ref name="ReferenceA" /> After Spider-Man defeats the Kravinoff family, they escape to the [[Savage Land]], where Alyosha is killed by Ana.<ref name="AS637" /><ref name="ReferenceD">{{cite comic|title=Scarlet Spider|volume=2|issue=6|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2012}}</ref>

===Nedrocci Tannengarden===
There was a third son named '''Nedrocci''' "'''Ned'''" '''Tannengarden''' who tried to kill Alyosha, but he was murdered by the Chameleon (who, during that time, believed himself to be the original Kraven the Hunter).<ref name="Spider-Man's Get Kraven #1-6"/>

===Xraven===
[[Mister Sinister]] made a deal with [[Cletus Kasady]] to obtain a sample of the [[Carnage (character)|Carnage]] symbiote. After removing the sentience from the Carnage symbiote sample, Sinister combined it with the DNA of the original X-Men ([[Cyclops (Marvel Comics)|Cyclops]], [[Jean Grey|Marvel Girl]], [[Iceman (Marvel Comics)|Iceman]], [[Warren Worthington III|Angel]], and [[Beast (Marvel Comics)|Beast]]) and that of Kraven the Hunter, creating Xraven. Xraven has all of the powers of the original X-Men as well as retractable claws.<ref>{{cite comic |title=X-Men/Spider-Man |issue=3 |publisher=Marvel Comics |date=2009}}</ref><ref>{{cite comic|title=X-Men/Spider-Man|issue=4|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2008}}</ref>

===Ana Kravinoff===
The daughter of Kraven the Hunter, '''Ana Kravinoff''' becomes the first female to take on the name of Kraven the Hunter. She first appears tracking Spider-Man to his apartment, but mistakenly believes Peter Parker's roommate [[Vin Gonzales]] was him. She then methodically ruins Vin's life, and captures him.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|issue=565|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2008}}</ref> Despite Vin's claims that he was not Spider-Man, Kraven prepared to hunt him, but was stopped by the real Spider-Man. At the end, it is revealed that she is the daughter of Kraven and Sasha Aleksandra Nikolaevich.<ref>{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|issue=611|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2010}}</ref>

Ana assists her half-brother [[#Alyosha Kravinoff|Alyosha Kravinoff]] into attempting to abduct [[Julia Carpenter|Arachne]], only to run afoul of Spider-Man, who repels them. Ana alongside Alyosha, [[Electro (Marvel Comics)|Electro]], and [[Diablo (Marvel Comics)|Diablo]] are present when Sasha sacrifices Mattie Franklin as part of a ritual that resurrects Vladimir Kravinoff as a lion-like creature.<ref name="ReferenceA" /> Ana alongside Vladimir and Alyosha attacks [[Anya Corazon]], only to end up fighting Spider-Man, Arachne, [[Kaine Parker|Kaine]], and [[Chameleon (character)|Chameleon]]. The Kravinoffs manage to overpower them and make off with Anya and Arachne. After Chameleon sheds his disguise and Spider-Man partially succumbs to the drugs the Kravinoffs have given him, Ana is present when Kaine (dressed as Spider-Man) is killed as a sacrifice as part of a ritual that revives her father Sergei Kravinoff.<ref name="AS635" /> Following the resurrection, Sergei gets acquainted with his family. When Kraven returns after preventing Vladimir from attacking the captives, he is shown to lack a sense of control, attacking Ana until he is stabbed in the heart by Ana in self-defense. Sergei recovers, stating that Sasha restored him with corrupted blood or the "unlife" as he puts it.<ref name="AS636" /> When the Kravinoff family is defeated, Ana alongside the other Kravinoffs escapes to the [[Savage Land]]. After Sasha and Vladimir are killed by Kraven and Alyosha flees, she runs off to hunt Alyosha to prove herself to Kraven. She tells Kraven that if she can take down Alyosha, Kraven should train her and help rebuild the Kravinoff family.<ref name="AS637" />

With the help of Ana, Kraven kidnaps Kaine's friends to motivate the Scarlet Spider to fight him. In the end, Kaine delivers a fatal blow to Kraven, which paralyzes his heart. But using the same attack, Kaine brings him back to life, supposedly breaking the curse. Following the fight, both Kravens disappear.<ref name="Scarlet Spider #23">{{cite comic|title=Scarlet Spider|volume=2|issue=23|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2013}}</ref>

Ana reveals that she has Inhuman ancestry on her mother's side, something she discovered while hunting down her mother's family members (and for which her father tried to kill her when she told him). Ana comes to Utolan looking for a cure, but is sedated by order of the councilwoman Sanara.<ref>{{cite comic|title=All-New Inhumans|issue=9|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2015}}</ref> Ana manages to escape and hunts down Sanara. She is exposed to Terrigen Mist during her hunt, activating her Inhuman abilities.<ref>{{cite comic|title=All-New Inhumans|issue=10|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2015}}</ref>

===Sasha Kravinoff===
Kraven's wife and Ana and Vladimir's mother '''Sasha Kravinoff''' was part of a Gauntlet on Spider-Man's life, causing chaos and weakening Spider-Man prior to the events that led to resurrecting Kraven from the grave.

===Last Son of Kraven===
The '''Last Son of Kraven''' (one of the many '''Sons of Kraven''') was cloned from Kraven's DNA by the [[High Evolutionary]]. He hunted his fellow clones and returned to Kraven, where he proved himself by presenting him with the skulls of his fellow clones.<ref name="Amazing Spider-Man #16"/> The Last Son of Kraven is manipulated into killing Kraven, who has disguised himself as Spider-Man.<ref name="Amazing Spider-Man #22"/> After attending his funeral of his "father", the Last Son of Kraven finds a letter written to him by his "father" behind Kraven's rifle on the wall. This led to the Last Son of Kraven taking on the aliases of both Sergei Kravinoff and Kraven. He cuts his hair, wipes off his tiger stripes, and dons a copy of Kraven's costume.<ref name="Amazing Spider-Man #23">{{cite comic|title=The Amazing Spider-Man|volume=5|issue=23|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2019}}</ref>

==Other versions==
Many alternate universe versions of Kraven the Hunter have appeared throughout the character's publication history.

===Amazing Spider-Man: Renew Your Vows===
In ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man: Renew Your Vows]]'', Kraven appears as a member of [[Regent (comics)|Regent]]'s Sinister Six.<ref>{{cite comic |title=Amazing Spider-Man: Renew Your Vows |volume=1 |issue=2–4 |publisher=Marvel Comics |date=July 8, 2015–August 19, 2015 |writer=Dan Slott |penciller=Adam Kubert}}</ref>

===Earth-31===
An alternate universe version of Sergei Kravinoff from Earth-31 called '''Hunter Spider''', who acquired spider-like abilities after being bitten by a giant spider, appears in ''[[End of the Spider-Verse]]''.<ref>{{cite comic|title=Edge of Spider-Verse|volume=2|issue=5|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2022}}</ref>

===Marvel Noir===
In ''[[Marvel Noir]]'', Kraven is a former animal trainer who is later killed by man-eating spiders.<ref>{{cite comic|title=Spider-Man Noir|issue=1–4|date=2008–2009|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

===Infinity Wars===
Bushman the Hunter, a composite character based on Kraven and [[Bushman (character)|Bushman]], appears in ''[[Infinity Wars]]''.<ref>{{cite comic|title=Secret Warps: Soldier Supreme Annual|issue=1|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2019}}</ref>

===Spider-Man: Life Story===
In ''[[Spider-Man: Life Story]]'', [[Venom (character)|Venom]] bonded with Kraven before he could kill himself, eventually reducing Kraven to a skeleton.<ref>{{cite comic|title=[[Spider-Man: Life Story]]|story=Our Secret Wars|issue=3|volume=1|writer=Zdarsky, Chip|penciller=Bagley, Mark|inker=Dell, John|colorist=D'Armata, Frank|letterer=Lanham, Travis|editor=Brevoort, Tom; Smith, Alanna; Ballesteros, Shannon Andrews|date=May 15, 2019|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref><ref>{{cite comic|title=Spider-Man: Life Story|issue=6|story=All My Enemies|volume=1|writer=Zdarsky, Chip|penciller=Bagley, Mark|inker=Hennessy, Andrew|colorist=D'Armata, Frank|letterer=Lanham, Travis|editor=Brevoort, Tom; Smith, Alanna; Ballesteros, Shannon Andrews|date=August 28, 2019|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

===Ultimate Marvel===
In the ''[[Ultimate Marvel]]'' universe, Sergei Kravinoff is a [[Russian Australians|Russian Australian]] and the host of a reality series where he hunts down dangerous animals. Kraven intends to hunt and kill [[Spider-Man (Ultimate Marvel character)|Spider-Man]] on live television to boost his ratings. He was easily beaten by Spider-Man following his victory over [[Doctor Octopus#Ultimate Marvel|Doctor Octopus]] and his show was cancelled.<ref>{{cite comic|title=Ultimate Spider-Man|issue=21|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2009}}</ref> Kraven receives enhancements that give him an animalistic appearance in the "[[Ultimate Six]]" storyline,<ref>{{cite comic|title=Ultimate Six|issue=1–7|publisher=Marvel Comics|date=2003–2004}}</ref> but later appears with his enhancements removed by "The Death of Spider-Man" storyline.<ref>{{cite comic|title=Ultimate Spider-Man|issue=156-159|date=2009|publisher=Marvel Comics}}</ref>

===Ultimate Universe===
In the [[Ultimate Universe]] imprint, Kraven is a billionaire oligarch whose family was exiled from Russia during the "Rasputin Purge". Additionally, he is a lieutenant of [[Kingpin (character)#Ultimate Universe|Wilson Fisk]] who controls [[Staten Island]] on his behalf and later joins his Sinister Six to combat the [[Harry Osborn#Ultimate Universe|Green Goblin]] and [[Spider-Man (Ultimate Universe character)|Spider-Man]].<ref>''Ultimate Spider Man'' (vol. 3) #8</ref>
